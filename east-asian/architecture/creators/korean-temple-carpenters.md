# Goryeo and Joseon temple carpenters

*고려·조선 사찰 목수*

**active 13th – 19th century** · Korean  
Styles & eras: [Three Kingdoms, Silla & Goryeo Buddhist architecture](../movements/korean-buddhist.md), [Joseon palaces & seowon](../movements/joseon-palaces-seowon.md)  

> The carpenters of Korea's mountain monasteries, who built the oldest surviving timber halls in Korea.

## Biography

Korean Buddhist temples were built and repaired by monk-carpenters and lay craftsmen, often over centuries, and their names appear only occasionally in inscriptions found on roof beams during repairs. Temples in remote mountains survived wars better than those in the cities, so they preserve the oldest timber buildings of the country.

Their halls of the Goryeo period, with column-head bracketing and gently swelling columns, are notable for restraint and proportion, and their storage halls at Haeinsa show remarkable practical knowledge of climate. Seven mountain monasteries were inscribed together as a World Heritage Site in 2018.

### Geungnakjeon of Bongjeongsa (13th century or earlier)

[![Geungnakjeon of Bongjeongsa](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f8/%EC%95%88%EB%8F%99%EB%B4%89%EC%A0%95%EC%82%AC%EC%A0%84%EA%B2%BD.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%EC%95%88%EB%8F%99%EB%B4%89%EC%A0%95%EC%82%AC%EC%A0%84%EA%B2%BD.jpg)

<sub>Bongjeongsa, Andong Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%EC%95%88%EB%8F%99%EB%B4%89%EC%A0%95%EC%82%AC%EC%A0%84%EA%B2%BD.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 13th century or earlier
- **Type:** Buddhist hall
- **Location:** Andong, North Gyeongsang Province, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Geungnakjeon%20of%20Bongjeongsa%20Goryeo%20and%20Joseon%20temple%20carpenters)

Bongjeongsa is a mountain temple near Andong founded in the late seventh century. Its Geungnakjeon, the 'Hall of Paradise', is a small hall of three bays by four under a gabled roof. A document found during dismantling in 1972 recorded a repair in 1363, implying that the hall was built earlier, probably in the thirteenth century, which makes it the oldest dated timber building in Korea. Its column-head brackets and proportions preserve the style of the Goryeo period. The temple was visited by Queen Elizabeth II in 1999, and it forms part of the Sansa World Heritage Site of Korean Buddhist mountain monasteries.

### Muryangsujeon of Buseoksa (1376 (rebuilt))

[![Muryangsujeon of Buseoksa](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/25/%EB%B6%80%EC%84%9D%EC%82%AC_%EB%AC%B4%EB%9F%89%EC%88%98%EC%A0%84.jpg/1280px-%EB%B6%80%EC%84%9D%EC%82%AC_%EB%AC%B4%EB%9F%89%EC%88%98%EC%A0%84.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%EB%B6%80%EC%84%9D%EC%82%AC_%EB%AC%B4%EB%9F%89%EC%88%98%EC%A0%84.jpg)

<sub>Muryangsujeon, Buseoksa Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%EB%B6%80%EC%84%9D%EC%82%AC_%EB%AC%B4%EB%9F%89%EC%88%98%EC%A0%84.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1376 (rebuilt)
- **Type:** Buddhist hall
- **Location:** Yeongju, North Gyeongsang Province, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Muryangsujeon%20of%20Buseoksa%20Goryeo%20and%20Joseon%20temple%20carpenters)

Buseoksa was founded in 676 by the monk Uisang, who brought the Hwaeom (Huayan) school of Buddhism from Tang China. Its main hall, the Muryangsujeon, 'Hall of Infinite Life' dedicated to the Buddha Amitabha, was rebuilt in 1376 after a fire, according to an inscription found during repairs, though parts may be older. It is five bays by three, with columns that swell gently in the middle and bracket sets only on the column heads, a simple and dignified structure under a hipped and gabled roof. Inside sits a clay Amitabha facing east, an unusual orientation. The temple is set on terraces on a mountainside with a sweeping view of ranges beyond. It is part of the World Heritage Site of Korean Buddhist mountain monasteries (Sansa).

### Janggyeong Panjeon of Haeinsa (1488)

[![Janggyeong Panjeon of Haeinsa](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/20170812.%ED%95%B4%EC%9D%B8%EC%82%AC_%EC%9E%A5%EA%B2%BD%ED%8C%90%EC%A0%84%28Haeinsa_Janggyeong_Panjeon%29.01.jpg/1280px-20170812.%ED%95%B4%EC%9D%B8%EC%82%AC_%EC%9E%A5%EA%B2%BD%ED%8C%90%EC%A0%84%28Haeinsa_Janggyeong_Panjeon%29.01.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:20170812.%ED%95%B4%EC%9D%B8%EC%82%AC_%EC%9E%A5%EA%B2%BD%ED%8C%90%EC%A0%84%28Haeinsa_Janggyeong_Panjeon%29.01.jpg)

<sub>Janggyeong Panjeon storage halls, Haeinsa Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:20170812.%ED%95%B4%EC%9D%B8%EC%82%AC_%EC%9E%A5%EA%B2%BD%ED%8C%90%EC%A0%84%28Haeinsa_Janggyeong_Panjeon%29.01.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1488
- **Type:** Woodblock repository
- **Location:** Mount Gaya, Hapcheon, South Gyeongsang Province, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Janggyeong%20Panjeon%20of%20Haeinsa%20Goryeo%20and%20Joseon%20temple%20carpenters)

The Tripitaka Koreana, the complete Buddhist canon carved on more than 81,000 wooden printing blocks between 1237 and 1248 as a prayer for protection against the Mongols, was moved to Haeinsa in 1398. The present storage halls were built or rebuilt in 1488 under the Joseon dynasty. Four long, plain timber buildings set on the slope are designed to control temperature and humidity naturally: windows of different sizes on the front and back walls, upper and lower, promote air circulation, and the earthen floors contain charcoal, lime and salt to absorb moisture. Modern climate-controlled storage built nearby proved less effective. The blocks have survived war and fire with little damage. The Janggyeong Panjeon became a World Heritage Site in 1995 and the woodblocks are on the Memory of the World register.
