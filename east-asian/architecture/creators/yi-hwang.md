# Yi Hwang

*이황 (李滉)*

**1501 – 1570** · Korean (Joseon)  
Born: Ongye, Andong · Died: Andong  
Styles & eras: [Joseon palaces & seowon](../movements/joseon-palaces-seowon.md)  
Also in: [Philosophy](../../philosophy/creators/yi-hwang.md)  

> The greatest Neo-Confucian philosopher of Joseon Korea, who built the Dosan study hall from which the Dosan Seowon academy grew.

## Biography

Yi Hwang, known as Toegye, passed the civil service examination in 1534 and held a series of offices, but repeatedly withdrew to his home district near Andong to study and teach. In 1561 he completed the Dosan Seodang, a small study hall and teaching building whose design he planned himself, on a site facing the Nakdong River. Disciples gathered there in great numbers.

After his death in 1570 his followers built the Dosan Seowon around the hall in 1574, and the king granted it a royal charter the following year. The modest seodang, with its tiny room, veranda and small pond, expresses Yi Hwang's ideal of a life of quiet self-cultivation, and the academy became the model for Confucian seowon across Korea.

## Key buildings & sites

### Dosan Seowon (1561 (Dosan Seodang); academy 1574)

[![Dosan Seowon](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6b/Dosanseowon1.jpg/1280px-Dosanseowon1.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dosanseowon1.jpg)

<sub>Dosan Seowon, Andong Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dosanseowon1.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1561 (Dosan Seodang); academy 1574
- **Type:** Confucian academy
- **Location:** Andong, North Gyeongsang Province, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dosan%20Seowon%20Yi%20Hwang)

Yi Hwang built the Dosan Seodang in 1561 as a place to study and teach, and planned it himself: a simple three-bay timber building with a small heated room, a wooden-floored hall and a kitchen, beside a small square lotus pond. After his death his disciples built the Dosan Seowon around it in 1574, with a lecture hall, dormitories for students, a printing block storehouse and a shrine to Yi Hwang at the top of the slope, and King Seonjo granted it a royal charter the following year. The academy's terraces rise up the hillside in an order that expresses the Confucian hierarchy of learning and ritual. It survived the closure of most seowon in 1871 and is one of nine Confucian academies inscribed together as the Seowon World Heritage Site in 2019.
