# Frank Lloyd Wright

*フランク・ロイド・ライト*

**1867 – 1959** · American  
Born: Richland Center, Wisconsin · Died: Phoenix, Arizona  
Styles & eras: [Meiji Western-style & early modern](../movements/meiji-western.md)  

> American architect whose Imperial Hotel in Tokyo, built 1917–23, was a landmark of modern architecture in Japan.

## Biography

Wright studied briefly at the University of Wisconsin and worked for Louis Sullivan in Chicago before opening his own practice in 1893. He visited Japan in 1905, became a major collector and dealer of Japanese woodblock prints, and acknowledged the influence of Japanese architecture on his open plans and low, sheltering roofs. From 1917 to 1922 he spent long periods in Tokyo designing the new Imperial Hotel, together with smaller works such as the Myōnichikan school building and the Yamamura house in Ashiya. His assistants there included Antonin Raymond and Arata Endō.

The Imperial Hotel, of brick and carved Ōya stone on a floating foundation, opened in 1923 and famously survived the Great Kantō earthquake. Its demolition in 1968 caused protests, and its entrance and lobby were reconstructed at the Meiji Mura museum in Inuyama. Wright's Japanese years left a body of buildings and pupils, and his 'organic architecture' found many admirers among later Japanese architects.

- **Date:** 1921
- **Type:** School building
- **Location:** Nishi-Ikebukuro, Toshima, Tokyo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jiy%C5%AB%20Gakuen%20My%C5%8Dnichikan%20Frank%20Lloyd%20Wright)

Hani Motoko and her husband Yoshikazu, journalists and educational reformers, founded the Jiyū Gakuen ('School of Freedom') for girls in 1921, and asked Frank Lloyd Wright, then in Tokyo for the Imperial Hotel, to design the building, with Endō Arata. The Myōnichikan, the 'House of Tomorrow', is a symmetrical, low timber and stucco building in the manner of Wright's Prairie houses, with a central hall lit by tall geometrical windows and two wings of classrooms embracing a lawn. After the school moved, the building was used by its alumnae. It was designated an Important Cultural Property in 1997 and restored, and is open to visitors and used for events.

### Imperial Hotel (1917–1923 (demolished 1968; lobby rebuilt at Meiji Mura 1976))

[![Imperial Hotel](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/54/Main_Entrance_Hall_and_Lobby%2C_Imperial_Hotel_in_Meiji_Mura_2022.jpg/1280px-Main_Entrance_Hall_and_Lobby%2C_Imperial_Hotel_in_Meiji_Mura_2022.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Main_Entrance_Hall_and_Lobby%2C_Imperial_Hotel_in_Meiji_Mura_2022.jpg)

<sub>Entrance hall and lobby of the Imperial Hotel, reconstructed at Meiji Mura, Inuyama Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Main_Entrance_Hall_and_Lobby%2C_Imperial_Hotel_in_Meiji_Mura_2022.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1917–1923 (demolished 1968; lobby rebuilt at Meiji Mura 1976)
- **Type:** Hotel
- **Location:** Uchisaiwaichō, Tokyo, Japan; entrance now at Meiji Mura, Inuyama
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Imperial%20Hotel%20Frank%20Lloyd%20Wright)

The Imperial Hotel, Japan's main hotel for foreign guests, commissioned Frank Lloyd Wright in 1916 to design a new building. Construction, delayed by costs and a fire, ran from 1919 to 1923, and Wright left before it was complete, with his Japanese assistant Endō Arata finishing it. The hotel had an H-shaped plan of low wings around courts and pools, built of reinforced concrete and brick faced with yellow tapestry brick and carved Ōya stone, a soft volcanic tuff, with intricate geometrical ornament. Wright designed its foundations to 'float' on the mud and cantilevered its floors to resist earthquakes. It came through the Great Kantō earthquake of 1 September 1923, the day of its formal opening, with little damage, a story Wright made famous. The building was demolished in 1968 despite protests, and its entrance hall and lobby were reconstructed at the Meiji Mura open-air museum in Inuyama.
