# Li Chun

*李春*

**active c. 595–605** · Chinese (Sui)  
Styles & eras: [Tang & Liao timber halls](../movements/tang-liao-timber.md)  

> Sui dynasty stonemason credited with the Anji (Zhaozhou) Bridge, the world's oldest surviving open-spandrel segmental stone arch bridge.

## Biography

Almost nothing is known of Li Chun's life. His name survives through a Tang dynasty inscription recording that the great stone bridge over the Xiao River at Zhaozhou, in present-day Hebei, was the work of the craftsman Li Chun under the Sui. The bridge is usually dated to about 595–605.

The bridge he built was remarkably advanced. Instead of a semicircular arch it uses a flat segmental arch of about 37 metres, and four small arches pierce the spandrels, saving stone, lightening the load on the foundations and letting floodwater pass. Comparable designs did not appear in Europe for many centuries. Li Chun is honoured in China as one of the great engineers of antiquity.

## Key buildings & sites

### Anji Bridge (Zhaozhou Bridge) (c. 595–605)

[![Anji Bridge (Zhaozhou Bridge)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/03/Zhaozhou_Bridge.jpg/1280px-Zhaozhou_Bridge.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Zhaozhou_Bridge.jpg)

<sub>Anji Bridge, Zhao County Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Zhaozhou_Bridge.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 595–605
- **Type:** Stone arch bridge
- **Location:** Zhao County, Hebei, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Anji%20Bridge%20%28Zhaozhou%20Bridge%29%20Li%20Chun)

The Anji Bridge crosses the Xiao River at Zhao County, and a Tang inscription records that it was built by the craftsman Li Chun in the Sui dynasty. Its single arch spans about 37 metres but rises only about 7 metres, a flat segment of a circle rather than a semicircle, so that carts could cross easily. Two small arches open in each spandrel, reducing the weight of the bridge by hundreds of tonnes and letting floodwater pass through. The arch is built of 28 parallel rings of limestone blocks tied with iron cramps. The design was centuries ahead of European practice, where open-spandrel segmental arches became common only in the modern era. The bridge has been repaired many times, most heavily in the 1950s, when its balustrades were largely replaced.
