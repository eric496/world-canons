# Fumihiko Maki

*槇文彦*

**1928 – 2024** · Japanese  
Born: Tokyo · Died: Tokyo  
Styles & eras: [Japanese modernism & Metabolism](../movements/metabolism-modernism.md), [Contemporary architecture](../movements/east-asian-contemporary-arch.md)  

> Japanese architect of quietly crafted modernism, associated with Metabolism, and winner of the Pritzker Prize in 1993.

## Biography

Maki studied under Kenzō Tange at the University of Tokyo and then at Cranbrook and Harvard, and taught at Washington University in St. Louis and Harvard. He took part in the Metabolism manifesto of 1960 with an essay on 'group form', the idea that cities grow from collections of related buildings rather than single megastructures. He returned to Tokyo to found his office in 1965.

His Hillside Terrace in Daikanyama, built in phases from 1969 to 1992, is a celebrated example of incremental urban design. Spiral (1985), the Tokyo Metropolitan Gymnasium (1990) and 4 World Trade Center in New York (2013) show his precise use of metal, glass and layered space. He continued to practise into his nineties and died in 2024.

### Hillside Terrace (1969–1992)

[![Hillside Terrace](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Hillside_Terrace%2C_Building_C_20241020.jpg/1280px-Hillside_Terrace%2C_Building_C_20241020.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hillside_Terrace%2C_Building_C_20241020.jpg)

<sub>Hillside Terrace, Building C Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hillside_Terrace%2C_Building_C_20241020.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1969–1992
- **Type:** Housing, shops and galleries
- **Location:** Daikanyama, Shibuya, Tokyo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Hillside%20Terrace%20Fumihiko%20Maki)

In 1967 the Asakura family, landowners in Daikanyama, asked Fumihiko Maki to design housing and shops on their property along the Kyū-Yamate Avenue. He built the complex in six phases between 1969 and 1992, adapting each to new needs, materials and regulations while keeping a consistent low scale, small courtyards, setbacks, passages and public spaces that open the buildings to the street. The early phases are in white concrete, the later ones in metal and glass. The result is a piece of city rather than a single object, an application of Maki's idea of 'group form'. Hillside Terrace helped turn Daikanyama into one of Tokyo's most pleasant neighbourhoods and is often cited as a model of patient, collaborative development.

### Spiral (1985)

[![Spiral](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Spiral_house_Tokyo.jpg/1280px-Spiral_house_Tokyo.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Spiral_house_Tokyo.jpg)

<sub>Spiral, Aoyama Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Spiral_house_Tokyo.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1985
- **Type:** Arts centre and shops
- **Location:** Minami-Aoyama, Minato, Tokyo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Spiral%20Fumihiko%20Maki)

Spiral was built in 1985 for the Wacoal company as a cultural complex on Aoyama-dōri, with a gallery, hall, shops and restaurant. Its street facade is a carefully composed collage of grids, a cone, a cube and fragments of aluminium and glass that mirror the variety of the surrounding city. Inside, a spiralling ramp curves up around the edge of a semicircular top-lit gallery behind the entrance, giving the building its name. It is often seen as one of the best works of 1980s Tokyo, showing how a small urban building can create a public interior.
