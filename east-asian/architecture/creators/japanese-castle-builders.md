# Japanese castle builders

*城郭の工人*

**active 1570s – 1650s** · Japanese  
Styles & eras: [Momoyama & early Edo castles and palaces](../movements/momoyama-castles.md)  

> The daimyō, master carpenters and stonemasons who built the castles of the age of unification.

## Biography

Castles were commissioned by warlords and planned by them and their retainers, with the stone bases built by specialist masons such as the Anō guild of Ōmi, and the timber keeps raised by master carpenters. Construction of a large castle mobilised thousands of workers under the direction of the daimyō.

Their castles combined defence against firearms with display of power, crowning high stone walls with multi-storeyed white or black keeps. In 1615 the Tokugawa limited each domain to one castle, and after 1873 most were demolished; only twelve original keeps survive.

### Matsumoto Castle (c. 1593–94; additions 1630s)

[![Matsumoto Castle](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e3/Matsumoto_Castle_Keep_Tower.jpg/1280px-Matsumoto_Castle_Keep_Tower.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Matsumoto_Castle_Keep_Tower.jpg)

<sub>Keep of Matsumoto Castle Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Matsumoto_Castle_Keep_Tower.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1593–94; additions 1630s
- **Type:** Castle
- **Location:** Matsumoto, Nagano Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Matsumoto%20Castle%20Japanese%20castle%20builders)

Matsumoto Castle was built on the flat land of the Matsumoto basin rather than on a hill, and so relies on a wide moat for defence. Its main keep and attached smaller keep are usually dated to the 1590s, when the domain was held by Ishikawa Kazumasa and his son Yasunaga, though the exact date is debated. Its walls are clad in black lacquered boards, which earned it the nickname 'Crow Castle'. In the more peaceful 1630s a turret for moon-viewing with an open veranda was added, a contrast between military and leisure architecture. After the Meiji Restoration the castle was sold for demolition, but local citizens bought it back, and it was later restored. Its keep is one of five designated National Treasures.

### Himeji Castle (1601–1609)

[![Himeji Castle](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c1/Himeji_castle_in_may_2015.jpg/1280px-Himeji_castle_in_may_2015.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Himeji_castle_in_may_2015.jpg)

<sub>Himeji Castle Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Himeji_castle_in_may_2015.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1601–1609
- **Type:** Castle
- **Location:** Himeji, Hyōgo Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Himeji%20Castle%20Japanese%20castle%20builders)

A fort on Himeyama hill was enlarged by Toyotomi Hideyoshi in the 1580s, and after the battle of Sekigahara Tokugawa Ieyasu's son-in-law Ikeda Terumasa rebuilt it on a grand scale between 1601 and 1609. The main keep rises from a high battered stone base to five roofs outside, with six floors and a basement inside, linked by corridors to three smaller keeps. Its walls are coated in white plaster for fire resistance, and its gables and layered roofs give it a soaring, bird-like appearance. A spiralling maze of gates, baileys and walls, with loopholes for guns and arrows and openings for dropping stones, protected the approach. Of the castle's buildings, more than eighty survive. It escaped the demolitions of the Meiji period and the bombing of 1945, and was restored in the 1950s and again from 2009 to 2015. In 1993 it became one of Japan's first World Heritage Sites.
