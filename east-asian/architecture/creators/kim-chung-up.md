# Kim Chung-up

*김중업 (金重業)*

**1922 – 1988** · Korean  
Born: Pyongyang · Died: Seoul  
Styles & eras: [Japanese modernism & Metabolism](../movements/metabolism-modernism.md)  

> South Korean architect who worked for Le Corbusier and designed the French Embassy in Seoul.

## Biography

Kim Chung-up studied architecture in Yokohama and in 1952 joined the studio of Le Corbusier in Paris, where he worked until 1956 on projects including Chandigarh. Back in Seoul he founded his own office and taught. His criticism of the government's urban policies led to his forced exile in 1971; he lived in France and the United States until 1979.

The French Embassy in Seoul (1962), with its sweeping concrete roofs evoking traditional Korean eaves, is regarded as a landmark of Korean modern architecture. Kim's other works include the Samil Building (1970), once the tallest building in Seoul, and the World Peace Gate at the Olympic Park (1988).

## Key buildings & sites

### Embassy of France, Seoul (1962)

- **Date:** 1962
- **Type:** Embassy
- **Location:** Hapdong, Seodaemun, Seoul, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Embassy%20of%20France%2C%20Seoul%20Kim%20Chung-up)

After winning the commission for the French Embassy in Seoul, Kim Chung-up, who had worked in Le Corbusier's studio, designed a complex of buildings completed in 1962 on a hillside. The ambassador's residence and offices are crowned by thin concrete roofs with dramatically upswept corners, a modern reinterpretation of the curved eaves of traditional Korean architecture, set on pilotis among gardens. The building is widely regarded as a landmark of Korean modern architecture, combining Le Corbusier's lessons with a search for national identity. The embassy has since been renovated, with Kim's main buildings kept.
