# SANAA (Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa)

*妹島和世・西沢立衛*

**founded 1995** · Japanese  
Born: Tokyo · Died: ?  
Styles & eras: [Contemporary architecture](../movements/east-asian-contemporary-arch.md)  

> Tokyo practice of Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa, known for light, transparent buildings, winner of the Pritzker Prize in 2010.

## Biography

Kazuyo Sejima (born 1956) studied at Japan Women's University and worked for Toyo Itō before founding her own office in 1987. Ryue Nishizawa (born 1966) studied at Yokohama National University and joined her; in 1995 they founded SANAA (Sejima and Nishizawa and Associates), while each keeps a separate practice. Sejima directed the Venice Architecture Biennale in 2010, the year the two received the Pritzker Prize.

SANAA's buildings dissolve mass into thin white planes, glass and open, non-hierarchical plans. The 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art in Kanazawa (2004), a glass disc containing scattered galleries, the New Museum in New York, the Rolex Learning Center in Lausanne and the Louvre-Lens are among their works. Nishizawa's Teshima Art Museum (2010), a concrete shell open to the sky, made with the artist Rei Naitō, is often counted among the finest recent Japanese buildings.

### 21st Century Museum of Contemporary Art, Kanazawa (2004)

- **Date:** 2004
- **Type:** Museum
- **Location:** Kanazawa, Ishikawa Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=21st%20Century%20Museum%20of%20Contemporary%20Art%2C%20Kanazawa%20SANAA%20%28Kazuyo%20Sejima%20and%20Ryue%20Nishizawa%29)

The 21st Century Museum in Kanazawa, opened in 2004, was designed by Kazuyo Sejima and Ryue Nishizawa as a single-storey disc about 112 metres in diameter, enclosed by glass walls and entered from four sides, so that it has no front or back and invites people in from the surrounding park and city. Within the disc, galleries of different sizes and heights are scattered like boxes, separated by corridors and light courts, with free public areas in between. The best-known artwork, Leandro Erlich's swimming pool, lets visitors appear to stand under water. The building won the Golden Lion at the Venice Biennale of 2004 and helped make SANAA internationally famous.

### Teshima Art Museum (2010)

[![Teshima Art Museum](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Teshima_Art_Museum_exterior_view_201310.jpg/1280px-Teshima_Art_Museum_exterior_view_201310.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Teshima_Art_Museum_exterior_view_201310.jpg)

<sub>Teshima Art Museum Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Teshima_Art_Museum_exterior_view_201310.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 2010
- **Type:** Museum
- **Location:** Teshima, Kagawa Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Teshima%20Art%20Museum%20SANAA%20%28Kazuyo%20Sejima%20and%20Ryue%20Nishizawa%29)

On a hillside among rice terraces on the island of Teshima in the Inland Sea, Ryue Nishizawa and the artist Rei Naitō created in 2010 a museum for a single work. The building is a thin, low concrete shell about 40 by 60 metres and only about 4.5 metres high at its highest point, with no columns, shaped like a drop of water. It was made by pouring concrete over a carefully shaped mound of earth, which was then dug out. Two large oval openings let in light, wind, rain and the sound of birds. On the floor, tiny springs release droplets of water that gather and flow across the surface in Naitō's work Matrix. Visitors are asked to remove their shoes and be silent. It is part of the Setouchi art islands project.
