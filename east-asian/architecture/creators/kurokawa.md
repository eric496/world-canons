# Kishō Kurokawa

*黒川紀章*

**1934 – 2007** · Japanese  
Born: Kanie, Aichi · Died: Tokyo  
Styles & eras: [Japanese modernism & Metabolism](../movements/metabolism-modernism.md), [Contemporary architecture](../movements/east-asian-contemporary-arch.md)  

> Metabolist architect of the Nakagin Capsule Tower and later the National Art Center, Tokyo, and a theorist of 'symbiosis'.

## Biography

Kurokawa studied at Kyoto University and then under Kenzō Tange at the University of Tokyo. At twenty-six he was the youngest member of the Metabolist group in 1960. He became one of the best-known architects in Japan, a media figure and prolific writer, and built extensively abroad, including the Kuala Lumpur International Airport and the Van Gogh Museum extension in Amsterdam.

His Nakagin Capsule Tower (1972) put the Metabolist idea of replaceable units into practice, with prefabricated capsules bolted to two concrete cores. Later he developed a philosophy of 'symbiosis' between tradition and technology and between humans and nature, seen in the undulating glass facade of the National Art Center, Tokyo (2007). He ran for governor of Tokyo in the year of his death.

### Nakagin Capsule Tower (1970–1972 (demolished 2022))

[![Nakagin Capsule Tower](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Nakagin.jpg/960px-Nakagin.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nakagin.jpg)

<sub>Nakagin Capsule Tower Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nakagin.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1970–1972 (demolished 2022)
- **Type:** Capsule apartments and offices
- **Location:** Ginza, Tokyo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Nakagin%20Capsule%20Tower%20Kish%C5%8D%20Kurokawa)

Kishō Kurokawa's Nakagin Capsule Tower, completed in 1972 in Ginza, consisted of 140 prefabricated steel capsules, each a tiny studio of about 10 square metres with a built-in bed, bathroom unit and a round window, attached to two concrete cores of eleven and thirteen storeys. The capsules were factory-made, delivered by truck and bolted to the cores, and were intended as pieds-à-terre for businessmen. In principle they could be replaced every few decades, the Metabolist ideal of a building that renews itself, but none ever was. The building decayed, and despite international campaigns for its preservation it was demolished in 2022. Some capsules were salvaged and restored for museums and other uses.

### National Art Center, Tokyo (2007)

[![National Art Center, Tokyo](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/The_National_Art_Center_%2C_Tokyo_-_panoramio_%281%29.jpg/1280px-The_National_Art_Center_%2C_Tokyo_-_panoramio_%281%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_National_Art_Center_%2C_Tokyo_-_panoramio_%281%29.jpg)

<sub>National Art Center, Tokyo Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_National_Art_Center_%2C_Tokyo_-_panoramio_%281%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 2007
- **Type:** Exhibition centre
- **Location:** Roppongi, Minato, Tokyo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=National%20Art%20Center%2C%20Tokyo%20Kish%C5%8D%20Kurokawa)

The National Art Center opened in Roppongi in 2007, shortly before Kishō Kurokawa's death, as a venue for temporary exhibitions without a permanent collection. Its main facade is a wall of glass louvres that ripples in a wave along the front of the building, filtering sunlight into a vast atrium. Inside the atrium two inverted concrete cones rise from the floor, carrying a restaurant and café at their tops. Behind lie twelve large exhibition rooms. The building embodies Kurokawa's late idea of symbiosis between architecture and nature, and has become one of the most visited art venues in Japan.
