# Tang dynasty carpenters

*唐代木匠*

**active 7th – 10th century** · Chinese  
Styles & eras: [Tang & Liao timber halls](../movements/tang-liao-timber.md)  

> The anonymous master carpenters of the Tang dynasty, whose only surviving buildings in China are a few remote temple halls.

## Biography

The great Tang capital of Chang'an and its palaces and monasteries were built by official workshops and guilds of carpenters whose names are almost entirely lost. The persecution of Buddhism in 845 and later wars destroyed nearly all Tang timber buildings, leaving only a handful of halls in the mountains of Shanxi.

Their work is known also from murals, tomb models and, above all, from Japan, where temples such as Tōshōdai-ji were built by craftsmen trained in Tang methods. The Tang system of columns, interlocking bracket sets and deep eaves became the classical language of East Asian timber architecture.

### Great Buddha Hall of Nanchan Temple (782)

[![Great Buddha Hall of Nanchan Temple](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/%E5%8D%97%E7%A6%85%E5%AF%BA%E5%A4%A7%E6%AE%BF%E6%AD%A3%E9%9D%A2.jpg/1280px-%E5%8D%97%E7%A6%85%E5%AF%BA%E5%A4%A7%E6%AE%BF%E6%AD%A3%E9%9D%A2.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%E5%8D%97%E7%A6%85%E5%AF%BA%E5%A4%A7%E6%AE%BF%E6%AD%A3%E9%9D%A2.jpg)

- **Date:** 782
- **Type:** Buddhist hall
- **Location:** Mount Wutai, Shanxi, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Great%20Buddha%20Hall%20of%20Nanchan%20Temple%20Tang%20dynasty%20carpenters)

The main hall of Nanchan Temple, on the slopes of the sacred Buddhist mountain Wutai, carries an inscription on a beam recording its rebuilding in 782. It is a modest hall of three bays by three under a hipped and gabled roof, with no interior columns, so that the roof is carried entirely by a frame of beams and bracket sets resting on the outer columns. Its small size and remote location probably saved it during the great persecution of Buddhism in 845, when thousands of temples were destroyed. The hall still contains a group of Tang clay sculptures on a platform. It was identified by Chinese researchers in the 1950s and restored in the 1970s, when later alterations were removed to bring it closer to its Tang form.

### East Hall of Foguang Temple (857)

[![East Hall of Foguang Temple](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/%E4%BD%9B%E5%85%89%E5%AF%BA%E4%B8%9C%E5%A4%A7%E6%AE%BF%E5%B7%A6%E4%BE%A7%E9%9D%A2.jpg/1280px-%E4%BD%9B%E5%85%89%E5%AF%BA%E4%B8%9C%E5%A4%A7%E6%AE%BF%E5%B7%A6%E4%BE%A7%E9%9D%A2.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%E4%BD%9B%E5%85%89%E5%AF%BA%E4%B8%9C%E5%A4%A7%E6%AE%BF%E5%B7%A6%E4%BE%A7%E9%9D%A2.jpg)

<sub>East Hall of Foguang Temple Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%E4%BD%9B%E5%85%89%E5%AF%BA%E4%B8%9C%E5%A4%A7%E6%AE%BF%E5%B7%A6%E4%BE%A7%E9%9D%A2.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 857
- **Type:** Buddhist hall
- **Location:** Mount Wutai, Shanxi, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=East%20Hall%20of%20Foguang%20Temple%20Tang%20dynasty%20carpenters)

Foguang Temple was rebuilt after the persecution of 845, and its East Hall, completed in 857, was paid for by a woman donor named Ning Gongyu, whose name Lin Huiyin found inscribed on a roof beam. The hall is seven bays wide and four deep, raised on a terrace on the hillside. Its powerful bracket sets, nearly half the height of the columns, carry deep eaves that project about four metres, and the interior combines an outer ring of columns and an inner ring around a platform crowded with Tang sculptures. In 1937 Liang Sicheng, Lin Huiyin and colleagues reached the temple by mule, guided by a Dunhuang mural that showed it, and identified it as a Tang building, then the only one known. Their survey made the hall the classic example of Tang timber architecture and confirmed the system described in later manuals. It is part of the Mount Wutai World Heritage Site.
