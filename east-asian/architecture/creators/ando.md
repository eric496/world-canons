# Tadao Andō

*安藤忠雄*

**1941** · Japanese  
Born: Osaka · Died: ?  
Styles & eras: [Contemporary architecture](../movements/east-asian-contemporary-arch.md)  

> Self-taught Japanese architect of meditative buildings in smooth concrete and light, winner of the Pritzker Prize in 1995.

## Biography

Andō grew up in Osaka, worked as a truck driver and boxed professionally before teaching himself architecture through books and travel, including visits to the buildings of Le Corbusier. He opened his office in Osaka in 1969. The small Row House in Sumiyoshi (Azuma House, 1976), with a courtyard open to the sky splitting the house in two, made his name.

Andō builds with precisely cast concrete, simple geometry, water and light, aiming at spaces of contemplation that echo the austerity of Zen and the tea house without imitating them. The Church of the Light (1989), the Water Temple on Awaji Island (1991) and the art museums on Naoshima, including the largely underground Chichu Art Museum (2004), are among his works; he has built widely in Europe, America and China.

### Row House in Sumiyoshi (Azuma House) (1976)

[![Row House in Sumiyoshi (Azuma House)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Azuma_house.JPG/960px-Azuma_house.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Azuma_house.JPG)

<sub>Row House in Sumiyoshi Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Azuma_house.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1976
- **Type:** House
- **Location:** Sumiyoshi, Osaka, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Row%20House%20in%20Sumiyoshi%20%28Azuma%20House%29%20Tadao%20And%C5%8D)

In a dense neighbourhood of old wooden row houses in Osaka, Tadao Andō replaced one narrow house with a closed box of exposed concrete, about 3.6 metres wide and 14 metres deep, with no windows to the street and only a single doorway. The interior is divided into three equal parts, of which the central one is an open courtyard; to go from the living room to the kitchen or the bedroom, the occupants must pass through the open air, even in rain. Andō argued that this restored contact with nature and the seasons in the city. The house won the Architectural Institute of Japan prize in 1979 and established his reputation and his language of concrete, light and geometry.

### Church of the Light (1989)

[![Church of the Light](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4b/Ibaraki_Kasugaoka_Church_light_cross.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ibaraki_Kasugaoka_Church_light_cross.jpg)

<sub>Cross of light, Church of the Light Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ibaraki_Kasugaoka_Church_light_cross.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1989
- **Type:** Church
- **Location:** Kasugaoka, Ibaraki, Osaka Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Church%20of%20the%20Light%20Tadao%20And%C5%8D)

The Ibaraki Kasugaoka Church, a small Protestant congregation in a suburb of Osaka, had a very limited budget for its new chapel, completed in 1989. Tadao Andō designed a concrete box crossed at an angle by a freestanding wall that defines the entrance. Behind the altar the end wall is cut by a cruciform opening from floor to ceiling, so that the cross is made of daylight; the floor and pews are of rough-sawn timber and descend towards it. Andō had wanted the openings left unglazed, but glass was installed. A Sunday school building was added in 1999. The chapel has become one of the most famous religious buildings of the late twentieth century.

### Chichu Art Museum (2004)

[![Chichu Art Museum](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/150505_Chichu_Art_Museum_Naoshima_Island_Kagawa_pref_Japan01s3.jpg/1280px-150505_Chichu_Art_Museum_Naoshima_Island_Kagawa_pref_Japan01s3.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:150505_Chichu_Art_Museum_Naoshima_Island_Kagawa_pref_Japan01s3.jpg)

<sub>Chichu Art Museum, Naoshima Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:150505_Chichu_Art_Museum_Naoshima_Island_Kagawa_pref_Japan01s3.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 2004
- **Type:** Museum
- **Location:** Naoshima, Kagawa Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Chichu%20Art%20Museum%20Tadao%20And%C5%8D)

The Chichu Art Museum was built in 2004 for the Benesse foundation on Naoshima, an island that has become a centre of contemporary art. To preserve the landscape of a former salt field, Tadao Andō placed the museum underground, revealing only geometric openings, courtyards and slots in the hillside. It houses permanent installations of a few artists: late Water Lilies by Claude Monet in a white room lit from above, a large work by Walter De Maria and light installations by James Turrell. The rooms are lit mainly by natural daylight, so that the experience of the art changes with the time of day and the season. It is one of several buildings by Andō on the island.
