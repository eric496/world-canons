# Silla builders

*신라 장인*

**active 6th – 10th century** · Korean  
Styles & eras: [Three Kingdoms, Silla & Goryeo Buddhist architecture](../movements/korean-buddhist.md)  

> The anonymous builders and craftsmen of the Silla kingdom at Gyeongju.

## Biography

After unifying most of the Korean peninsula in 668, Silla made its capital Gyeongju a city of palaces, temples, tombs and gardens. Royal patrons and ministers are known by name, but the builders of its works are not.

Their surviving works, in stone and earth, show precise geometry and refined proportions: the observatory tower of Cheomseongdae, the pond garden of Wolji and the masonry of Bulguksa and Seokguram. The Gyeongju Historic Areas were inscribed as a World Heritage Site in 2000.

### Cheomseongdae (7th century)

[![Cheomseongdae](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Cheomseongdae_Observatory_under_blue_sky_in_Gyeongju_South_Korea.jpg/1280px-Cheomseongdae_Observatory_under_blue_sky_in_Gyeongju_South_Korea.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cheomseongdae_Observatory_under_blue_sky_in_Gyeongju_South_Korea.jpg)

<sub>Cheomseongdae, Gyeongju Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cheomseongdae_Observatory_under_blue_sky_in_Gyeongju_South_Korea.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 7th century
- **Type:** Astronomical tower
- **Location:** Gyeongju, North Gyeongsang Province, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Cheomseongdae%20Silla%20builders)

Cheomseongdae stands in the old Silla capital Gyeongju and is traditionally dated to the reign of Queen Seondeok (632–647). It is a tower about 9 metres high made of 27 courses of curved granite blocks forming a bottle-like profile, topped by a square frame of long stones, with a square opening halfway up. Its use has been debated: the usual view is that it was an astronomical observatory, but some scholars have seen it as a symbolic monument relating to the calendar or Buddhist cosmology, or as a ritual altar. Numerological readings of the numbers of stones and courses are popular but uncertain. It is part of the Gyeongju Historic Areas World Heritage Site.

### Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond (674)

[![Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Water_reflection_of_Donggung_Palace_in_Wolji_Pond_at_blue_hour_in_Gyeongju_South_Korea.jpg/1280px-Water_reflection_of_Donggung_Palace_in_Wolji_Pond_at_blue_hour_in_Gyeongju_South_Korea.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Water_reflection_of_Donggung_Palace_in_Wolji_Pond_at_blue_hour_in_Gyeongju_South_Korea.jpg)

<sub>Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond, Gyeongju Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Water_reflection_of_Donggung_Palace_in_Wolji_Pond_at_blue_hour_in_Gyeongju_South_Korea.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 674
- **Type:** Palace garden
- **Location:** Gyeongju, North Gyeongsang Province, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Donggung%20Palace%20and%20Wolji%20Pond%20Silla%20builders)

After the unification of the Korean peninsula under Silla, King Munmu created in 674 a pond with islands and hills in the grounds of the palace of the crown prince, where banquets were held for envoys and court. The pond, with irregular shores designed so that its full extent can never be seen from one point, was planted with flowers and stocked with rare birds and animals. The palace fell into ruin and the pond was later called Anapji, 'pond of geese and ducks'. Dredging in the 1970s recovered tens of thousands of objects, including roof tiles, a wooden game die and the remains of a boat, and several pavilions were reconstructed on their original foundations. It is part of the Gyeongju Historic Areas World Heritage Site.
