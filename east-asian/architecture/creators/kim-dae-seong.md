# Kim Dae-seong

*김대성 (金大城)*

**700 – 774** · Korean (Silla)  
Styles & eras: [Three Kingdoms, Silla & Goryeo Buddhist architecture](../movements/korean-buddhist.md)  

> Unified Silla minister traditionally credited with founding Bulguksa and the Seokguram grotto near Gyeongju.

## Biography

Kim Dae-seong was a high official of the Unified Silla kingdom who served as chief minister in the 740s and 750s. The Samguk yusa, written five centuries later, tells the legend that he was reborn into a noble family as a reward for piety in a previous life, and that in 751 he began Bulguksa for his parents in this life and Seokguram for his parents in his former life.

He died in 774 before the works were finished, and the state completed them. How far he directed the design is unknown, and the account is legendary, but the date and the high quality of the two monuments point to court patronage of the highest level. Together they are regarded as the summit of Silla Buddhist architecture and sculpture.

### Bulguksa (751–774; halls rebuilt 17th–20th century)

[![Bulguksa](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/eb/Lotus_Flower_Bridge_and_Seven_Treasure_Bridge_at_Bulguksa_in_Gyeongju%2C_Korea.jpg/1280px-Lotus_Flower_Bridge_and_Seven_Treasure_Bridge_at_Bulguksa_in_Gyeongju%2C_Korea.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lotus_Flower_Bridge_and_Seven_Treasure_Bridge_at_Bulguksa_in_Gyeongju%2C_Korea.jpg)

<sub>Lotus Flower and Seven Treasures Bridges, Bulguksa Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lotus_Flower_Bridge_and_Seven_Treasure_Bridge_at_Bulguksa_in_Gyeongju%2C_Korea.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 751–774; halls rebuilt 17th–20th century
- **Type:** Buddhist temple
- **Location:** Mount Toham, Gyeongju, North Gyeongsang Province, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Bulguksa%20Kim%20Dae-seong)

Bulguksa, the 'Temple of the Buddha Land', was enlarged from a smaller temple from 751, according to tradition by the minister Kim Dae-seong, and completed by the state after his death in 774. It stands on great stone terraces approached by two pairs of stone stairways treated as bridges, the Blue Cloud and White Cloud Bridges and the Lotus Flower and Seven Treasures Bridges, symbolising passage from the world into the Buddha's realm. In the courtyard before the main hall stand two contrasting stone pagodas: the austere Seokgatap and the intricate Dabotap, which translates a complex timber structure into stone. In 1966 a printed dharani sutra, one of the oldest printed texts in the world, was found inside Seokgatap. The timber halls were burned during the Japanese invasions of 1592 and rebuilt; the temple was extensively restored in 1969–73. With Seokguram it became a World Heritage Site in 1995.

### Seokguram Grotto (751–774)

[![Seokguram Grotto](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Front_view_of_Seokguram_from_front_chamber.jpg/1280px-Front_view_of_Seokguram_from_front_chamber.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Front_view_of_Seokguram_from_front_chamber.jpg)

<sub>The Buddha of Seokguram seen from the antechamber Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Front_view_of_Seokguram_from_front_chamber.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 751–774
- **Type:** Artificial cave temple
- **Location:** Mount Toham, Gyeongju, North Gyeongsang Province, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Seokguram%20Grotto%20Kim%20Dae-seong)

Seokguram, built at the same time as Bulguksa and traditionally credited to Kim Dae-seong, translates the cave temples of India and China into a constructed form: since the granite of Mount Toham could not easily be carved, the builders assembled a grotto of cut stone blocks and covered it with earth. A rectangular antechamber and a short corridor lead to a circular rotunda under a dome of stones held by projecting stone 'rivets', in which a seated Buddha more than three metres high faces east towards the sea, surrounded by reliefs of bodhisattvas, disciples and guardians. The geometry of the plan is based on careful proportions. Restorations under Japanese rule in the 1910s used concrete and caused damp problems, and the interior is now behind glass and climate-controlled. It is a World Heritage Site with Bulguksa.
