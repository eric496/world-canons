# Lin Huiyin

*林徽因*

**1904 – 1955** · Chinese  
Born: Hangzhou · Died: Beijing  
Styles & eras: [Meiji Western-style & early modern](../movements/meiji-western.md), [Japanese modernism & Metabolism](../movements/metabolism-modernism.md)  

> Architect, architectural historian and poet who worked with Liang Sicheng on the survey of China's ancient buildings and the designs of the early People's Republic.

## Biography

Lin Huiyin grew up in a scholarly official family and travelled to London with her father as a teenager. She studied at the University of Pennsylvania from 1924, taking a degree in fine arts because the architecture school did not then award degrees to women, though she took its courses, and then studied stage design at Yale. She married Liang Sicheng in 1928 and became his partner in teaching and research, taking part in field surveys across northern China, including the identification of Foguang Temple in 1937. She was also a celebrated modernist poet and writer.

Lin contributed historical essays and much of the analysis in their joint work, and in the 1950s she was a professor at Tsinghua University. She took part in designing the national emblem of the People's Republic and the decorative reliefs and patterns of the Monument to the People's Heroes, and worked to revive the craft of Beijing cloisonné. Long ill with tuberculosis, she died in 1955 at the age of fifty.
