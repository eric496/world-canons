# Junzō Sakakura

*坂倉準三*

**1901 – 1969** · Japanese  
Born: Hashima, Gifu · Died: Tokyo  
Styles & eras: [Japanese modernism & Metabolism](../movements/metabolism-modernism.md)  

> Japanese architect who worked for Le Corbusier, designed the Japanese pavilion at the 1937 Paris Exposition and the Museum of Modern Art in Kamakura.

## Biography

Sakakura studied art history at Tokyo Imperial University and then went to Paris, where he worked in Le Corbusier's studio from 1931 to 1936. His Japanese pavilion at the Paris International Exposition of 1937, a light steel frame with ramps and glass, won a grand prize. In Japan he founded a large practice and helped bring Charlotte Perriand to Japan in 1940.

His Museum of Modern Art in Kamakura (1951), a white box raised on slender columns over a lotus pond in the precinct of the Tsurugaoka Hachimangū shrine, was the first public modern art museum in Japan and a model of postwar modernism. He later designed the redevelopment of Shinjuku Station's west exit plaza and many public buildings.

## Key buildings & sites

### Museum of Modern Art, Kamakura (1951)

- **Date:** 1951
- **Type:** Museum
- **Location:** Tsurugaoka Hachimangū, Kamakura, Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Museum%20of%20Modern%20Art%2C%20Kamakura%20Junz%C5%8D%20Sakakura)

Junzō Sakakura designed the Museum of Modern Art, Kamakura, opened in 1951 in the grounds of the Tsurugaoka Hachimangū shrine, as a small, inexpensive building in the difficult years after the war. A two-storey box clad in white panels encloses a courtyard, and part of it is raised on slender steel columns over the Heike pond, so that reflected light plays on its underside. Open-air terraces and a stair lead from the courtyard to the galleries. The building applied lessons from Le Corbusier's museum projects to a Japanese site with remarkable delicacy. The museum moved out in 2016 when its lease ended; the shrine preserved the building, which reopened in 2019 as the Kamakura Bunkakan Tsurugaoka Museum.
