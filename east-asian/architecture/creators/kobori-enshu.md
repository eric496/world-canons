## Biography

Kobori Masakazu, called Enshū after his court title, was the son of a vassal of Toyotomi Hideyoshi and became a minor daimyō under the Tokugawa. As commissioner of works (sakuji bugyō) he supervised building at imperial palaces, castles such as Fushimi and Nijō, and temples in Kyoto. He studied tea with Furuta Oribe and became tea instructor to the third shōgun, Tokugawa Iemitsu. The Enshū school of tea continues today.

Enshū's style, often described as kirei-sabi or 'elegant rusticity', added brightness, clear geometry and courtly refinement to Rikyū's austerity. Works associated with him include the crane-and-turtle garden of Konchi-in at Nanzen-ji, the Kohō-an sub-temple of Daitoku-ji with its tea room Bōsen, and work on the Ninomaru garden of Nijō Castle. Many other gardens, including Katsura, were later credited to him by tradition, and such attributions are often doubtful.

## Key buildings & sites

### Crane and Turtle Garden of Konchi-in (c. 1632)

[![Crane and Turtle Garden of Konchi-in](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/251218_Konchi-in_Kyoto_Japan11s3.jpg/1280px-251218_Konchi-in_Kyoto_Japan11s3.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:251218_Konchi-in_Kyoto_Japan11s3.jpg)

<sub>Konchi-in, Kyoto Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:251218_Konchi-in_Kyoto_Japan11s3.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1632
- **Type:** Temple garden
- **Location:** Nanzen-ji, Kyoto, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Crane%20and%20Turtle%20Garden%20of%20Konchi-in%20Kobori%20Ensh%C5%AB)

Konchi-in is a subtemple of Nanzen-ji, moved to its present site in 1605 by the abbot Ishin Sūden, a powerful adviser to Tokugawa Ieyasu who also enshrined Ieyasu there in a small Tōshō-gū. Around 1632 Kobori Enshū designed the garden in front of the abbot's quarters, one of the few gardens firmly documented as his, built by the gardener Kentei. A broad expanse of white gravel stands for the sea; on either side, stones and clipped shrubs form a crane island and a turtle island, symbols of longevity, framing a large flat worship stone at the centre, with a hillside of trees behind. The temple also contains the Hassō-seki tea room, attributed to Enshū. The garden shows his combination of bold, clear composition with the auspicious imagery favoured by the Tokugawa.
