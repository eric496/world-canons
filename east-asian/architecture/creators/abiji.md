# Abiji

*阿非知*

**active 7th century** · Korean (Baekje)  
Born: Baekje · Died: ?  
Styles & eras: [Three Kingdoms, Silla & Goryeo Buddhist architecture](../movements/korean-buddhist.md)  

> Baekje master builder who, according to tradition, raised the nine-storey wooden pagoda of Hwangnyongsa in the Silla capital in 645.

## Biography

Abiji is known only from the thirteenth-century Samguk yusa, which records that Queen Seondeok of Silla, advised by the monk Jajang, invited a master craftsman from the rival kingdom of Baekje to build a great pagoda at the royal temple of Hwangnyongsa in Gyeongju. Abiji led some two hundred workers, and the pagoda was completed in 645.

The pagoda, said to have been about eighty metres tall, had nine storeys symbolising the nine neighbouring peoples whom Silla hoped to overcome. It was struck by lightning and repaired several times and finally burned during the Mongol invasion of 1238. Only its foundation stones remain. Abiji's story illustrates the reputation of Baekje builders, who also built Japan's first temples.

## Key buildings & sites

### Nine-storey Pagoda of Hwangnyongsa (645 (destroyed 1238))

- **Date:** 645 (destroyed 1238)
- **Type:** Timber pagoda (lost)
- **Location:** Gyeongju, North Gyeongsang Province, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Nine-storey%20Pagoda%20of%20Hwangnyongsa%20Abiji)

Hwangnyongsa, the 'Temple of the Yellow Dragon', was founded in 553 by King Jinheung of Silla as the kingdom's main royal temple. In the 640s Queen Seondeok, on the advice of the monk Jajang, ordered a nine-storey wooden pagoda to protect the realm, and according to the Samguk yusa the Baekje master builder Abiji raised it, completing it in 645. Its height is recorded as about 80 metres, which would have made it the tallest structure in East Asia. It was repaired several times and burned with the whole temple during the Mongol invasion of 1238. Excavations from 1976 uncovered the foundation stones of the pagoda and halls, and the temple's plan and a scale model of the pagoda can be seen at a museum on the site, which is part of the Gyeongju Historic Areas World Heritage Site.
