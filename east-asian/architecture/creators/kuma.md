# Kengo Kuma

*隈研吾*

**1954** · Japanese  
Born: Yokohama · Died: ?  
Styles & eras: [Contemporary architecture](../movements/east-asian-contemporary-arch.md)  

> Japanese architect known for buildings of timber slats, bamboo and stone that seek to 'erase' architecture, including the Japan National Stadium.

## Biography

Kuma studied at the University of Tokyo and at Columbia University, and opened his office in Tokyo in 1990. After an early postmodern phase he turned, in the 1990s, to what he called 'anti-object' architecture, in which buildings are broken down into small elements of natural material and dissolve into their surroundings. Commissions for small towns such as Yusuhara in Kōchi helped him develop work with local craftsmen.

His Nezu Museum in Tokyo (2009), with a deep sheltering roof and bamboo-lined approach, the V&A Dundee in Scotland (2018) and the Japan National Stadium (2019), built for the Tokyo Olympics with eaves of Japanese cedar, are among his best-known works. His office has become one of the most prolific in the world.

### Nezu Museum (2009)

[![Nezu Museum](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/da/2018_Nezu_Museum_1.jpg/1280px-2018_Nezu_Museum_1.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2018_Nezu_Museum_1.jpg)

<sub>Nezu Museum Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:2018_Nezu_Museum_1.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 2009
- **Type:** Museum
- **Location:** Minami-Aoyama, Minato, Tokyo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Nezu%20Museum%20Kengo%20Kuma)

The Nezu Museum holds the collection of Japanese and East Asian art formed by the businessman Nezu Kaichirō, including famous screens by Ogata Kōrin. Its new building by Kengo Kuma, opened in 2009, presents to the busy avenue of Aoyama a long approach under bamboo, then a low building dominated by a large pitched roof of dark tiles with deep eaves, supported on thin steel. Inside, glass walls look out over the museum's large garden of ponds, tea houses and stone lanterns. The building was praised for its calm and its relation to the garden, and is one of Kuma's best-known works in Tokyo.

### Japan National Stadium (2016–2019)

[![Japan National Stadium](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Japan_National_Stadium_C-gate_on_2021_July_28.jpg/1280px-Japan_National_Stadium_C-gate_on_2021_July_28.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Japan_National_Stadium_C-gate_on_2021_July_28.jpg)

<sub>Japan National Stadium Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Japan_National_Stadium_C-gate_on_2021_July_28.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 2016–2019
- **Type:** Stadium
- **Location:** Kasumigaoka, Shinjuku, Tokyo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Japan%20National%20Stadium%20Kengo%20Kuma)

The stadium for the 2020 Tokyo Olympics was first to be built to a design by Zaha Hadid, but the government cancelled it in 2015 because of its cost. A new competition was won by a team of Kengo Kuma with the construction company Taisei. The stadium, completed in 2019 on the site of the 1958 National Stadium, is lower and flatter than Hadid's design, with tiers of eaves faced with timber louvres of cedar and larch from all of Japan's forty-seven prefectures, planting along the terraces and a roof of steel and timber. It held the opening and closing ceremonies of the Olympics, postponed to 2021 because of the pandemic and held without spectators.
