# Kōra Munehiro

*甲良宗広*

**1574 – 1646** · Japanese  
Born: Ōmi Province · Died: ?  
Styles & eras: [Momoyama & early Edo castles and palaces](../movements/momoyama-castles.md)  

> Master carpenter of the Tokugawa shogunate who directed the lavish rebuilding of Nikkō Tōshō-gū in 1634–36.

## Biography

Kōra Munehiro came from Kōra in Ōmi Province, a region known for its builders, and entered the service of the Tokugawa, working on Fushimi Castle and then in Edo. He became one of the shogunate's master carpenters and founded a family that held that office for generations. His best-known project was the rebuilding of the shrine-mausoleum of Tokugawa Ieyasu at Nikkō, ordered by the third shōgun Iemitsu.

At Nikkō Munehiro and his workshop produced the most elaborate ensemble of Japanese architecture: gongen-zukuri shrine halls joined by a linking room, covered with lacquer, gold leaf and dense polychrome carving, culminating in the Yōmeimon gate. Later taste often found it excessive, but it marks the apex of Momoyama and early Edo decorative ambition.

## Key buildings & sites

### Nikkō Tōshō-gū (1617; rebuilt 1634–36)

[![Nikkō Tōshō-gū](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/200801_Nikko_Tosho-gu_Nikko_Japan03s3.jpg/1280px-200801_Nikko_Tosho-gu_Nikko_Japan03s3.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:200801_Nikko_Tosho-gu_Nikko_Japan03s3.jpg)

<sub>Yōmeimon gate, Nikkō Tōshō-gū Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:200801_Nikko_Tosho-gu_Nikko_Japan03s3.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1617; rebuilt 1634–36
- **Type:** Shinto shrine-mausoleum
- **Location:** Nikkō, Tochigi Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Nikk%C5%8D%20T%C5%8Dsh%C5%8D-g%C5%AB%20K%C5%8Dra%20Munehiro)

Tokugawa Ieyasu, founder of the shogunate, was deified after his death in 1616 as Tōshō Daigongen, and a shrine was built for him at Nikkō in the mountains north of Edo in 1617. His grandson Iemitsu had it rebuilt on a far grander scale in 1634–36, under the master carpenter Kōra Munehiro. The main shrine follows the gongen-zukuri plan, in which a worship hall and the main sanctuary are joined by a linking room under a complex roof. Every surface of the buildings is lacquered, gilded or carved with dragons, birds, flowers and figures from Chinese legend, among them the famous three monkeys and the sleeping cat; the Yōmeimon gate alone carries more than five hundred carvings. The shrine is set among tall cryptomeria trees approached by stairs. It is part of the Shrines and Temples of Nikkō World Heritage Site.
