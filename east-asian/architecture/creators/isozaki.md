# Arata Isozaki

*磯崎新*

**1931 – 2022** · Japanese  
Born: Ōita · Died: Naha, Okinawa  
Styles & eras: [Japanese modernism & Metabolism](../movements/metabolism-modernism.md), [Contemporary architecture](../movements/east-asian-contemporary-arch.md)  

> Japanese architect and theorist who moved from Metabolism to a postmodern play of forms, winner of the Pritzker Prize in 2019.

## Biography

Isozaki grew up in Ōita, which was bombed in the war, and studied and worked under Kenzō Tange before opening his own office in 1963. Close to the Metabolists but critical of their optimism, he wrote of the city as ruin. His early works in Ōita, including the Ōita Prefectural Library (1966), were followed by the Gunma Prefectural Museum of Modern Art (1974), built as a framework of cubes.

The Tsukuba Center Building (1983), quoting Michelangelo's Campidoglio and other Western sources, became a landmark of postmodernism, and his Museum of Contemporary Art in Los Angeles (1986) and Palau Sant Jordi in Barcelona brought him international fame. He was a leading intellectual figure who wrote on Japanese concepts of space, such as ma, and mentored younger architects worldwide.

### Museum of Modern Art, Gunma (1974)

- **Date:** 1974
- **Type:** Museum
- **Location:** Takasaki, Gunma Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Museum%20of%20Modern%20Art%2C%20Gunma%20Arata%20Isozaki)

The Gunma Prefectural Museum of Modern Art, opened in 1974 in a park at Takasaki, was designed by Arata Isozaki as a framework of cubes of about twelve metres a side, defining a geometric grid against which the galleries are arranged. The cubes are clad in square aluminium panels and glass, and a separate wing for Japanese art is set at an angle, projecting over a pool. Isozaki described the cube as a neutral 'framework' for art, rejecting the expressive forms of his elders. Together with his writings of the period, the building marked his turn to an architecture of pure geometry, ideas and quotation.

### Tsukuba Center Building (1983)

[![Tsukuba Center Building](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Tsukuba_Center_Building_2008.jpg/1280px-Tsukuba_Center_Building_2008.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tsukuba_Center_Building_2008.jpg)

<sub>Tsukuba Center Building Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tsukuba_Center_Building_2008.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1983
- **Type:** Civic centre with hotel and concert hall
- **Location:** Tsukuba, Ibaraki Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Tsukuba%20Center%20Building%20Arata%20Isozaki)

Tsukuba Science City was built by the Japanese state from the 1960s as a new town of research institutes. Its centre, designed by Arata Isozaki and completed in 1983, contains a hotel, concert hall, information centre and shops around a sunken oval plaza. The plaza is an inverted copy of Michelangelo's Piazza del Campidoglio in Rome, and the buildings quote Ledoux, Michael Graves and other sources, with rusticated stone, metal and tile. Isozaki said that a city of bureaucratic planning had no real centre, and that his quotation of Western forms around an empty space expressed this absence. The building became one of the most discussed examples of postmodernism.
