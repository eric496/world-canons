# Shigeru Ban

*坂茂*

**1957** · Japanese  
Born: Tokyo · Died: ?  
Styles & eras: [Contemporary architecture](../movements/east-asian-contemporary-arch.md)  

> Japanese architect known for structures of paper tubes and for disaster-relief architecture, winner of the Pritzker Prize in 2014.

## Biography

Ban studied at the Southern California Institute of Architecture and Cooper Union in New York, and opened his office in Tokyo in 1985. From the late 1980s he developed structures of recycled cardboard tubes, which are cheap, strong and easily assembled. After the Kobe earthquake of 1995 he built emergency shelters and a paper church for the victims, and he has since built relief housing and community buildings after disasters around the world.

Alongside this humanitarian work Ban designs museums and houses that explore woven timber, paper and movable walls, among them the Centre Pompidou-Metz (2010), the Cardboard Cathedral in Christchurch (2013) and the Ōita Prefectural Art Museum (2015). The Pritzker jury praised his combination of innovation and responsibility.

- **Date:** 2013
- **Type:** Cathedral
- **Location:** Christchurch, New Zealand
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Cardboard%20Cathedral%20Shigeru%20Ban)

The Christchurch earthquake of February 2011 badly damaged the city's Anglican cathedral, and Shigeru Ban offered to design a transitional cathedral, opened in 2013. Its triangular A-frame is formed of large cardboard tubes, coated against water and fire and stiffened by timber, rising from a base of shipping containers that house side rooms, under a roof of polycarbonate. The triangular front is filled with coloured glass with images from the earlier cathedral's rose window. Seating some 700 people, it serves as a church and concert venue. It was intended to last some decades while the permanent cathedral is restored, and became a symbol of the city's recovery.

### Ōita Prefectural Art Museum (2015)

[![Ōita Prefectural Art Museum](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Oita_Prefectural_Art_Museum_20221023.jpg/1280px-Oita_Prefectural_Art_Museum_20221023.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oita_Prefectural_Art_Museum_20221023.jpg)

<sub>Ōita Prefectural Art Museum Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oita_Prefectural_Art_Museum_20221023.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 2015
- **Type:** Museum
- **Location:** Ōita, Ōita Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=%C5%8Cita%20Prefectural%20Art%20Museum%20Shigeru%20Ban)

The Ōita Prefectural Art Museum, opened in 2015 in the centre of Ōita, was designed by Shigeru Ban to be a 'living room' for the city. Along the main street, the ground-floor facade is made of tall glass panels that can be folded away like sliding doors, so that the atrium, used for events and free exhibitions, merges with the pavement. The upper floor is wrapped in a lattice of timber and steel inspired by traditional bamboo craft, a local industry, and the interior uses timber structures that Ban has explored throughout his career. The building is an example of his ideal of an open, flexible public architecture.
