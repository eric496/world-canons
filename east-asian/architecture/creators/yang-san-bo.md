# Yang San-bo

*양산보 (梁山甫)*

**1503 – 1557** · Korean (Joseon)  
Born: Damyang · Died: Damyang  
Styles & eras: [Joseon palaces & seowon](../movements/joseon-palaces-seowon.md)  

> Joseon scholar who withdrew from public life and created Soswaewon, the most celebrated private garden of Korea.

## Biography

Yang San-bo was a disciple of the reforming Confucian official Jo Gwang-jo. When Jo was purged and executed in 1519, Yang, still a young man, renounced an official career and retired to his home in Damyang, South Jeolla Province, where he built the garden of Soswaewon ('Garden of Clear and Fresh Breeze').

Soswaewon is a small garden along a mountain stream, with pavilions, an earthen wall and bamboo groves, designed to blend with the existing landscape rather than reshape it. It became a gathering place for poets such as Song Sun and Jeong Cheol, and a woodblock print of 1755 records its layout. It is the classic expression of the Korean ideal of a scholar's retreat in nature.

### Soswaewon (c. 1520s–1530s)

- **Date:** c. 1520s–1530s
- **Type:** Scholar garden
- **Location:** Damyang, South Jeolla Province, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Soswaewon%20Yang%20San-bo)

Yang San-bo created Soswaewon after withdrawing from public life following the purge of his teacher Jo Gwang-jo in 1519. The garden lies along a small valley where a stream falls over rocks, entered through a bamboo grove. Two pavilions, the Gwangpunggak ('Pavilion of Light and Wind') and the Jewoldang ('Hall of Clear Moon'), are placed to overlook the stream, and an earthen and stone wall, through which the water passes, bends around the slope. Ponds, a wooden water conduit and old trees complete it, with very little alteration of the natural landscape. A woodblock print of 1755 records its forty-eight scenes. Soswaewon was a meeting place for poets of the region and remains the model of the Korean scholar's garden.
