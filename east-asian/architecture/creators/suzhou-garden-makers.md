# Suzhou garden makers

*蘇州造園家*

**active 16th – 19th century** · Chinese  
Styles & eras: [Chinese scholar gardens](../movements/chinese-scholar-gardens.md)  

> The owners, painters, rockery masters and craftsmen who created the classical gardens of Suzhou and the lower Yangzi region.

## Biography

Chinese scholar gardens were usually made by their owners, retired officials, merchants and scholars, in collaboration with painters and with specialist rockery masters such as Zhang Nanyang, Zhang Lian and Ge Yuliang, whose names are recorded in literature. The work of the craftsmen is anonymous. Gardens changed hands often and were remade by each owner, so that most of what survives dates from the nineteenth century.

These makers composed gardens as sequences of framed views, drawing on landscape painting and poetry. Their principles were written down by Ji Cheng in the Yuanye and by other late Ming authors. The gardens of Suzhou were inscribed together on the World Heritage List in 1997 and 2000.

### Humble Administrator's Garden (1509–c. 1530s)

[![Humble Administrator's Garden](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Suzhou_Zhuozheng_Yuan_2015.04.23_08-13-49.jpg/1280px-Suzhou_Zhuozheng_Yuan_2015.04.23_08-13-49.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Suzhou_Zhuozheng_Yuan_2015.04.23_08-13-49.jpg)

<sub>Humble Administrator's Garden, Suzhou Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Suzhou_Zhuozheng_Yuan_2015.04.23_08-13-49.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1509–c. 1530s
- **Type:** Scholar garden
- **Location:** Suzhou, Jiangsu, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Humble%20Administrator%27s%20Garden%20Suzhou%20garden%20makers)

The garden was created from about 1509 by Wang Xianchen, a censor who retired to Suzhou after a troubled career; its name alludes to a line by the Jin poet Pan Yue about the retired man's simple life of tending a garden as the 'administration' of a humble man. The painter Wen Zhengming, a friend of Wang, painted and described its views in an album of 1533, and he is often said to have helped design it, though this is uncertain. The garden has since been divided, sold and rebuilt many times; its present form largely dates from the nineteenth century. Covering about five hectares, it is mostly water, with islands, zigzag bridges, halls and galleries arranged so that views open and close in sequence, and borrows the distant North Temple Pagoda as part of its scenery. It is one of the Classical Gardens of Suzhou on the World Heritage list.

### Yu Garden (1559–1577)

[![Yu Garden](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Shanghai_-_Yu_Garden_-_0035.jpg/1280px-Shanghai_-_Yu_Garden_-_0035.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shanghai_-_Yu_Garden_-_0035.jpg)

<sub>Yu Garden, Shanghai Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shanghai_-_Yu_Garden_-_0035.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1559–1577
- **Type:** Scholar garden
- **Location:** Shanghai, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Yu%20Garden%20Suzhou%20garden%20makers)

The official Pan Yunduan began the Yu Garden, the 'Garden of Pleasing', in 1559 to give his aged parents a pleasant retreat, completing it around 1577. Its great rockery of yellow stones is attributed to the noted rockery maker Zhang Nanyang. The garden decayed after the Pan family declined, was restored by merchants in the eighteenth century and was damaged in the Opium War and the Taiping rebellion, then restored again from the 1950s. Today it covers about two hectares of halls, ponds, rockeries and pavilions divided by walls topped with undulating dragons. The surrounding streets have been rebuilt as a bazaar in traditional style, and the garden is one of the most visited sights of Shanghai.

### Lingering Garden (1593; remodelled 19th century)

[![Lingering Garden](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/Lingering_Garden.jpg/1280px-Lingering_Garden.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lingering_Garden.jpg)

<sub>Lingering Garden, Suzhou Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lingering_Garden.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1593; remodelled 19th century
- **Type:** Scholar garden
- **Location:** Suzhou, Jiangsu, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Lingering%20Garden%20Suzhou%20garden%20makers)

The Lingering Garden was begun in 1593 by the official Xu Taishi as the East Garden, and after changes of ownership was rebuilt in the nineteenth century, taking its present name under the Sheng family. It is known for its sophisticated sequence of spaces: from a narrow, dim entrance passage the visitor moves through small courtyards and lattice windows into a central pond and hill garden, and then to eastern courtyards of halls and rocks. Long covered corridors, their walls pierced by windows with different lattice patterns, frame changing views. The Crown of Clouds Peak (Guanyun feng), a tall perforated Lake Tai rock about 6.5 metres high, is one of the most celebrated garden stones in China. It is part of the Classical Gardens of Suzhou World Heritage Site.

### Master of the Nets Garden (12th century; rebuilt 1770s)

[![Master of the Nets Garden](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Master_of_the_Nets_Garden_Suzhou.jpg/1280px-Master_of_the_Nets_Garden_Suzhou.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Master_of_the_Nets_Garden_Suzhou.jpg)

<sub>Master of the Nets Garden, Suzhou Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Master_of_the_Nets_Garden_Suzhou.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 12th century; rebuilt 1770s
- **Type:** Scholar garden
- **Location:** Suzhou, Jiangsu, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Master%20of%20the%20Nets%20Garden%20Suzhou%20garden%20makers)

A garden called the Fisherman's Retreat was made on this site by a Song dynasty official, and in the 1770s the retired official Song Zongyuan rebuilt it, choosing the name 'Master of the Nets' with its ideal of a hermit fisherman. Later owners added to it. It is compact, about half a hectare, and integrated with the family residence: a series of halls and courtyards leads to a central pond around which buildings, rockeries and a covered walkway are arranged at a carefully judged scale, so that the small space seems larger. A courtyard of the garden served as the model for the Astor Court built in 1981 at the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York. It is one of the Classical Gardens of Suzhou World Heritage Site.
