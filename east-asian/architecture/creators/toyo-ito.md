# Toyo Itō

*伊東豊雄*

**1941** · Japanese  
Born: Seoul, Korea · Died: ?  
Styles & eras: [Contemporary architecture](../movements/east-asian-contemporary-arch.md)  

> Japanese architect of light, fluid buildings such as the Sendai Mediatheque, winner of the Pritzker Prize in 2013.

## Biography

Itō was born in Seoul, then under Japanese rule, and grew up in Nagano. After studying at the University of Tokyo he worked for Kiyonori Kikutake and opened his own office in 1971. Kazuyo Sejima was among his assistants. His early houses, such as White U (1976), and the Tower of Winds in Yokohama (1986) explored lightness, transparency and the ephemeral city.

The Sendai Mediatheque (2001), with floors carried on tree-like tubes of steel lattice, opened a period of structural experiment that continued with the Tama Art University Library (2007), the TOD'S building in Omotesandō and the National Taichung Theater in Taiwan (2016). After the 2011 earthquake and tsunami he led the Home-for-All project of community buildings for survivors.

### Sendai Mediatheque (1995–2001)

[![Sendai Mediatheque](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/14/Sendai_Mediatheque_2009.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sendai_Mediatheque_2009.jpg)

<sub>Sendai Mediatheque Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sendai_Mediatheque_2009.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1995–2001
- **Type:** Library and media centre
- **Location:** Kasugamachi, Aoba, Sendai, Miyagi Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sendai%20Mediatheque%20Toyo%20It%C5%8D)

Toyo Itō won the competition for a library, gallery and media centre in Sendai in 1995, and the building opened in 2001. It is a cube of glass about 50 metres square, with seven thin floor plates of steel honeycomb supported by thirteen hollow tubes of welded steel pipes that rise through the building at irregular positions, swaying like seaweed in water. The tubes contain stairs, lifts and services and bring daylight down through the floors. The building has no conventional walls inside, and each floor has a different layout and furniture. It survived the strong shaking of the 2011 earthquake with damage mostly to ceilings, and is regarded as one of the defining buildings of its decade.

### Tama Art University Library (2007)

[![Tama Art University Library](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/96/Tama_bijutsu_daigaku_library.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tama_bijutsu_daigaku_library.jpg)

<sub>Tama Art University Library Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tama_bijutsu_daigaku_library.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 2007
- **Type:** University library
- **Location:** Hachiōji Campus, Tokyo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Tama%20Art%20University%20Library%20Toyo%20It%C5%8D)

For the library of the Tama Art University campus at Hachiōji, completed in 2007, Toyo Itō designed a structure of thin arches of concrete with a steel core, arranged along curving lines that cross one another at irregular intervals, so that the arches have different spans and the spaces between them shift continuously. The ground floor follows the natural slope of the site, rising as a gentle incline. The glazed facades are flush with the arches, so that the building looks like a light, cave-like grid. The open, flowing interior is used both as a library and a common space for students, and the building is often cited as a key work of Itō's structural experiments.
