# Nakai Masakiyo

*中井正清*

**1565 – 1619** · Japanese  
Born: Yamato Province · Died: ?  
Styles & eras: [Momoyama & early Edo castles and palaces](../movements/momoyama-castles.md)  

> Master carpenter to Tokugawa Ieyasu, who directed the building of the shogunate's castles and palaces in the early seventeenth century.

## Biography

Nakai Masakiyo came from a family of carpenters in Yamato, the province of the great Nara temples. He entered the service of Tokugawa Ieyasu and after the battle of Sekigahara in 1600 became head carpenter (daiku-gashira) of the new shogunate, receiving a court rank and an income of a lord. He directed work on Fushimi Castle, Nijō Castle in Kyoto, Edo Castle, Sunpu Castle and Nagoya Castle, and on major temples, organising the carpenters of the Kyoto region.

The Nakai family remained hereditary heads of the shogunate's carpenters in the Kyoto region throughout the Edo period, and their archive of plans is an important source for the history of Japanese construction. Masakiyo's work at Nijō Castle, with its sequence of audience halls in the shoin style, shows the formal language of Tokugawa power at its height.

## Key buildings & sites

### Nijō Castle (1601–1603; enlarged 1624–26)

[![Nijō Castle](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dd/NinomaruPalace.jpg/1280px-NinomaruPalace.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NinomaruPalace.jpg)

<sub>Ninomaru Palace, Nijō Castle Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NinomaruPalace.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1601–1603; enlarged 1624–26
- **Type:** Castle and palace
- **Location:** Kyoto, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Nij%C5%8D%20Castle%20Nakai%20Masakiyo)

Tokugawa Ieyasu built Nijō Castle as his residence in the imperial capital, with Nakai Masakiyo as master carpenter, completing it in 1603, the year he became shōgun. It was enlarged in 1624–26 for the visit of Emperor Go-Mizunoo, when Kobori Enshū was among those working on the gardens. The Ninomaru Palace that survives is a sequence of halls connected diagonally, in the shoin style, whose rooms rise in rank towards the shōgun's audience chamber; their walls and sliding doors are painted by the Kanō school with pines, tigers and flowers on gold ground, and the corridors have 'nightingale floors' that squeak underfoot. The castle's keep burned in 1750. In 1867 the last shōgun, Tokugawa Yoshinobu, announced the return of power to the emperor there. It is part of the Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto World Heritage Site.
