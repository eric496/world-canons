# Antonin Raymond

*アントニン・レーモンド*

**1888 – 1976** · Czech-American  
Born: Kladno, Bohemia · Died: Langhorne, Pennsylvania  
Styles & eras: [Meiji Western-style & early modern](../movements/meiji-western.md), [Japanese modernism & Metabolism](../movements/metabolism-modernism.md)  

> Czech-born American architect who came to Tokyo with Frank Lloyd Wright and became a pioneer of modern architecture in Japan.

## Biography

Raymond studied in Prague, emigrated to the United States in 1910 and worked for Cass Gilbert and then Frank Lloyd Wright, with whom he travelled to Tokyo in 1919 to work on the Imperial Hotel. He soon set up his own office in Tokyo and stayed, with interruptions for the war, until 1973. His own house at Reinanzaka (1924) was one of the first modernist houses in exposed reinforced concrete anywhere. Kunio Maekawa and Junzō Yoshimura were among his assistants.

Raymond combined modern concrete construction with lessons from Japanese timber building: frank structure, natural materials, sliding screens and integration with gardens. St Paul's Church in Karuizawa (1935), with its exposed timber frame, and the Gunma Music Center (1961) are among his best-known works. He was an important link between Wright, European modernism and the postwar generation of Japanese architects.

## Key buildings & sites

### St Paul's Catholic Church, Karuizawa (1935)

[![St Paul's Catholic Church, Karuizawa](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/St._Paul%27s_Catholic_Church_Karuizawa_Japan_2010.jpg/1280px-St._Paul%27s_Catholic_Church_Karuizawa_Japan_2010.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St._Paul%27s_Catholic_Church_Karuizawa_Japan_2010.jpg)

<sub>St Paul's Catholic Church, Karuizawa Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St._Paul%27s_Catholic_Church_Karuizawa_Japan_2010.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1935
- **Type:** Church
- **Location:** Karuizawa, Nagano Prefecture, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=St%20Paul%27s%20Catholic%20Church%2C%20Karuizawa%20Antonin%20Raymond)

Karuizawa, a summer resort in the mountains favoured by missionaries and foreigners, was where Antonin Raymond built this small Catholic church in 1935. It has a tall, steep roof and a pointed bell tower, with walls of rough timber and an interior of exposed scissor trusses and diagonal bracing in unpainted wood, made by Japanese carpenters. Raymond acknowledged that the design was inspired by an unbuilt house project by Le Corbusier, the Errázuriz House for Chile, and adapted it to Japanese building craft. The result combines the forms of Central European rural churches with Japanese carpentry and has become a much-loved place for weddings. It was later celebrated as an early example of a regional modern architecture in Japan.
