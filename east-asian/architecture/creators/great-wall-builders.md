# Builders of the Great Wall

*長城建造者*

**active 7th century BC – 17th century** · Chinese  
Styles & eras: [The Great Wall](../movements/great-wall.md)  

> The soldiers, conscripts, convicts and craftsmen of many dynasties who built and rebuilt the frontier walls of northern China.

## Biography

From the Warring States to the Ming, walls along the northern frontier were built by armies and by forced labour drafted from the population, often in harsh conditions; legends such as that of Lady Meng Jiang, whose tears brought down a section of wall where her husband had died, preserve the memory of their suffering. Under the Ming, military officials such as Qi Jiguang directed the construction of brick watchtowers and garrisons.

Over two thousand years their methods changed from tamped earth and reed layers in the western deserts to fired brick and dressed stone near Beijing. The collective result is the largest building project in human history, though it was never a single wall and never completed as a unified plan.

## Key buildings & sites

### Great Wall of China (7th century BC – 17th century)

[![Great Wall of China](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/The_Great_Wall_of_China_at_Jinshanling-edit.jpg/1280px-The_Great_Wall_of_China_at_Jinshanling-edit.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Great_Wall_of_China_at_Jinshanling-edit.jpg)

<sub>The Ming Great Wall at Jinshanling, Hebei Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Great_Wall_of_China_at_Jinshanling-edit.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 7th century BC – 17th century
- **Type:** Frontier fortification
- **Location:** Northern China, from Shanhaiguan to Jiayuguan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Great%20Wall%20of%20China%20Builders%20of%20the%20Great%20Wall)

From the seventh century BC the rival states of China built walls of rammed earth against each other and against the peoples of the steppe. After unification in 221 BC the Qin linked and extended the northern walls, and the Han pushed them far west along the Gansu corridor. Later dynasties built, neglected and rebuilt in turn; the most famous stretches, faced with brick and stone and climbing mountain ridges with watchtowers at short intervals, date from the Ming, who rebuilt the defences on a vast scale after the Mongols were driven out in 1368. The Wall was never a single continuous structure: it was a system of walls, ditches, passes, beacon towers and garrisons, and its military effectiveness is debated by historians. Its total length, including all branches and periods, was measured by a Chinese survey in 2012 at over 21,000 kilometres. Much is ruined, while sections such as Badaling and Mutianyu have been heavily restored.
