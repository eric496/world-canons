# Kiyonori Kikutake

*菊竹清訓*

**1928 – 2011** · Japanese  
Born: Kurume, Fukuoka · Died: Tokyo  
Styles & eras: [Japanese modernism & Metabolism](../movements/metabolism-modernism.md)  

> Founding member of the Metabolist group, known for his Sky House and visionary floating Marine City.

## Biography

Kikutake studied at Waseda University and opened his office in Tokyo in 1953. In 1960 he was one of the authors of the Metabolism manifesto presented at the World Design Conference in Tokyo, contributing projects for cities floating on the sea. Toyo Itō was among his assistants.

His own Sky House (1958), a single square room raised on four concrete panels with service units that could be replaced, was a manifesto of Metabolist thinking. He designed the administration building of Izumo Taisha (1963), the floating Aquapolis for the 1975 Okinawa Expo and the Edo-Tokyo Museum (1993), lifted high on four massive legs. He saw architecture as a framework that could change and grow over time.

### Sky House (1958)

[![Sky House](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/27/Sky_house%2C_Bukyo.jpg/1280px-Sky_house%2C_Bukyo.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sky_house%2C_Bukyo.jpg)

<sub>Sky House, Tokyo Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sky_house%2C_Bukyo.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1958
- **Type:** House
- **Location:** Ōtsuka, Bunkyō, Tokyo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sky%20House%20Kiyonori%20Kikutake)

Kiyonori Kikutake built the Sky House for himself and his wife, the poet Norie Kikutake, on a slope in Tokyo in 1958. A single square room of about ten metres a side, under a hyperbolic paraboloid concrete roof, is lifted high above the ground on four wall-like piers set at the midpoint of each side. Around its edge the kitchen, bathroom and storage were designed as 'move-net' units that could be replaced as needs changed, and a children's room was later hung beneath the floor. The house thus expressed in miniature the Metabolist idea of a permanent structure supporting changeable parts, two years before the group's manifesto. It has been altered over time and is still a private house.

### Edo-Tokyo Museum (1993)

[![Edo-Tokyo Museum](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Edo-Tokyo_Museum.jpg/1280px-Edo-Tokyo_Museum.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edo-Tokyo_Museum.jpg)

<sub>Edo-Tokyo Museum Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edo-Tokyo_Museum.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1993
- **Type:** Museum
- **Location:** Ryōgoku, Sumida, Tokyo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Edo-Tokyo%20Museum%20Kiyonori%20Kikutake)

Kiyonori Kikutake's museum of the history of Edo and Tokyo opened in 1993 near the Ryōgoku sumo arena. Its main exhibition floors are raised on four enormous concrete legs, leaving a large open plaza beneath, and the building's height of about 62 metres was set to match that of the long-vanished keep of Edo Castle. The form is said to evoke a traditional raised storehouse. Inside, full-size reconstructions include half of the Nihonbashi bridge and a kabuki theatre facade. The museum's massive scale divided critics, but it became a familiar Tokyo landmark. It closed for a long renovation in 2022.
