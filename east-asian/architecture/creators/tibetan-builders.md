# Tibetan builders

*藏族工匠*

**active 7th – 20th century** · Tibetan  
Styles & eras: [Ming–Qing imperial architecture](../movements/ming-qing-imperial.md)  

> The masons, carpenters and artists of Tibet, who developed a distinct architecture of massive battered walls and flat roofs.

## Biography

Tibetan architecture grew from its own traditions and from Indian and Nepalese Buddhist models rather than from Chinese timber building. Palaces, monasteries and fortresses were built by local masons and carpenters, often with corvée labour, under the direction of lamas and officials; Newar craftsmen from Nepal contributed metalwork and painting.

Its buildings rise from stone and rammed-earth walls that slope inward, with small windows framed in black, painted timber interiors and gilded roofs. The Potala Palace is its supreme monument, and Qing emperors copied it at Chengde.

## Key buildings & sites

### Potala Palace (1645–1694)

[![Potala Palace](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Potala_Palace_HQ.jpg/1280px-Potala_Palace_HQ.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Potala_Palace_HQ.jpg)

<sub>Potala Palace, Lhasa Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Potala_Palace_HQ.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1645–1694
- **Type:** Palace and monastery
- **Location:** Lhasa, Tibet, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Potala%20Palace%20Tibetan%20builders)

The Potala belongs to the Tibetan, not the Chinese, building tradition. The Fifth Dalai Lama began it in 1645 on the site of a seventh-century palace of the king Songtsen Gampo, and the White Palace, his residence and seat of government, was finished in 1648; the central Red Palace, containing chapels and the gilded tombs of Dalai Lamas, was completed in 1694. The complex climbs the hill to a height of over 110 metres, with thick, inward-sloping walls of stone and rammed earth, small windows and flat roofs, crowned by gilded pavilion roofs. It contains more than a thousand rooms. The Dalai Lamas lived there until the fourteenth fled into exile in 1959. Now a museum, it was inscribed as a World Heritage Site in 1994; the Qing emperors copied its form at Chengde.
