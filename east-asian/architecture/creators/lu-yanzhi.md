# Lü Yanzhi

*呂彥直*

**1894 – 1929** · Chinese  
Born: Tianjin · Died: Shanghai  
Styles & eras: [Meiji Western-style & early modern](../movements/meiji-western.md)  

> Architect who won the competition for the Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum in Nanjing and became the pioneer of a modern Chinese national style.

## Biography

Lü Yanzhi studied at Tsinghua and then at Cornell University, graduating in architecture in 1918. He worked in the office of the American architect Henry Murphy, who was adapting Chinese palace forms to modern college buildings, and returned to China to practise in Shanghai. In 1925 he won the competition for the mausoleum of Sun Yat-sen on Purple Mountain outside Nanjing, and in 1926 the competition for the Sun Yat-sen Memorial Hall in Guangzhou.

His designs used modern construction in reinforced concrete under traditional blue-tiled roofs, and the mausoleum's plan, a long ascending stairway towards a memorial hall, was read as a bell, a symbol of awakening. Lü died of cancer at the age of thirty-five, before either building was complete. His work set the pattern for the official 'Chinese renaissance' style of the Nationalist government.

## Key buildings & sites

### Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum (1926–1929)

[![Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/64/Sun_Yat-sen_Mausoleum_-_54400198691.jpg/1280px-Sun_Yat-sen_Mausoleum_-_54400198691.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sun_Yat-sen_Mausoleum_-_54400198691.jpg)

<sub>Sun Yat-sen Mausoleum, Nanjing Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sun_Yat-sen_Mausoleum_-_54400198691.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1926–1929
- **Type:** Mausoleum
- **Location:** Purple Mountain, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sun%20Yat-sen%20Mausoleum%20L%C3%BC%20Yanzhi)

Sun Yat-sen died in 1925 having asked to be buried on Purple Mountain outside Nanjing. An international competition was held, and Lü Yanzhi's design won; construction began in 1926 and Sun's body was interred in 1929, although work continued into the early 1930s and Lü himself died before it was complete. The mausoleum is approached through a memorial arch and a gate, then up a broad stairway of 392 steps rising about 70 metres to a memorial hall of white granite with a blue-tiled roof, in reinforced concrete. Behind it lies a domed chamber with a marble effigy of Sun above the burial vault. The plan, seen from above, was said to resemble a bell, an image of awakening the nation. The mausoleum became the model for the Nationalist government's 'Chinese renaissance' architecture and is one of the most visited sites in China.
