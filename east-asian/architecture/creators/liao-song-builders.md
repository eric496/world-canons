# Liao and Song builders

*遼宋工匠*

**active 10th – 13th century** · Chinese  
Styles & eras: [Song architecture & the Yingzao Fashi](../movements/song-yingzao-fashi.md)  

> The carpenters and masons of the Liao and Song dynasties, whose pagodas and halls are the best-preserved medieval buildings of China.

## Biography

Under the Khitan Liao in the north and the Song in the centre and south, building was organised by official bureaus and local workshops, and donors' names are recorded in inscriptions, but the carpenters who designed the buildings are seldom named. Their practices were codified in the Song Yingzao Fashi of 1103.

Liao builders preserved the robust proportions of Tang architecture, while the Song developed slenderer forms and more elaborate bracketing and decoration. Their surviving works, including the world's tallest timber pagoda and the Iron Pagoda of Kaifeng, were among the first buildings studied by Liang Sicheng and his colleagues in the 1930s.

### Guanyin Pavilion of Dule Temple (984)

[![Guanyin Pavilion of Dule Temple](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a3/Guanyin_Pavilion_of_Dule_Temple.JPG/1280px-Guanyin_Pavilion_of_Dule_Temple.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Guanyin_Pavilion_of_Dule_Temple.JPG)

<sub>Guanyin Pavilion of Dule Temple Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Guanyin_Pavilion_of_Dule_Temple.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 984
- **Type:** Buddhist pavilion
- **Location:** Jizhou, Tianjin, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Guanyin%20Pavilion%20of%20Dule%20Temple%20Liao%20and%20Song%20builders)

Dule Temple was rebuilt in 984 under the Liao, a Khitan dynasty ruling northern China, and its Guanyin Pavilion is one of the oldest surviving multi-storey timber buildings in China. From outside it appears to have two storeys, but inside a hidden mezzanine makes three structural levels, rising around a central void that holds a clay statue of the eleven-headed Guanyin about 16 metres tall, one of the largest of its kind. Twenty-four different types of bracket sets tie the frame together. The pavilion survived numerous earthquakes, including a strong one in 1679 that destroyed most buildings in the area. Liang Sicheng surveyed it in 1932, his first major field study, and called it a key link between Tang and Song architecture.

### Hall of the Holy Mother, Jinci (1023–32; repaired 1102)

[![Hall of the Holy Mother, Jinci](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2e/Goddess_Temple_Jinsi.JPG/1280px-Goddess_Temple_Jinsi.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Goddess_Temple_Jinsi.JPG)

<sub>Hall of the Holy Mother, Jinci Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Goddess_Temple_Jinsi.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1023–32; repaired 1102
- **Type:** Temple hall
- **Location:** Jinci, Taiyuan, Shanxi, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Hall%20of%20the%20Holy%20Mother%2C%20Jinci%20Liao%20and%20Song%20builders)

Jinci is a complex of shrines at the source of the Jin River south-west of Taiyuan, dedicated to the founder of the ancient state of Jin. Its centrepiece, the Hall of the Holy Mother, honours his mother and was built under the Northern Song between 1023 and 1032 and repaired in 1102. The hall has a double-eaved roof and a deep open portico at the front, whose eight columns are encircled by carved wooden dragons, the oldest of their kind. Inside, the absence of interior columns creates a single space for some forty painted clay figures of court ladies, eunuchs and attendants, celebrated for their lifelike individual expressions. In front of the hall, a cross-shaped bridge over a fish pond is also of Song date.

### Iron Pagoda of Kaifeng (1049)

[![Iron Pagoda of Kaifeng](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/Iron_Pagoda_Cropped.jpg/1280px-Iron_Pagoda_Cropped.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Iron_Pagoda_Cropped.jpg)

<sub>Iron Pagoda, Kaifeng Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Iron_Pagoda_Cropped.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1049
- **Type:** Glazed brick pagoda
- **Location:** Kaifeng, Henan, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Iron%20Pagoda%20of%20Kaifeng%20Liao%20and%20Song%20builders)

The pagoda of Youguo Temple in Kaifeng was built in 1049 to replace a timber pagoda destroyed by lightning. It is octagonal, with thirteen storeys rising about 55 metres, and is faced with brown glazed tiles moulded with Buddhas, flying celestials, lions and floral patterns; their dark colour gave it the name Iron Pagoda. The brick walls imitate timber posts, beams and brackets. Kaifeng was repeatedly flooded by the Yellow River, and the pagoda's base now lies buried beneath silt. It survived these floods, earthquakes and shelling in 1938, and is one of the finest examples of the use of glazed ceramic in Song architecture.

### Longxing Temple (founded 586; main halls 11th–12th century)

[![Longxing Temple](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Zhengding_Longxing_Si_2013.08.31_15-50-10.jpg/1280px-Zhengding_Longxing_Si_2013.08.31_15-50-10.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Zhengding_Longxing_Si_2013.08.31_15-50-10.jpg)

<sub>Longxing Temple, Zhengding Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Zhengding_Longxing_Si_2013.08.31_15-50-10.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** founded 586; main halls 11th–12th century
- **Type:** Buddhist temple
- **Location:** Zhengding, Hebei, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Longxing%20Temple%20Liao%20and%20Song%20builders)

Longxing Temple was founded under the Sui in 586, but its present buildings date mainly from the Northern Song. The Hall of Manichudana (Moni Hall), built in 1052, has a square core with porticoes projecting from each side, giving a cross-shaped plan with gable fronts that is known from Song paintings but survives nowhere else. Further back, the Pavilion of Great Mercy houses a bronze Guanyin with forty-two arms, about 21 metres tall, cast in 971 on the orders of the first Song emperor. The temple also preserves a rare revolving sutra cabinet in its own pavilion. Liang Sicheng surveyed it in 1933 and considered the Moni Hall one of the most important examples of Song architecture.

### Pagoda of Fogong Temple (Yingxian Wooden Pagoda) (1056)

[![Pagoda of Fogong Temple (Yingxian Wooden Pagoda)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Pagodaoffogongtemple2019.jpg/1280px-Pagodaoffogongtemple2019.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pagodaoffogongtemple2019.jpg)

<sub>Pagoda of Fogong Temple, Ying County Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pagodaoffogongtemple2019.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1056
- **Type:** Timber pagoda
- **Location:** Ying County, Shanxi, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pagoda%20of%20Fogong%20Temple%20%28Yingxian%20Wooden%20Pagoda%29%20Liao%20and%20Song%20builders)

The Shijia Pagoda of Fogong Temple was built in 1056 by the Liao emperor Daozong on the site of his grandmother's family home. Octagonal in plan, it shows five storeys outside but has four hidden mezzanines, making nine structural levels, each a ring of columns linked by beams and stiffened by diagonal bracing. Some fifty-four different kinds of bracket sets have been counted. Each main storey houses Buddhist statues, and inside the pagoda relics and printed sutras of the Liao were found during repairs in the 1970s. Its height of about 67 metres makes it the tallest pre-modern timber building in China. It has survived earthquakes and war damage but now leans, and the best way to stabilise it has been debated by engineers for decades.
