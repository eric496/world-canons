# Lei family (Yangshi Lei)

*樣式雷*

**1619 – early 20th century** · Chinese (Qing)  
Born: Yongxiu, Jiangxi · Died: ?  
Styles & eras: [Ming–Qing imperial architecture](../movements/ming-qing-imperial.md)  

> Family of designers who ran the Qing court's design office for some two hundred years, producing plans and models for palaces, gardens and imperial tombs.

## Biography

The family's founder, Lei Fada (1619–1693), was a carpenter from Jiangxi who came to Beijing and worked on the rebuilding of palace halls under the Kangxi Emperor. His descendants held the post of head of the imperial design office, the yangshi fang, for seven or eight generations until the end of the Qing, which is why they were known as 'Pattern Lei'. They worked on the Old Summer Palace, the Summer Palace, Chengde and the Eastern and Western Qing Tombs, among many other projects.

The Lei produced drawings and finely made architectural models (tangyang) of pasteboard that were shown to the emperor for approval, showing rooms, furniture and gardens at scale. Thousands of their drawings and models survive in the National Library of China, the Palace Museum and other collections, and were inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World register in 2007. They are the best documentation of how Qing imperial architecture was designed.

### Old Summer Palace (Yuanmingyuan) (c. 1707–1860)

[![Old Summer Palace (Yuanmingyuan)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/73/Yuanmingyuan_Ruins_of_Dashuifa_20120715.JPG/1280px-Yuanmingyuan_Ruins_of_Dashuifa_20120715.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yuanmingyuan_Ruins_of_Dashuifa_20120715.JPG)

- **Date:** c. 1707–1860
- **Type:** Imperial garden palace (ruins)
- **Location:** Haidian, Beijing, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Old%20Summer%20Palace%20%28Yuanmingyuan%29%20Lei%20family%20%28Yangshi%20Lei%29)

The Yuanmingyuan, the 'Garden of Perfect Brightness', began as a gift from the Kangxi Emperor to his son, the future Yongzheng Emperor, in 1707, and grew under the Qianlong Emperor into a complex of three gardens covering about 350 hectares, with hundreds of halls, pavilions and scenes around lakes and canals. The emperors lived and governed there for much of the year. The Lei family served as court designers for many of its buildings. In one corner Jesuit missionaries, among them Giuseppe Castiglione, designed the Xiyang Lou, Western-style palaces of stone with fountains and mazes. In October 1860, during the Second Opium War, British and French troops looted the palace and the British commander ordered it burned. Further destruction followed. The ruins of the European buildings are now its best-known image, and the site has become a memorial to national humiliation.

### Summer Palace (1750; rebuilt 1886–95)

[![Summer Palace](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Longevity_Hill_of_the_Summer_Palace.jpg/1280px-Longevity_Hill_of_the_Summer_Palace.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Longevity_Hill_of_the_Summer_Palace.jpg)

<sub>Longevity Hill and the Tower of Buddhist Incense, Summer Palace Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Longevity_Hill_of_the_Summer_Palace.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1750; rebuilt 1886–95
- **Type:** Imperial garden palace
- **Location:** Haidian, Beijing, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Summer%20Palace%20Lei%20family%20%28Yangshi%20Lei%29)

The Qianlong Emperor created the Garden of Clear Ripples in 1750 around a hill and an enlarged lake north-west of Beijing, in honour of his mother's birthday. Burned by British and French troops in 1860, it was rebuilt from 1886, reportedly with funds diverted from the navy, for the Empress Dowager Cixi, and renamed Yiheyuan, the 'Garden of Preserved Harmony'; it was damaged again in 1900 and repaired. The court designers of the Lei family prepared plans and models for the reconstruction. The design borrows scenes from across China, especially from the lakes of Hangzhou and the gardens of the south: the Tower of Buddhist Incense crowns Longevity Hill, a painted Long Corridor of over 700 metres runs along the shore, and causeways, a seventeen-arch bridge and a marble boat dot the lake. It is a World Heritage Site and the best preserved Chinese imperial garden.
