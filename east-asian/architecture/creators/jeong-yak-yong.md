# Jeong Yak-yong

*정약용 (丁若鏞)*

**1762 – 1836** · Korean (Joseon)  
Born: Majae, Gwangju, Gyeonggi Province · Died: Majae, Gwangju, Gyeonggi Province  
Styles & eras: [Joseon palaces & seowon](../movements/joseon-palaces-seowon.md)  
Also in: [Philosophy](../../philosophy/creators/jeong-yak-yong.md), [Science & Technology](../../science/creators/jeong-yak-yong.md)  

> Joseon Silhak scholar who designed the Hwaseong Fortress at Suwon for King Jeongjo, using cranes and pulleys of his own design.

## Biography

Jeong Yak-yong, known as Dasan, passed the civil service examination in 1789 and became a favourite of King Jeongjo. In 1792 the king commissioned him to plan the walls of the new city of Hwaseong at Suwon, around the tomb of the king's father. Drawing on Chinese and Western books, Jeong designed the fortifications and lifting devices, including the geojunggi crane, that sped up construction between 1794 and 1796. After Jeongjo's death in 1800 he was exiled for eighteen years because of his links with Catholicism.

In exile at Gangjin he wrote some five hundred volumes on government, law, medicine and the classics, most famously the Mongmin simseo on the duties of local officials. Hwaseong combined Korean and Chinese fortification practice with brick construction and new technology, and its detailed building record, the Hwaseong seongyeok uigwe, made its faithful restoration possible after the Korean War.

## Key buildings & sites

### Hwaseong Fortress (1794–1796)

[![Hwaseong Fortress](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Hwaseong_Fortress%2C_Suwon%2C_Gyeonggi-do%2C_Republic_of_Korea_%282%29.jpg/1280px-Hwaseong_Fortress%2C_Suwon%2C_Gyeonggi-do%2C_Republic_of_Korea_%282%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hwaseong_Fortress%2C_Suwon%2C_Gyeonggi-do%2C_Republic_of_Korea_%282%29.jpg)

- **Date:** 1794–1796
- **Type:** Fortified city walls
- **Location:** Suwon, Gyeonggi Province, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Hwaseong%20Fortress%20Jeong%20Yak-yong)

King Jeongjo moved the tomb of his father, Crown Prince Sado, to Suwon in 1789 and decided to build a new walled city there, partly as a political base. Jeong Yak-yong planned the fortifications, combining Korean and Chinese practice, and designed lifting devices including the geojunggi crane, which used pulleys to raise heavy stones. Built between 1794 and 1796, the walls run about 5.7 kilometres over hills and plains, with four main gates, sluice gates, command posts, beacon towers and gun emplacements, using both stone and brick. In 1801 a detailed report, the Hwaseong seongyeok uigwe, recorded every stage of construction, with illustrations, costs and the names of workers. When much of the fortress was damaged in the Korean War, this record made its faithful reconstruction possible. It was inscribed as a World Heritage Site in 1997.
