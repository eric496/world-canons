# Qing imperial builders

*清代宮廷工匠*

**active 1644–1912** · Chinese  
Styles & eras: [Ming–Qing imperial architecture](../movements/ming-qing-imperial.md)  

> The court designers, craftsmen and labourers of the Qing dynasty, who built palaces, gardens and temples across the empire.

## Biography

The Qing court continued the Ming system of imperial construction under the Ministry of Works and the Imperial Household Department, with hereditary design families such as the Lei. In 1734 the court issued its own building regulations, the Gongcheng zuofa zeli, which fixed the dimensions and labour for official buildings.

Qing builders expanded the imperial repertoire to include Tibetan and Mongolian Buddhist architecture, European-style pavilions designed by Jesuits and vast garden palaces. Their work at Chengde and Beijing reflects the multi-ethnic character of the empire.

## Key buildings & sites

### Chengde Mountain Resort and Outlying Temples (1703–1792)

[![Chengde Mountain Resort and Outlying Temples](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fa/Chengde_Mountain_Resort_22944-Chengde_%2844830038471%29.jpg/1280px-Chengde_Mountain_Resort_22944-Chengde_%2844830038471%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chengde_Mountain_Resort_22944-Chengde_%2844830038471%29.jpg)

<sub>Chengde Mountain Resort Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chengde_Mountain_Resort_22944-Chengde_%2844830038471%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1703–1792
- **Type:** Imperial summer palace and temples
- **Location:** Chengde, Hebei, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Chengde%20Mountain%20Resort%20and%20Outlying%20Temples%20Qing%20imperial%20builders)

The Kangxi Emperor began a summer palace at Chengde, north-east of Beijing beyond the Great Wall, in 1703, and the Qianlong Emperor enlarged it until 1792. The resort, enclosed by a wall about ten kilometres long, combines restrained grey-tiled palace halls, lakes with scenes copied from southern gardens, a plain and a mountainous park. It was used for hunting and for receiving Mongol, Tibetan and other envoys of the multi-ethnic empire. Around it the emperors built a ring of Buddhist temples in Chinese, Tibetan and mixed styles, including the Putuo Zongcheng Temple (1771), modelled on the Potala Palace in Lhasa, and the Xumi Fushou Temple based on the Tashilhunpo monastery. The ensemble is a World Heritage Site and shows how Qing architecture expressed political rule over Inner Asia.
