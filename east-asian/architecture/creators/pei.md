# I. M. Pei

*貝聿銘*

**1917 – 2019** · Chinese-American  
Born: Guangzhou, China · Died: New York City  
Styles & eras: [Contemporary architecture](../movements/east-asian-contemporary-arch.md)  

> Chinese-American architect of geometric modern buildings, whose late works in Hong Kong and Suzhou engaged directly with Chinese tradition.

## Biography

Ieoh Ming Pei grew up in Hong Kong, Shanghai and his family's home city of Suzhou, where he played in the Lion Grove Garden, then owned by his family. He went to the United States in 1935 to study architecture at the University of Pennsylvania and MIT, and later at Harvard under Gropius. He worked for the developer William Zeckendorf before founding his own firm, whose works included the East Building of the National Gallery in Washington and the glass pyramid of the Louvre. He received the Pritzker Prize in 1983.

In China Pei designed the Fragrant Hill Hotel near Beijing (1982) as an attempt at a modern Chinese architecture, then the Bank of China Tower in Hong Kong (1990), a soaring triangulated prism, and in his late eighties the Suzhou Museum (2006), a reinterpretation of the white walls, grey roofs and rockeries of the Suzhou garden. His Chinese work shows a lifelong concern with how modern geometry might carry a sense of place.

### Bank of China Tower (1985–1990)

[![Bank of China Tower](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/25/HK_Bank_of_China_Tower_2008_%282%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:HK_Bank_of_China_Tower_2008_%282%29.jpg)

<sub>Bank of China Tower, Hong Kong Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:HK_Bank_of_China_Tower_2008_%282%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1985–1990
- **Type:** Office skyscraper
- **Location:** Central, Hong Kong
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Bank%20of%20China%20Tower%20I.%20M.%20Pei)

The Bank of China commissioned I. M. Pei, whose father had been a senior figure at the bank, to design its Hong Kong headquarters, completed in 1990 ahead of the return of Hong Kong to China. The tower is a square in plan divided diagonally into four triangular prisms that terminate at different heights, so that the building narrows as it rises to 315 metres (367 metres with its masts). Its structure is a space frame of steel and concrete with giant diagonal braces expressed on the facade, which allowed it to use much less steel than conventional towers of its height. The design was inspired by the image of growing bamboo. Its sharp edges were criticised by some local practitioners of feng shui. It was the tallest building in Asia until 1992 and remains a defining element of the Hong Kong skyline.

### Suzhou Museum (2002–2006)

- **Date:** 2002–2006
- **Type:** Museum
- **Location:** Suzhou, Jiangsu, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Suzhou%20Museum%20I.%20M.%20Pei)

In his late eighties I. M. Pei accepted the commission for a new museum of Suzhou's art and history, next to the Humble Administrator's Garden and in the city where his family had lived for generations. The museum, opened in 2006, takes the white plastered walls and grey roofs of local houses and translates them into crisp geometry, with roofs of dark grey granite, steel frames and skylights that filter light through wooden louvres. Galleries are arranged around courtyards and a central garden with a pond, a bridge and a 'rock painting' of sliced stones set against a white wall, a modern reinterpretation of the garden rockery. Pei described it as his 'little daughter', and it is often considered one of the most successful attempts to create a modern Chinese architecture.
