# Liang Sicheng

*梁思成*

**1901 – 1972** · Chinese  
Born: Tokyo, Japan · Died: Beijing  
Styles & eras: [Meiji Western-style & early modern](../movements/meiji-western.md), [Japanese modernism & Metabolism](../movements/metabolism-modernism.md)  

> Architect and historian who founded the modern study of Chinese architecture and, with Lin Huiyin, rediscovered the Tang hall of Foguang Temple.

## Biography

Liang Sicheng was the eldest son of the reformer Liang Qichao, born in Tokyo during his father's exile. He studied architecture at the University of Pennsylvania from 1924 to 1927, married the architect and poet Lin Huiyin, and in 1928 founded the architecture department of Northeastern University in Shenyang. From 1931 he led field research at the Society for Research in Chinese Architecture, surveying hundreds of old buildings in difficult conditions, and in 1937 identified the Tang dynasty East Hall of Foguang Temple. After 1946 he founded the architecture department at Tsinghua University.

Liang decoded the Song Yingzao Fashi and the Qing building regulations and wrote the first modern history of Chinese architecture, illustrated with his own measured drawings. After 1949 he helped design the national emblem and the Monument to the People's Heroes, and argued, unsuccessfully, for preserving the walls and gates of old Beijing. He was persecuted during the Cultural Revolution and died in 1972; his reputation and his conservation ideas were restored after his death.

## Key buildings & sites

### Monument to the People's Heroes (1952–1958)

- **Date:** 1952–1958
- **Type:** Monument
- **Location:** Tiananmen Square, Beijing, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Monument%20to%20the%20People%27s%20Heroes%20Liang%20Sicheng)

In 1949 the new government decided to raise a monument to those who had died in the revolutionary struggles of the previous century, and a design committee was formed that included Liang Sicheng as a leading designer and Lin Huiyin, who was responsible for much of the decorative design. Built from 1952 and unveiled in 1958, it is a granite obelisk about 38 metres high, standing on a two-tiered terrace with white marble balustrades. The front carries an inscription in Mao Zedong's calligraphy, and the base is surrounded by eight large marble reliefs depicting events from the Opium War to the crossing of the Yangzi in 1949. Liang argued for a monument with a traditional Chinese character, a stele rather than a Western obelisk, and the result combines both. The monument has been a focus of mourning and protest, notably in 1976 and 1989.
