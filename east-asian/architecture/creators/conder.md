# Josiah Conder

*ジョサイア・コンドル*

**1852 – 1920** · British  
Born: London · Died: Tokyo  
Styles & eras: [Meiji Western-style & early modern](../movements/meiji-western.md)  

> English architect who taught the first generation of Japanese architects in Meiji Tokyo and designed the Rokumeikan.

## Biography

Josiah Conder trained in London under the Gothic Revival architect William Burges and won the Soane Medallion in 1876. In 1877, aged twenty-four, he was hired by the Meiji government to teach architecture at the Imperial College of Engineering in Tokyo, where his first students included Tatsuno Kingo, Katayama Tōkuma and Sone Tatsuzō. He went on to design government buildings, the Rokumeikan reception hall (1883), the Russian Orthodox Nikolai Cathedral, offices for Mitsubishi and mansions for the Iwasaki family. He stayed in Japan for the rest of his life, married a Japanese wife and died in Tokyo in 1920.

Conder's buildings mixed Gothic, Renaissance and colonial motifs, sometimes with Indo-Saracenic touches meant to evoke 'the East'. More lasting was his teaching, which created the Japanese architectural profession, and his deep interest in Japanese culture: he studied painting with Kawanabe Kyōsai and wrote pioneering English books on Japanese gardens and flower arrangement. A statue of him stands at the University of Tokyo.

### Rokumeikan (1883 (demolished 1940))

[![Rokumeikan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/94/Tokyo-Rokumeikan-zenmen-1883-1900.jpg/1280px-Tokyo-Rokumeikan-zenmen-1883-1900.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tokyo-Rokumeikan-zenmen-1883-1900.jpg)

<sub>The Rokumeikan, photograph c. 1883–1900 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tokyo-Rokumeikan-zenmen-1883-1900.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1883 (demolished 1940)
- **Type:** Reception hall
- **Location:** Hibiya, Tokyo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Rokumeikan%20Josiah%20Conder)

The Rokumeikan, the 'Deer-Cry Pavilion', was commissioned by the foreign minister Inoue Kaoru as a place where Japanese officials could entertain foreign diplomats in Western style, as part of an effort to persuade the Western powers to revise the unequal treaties. Designed by Josiah Conder and opened in 1883, it was a two-storey building of brick and stucco with arcaded verandas, combining French Second Empire and Italianate features with Indo-Saracenic touches. Balls, concerts and bazaars were held there, and the 'Rokumeikan era' became a byword for superficial Westernisation, mocked by critics and cartoonists. After the policy changed the building became a club for the peerage. It was demolished in 1940.

### Former Iwasaki Residence (1896)

[![Former Iwasaki Residence](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/11/Kyu_Iwasaki_tei_in_Tait%C5%8D-ku%2CTokyo_-_Entrance_Hall.jpg/1280px-Kyu_Iwasaki_tei_in_Tait%C5%8D-ku%2CTokyo_-_Entrance_Hall.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kyu_Iwasaki_tei_in_Tait%C5%8D-ku%2CTokyo_-_Entrance_Hall.jpg)

<sub>Entrance hall of the Former Iwasaki Residence Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kyu_Iwasaki_tei_in_Tait%C5%8D-ku%2CTokyo_-_Entrance_Hall.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1896
- **Type:** Residence
- **Location:** Ikenohata, Taitō, Tokyo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Former%20Iwasaki%20Residence%20Josiah%20Conder)

In 1896 Josiah Conder completed a mansion for Iwasaki Hisaya, the third head of the Mitsubishi company, on an estate near Ueno. The main house, built of timber, is in a Jacobean manner with a two-storey veranda of colonnades towards the garden and richly carved interiors, and a separate billiard room is built like a Swiss chalet. A large Japanese-style residence was attached, of which only a part survives. The combination of Western reception rooms and Japanese living quarters was typical of Meiji elite houses. The surviving buildings are Important Cultural Properties, and the house and garden are open to the public as the Kyū-Iwasaki-tei Garden.
