# Shunjōbō Chōgen

*俊乗房重源*

**1121 – 1206** · Japanese  
Born: Kyoto · Died: Nara  
Styles & eras: [Kamakura Buddhist & Zen architecture](../movements/kamakura-zen.md)  

> Buddhist monk who led the rebuilding of Tōdai-ji after 1181, introducing the bold Daibutsuyō ('Great Buddha style') from southern China.

## Biography

Chōgen was born into a minor warrior family in Kyoto, became a monk of the Shingon school at Daigo-ji and practised as a wandering ascetic devoted to the Pure Land. By his own account he travelled to Song China several times, and he came back with knowledge of Chinese building and engineering. When Tōdai-ji in Nara was burned by Taira forces in 1180, the court appointed him in 1181, at about sixty, to raise funds and direct the reconstruction, which he did for the rest of his life, working with Chinese craftsmen such as the caster Chen Heqing.

For Tōdai-ji Chōgen used a structural system from Fujian: tall columns tied together by penetrating tie-beams and bracket arms inserted directly into the columns, with exposed rafters and no ceiling. It allowed enormous buildings to be built quickly from smaller timbers. The Great South Gate (Nandaimon) and the Jōdo-dō of Jōdo-ji at Ono are the main surviving examples. A celebrated wooden portrait statue of the aged Chōgen is preserved at Tōdai-ji.

## Key buildings & sites

### Great South Gate (Nandaimon) of Tōdai-ji (1199–1203)

[![Great South Gate (Nandaimon) of Tōdai-ji](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Nandaimon%2C_Todaiji_2013-01-01.jpg/1280px-Nandaimon%2C_Todaiji_2013-01-01.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nandaimon%2C_Todaiji_2013-01-01.jpg)

<sub>Great South Gate (Nandaimon) of Tōdai-ji Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nandaimon%2C_Todaiji_2013-01-01.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1199–1203
- **Type:** Temple gate
- **Location:** Nara, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Great%20South%20Gate%20%28Nandaimon%29%20of%20T%C5%8Ddai-ji%20Shunj%C5%8Db%C5%8D%20Ch%C5%8Dgen)

The Great South Gate was raised in 1199 as part of Chōgen's reconstruction of Tōdai-ji and completed in 1203. About 25 metres high, it uses the Daibutsuyō ('Great Buddha style') that Chōgen introduced from southern China: eighteen tall columns run straight up to the roof, tied together by horizontal penetrating tie-beams, and bracket arms are inserted directly into the columns in six steps to carry the deep eaves. There is no ceiling, so the whole structure is visible from below. The method allowed a huge gate to be built quickly and economically. In 1203 the sculptors Unkei and Kaikei and their workshop carved the two Kongō Rikishi guardian figures, each about 8.4 metres tall, in only sixty-nine days. The gate and statues are National Treasures and among the most powerful works of Kamakura architecture and sculpture.
