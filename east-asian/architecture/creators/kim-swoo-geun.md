# Kim Swoo-geun

*김수근 (金壽根)*

**1931 – 1986** · Korean  
Born: Cheongjin, Hamgyong Province · Died: Seoul  
Styles & eras: [Japanese modernism & Metabolism](../movements/metabolism-modernism.md)  

> South Korean architect of the Space Group building and the Seoul Olympic Stadium, a central figure in Korean modern architecture.

## Biography

Kim Swoo-geun was born in the north of Korea under Japanese rule, studied at Seoul National University and then in Tokyo at the Tokyo University of the Arts and the University of Tokyo. Returning to Seoul in 1960 he won major commissions and in 1966 founded the magazine Space, which became the centre of Korean architectural and artistic debate. His Space Group office trained many Korean architects.

Kim's early concrete works showed the influence of Japanese modernism, and his design for the Buyeo National Museum (1967) was criticised for resembling a Japanese shrine. He later developed a language of dark brick, intimate scale and layered space inspired by traditional Korean houses, seen in the Space Group building (1971–77) and the Kyungdong Church (1981). His Olympic Stadium (1984), with curves derived from Joseon porcelain, was built for the 1988 Seoul Olympics.

### Kyungdong Presbyterian Church (1981)

[![Kyungdong Presbyterian Church](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/Kyungdong_Presbyterian_Church.jpg/1280px-Kyungdong_Presbyterian_Church.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kyungdong_Presbyterian_Church.jpg)

<sub>Kyungdong Presbyterian Church, Seoul Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kyungdong_Presbyterian_Church.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1981
- **Type:** Church
- **Location:** Jangchung-dong, Jung, Seoul, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Kyungdong%20Presbyterian%20Church%20Kim%20Swoo-geun)

Kim Swoo-geun designed the Kyungdong Presbyterian Church, completed in 1981, for a congregation in central Seoul. The exterior is a series of massive, fluted brick volumes without obvious windows, built of dark, roughly textured brick, so that the church appears like a closed fortress or a pair of praying hands. Worshippers enter by a long outer stair around the side, and the interior is lit by a concealed skylight above the altar. The building shows Kim's late architecture of brick, intimate passages and controlled light, with its roots in Korean traditional space, and is considered one of the key works of Korean modern architecture.

### Seoul Olympic Stadium (1977–1984)

[![Seoul Olympic Stadium](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Seoul_Jamsil_Main_Stadium.jpg/1280px-Seoul_Jamsil_Main_Stadium.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Seoul_Jamsil_Main_Stadium.jpg)

<sub>Seoul Olympic Stadium Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Seoul_Jamsil_Main_Stadium.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1977–1984
- **Type:** Stadium
- **Location:** Jamsil, Songpa, Seoul, South Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Seoul%20Olympic%20Stadium%20Kim%20Swoo-geun)

The Seoul Olympic Stadium, at the Seoul Sports Complex in Jamsil, was designed by Kim Swoo-geun and completed in 1984 as the main venue for the 1986 Asian Games and the 1988 Olympics. Its gently curving roof line, rising and falling around the oval, was inspired by the profile of white porcelain jars of the Joseon period. It seats around 70,000 spectators and was the setting for the opening and closing ceremonies and athletics of the 1988 Games, a key moment in South Korea's emergence on the world stage. Kim died in 1986 before the Games took place.
