## Biography

Zhu Xi spent most of his life as a teacher and minor official in Fujian, building the Neo-Confucian synthesis whose commentaries on the Four Books became the basis of the civil-service examinations from 1313. His life and philosophy are described in the philosophy section.

As a historian he believed that history should make moral principle visible. He planned a condensation of Sima Guang's Comprehensive Mirror in which large-type 'outline' headings pass judgement in the manner of the Spring and Autumn Annals and small-type 'details' give the narrative. Completed by his disciples, the Gangmu shaped popular historical understanding in China, Korea and Japan for centuries.

## Key histories & documents

### Outline and Details of the Comprehensive Mirror (Tongjian gangmu) (c. 1172; printed 1219)

- **Date:** c. 1172; printed 1219
- **Type:** Moralised chronological history
- **Written / held:** Planned by Zhu Xi and completed by his disciples, notably Zhao Shiyuan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Outline%20and%20Details%20of%20the%20Comprehensive%20Mirror%20%28Tongjian%20gangmu%29%20Zhu%20Xi)

Zhu Xi admired Sima Guang's history but disagreed with some of its judgements, notably its treatment of Cao Cao's Wei rather than Liu Bei's Shu Han as the legitimate dynasty of the Three Kingdoms. He designed a work in which brief headings in large characters, framed in the 'praise and blame' language of the Spring and Autumn Annals, state the moral meaning of each event, followed by details in smaller characters. His students completed the 59 chapters, and it was printed in 1219. Under the Ming and Qing it was enlarged, issued in imperial editions and widely taught; in Korea and Japan it shaped the writing of national histories such as An Jeong-bok's Dongsa gangmok and the Honchō tsugan. Modern historians value it less as a source than as evidence of how Neo-Confucians read the past.
