# Fukuzawa Yukichi

*福澤諭吉*

**1835 – 1901** · Japanese  
Born: Osaka · Died: Tokyo  
Traditions & eras: [Meiji constitutionalism & modern historiography](../movements/meiji-documents.md)  
Also in: [Philosophy](../../philosophy/creators/fukuzawa-yukichi.md)  

> Founder of Keiō University and the most influential popular educator of Meiji Japan, whose Conditions in the West introduced Western institutions to a mass readership.

## Biography

Born to a low-ranking samurai family of the Nakatsu domain, Fukuzawa studied Dutch learning in Nagasaki and Osaka, then taught himself English and travelled with the shogunate's missions to the United States (1860) and Europe (1862). He opened the school that became Keiō Gijuku, founded the newspaper Jiji shinpō, and refused government office. His ideas are treated in the philosophy section.

His Seiyō jijō, a lucid account of Western governments, taxation, schools, hospitals and banks, sold in huge numbers in the late 1860s and shaped Meiji reform. His later writings on Asia, especially the editorial known as 'Leaving Asia' (1885), are debated by historians for their role in Japanese attitudes to China and Korea.

## Key histories & documents

### Conditions in the West (Seiyō jijō) (1866–1870)

[![Conditions in the West (Seiyō jijō)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Fukuzawa_Yukichi_-_Seiyo-jijo.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fukuzawa_Yukichi_-_Seiyo-jijo.jpg)

<sub>Title page of Seiyō jijō, 1866 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fukuzawa_Yukichi_-_Seiyo-jijo.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1866–1870
- **Type:** Account of Western institutions (10 volumes)
- **Written / held:** Published in Edo/Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Conditions%20in%20the%20West%20%28Seiy%C5%8D%20jij%C5%8D%29%20Fukuzawa%20Yukichi)

Drawing on his travels and on English and American books, including a Chambers's educational text on political economy, Fukuzawa described in plain Japanese how Western countries were governed and how their schools, hospitals, newspapers, banks, steam engines and postal systems worked, with sketches of the history of the United States, Britain, the Netherlands and other countries. The first volume included a translation of the American Declaration of Independence. Said to have sold some quarter of a million copies including pirated editions, it informed both the new government and ordinary readers and shaped the vocabulary of Meiji reform.
