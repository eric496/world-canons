# Fan Ye

*范曄*

**398 – 445** · Chinese  
Born: Shunyang (Henan) · Died: Jiankang (Nanjing), executed  
Traditions & eras: [Han historians & the early standard histories](../movements/han-histories.md)  

> Liu Song official and historian whose Book of the Later Han became the standard history of the Eastern Han dynasty.

## Biography

Fan Ye belonged to a family of officials of the Southern Dynasties and rose in the service of the Liu Song. Demoted to a provincial post around 432, he used his leisure to write a history of the Eastern Han, drawing on numerous earlier histories of the period, most now lost. In 445 he was accused of joining a plot to depose Emperor Wen and was executed before he could complete the treatises.

The Book of the Later Han is admired for its polished prose and its appraisals at the end of chapters. Fan Ye added new categories of collective biography, including chapters on exemplary women, recluses and the eunuchs and partisan factions of the late Han. The treatises now printed with it were taken from Sima Biao's earlier history and attached in the sixth century.

- **Date:** c. 445
- **Type:** Dynastic history (120 chapters)
- **Written / held:** Written in Liu Song China, Jiankang (Nanjing)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Book%20of%20the%20Later%20Han%20%28Hou%20Hanshu%29%20Fan%20Ye)

Fan Ye wrote ten annals and eighty chapters of biographies, synthesising about a dozen earlier histories of the Eastern Han. He added new categories, including the biographies of eunuchs, of the scholars persecuted in the 'partisan prohibitions', of recluses and of exemplary women, which reflected his interest in moral character. His planned treatises were never written because of his execution; in the sixth century Liu Zhao attached thirty chapters of treatises from Sima Biao's earlier history. The work contains the earliest Chinese reference to an embassy from 'Da Qin', the Roman Empire, in 166, and records a gold seal given to a king of Na in Wa in 57, a seal believed to have been found in Kyushu in 1784.
