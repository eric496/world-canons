## Biography

Zhang Tingyu was the son of the grand secretary Zhang Ying and passed the jinshi in 1700. He became one of the most trusted ministers of the Yongzheng Emperor, helping to organise the Grand Council, and he was the only Han Chinese official granted the honour of enshrinement in the Qing imperial ancestral temple. He served the Kangxi, Yongzheng and Qianlong emperors.

The History of Ming had been under preparation since 1679, drawing on a draft by Wan Sitong and Wang Hongxu; Zhang presided over its final revision and presented it to the throne in 1739. It is regarded as one of the best-organised of the standard histories, though written under a conquest dynasty that was sensitive about Ming loyalism and its own Manchu origins.

## Key histories & documents

### History of Ming (Mingshi) (1739)

- **Date:** 1739
- **Type:** Dynastic history (332 chapters)
- **Written / held:** Compiled at the Qing court, Beijing
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=History%20of%20Ming%20%28Mingshi%29%20Zhang%20Tingyu)

The Qing opened a History Office for the Ming in 1645 and relaunched it in 1679 with scholars recruited through a special examination, many of them sons of Ming loyalists. The private scholar Wan Sitong, who refused office, shaped the draft for two decades, and Wang Hongxu's version became the basis of the final text presented by Zhang Tingyu in 1739. It is praised for its organisation and its treatises, and includes chapters on eunuchs, corrupt officials and 'roving bandits'. Its treatment of the Manchu rise and of southern Ming resistance was constrained by Qing sensitivities, and contemporary literary inquisitions punished unauthorised Ming histories. With it the canon of the Twenty-Four Histories was declared complete in the Qianlong reign.
