<sub>Portrait: Qing-dynasty portrait of Ji Yun · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ji_Yun.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Ji Yun passed the jinshi in 1754 and served in the Hanlin Academy. Banished to Ürümqi in 1768 for warning a relative of an impending investigation, he was recalled in 1771 and in 1773 appointed one of the chief editors of the Qianlong Emperor's great library project, a post he held for over a decade. He later rose to be minister of rites and was famous for his wit.

He is credited with the final shaping of the Annotated General Catalogue of the Four Treasuries, which reviews more than ten thousand titles and remains a basic guide to traditional Chinese literature. He also wrote the Notes of the Thatched Abode of Close Scrutiny (Yuewei caotang biji), a popular collection of ghost and anecdote stories.

## Key histories & documents

### Complete Library of the Four Treasuries (Siku quanshu) (1773–1782)

[![Complete Library of the Four Treasuries (Siku quanshu)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/The_Complete_Library_in_Four_Sections_%28Siku_Quanshu%29_WDL3020.jpg/960px-The_Complete_Library_in_Four_Sections_%28Siku_Quanshu%29_WDL3020.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Complete_Library_in_Four_Sections_%28Siku_Quanshu%29_WDL3020.jpg)

<sub>Manuscript volume of the Siku quanshu (World Digital Library) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Complete_Library_in_Four_Sections_%28Siku_Quanshu%29_WDL3020.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1773–1782
- **Type:** Imperial manuscript library
- **Written / held:** Compiled at the Qing court, Beijing; seven manuscript sets, of which the Wenyuan Pavilion copy is now in the National Palace Museum, Taipei
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Complete%20Library%20of%20the%20Four%20Treasuries%20%28Siku%20quanshu%29%20Ji%20Yun)

In 1773 the Qianlong Emperor ordered a search for books across the empire and the copying of the most important into a single library, arranged in the four traditional divisions of classics, histories, philosophers and belles-lettres. Hundreds of scholars under Ji Yun and Lu Xixiong collated texts, including several hundred works recovered from the Ming Yongle Encyclopedia, and thousands of copyists produced seven sets housed in purpose-built pavilions. The annotated catalogue, the Siku quanshu zongmu tiyao, reviews each work. The project also served the court's political aims: works judged hostile to the Manchus or to imperial authority were altered, excluded or destroyed, and historians debate how to weigh its preservation of learning against this censorship. Several sets were lost in the Taiping and other wars; four survive.
