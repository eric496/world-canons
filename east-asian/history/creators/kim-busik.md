# Kim Busik

*金富軾*

**1075 – 1151** · Korean (Goryeo)  
Born:  · Died: Gaegyeong (Kaesong)  
Traditions & eras: [Three Kingdoms & Goryeo histories](../movements/korean-dynastic-histories.md)  

> Goryeo statesman and Confucian scholar who compiled the History of the Three Kingdoms (Samguk sagi), Korea's oldest surviving history.

## Biography

Kim Busik came from a family descended from the Silla royal house of Gyeongju. He passed the civil examination, served on missions to Song China and rose to chief minister. In 1135 he led the government forces that suppressed the rebellion of the monk Myocheong, who had urged moving the capital to Pyongyang and a war against the Jurchen Jin; he retired in 1142.

King Injong commissioned him to compile the Samguk sagi, presented in 1145. Kim followed Chinese historiographical forms and Confucian moral judgement. Twentieth-century nationalist historians such as Sin Chae-ho criticised him for a Silla-centred, China-deferential outlook, while other scholars emphasise his careful use of sources and his insistence that Koreans know their own history.

## Key histories & documents

### History of the Three Kingdoms (Samguk sagi) (1145)

[![History of the Three Kingdoms (Samguk sagi)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/de/Samguk.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Samguk.JPG)

<sub>Printed edition of the Samguk sagi Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Samguk.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1145
- **Type:** Official history (50 gwon)
- **Written / held:** Compiled at the Goryeo court, Gaegyeong (Kaesong); earliest complete printing 1512, Gyeongju
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=History%20of%20the%20Three%20Kingdoms%20%28Samguk%20sagi%29%20Kim%20Busik)

Commissioned by King Injong and compiled by Kim Busik with ten assistants, the Samguk sagi follows the Chinese composite form: twenty-eight gwon of basic annals for the three kingdoms, three of chronological tables, nine of treatises on rites, music, costume, carriages, households, geography and offices, and ten of biographies of generals, scholars, loyal subjects and rebels, including Kim Yu-sin and Eulji Mundeok. Kim drew on lost Korean records as well as Chinese histories, and added his own Confucian commentary. Nationalist historians later accused him of treating Silla as the centre and of deferring to China; other scholars stress that he wanted Koreans to know their own history as well as China's. It remains the foundation of Korean ancient history.
