## Biography

Sin Suk-ju was one of the brilliant young scholars of Sejong's Hall of Worthies, contributing to the phonological work behind Hangul. In 1443 he travelled to Japan as an envoy's secretary. He supported Prince Suyang's seizure of the throne as King Sejo in 1455, a choice that brought him high office as chief state councillor but which later moralists contrasted unfavourably with the 'six martyred ministers' who died opposing the usurpation.

His Haedong jegukgi (1471) describes Japanese geography, lords, customs and diplomatic protocol, with early maps of Japan and Ryukyu, and remained the basis of Joseon policy toward Japan for over a century.

## Key histories & documents

### Record of the Countries across the Eastern Sea (Haedong jegukgi) (1471)

- **Date:** 1471
- **Type:** Geographical and diplomatic record
- **Written / held:** Compiled at the Joseon court, Hanseong (Seoul)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Record%20of%20the%20Countries%20across%20the%20Eastern%20Sea%20%28Haedong%20jegukgi%29%20Sin%20Suk-ju)

Ordered by King Seongjong, the Haedong jegukgi drew on Sin Suk-ju's own journey to Japan in 1443 and on Joseon diplomatic records. It describes the geography and provinces of Japan, the succession of its emperors and shoguns, the major lords and island rulers who sent missions to Korea, customs and languages, and the Ryukyu kingdom, and lays down the rules for receiving Japanese envoys and traders in the three ports open to them. Its maps are among the earliest surviving printed maps of Japan and Ryukyu. Sin's closing advice to maintain peaceful relations with Japan was recalled with regret after the invasions of the 1590s.
