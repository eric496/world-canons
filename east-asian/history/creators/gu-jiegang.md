# Gu Jiegang

*顧頡剛*

**1893 – 1980** · Chinese  
Born: Suzhou, Jiangsu · Died: Beijing  
Traditions & eras: [Revolution, nationalism & the new history](../movements/new-history.md)  

> Leader of the 'Doubting Antiquity' school, who argued that China's ancient history had been built up in layers by later writers.

## Biography

Gu Jiegang studied at Peking University under Hu Shi during the May Fourth era and became a leading historian and folklorist, collecting folk songs and studying the legend of Meng Jiangnü. He taught at Sun Yat-sen, Yenching and other universities, founded the journal Yugong on historical geography, and after 1949 worked at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, editing the punctuated edition of the Twenty-Four Histories.

His theory of 'layered accumulation' held that the further back a legendary ruler stood in the traditional chronology, the later he had entered the record. The debates collected in Gushi bian challenged the historicity of the sage kings and opened ancient texts to critical analysis; archaeology later confirmed some sceptical conclusions and overturned others, notably by verifying the Shang kings.

## Key histories & documents

### Debates on Ancient History (Gushi bian) (1926–1941)

- **Date:** 1926–1941
- **Type:** Collected debates (7 volumes)
- **Written / held:** Published in Beijing and Shanghai
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Debates%20on%20Ancient%20History%20%28Gushi%20bian%29%20Gu%20Jiegang)

The first volume, edited by Gu Jiegang in 1926, opens with a long autobiographical preface explaining how his study of folk songs and drama led him to see ancient history as legend accumulated in layers. Later volumes, edited with Luo Genze, Lü Simian and others, gathered essays by dozens of scholars debating the dates and authenticity of the classics, the historicity of Yu the Great, and the origins of the Five Emperors. The collection made source criticism central to Chinese ancient history, though later archaeology and excavated manuscripts led some scholars to call for 'walking out of the age of doubt'. As a twentieth-century work it remains in copyright.
