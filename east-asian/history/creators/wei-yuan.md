<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Wei Yuan · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wei_Yuan.png)</sub>

## Biography

Wei Yuan belonged to the New Text and statecraft (jingshi) circles of the early nineteenth century, helping to compile the Collected Writings on Statecraft of the Qing (1826) and advising officials on salt administration and grain transport. Shocked by the Opium War, he wrote a military history of the Qing (Shengwu ji, 1842) and, using Lin Zexu's materials, the Haiguo tuzhi.

He urged China to 'learn the superior techniques of the barbarians in order to control them', advocating shipyards, arsenals and naval defence. His book had limited effect at the Qing court at first but was widely read in Japan in the 1850s by reformers such as Sakuma Shōzan and Yoshida Shōin, and it later inspired China's Self-Strengthening Movement.

### Record of the Sacred Wars (Shengwu ji) (1842)

- **Date:** 1842
- **Type:** Military history
- **Written / held:** Written in Yangzhou; first printed 1842
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Record%20of%20the%20Sacred%20Wars%20%28Shengwu%20ji%29%20Wei%20Yuan)

Completed in the year the Treaty of Nanking was signed, the Shengwu ji narrates the Qing conquest and campaigns from the dynasty's founding to the early nineteenth century, including wars against the Zunghars, in Tibet, Burma and Vietnam, and against rebellions such as the White Lotus. Wei Yuan drew on archives he had access to in Beijing and on official compilations, and added essays on military finance, organisation and coastal defence. By recalling the dynasty's past strength and analysing its decline, he argued for reform of the military and administration. It was widely read and reprinted, including in Japan.

### Illustrated Treatise on the Maritime Kingdoms (Haiguo tuzhi) (1843; enlarged 1847, 1852)

[![Illustrated Treatise on the Maritime Kingdoms (Haiguo tuzhi)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/af/Haiguotuzhi.jpg/960px-Haiguotuzhi.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Haiguotuzhi.jpg)

<sub>Page from the Haiguo tuzhi Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Haiguotuzhi.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1843; enlarged 1847, 1852
- **Type:** Geography and history of the world
- **Written / held:** Compiled at Yangzhou; first edition in 50 chapters, final edition in 100
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Illustrated%20Treatise%20on%20the%20Maritime%20Kingdoms%20%28Haiguo%20tuzhi%29%20Wei%20Yuan)

Wei Yuan expanded Lin Zexu's translated Gazetteer of the Four Continents with Chinese and Western sources into a geography and history of the world, arranged by ocean and continent, with maps, descriptions of governments and religions, and essays on maritime defence and the manufacture of ships and guns. His famous strategy was to learn the superior techniques of the foreigners in order to control them. The book went through three editions, reaching 100 chapters in 1852. It reached Japan in the 1850s, where it was reprinted many times and read by reformers in the years of Perry's arrival, while in China it influenced the Self-Strengthening officials of the 1860s.
