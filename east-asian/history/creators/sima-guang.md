## Biography

Sima Guang passed the jinshi at twenty and became a leading official and scholar. A conservative opponent of Wang Anshi's New Policies, he withdrew to Luoyang in 1071 and spent fifteen years there with his assistants Liu Shu, Liu Ban and Fan Zuyu completing his history, which Emperor Shenzong had sponsored and named. After Shenzong's death in 1085 he was recalled as chancellor and reversed many of the reforms before dying the next year. A childhood story in which he saved a playmate by smashing a water jar is still told to Chinese children.

The Comprehensive Mirror covers 1,362 years in a single year-by-year narrative, an alternative to the fragmented dynastic histories. Sima Guang's method of first assembling a draft of all sources, then weighing them and recording his reasons in a separate Examination of Discrepancies, made it a model of critical compilation. His comments, introduced with 'Your servant Guang remarks', frame history as lessons for rulers.

## Key histories & documents

### Comprehensive Mirror to Aid in Government (Zizhi tongjian) (1084)

[![Comprehensive Mirror to Aid in Government (Zizhi tongjian)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/05/Zizhi_Tongjian_by_Sima_Guang.jpg/1280px-Zizhi_Tongjian_by_Sima_Guang.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Zizhi_Tongjian_by_Sima_Guang.jpg)

<sub>Page from an edition of the Zizhi tongjian Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Zizhi_Tongjian_by_Sima_Guang.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1084
- **Type:** Chronological history (294 chapters)
- **Written / held:** Compiled in Luoyang and presented at the Song court, Kaifeng
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Comprehensive%20Mirror%20to%20Aid%20in%20Government%20%28Zizhi%20tongjian%29%20Sima%20Guang)

Sima Guang conceived the work as a single, readable narrative of statecraft to replace the sprawl of the dynastic histories. Emperor Yingzong authorised the project in 1066 and Emperor Shenzong gave it its title, 'mirror for aiding government'. Working with Liu Shu, Liu Ban and Fan Zuyu, Sima Guang first assembled a 'long draft' of all sources arranged by date, then cut it down, writing a thirty-chapter Examination of Discrepancies to explain his choices among conflicting sources. The finished work covers the sixteen dynasties from the Warring States to the end of the Five Dynasties, with Sima Guang's comments on the lessons of each episode. It was the favourite history of emperors and officials in China, Korea and Japan, inspired abridgements and continuations, and Mao Zedong is said to have read it many times.
