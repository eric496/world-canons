# Chen Shou

*陳壽*

**233 – 297** · Chinese  
Born: Anhan, Baxi commandery (Nanchong, Sichuan) · Died: Luoyang  
Traditions & eras: [Han historians & the early standard histories](../movements/han-histories.md)  

> Historian of the Western Jin whose Records of the Three Kingdoms is the standard history of the Wei, Shu and Wu states.

## Biography

Chen Shou was born in the state of Shu Han and served it as a minor official, studying under the scholar Qiao Zhou. After Shu fell to Wei in 263 he entered the service of the Jin dynasty, where the minister Zhang Hua recognised his talent. He compiled the collected writings of Zhuge Liang and then wrote the Records of the Three Kingdoms, covering the period from the collapse of the Han to the Jin reunification in 280.

His history is concise and careful, treating Wei as the legitimate dynasty while giving Shu and Wu their own books. Its terseness prompted Pei Songzhi to add a vast commentary in 429, quoting more than two hundred other sources, and together they became the basis for the later Romance of the Three Kingdoms. The chapter on the eastern peoples, with its account of Wa (Japan) and Queen Himiko, is the earliest substantial written description of Japan.

- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Records%20of%20the%20Three%20Kingdoms%20%28Sanguozhi%29%20Chen%20Shou)

Chen Shou divided his history into three books, thirty chapters on Wei, fifteen on Shu and twenty on Wu, giving imperial annals only to Wei, which the Jin regarded as the legitimate predecessor. His account of figures such as Cao Cao, Liu Bei and Zhuge Liang is spare and balanced. In 429 Pei Songzhi added a commentary that roughly doubled the text with extracts from more than two hundred other works, most now lost, so that the combined book is a treasury of third-century sources. Its section on the eastern barbarians contains the 'Account of the People of Wa', describing Queen Himiko of Yamatai, which is the starting point for early Japanese history. The fourteenth-century novel built on it made its characters household names across East Asia.
