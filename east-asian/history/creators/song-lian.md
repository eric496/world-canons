## Biography

Song Lian was a respected teacher in Zhejiang under the Yuan who joined Zhu Yuanzhang, the future Hongwu Emperor, in 1360. He became tutor to the heir apparent, drafted court rituals and documents, and was celebrated as the foremost writer of the dynasty's founding. In 1380 his grandson was implicated in the alleged conspiracy of the chancellor Hu Weiyong; Song Lian was spared execution at the plea of the empress but banished, and died on the road.

He headed the commission that compiled the History of Yuan in two hurried sessions in 1369 and 1370. The speed showed: later scholars criticised duplicated biographies and inconsistent transcriptions of Mongol names, and the Republican scholar Ke Shaomin wrote a New History of Yuan to replace it. Yet its rapid compilation also preserved large amounts of Yuan documentary material verbatim.

- **Date:** 1370
- **Type:** Dynastic history (210 chapters)
- **Written / held:** Compiled at the Ming court, Nanjing
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=History%20of%20Yuan%20%28Yuanshi%29%20Song%20Lian)

The Hongwu Emperor ordered the history of the Yuan in 1369 to proclaim that its mandate had ended. Song Lian and Wang Wei led a team that worked from the Yuan veritable records and the administrative compendium Jingshi dadian, producing the first draft in 188 days and completing it in 1370 after a second session to cover the last reign. Its haste caused duplications and inconsistent renderings of Mongol and Central Asian names, but it preserves many Yuan documents verbatim, including much on the empire's institutions, Tibetan Buddhism and the Grand Canal. Modern scholarship combines it with Persian sources such as Rashid al-Din and with the Secret History of the Mongols.
