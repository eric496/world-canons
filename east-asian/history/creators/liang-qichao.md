# Liang Qichao

*梁啓超*

**1873 – 1929** · Chinese  
Born: Xinhui, Guangdong · Died: Beijing  
Traditions & eras: [Revolution, nationalism & the new history](../movements/new-history.md)  
Also in: [Philosophy](../../philosophy/creators/liang-qichao.md)  

> Reformer, journalist and scholar who called for a 'new history' of the Chinese nation and wrote China's first modern works of historical method.

## Biography

A student of Kang Youwei and leader of the failed Hundred Days' Reform of 1898, Liang fled to Japan, where he edited influential journals and absorbed Japanese and Western scholarship. He returned to China after 1911, served briefly in Republican governments and spent his last years teaching at Tsinghua. His political thought is treated in the philosophy section.

In New Historiography (1902) he attacked the dynastic histories as 'the family records of twenty-four households' and argued that history should describe the evolution of the nation and its people. His later Methods for the Study of Chinese History (1922) and intellectual histories helped found the modern discipline in China.

### New Historiography (Xin shixue) (1902)

- **Date:** 1902
- **Type:** Essay on historical method
- **Written / held:** Serialised in Xinmin congbao (New Citizen Journal), Yokohama
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=New%20Historiography%20%28Xin%20shixue%29%20Liang%20Qichao)

Written in exile in Japan, New Historiography argued that traditional histories recorded courts rather than nations, individuals rather than groups, past facts rather than present relevance, and events rather than ideas. Liang proposed that history should trace the evolution of the national group and discover the principles of its progress, drawing on Social Darwinism and Japanese and European historiography. The essay also coined or popularised concepts such as the 'Chinese nation' and a periodisation into ancient, medieval and modern. It launched the movement for a 'new history' in China and influenced both Nationalist and Marxist historians.

### Methods for the Study of Chinese History (Zhongguo lishi yanjiufa) (1922)

- **Date:** 1922
- **Type:** Treatise on historical method
- **Written / held:** Lectures at Nankai University, Tianjin, published in Shanghai
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Methods%20for%20the%20Study%20of%20Chinese%20History%20%28Zhongguo%20lishi%20yanjiufa%29%20Liang%20Qichao)

Based on lectures of 1921, the book defines history as the record of human activity in society, surveys the Chinese historiographical tradition from the Shiji to Zhang Xuecheng, and sets out how historians should collect, criticise and interpret sources, including archaeology, inscriptions and non-Chinese records. It combines Western methods, drawn in part from the French manual of Langlois and Seignobos, with Qing evidential scholarship. A supplementary volume followed in 1926. It was widely used as a textbook and shaped the training of Chinese historians in the Republican period.
