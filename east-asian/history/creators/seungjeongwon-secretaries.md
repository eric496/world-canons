# Royal Secretariat of Joseon

*承政院*

**1400 – 1910** · Korean (Joseon)  
Born: Hanseong (Seoul) · Died:   
Traditions & eras: [Joseon Veritable Records & court diaries](../movements/joseon-records.md)  

> The secretaries of the Seungjeongwon, the office that transmitted royal orders and kept the daily Diaries of the Royal Secretariat.

## Biography

The Seungjeongwon was the channel through which documents passed between the king and the government. Its six royal secretaries and their recorders kept a daily diary of the king's schedule, audiences, memorials, orders and even the weather.

Unlike the annals, the diaries were not abridged, so they preserve an exceptionally detailed record of court government. The volumes before 1623 were lost in war and fires, but those from 1623 to 1910 survive.

- **Date:** 1623–1910
- **Type:** Court diary (3,243 volumes)
- **Written / held:** Kept in the palace, Hanseong (Seoul); now in the Kyujanggak Institute, Seoul National University
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Diaries%20of%20the%20Royal%20Secretariat%20%28Seungjeongwon%20ilgi%29%20Royal%20Secretariat%20of%20Joseon)

The Royal Secretariat recorded the king's daily activities, audiences, memorials received, orders issued and even weather each day, and each month's record was bound as a volume. Records before 1623 were destroyed in the Japanese invasions and in later palace fires, but 3,243 volumes from the reign of King Injo to the end of the Korean Empire survive, containing an estimated 240 million characters. Because they were not edited like the Annals, they preserve the language of court exchanges and much detail omitted elsewhere, including meteorological data used by modern climate scientists. The diaries were inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World in 2001, and a modern translation project has been under way for decades.
