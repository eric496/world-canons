## Biography

Zhangsun Wuji was the brother of Empress Zhangsun and a close friend of Li Shimin, the future Emperor Taizong, whom he helped seize the throne in 626. He headed the list of the twenty-four meritorious ministers portrayed in the Lingyan Pavilion, served as chancellor under Taizong and Gaozong, and directed major compilations, including the Book of Sui's treatises. He opposed Gaozong's elevation of Wu Zetian as empress, was accused of treason, exiled and forced to take his own life in 659.

His commission presented the Tang Code with its official commentary (Tanglü shuyi) in 653. The code's clear structure, graded punishments and integration of Confucian family hierarchy into law became the template for the codes of later Chinese dynasties and for Japan, Korea and Vietnam.

## Key histories & documents

### Tang Code (Tanglü shuyi) (653)

- **Date:** 653
- **Type:** Legal code with commentary
- **Written / held:** Promulgated at the Tang court, Chang'an; earliest fragments from Dunhuang and Turfan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Tang%20Code%20%28Tangl%C3%BC%20shuyi%29%20Zhangsun%20Wuji)

The Tang Code, revised several times in the seventh century, contains 502 articles in twelve sections, from general principles and the 'ten abominations' through palace security, households and marriage, fraud, assault and theft to judicial procedure. In 653 a commission under Zhangsun Wuji added an official subcommentary that explains each article with questions and answers. The code grades punishments according to the relationships of offender and victim, building Confucian family hierarchy into law, and specifies five standard punishments from beating to death. It served as the basis of the Song, Ming and Qing codes and was adapted in Japan's Taihō and Yōrō codes, Goryeo law and the Vietnamese Lê code, making it one of the most influential legal texts in world history.
