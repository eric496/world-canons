# Treaty of Nanking negotiators

*南京條約談判代表*

**1842 – 1842** · Chinese and British  
Traditions & eras: [Opium War, treaties & Western learning](../movements/treaty-ports.md)  

> The Qing commissioners Qiying and Yilibu and the British plenipotentiary Sir Henry Pottinger, who negotiated and signed the Treaty of Nanking in 1842.

## Biography

After British forces captured Zhenjiang and threatened Nanjing in the summer of 1842, the Daoguang Emperor sent the Manchu imperial clansman Qiying and the elderly official Yilibu, with the Liangjiang governor-general Niu Jian, to negotiate. Their counterpart, Sir Henry Pottinger, a veteran of British India, had been sent to press British demands by force. The treaty was signed on 29 August 1842 aboard HMS Cornwallis on the Yangzi.

Qiying went on to negotiate the supplementary Treaty of the Bogue and the treaties with the United States and France in 1844, and was later forced to commit suicide after failing in further negotiations in 1858. Pottinger became the first governor of Hong Kong. The settlement they made opened what Chinese historians call the era of unequal treaties.

### Treaty of Nanking (1842)

- **Date:** 1842
- **Type:** Peace treaty
- **Written / held:** Signed aboard HMS Cornwallis at Nanjing, 29 August 1842
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Treaty%20of%20Nanking%20Treaty%20of%20Nanking%20negotiators)

The Treaty of Nanking's thirteen articles required the Qing to pay twenty-one million silver dollars, open Canton, Amoy (Xiamen), Fuzhou, Ningbo and Shanghai to British residence and trade with consuls, cede Hong Kong Island in perpetuity, abolish the Cohong merchant monopoly and conduct official correspondence on equal terms. The supplementary Treaty of the Bogue (1843) added extraterritoriality and most-favoured-nation treatment, and similar treaties with the United States and France followed in 1844. In Chinese historiography it marks the beginning of the 'century of humiliation' and of the unequal treaties; in Britain it was long seen as opening China to free trade. Hong Kong was returned to China in 1997.
