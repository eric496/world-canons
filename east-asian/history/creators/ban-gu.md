**AD 32 – AD 92** · Chinese  
Born: Anling, near Xianyang (Shaanxi) · Died: Luoyang (in prison)  
Traditions & eras: [Han historians & the early standard histories](../movements/han-histories.md)  

> Eastern Han historian and poet whose Book of Han was the first history devoted to a single dynasty, the model for the official histories that followed.

<sub>Portrait: Imagined portrait of Ban Gu from a later Chinese illustrated book · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ban_Gu.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Ban Gu came from a distinguished family of scholars and generals: his father Ban Biao had begun a continuation of the Shiji, his twin brother Ban Chao was the general who restored Han power in Central Asia, and his sister Ban Zhao was a renowned scholar. After his father's death Ban Gu was briefly imprisoned for privately writing history, but Emperor Ming read his drafts, released him and appointed him to the palace library. He also wrote rhapsodies, notably the Rhapsody on the Two Capitals, and edited the record of the White Tiger Hall debates on the classics. Attached to the general Dou Xian, he was arrested when Dou fell from power in 92 and died in prison.

The Book of Han narrowed Sima Qian's universal history to one dynasty, the Former Han, a choice that made the dynastic history the standard unit of Chinese historiography. Its treatises on law, economics, geography and literature, including the bibliography of the imperial library, are among the most valuable sources for Han China. Its style is more regular and Confucian in tone than the Shiji, and the two works were paired for centuries as 'Shi-Han'.

- **Date:** c. AD 111
- **Type:** Dynastic history (100 chapters)
- **Written / held:** Written at the Eastern Han court, Luoyang
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Book%20of%20Han%20%28Hanshu%29%20Ban%20Gu)

Ban Biao began a continuation of the Shiji; his son Ban Gu reorganised it into a history of the Former Han alone, and his daughter Ban Zhao and the scholar Ma Xu completed it after Ban Gu's death in 92. It keeps Sima Qian's structure but drops the hereditary houses, with twelve annals, eight tables, ten treatises and seventy biographies. Its treatises are its glory: they cover law, food and money, geography, the five elements, rites and music, and in the 'Treatise on Literature' preserve the catalogue of the Han imperial library. The Book of Han set the template for the official dynastic history, and its sober style and orthodox Confucian tone were long preferred to the Shiji by court scholars.
