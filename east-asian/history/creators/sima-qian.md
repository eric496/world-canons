# Sima Qian

> Grand Historian of the Han court, whose Shiji (Records of the Grand Historian) created the model for two thousand years of Chinese historical writing.

<sub>Portrait: Imagined portrait of Sima Qian from a later Chinese illustrated book · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Si_maqian.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Sima Qian was the son of Sima Tan, Grand Scribe-Astrologer at the court of Emperor Wu, who began a history of the world as the Chinese knew it. As a young man Sima Qian travelled widely across the empire, and he succeeded his father in office around 108 BC, taking part in the calendar reform of 104 BC. In 99 BC he defended the general Li Ling, who had surrendered to the Xiongnu, and was sentenced to castration. He chose the punishment rather than suicide so that he could finish his father's book, as he explained in his famous letter to Ren An.

The Shiji, completed around 91 BC, runs from the legendary Yellow Emperor to Sima Qian's own day. He invented its composite form of annals, tables, treatises, hereditary houses and biographies, which every later dynastic history adopted. He drew on archives, older texts, inscriptions and interviews, and his portraits of assassins, merchants, rebels and failures as well as emperors give the work an extraordinary human range. Admired equally as literature and as history, he is the founding figure of the Chinese historiographical tradition.

### Records of the Grand Historian (Shiji) (c. 91 BC)

[![Records of the Grand Historian (Shiji)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ed/%E5%8F%B2%E8%AE%B0.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%E5%8F%B2%E8%AE%B0.jpg)

<sub>Page of a traditional printed edition of the Shiji Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%E5%8F%B2%E8%AE%B0.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 91 BC
- **Type:** Universal history (130 chapters)
- **Written / held:** Written at the Han court, Chang'an
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Records%20of%20the%20Grand%20Historian%20%28Shiji%29%20Sima%20Qian)

Begun by Sima Tan and completed by his son Sima Qian, the Shiji spans some two and a half thousand years in 130 chapters divided into five sections: twelve basic annals of dynasties and rulers, ten chronological tables, eight treatises on topics such as rites, music, the calendar, waterways and economics, thirty hereditary houses of feudal lords, and seventy biographies and collective accounts. The biographies range from Confucius and Laozi to assassins, wandering knights, merchants, jesters and foreign peoples such as the Xiongnu. Sima Qian's critical use of sources, his comments signed 'the Grand Historian says', and his sympathy for the defeated give the work extraordinary moral depth. It was circulating by the first century BC, became the first of the Twenty-Four Histories, and is equally a classic of Chinese prose.

### Letter to Ren An (Bao Ren An shu) (c. 91 BC)

- **Date:** c. 91 BC
- **Type:** Letter
- **Written / held:** Written in Chang'an; preserved in the Book of Han and the Wenxuan anthology
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Letter%20to%20Ren%20An%20%28Bao%20Ren%20An%20shu%29%20Sima%20Qian)

Replying to his friend Ren An, who was awaiting execution, Sima Qian recounts the Li Ling affair, his punishment and the humiliation he has lived with since. He explains that he chose to live because his history was unfinished, and lists earlier men, Confucius, Qu Yuan, Zuo Qiuming, Sunzi and others, who wrote their great works out of adversity. He describes his aim as to examine the boundary between heaven and humanity, to understand the changes of past and present, and to make a school of his own. The letter is one of the most moving documents of Chinese literature and the key to its greatest history's purpose.
