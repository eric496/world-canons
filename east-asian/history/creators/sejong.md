# Sejong the Great

*世宗*

**1397 – 1450** · Korean (Joseon)  
Born: Hanseong (Seoul) · Died: Hanseong (Seoul)  
Traditions & eras: [Joseon Veritable Records & court diaries](../movements/joseon-records.md)  

> Fourth king of Joseon, who created the Korean alphabet and promulgated it in the Hunminjeongeum of 1446.

## Biography

Sejong was the third son of King Taejong and succeeded him in 1418. He expanded the Hall of Worthies (Jiphyeonjeon), a royal research institute, and sponsored advances in astronomy, rain gauges, water clocks, agriculture, medicine, music and printing. His reign also saw campaigns against Jurchen groups in the north, extending Joseon's border to the Yalu and Tumen rivers.

Concerned that ordinary people could not read Chinese characters, he devised a phonetic script of twenty-eight letters, announced in 1443 and promulgated in 1446 as the Hunminjeongeum, 'the correct sounds for instructing the people'. Some officials opposed it, and it was long used mainly by women and commoners, but Hangul is now the national script of Korea and Sejong is the most revered of Korean kings.

## Key histories & documents

### Hunminjeongeum Haerye (Explanations and Examples of the Correct Sounds) (1446)

[![Hunminjeongeum Haerye (Explanations and Examples of the Correct Sounds)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Hunminjeongum.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hunminjeongum.jpg)

<sub>Pages of the Hunminjeongeum Haerye, 1446 (Gansong Art Museum) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hunminjeongum.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1446
- **Type:** Royal proclamation and linguistic treatise
- **Written / held:** Issued at the Joseon court, Hanseong (Seoul); the only known original copy is in the Gansong Art Museum, Seoul
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Hunminjeongeum%20Haerye%20%28Explanations%20and%20Examples%20of%20the%20Correct%20Sounds%29%20Sejong%20the%20Great)

King Sejong announced his new script in the last month of 1443, and in 1446 it was promulgated with this explanatory edition. It opens with Sejong's own preface, explaining that the people cannot write their language in Chinese characters and that he has made twenty-eight letters that are easy to learn and use daily, followed by short descriptions of the letters. The explanations by scholars of the Hall of Worthies set out how consonant shapes imitate the organs of speech and how vowels are built from symbols of heaven, earth and humanity, with examples, and Jeong In-ji's postface describes the purpose of the work. The book was lost for centuries until a copy was found in Andong in 1940 and acquired by the collector Jeon Hyeong-pil, who protected it through the colonial period and the Korean War. It is a Korean National Treasure and on UNESCO's Memory of the World register, and its date is celebrated as Hangul Day.
