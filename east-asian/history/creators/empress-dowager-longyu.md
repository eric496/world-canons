# Empress Dowager Longyu

*隆裕太后*

**1868 – 1913** · Chinese (Manchu)  
Born: Beijing · Died: Beijing  
Traditions & eras: [Revolution, nationalism & the new history](../movements/new-history.md)  

> Last empress of the Guangxu Emperor and regent for the child emperor Puyi, in whose name she issued the Qing abdication edict of 1912.

## Biography

Born into the Yehe Nara clan as a niece of the Empress Dowager Cixi, she was married to the Guangxu Emperor in 1889, an unhappy match arranged by Cixi. After the deaths of Guangxu and Cixi in 1908 she became empress dowager and guardian of the infant Xuantong Emperor, Puyi.

When revolution broke out in 1911, she was persuaded by Yuan Shikai and court officials that abdication on favourable terms was preferable to civil war. On 12 February 1912 she issued the edict in Puyi's name. She died a year later and was honoured by the Republican government for having enabled a relatively peaceful transfer of power.

## Key histories & documents

### Imperial Edict of the Abdication of the Qing Emperor (1912)

<sub>The abdication edict of 1912 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Qingtuiweizhaoshu.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1912
- **Type:** Imperial edict
- **Written / held:** Issued in Beijing, 12 February 1912
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Imperial%20Edict%20of%20the%20Abdication%20of%20the%20Qing%20Emperor%20Empress%20Dowager%20Longyu)

After the Wuchang uprising of October 1911 most provinces declared independence, and a provisional republican government under Sun Yat-sen was formed in Nanjing. Negotiations led by Yuan Shikai produced terms under which the Qing court would abdicate in return for favourable treatment of the imperial family, the Manchus, Mongols, Muslims and Tibetans. The edict, issued by the Empress Dowager Longyu on behalf of the six-year-old Puyi, declares that the will of the people has turned to a republic and charges Yuan Shikai with organising a provisional government; drafting is traditionally credited to the reformer Zhang Jian with revisions by Yuan's circle. Historians discuss the edict as a constitutional document that linked the Qing territories to the new republic.
