# Sin Chae-ho

*申采浩*

**1880 – 1936** · Korean  
Born: Daedeok (Daejeon), Chungcheong · Died: Lüshun prison, Manchuria  
Traditions & eras: [Revolution, nationalism & the new history](../movements/new-history.md)  

> Journalist, historian and independence activist who founded nationalist historiography in Korea.

## Biography

Sin Chae-ho studied at the Seonggyungwan Confucian academy and became an editor of the Hwangseong sinmun and Daehan maeil sinbo newspapers, writing biographies of Korean heroes such as Yi Sun-sin and Eulji Mundeok. He left Korea in 1910 after annexation and worked in China and Russia for the independence movement, briefly joining the Provisional Government in Shanghai before breaking with it. Drawn to anarchism, he wrote the Declaration of the Korean Revolution (1923) for the militant Righteous Brotherhood. He was arrested by Japanese police in 1928 and died in prison.

In A New Reading of History (1908) and the Early History of Korea, he defined history as the struggle between the self (a) and the non-self (bi-a), rejected the Sinocentric framework of earlier histories and placed Dangun, Buyeo and Goguryeo at the centre of the national past. His work founded a nationalist school that shaped Korean historiography in both Koreas.

### A New Reading of History (Doksa sillon) (1908)

- **Date:** 1908
- **Type:** Historical essay
- **Written / held:** Serialised in Daehan maeil sinbo, Seoul
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20New%20Reading%20of%20History%20%28Doksa%20sillon%29%20Sin%20Chae-ho)

Written as Korea fell under Japanese protectorate rule, A New Reading of History argued that a nation's history is the story of its people's rise and fall, and that Korean history should centre on the descendants of Dangun through Buyeo and Goguryeo rather than on Silla and Chinese-derived figures such as Gija. Sin criticised Kim Busik and the Confucian historiographers for subordinating Korea to China, and Japanese historians for distorting the Korean past. Unfinished in its serial form, it was nevertheless the manifesto of nationalist historiography. His later Early History of Korea (Joseon sanggosa), serialised in 1931, developed its ideas with the theory of history as struggle between self and non-self.

### Early History of Korea (Joseon sanggosa) (serialised 1931; published 1948)

- **Date:** serialised 1931; published 1948
- **Type:** National history
- **Written / held:** Serialised in the Chosun Ilbo, Seoul; first book edition 1948
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Early%20History%20of%20Korea%20%28Joseon%20sanggosa%29%20Sin%20Chae-ho)

Written in exile and serialised while Sin was in prison, the Joseon sanggosa covers Korean history from Dangun to the fall of Baekje and Goguryeo. Its introduction defines history as the record of the struggle between the self (a) and the non-self (bi-a), and argues that a people must know its own past to survive. Sin stressed the extent of ancient Korean states in Manchuria, criticised Confucian historians for subordinating Korea to China and challenged colonial Japanese scholarship. Later scholars have questioned some of his geographical claims while acknowledging his role in founding modern Korean historiography. It is studied in both North and South Korea.
