## Biography

Jeong In-ji passed the civil examination in 1414 and became a leading member of King Sejong's Hall of Worthies, working on astronomy, calendars and music as well as history. He co-wrote the Songs of the Dragons Flying to Heaven (Yongbi eocheonga), the first work in Hangul, and later served as chief state councillor under King Sejo.

He wrote the postface to the 1446 explanatory edition of the Hunminjeongeum, which explains the purpose of the script, and headed the commission that completed the Goryeosa in 1451, presenting the fallen dynasty from the viewpoint of its Joseon successors.

### Songs of the Dragons Flying to Heaven (Yongbi eocheonga) (1445; printed 1447)

- **Date:** 1445; printed 1447
- **Type:** Eulogistic cycle of songs (125 cantos)
- **Written / held:** Compiled at the Joseon court, Hanseong (Seoul)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Songs%20of%20the%20Dragons%20Flying%20to%20Heaven%20%28Yongbi%20eocheonga%29%20Jeong%20In-ji)

Commissioned by King Sejong and composed by Jeong In-ji, Gwon Je and An Ji, the cycle of 125 cantos praises six generations of the founding family, from Yi Seong-gye's ancestors to Taejo and Taejong, pairing their deeds with those of Chinese founders to show that Joseon held the Mandate of Heaven. Each canto is given in Korean, in the new alphabet, followed by a Chinese version and extensive historical annotations. Written before the alphabet's public promulgation, it served as a trial of the script and as dynastic legitimation, and its notes are a valuable source for the history of the Joseon founding and the Korean language.

### History of Goryeo (Goryeosa) (1451)

[![History of Goryeo (Goryeosa)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f9/Goryeosa%2C_National_Museum_of_Korea_03.jpg/1280px-Goryeosa%2C_National_Museum_of_Korea_03.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Goryeosa%2C_National_Museum_of_Korea_03.jpg)

<sub>Printed volumes of the Goryeosa (National Museum of Korea) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Goryeosa%2C_National_Museum_of_Korea_03.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1451
- **Type:** Official history (139 gwon)
- **Written / held:** Compiled at the Joseon court, Hanseong (Seoul)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=History%20of%20Goryeo%20%28Goryeosa%29%20Jeong%20In-ji)

The new Joseon dynasty began writing Goryeo's history soon after its founding in 1392, but successive drafts were rejected over questions of how to present the fall of Goryeo and the founding of Joseon and whether to keep Goryeo's imperial-style terminology. The final version, presented by Jeong In-ji and others in 1451, uses the Chinese composite form with forty-six gwon of 'hereditary houses' for the kings, reflecting the status of a tributary kingdom rather than imperial annals, together with treatises, tables and biographies, including chapters on 'rebels' that present the last Goryeo kings unfavourably. A companion chronological history, the Goryeosa jeolyo, followed in 1452. Despite its Joseon viewpoint it is the essential source for Goryeo history, especially its treatises on land, military and government.
