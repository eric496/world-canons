# An Jeong-bok

*安鼎福*

**1712 – 1791** · Korean (Joseon)  
Born: Jecheon, Chungcheong · Died: Gwangju, Gyeonggi  
Traditions & eras: [Joseon Veritable Records & court diaries](../movements/joseon-records.md)  

> Silhak scholar, disciple of Yi Ik, whose Annotated Account of Korean History (Dongsa gangmok) was the most rigorous Korean history of the Joseon period.

## Biography

An Jeong-bok came from a family of Southerner faction scholars and studied under the Silhak (practical learning) thinker Yi Ik. He held minor offices, including as tutor to the crown prince who became King Jeongjo, and spent most of his life writing.

His Dongsa gangmok, completed in 1778, applied the outline-and-details form of Zhu Xi's Gangmu to Korean history from Gija Joseon to the end of Goryeo, with careful source criticism and attention to historical geography. He also wrote critiques of Catholicism as it spread among Southerner scholars.

## Key histories & documents

### Annotated Account of Korean History (Dongsa gangmok) (1778)

- **Date:** 1778
- **Type:** Chronological history (20 gwon)
- **Written / held:** Written in Gwangju, Gyeonggi province
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Annotated%20Account%20of%20Korean%20History%20%28Dongsa%20gangmok%29%20An%20Jeong-bok)

An Jeong-bok adopted the outline-and-details form of Zhu Xi's Gangmu to write a moralised but carefully researched chronological history of Korea. He set out a scheme of legitimate succession running from Dangun and Gija through Mahan and Unified Silla to Goryeo, examined conflicting sources and recorded his doubts, and appended studies of historical geography and questions of dating. Encouraged by his teacher Yi Ik, he aimed to free Korean history from reliance on Chinese accounts. The work circulated in manuscript and was printed only in the early twentieth century; it is regarded as the most rigorous Korean history before modern scholarship.
