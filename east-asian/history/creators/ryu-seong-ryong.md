# Ryu Seong-ryong

*柳成龍*

**1542 – 1607** · Korean (Joseon)  
Born: Uiseong, North Gyeongsang · Died: Andong, North Gyeongsang  
Traditions & eras: [Joseon Veritable Records & court diaries](../movements/joseon-records.md)  

> Chief state councillor of Joseon during the Japanese invasions of 1592–98, whose Book of Corrections (Jingbirok) is the classic Korean account of the war.

## Biography

Ryu Seong-ryong was a disciple of the philosopher Yi Hwang and passed the civil examination in 1566. He rose to chief state councillor and directed Joseon's war effort after Toyotomi Hideyoshi's armies invaded in 1592: he recommended Yi Sun-sin as naval commander, organised supply and militia, and worked with the Ming relief armies. He was dismissed in factional strife in 1598 and retired to Hahoe village near Andong.

In retirement he wrote the Jingbirok, reflecting on the government's failures before and during the war so that later generations would be better prepared. It was printed in Joseon in the seventeenth century, and an edition published in Kyoto in 1695 made it known in Japan as well.

## Key histories & documents

### Book of Corrections (Jingbirok) (c. 1604)

[![Book of Corrections (Jingbirok)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/20/Chingbirok.jpg/960px-Chingbirok.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chingbirok.jpg)

<sub>Manuscript of the Jingbirok Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chingbirok.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1604
- **Type:** Memoir and history of the Imjin War
- **Written / held:** Written at Hahoe, Andong; autograph manuscript preserved by the Ryu family; printed 1633 and 1647; Kyoto edition 1695
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Book%20of%20Corrections%20%28Jingbirok%29%20Ryu%20Seong-ryong)

The title takes a line from the Book of Songs about being chastened by past errors in order to guard against future trouble. Ryu begins with the diplomatic exchanges with Toyotomi Hideyoshi before the war, including the divided reports of the envoys sent to Japan in 1590, then narrates the fall of Busan and Seoul, the king's flight to the north, the arrival of the Ming armies, Yi Sun-sin's naval victories and the long negotiations and second invasion. He frankly describes the failures of the government, including his own, and the suffering of the people. The autograph manuscript is a Korean National Treasure, and the work was printed in Japan in 1695, where it was read as a source on Hideyoshi's wars.
