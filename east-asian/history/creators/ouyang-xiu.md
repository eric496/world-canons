# Ouyang Xiu

*歐陽修*

**1007 – 1072** · Chinese  
Born: Mianzhou (Mianyang, Sichuan) · Died: Yingzhou (Fuyang, Anhui)  
Traditions & eras: [Song comprehensive & institutional history](../movements/song-comprehensive-history.md)  

> Song statesman, essayist and historian who led the compilation of the New Book of Tang and wrote the New History of the Five Dynasties.

<sub>Portrait: Traditional portrait of Ouyang Xiu · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ouyang-Xiu.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Ouyang Xiu lost his father as a child and was taught to write by his mother with reeds in the sand. He passed the jinshi in 1030 and became one of the leading statesmen of the Northern Song, a reformer under Fan Zhongyan, a champion of plain 'ancient-style' prose and a patron of younger writers such as Su Shi and Zeng Gong. He was also a pioneer of epigraphy, collecting rubbings of ancient inscriptions.

As a historian he combined moral judgement in the manner of the Spring and Autumn Annals with a prose style of great concision. He directed the New Book of Tang with Song Qi, presented in 1060, and privately wrote the New History of the Five Dynasties, whose verdicts on disloyal officials and chaotic rulers carry a strong moral message. Both entered the official canon of standard histories.

### Historical Records of the Five Dynasties (Xin Wudai shi) (c. 1053; printed 1077)

- **Date:** c. 1053; printed 1077
- **Type:** Dynastic history (74 chapters)
- **Written / held:** Written privately by Ouyang Xiu; adopted as an official history after his death
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Historical%20Records%20of%20the%20Five%20Dynasties%20%28Xin%20Wudai%20shi%29%20Ouyang%20Xiu)

Ouyang Xiu wrote this history on his own initiative over many years, modelling its terse judgements on the Spring and Autumn Annals. Each section begins with a lament introduced by the exclamation 'Alas!', and he grouped officials into categories such as those who died for their dynasty and those who served several houses, condemning the latter, notably the statesman Feng Dao who served ten rulers. After his death the court ordered it printed in 1077, and it was later ranked among the official standard histories alongside the older Five Dynasties history of Xue Juzheng. Its moral approach influenced Song and later ideas of loyalty, and it is admired as prose.

- **Date:** 1060
- **Type:** Dynastic history (225 chapters)
- **Written / held:** Compiled at the Song court, Kaifeng
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=New%20Book%20of%20Tang%20%28Xin%20Tangshu%29%20Ouyang%20Xiu)

Dissatisfied with the Old Book of Tang compiled in 945, the Song court commissioned a new history in 1044. Song Qi wrote most of the biographies and Ouyang Xiu the annals, treatises and tables; it was presented in 1060. The authors added treatises on the military, examinations and official selection, and expanded the tables of chancellors and genealogies, but compressed many documents and speeches into the terse 'ancient-style' prose they championed. Critics have long noted that the Old and New histories complement each other, the older one preserving more original documents and the newer offering more systematic treatises and fuller coverage of the late Tang. Both are counted among the Twenty-Four Histories.
