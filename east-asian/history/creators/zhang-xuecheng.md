# Zhang Xuecheng

*章學誠*

**1738 – 1801** · Chinese  
Born: Kuaiji (Shaoxing), Zhejiang · Died: Shaoxing, Zhejiang  
Traditions & eras: [Qing scholarship & evidential history](../movements/qing-scholarship.md)  

> Qing philosopher of history who argued that 'the Six Classics are all history' and theorised the writing of local and general histories.

## Biography

Zhang Xuecheng passed the jinshi in 1778 but never held significant office. He made a living as a private secretary, academy teacher and compiler of local gazetteers, working for patrons such as the official Bi Yuan. Out of step with the philological fashion of his day, he was little known in his lifetime.

His essays, collected as General Principles of Literature and History (Wenshi tongyi), argue that the classics were records of ancient institutions rather than timeless doctrines, that historical writing should be guided by a grasp of underlying meaning rather than by accumulation of facts, and that local gazetteers deserve the seriousness of national history. Rediscovered by Japanese and Chinese scholars in the early twentieth century, notably Naitō Konan and Hu Shi, he is now considered the most original Chinese historical thinker of the Qing.

- **Type:** Essays on historiography
- **Written / held:** Written in various posts in China; first printed by Zhang's son in 1832
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=General%20Principles%20of%20Literature%20and%20History%20%28Wenshi%20tongyi%29%20Zhang%20Xuecheng)

Zhang Xuecheng wrote these essays over three decades, against the dominant evidential scholarship of the Qianlong era. He argued that the Six Classics were the records and institutions of the ancient kings, so that the Way could be found only in historical change; that great history required 'historical virtue' and a grasp of meaning beyond accumulation of detail; and that local gazetteers should be designed as serious histories of their regions. He distinguished records kept for reference from interpretive histories written to convey understanding. Neglected until the late Qing, the essays were championed in the twentieth century and are now read alongside Vico and Herder as a major philosophy of history.
