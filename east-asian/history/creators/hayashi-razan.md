## Biography

Hayashi Razan studied Zhu Xi Neo-Confucianism under Fujiwara Seika and served four Tokugawa shoguns as scholar and secretary, drafting documents and advising on ritual and precedent. He founded the Hayashi family school in Edo, later the official shogunal academy. His thought is described in the philosophy section.

In 1644 the shogunate ordered Razan to compile a chronological history of Japan; after his death his son Hayashi Gahō (Shunsai) completed it in 1670 as the Honchō tsugan. The project brought Chinese-style comprehensive history to Japan under Tokugawa patronage.

## Key histories & documents

### Comprehensive Mirror of Our Realm (Honchō tsugan) (1670)

- **Date:** 1670
- **Type:** Chronological history (310 chapters)
- **Written / held:** Compiled at the Hayashi school, Edo, for the Tokugawa shogunate
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Comprehensive%20Mirror%20of%20Our%20Realm%20%28Honch%C5%8D%20tsugan%29%20Hayashi%20Razan)

Ordered by the shogunate in 1644, the history was begun by Hayashi Razan as the Honchō hennenroku and, after a fire and Razan's death in 1657, completed by his son Hayashi Gahō with a large staff in 1670. Written in classical Chinese, it covers Japanese history from the age of the gods to the accession of Emperor Go-Mizunoo, treating the early myths with Confucian rationalism. One passage, which recorded the theory that the imperial line descended from the Chinese Wu Taibo, is said to have angered Tokugawa Mitsukuni and spurred his rival Dai Nihonshi. The Honchō tsugan established Japanese history as a field of Confucian scholarship under the Tokugawa.
