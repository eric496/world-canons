# Signatories of the March First Declaration

*民族代表 33人*

**1919 – 1919** · Korean  
Born: Seoul · Died:   
Traditions & eras: [Revolution, nationalism & the new history](../movements/new-history.md)  

> The thirty-three 'national representatives', leaders of the Cheondogyo, Protestant and Buddhist communities, who signed the Korean Declaration of Independence of 1 March 1919.

## Biography

The signatories, led by the Cheondogyo leader Son Byong-hi, included fifteen members of Cheondogyo, sixteen Protestants and two Buddhists, among them the poet-monk Han Yong-un. The text was drafted by the writer Choe Nam-seon, and Han Yong-un is credited with the three closing pledges calling for orderly, non-violent action. On 1 March 1919 the signatories read it at the Taehwagwan restaurant in Seoul and then notified the Japanese police; at nearby Tapgol Park students read it to a crowd.

Most were imprisoned. The peaceful demonstrations that followed spread across Korea for months and were met with force by the colonial authorities. In later life some of the signatories, and the drafter Choe Nam-seon, collaborated with the colonial regime, which complicates their memory in Korea today.

## Key histories & documents

### Korean Declaration of Independence (Gimi dongnip seoneonseo) (1919)

- **Date:** 1919
- **Type:** Declaration of independence
- **Written / held:** Read at Taehwagwan and Tapgol Park, Seoul, 1 March 1919; printed at the Bosŏngsa press
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Korean%20Declaration%20of%20Independence%20%28Gimi%20dongnip%20seoneonseo%29%20Signatories%20of%20the%20March%20First%20Declaration)

Inspired in part by the principle of national self-determination proclaimed at the end of the First World War and by the death of the former emperor Gojong in January 1919, religious leaders secretly prepared a declaration drafted by Choe Nam-seon and printed in some twenty thousand copies. It proclaims Korea an independent state and Koreans a free people, appeals to justice and humanity rather than hatred, argues that independence would serve the peace of East Asia, including Japan's true interest, and closes with three pledges to act in an orderly, peaceful way. Its reading on 1 March set off demonstrations involving hundreds of thousands across Korea and among Koreans abroad, which were suppressed with many deaths and arrests. The movement led to the founding of the Provisional Government of the Republic of Korea in Shanghai in April 1919, and 1 March is a national holiday in South Korea.
