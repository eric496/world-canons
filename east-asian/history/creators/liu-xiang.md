# Liu Xiang

*劉向*

**79 BC – 8 BC** · Chinese  
Born: Pei (Jiangsu) · Died: Chang'an  
Traditions & eras: [Han historians & the early standard histories](../movements/han-histories.md)  

> Han imperial librarian who collated the palace collection and compiled the Strategies of the Warring States (Zhanguo ce).

## Biography

Liu Xiang was a member of the Han imperial clan who served at court under Emperors Xuan, Yuan and Cheng, surviving several political disgraces. From 26 BC he led the great collation of the imperial library, comparing variant manuscripts, fixing texts and writing a summary for each work; his son Liu Xin completed the project and its catalogue, which became the bibliographic treatise of the Book of Han.

Much of pre-Qin literature reaches us in the form Liu Xiang gave it. Besides the Zhanguo ce he compiled the Biographies of Exemplary Women (Lienü zhuan) and the anecdote collections Shuoyuan and Xinxu, all designed to offer the throne historical examples of good and bad conduct. He is a key figure in the transmission rather than the authorship of ancient texts.

## Key histories & documents

### Strategies of the Warring States (Zhanguo ce) (compiled c. 20 BC)

[![Strategies of the Warring States (Zhanguo ce)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/ZhanGuoCe.jpeg/1280px-ZhanGuoCe.jpeg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ZhanGuoCe.jpeg)

<sub>Page of a printed edition of the Zhanguo ce Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ZhanGuoCe.jpeg).</sub>

- **Date:** compiled c. 20 BC
- **Type:** Collection of political anecdotes
- **Written / held:** Compiled by Liu Xiang in the Han imperial library, Chang'an
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Strategies%20of%20the%20Warring%20States%20%28Zhanguo%20ce%29%20Liu%20Xiang)

In the imperial library Liu Xiang found manuscripts with titles such as 'Plans of the States' and 'Long Book', containing speeches and stories of the travelling persuaders who advised the rulers of the Warring States. He arranged them by state into thirty-three chapters, from the fifth century to the Qin unification. The stories, featuring strategists such as Su Qin and Zhang Yi, celebrate cunning, eloquence and realpolitik, which made the book suspect to Confucian moralists but a favourite of writers. Sima Qian used much of the same material. A silk manuscript of related persuasions found at Mawangdui in 1973 confirmed the age of its sources.
