## Biography

Gu Yanwu took part in resistance to the Manchu conquest and, after its failure, refused to serve the Qing. He spent much of his later life travelling in north China, studying inscriptions and topography and managing land. His life and ideas are discussed more fully in the philosophy section.

As a historical scholar he insisted on evidence from texts, inscriptions and on-the-ground observation, and he blamed the Ming collapse in part on empty philosophising. His compilations on geography, administration and economy were intended as practical guides for reform, and they made him a founder of Qing evidential learning (kaozheng).

## Key histories & documents

### Strengths and Weaknesses of the Provinces of the Realm (Tianxia junguo libing shu) (c. 1639–1662)

- **Date:** c. 1639–1662
- **Type:** Historical geography
- **Written / held:** Compiled by Gu Yanwu; circulated in manuscript and printed in the 19th century
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Strengths%20and%20Weaknesses%20of%20the%20Provinces%20of%20the%20Realm%20%28Tianxia%20junguo%20libing%20shu%29%20Gu%20Yanwu)

Gu Yanwu began this work as a young man in the last years of the Ming, reading through gazetteers, histories and official documents to extract information on the practical condition of every province. After the Manchu conquest he continued it during his travels in the north. Arranged by region, it gathers material on fortifications, garrisons, rivers and canals, land registration, salt, coastal defence and trade, with Gu's comments on the causes of strength and weakness. It is a monument of the turn from philosophy to practical, evidence-based learning that marked the early Qing and an essential source for late Ming social and economic history.
