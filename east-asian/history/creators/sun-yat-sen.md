# Sun Yat-sen

*孫中山*

**1866 – 1925** · Chinese  
Born: Cuiheng, Xiangshan (Zhongshan), Guangdong · Died: Beijing  
Traditions & eras: [Revolution, nationalism & the new history](../movements/new-history.md)  

> Revolutionary leader and first provisional president of the Republic of China, whose Three Principles of the People became the founding ideology of the Nationalist Party.

## Biography

Sun Yat-sen (Sun Wen, also known as Sun Zhongshan) was educated in Hawaii and Hong Kong and trained as a doctor. He founded the Revive China Society in 1894 and the Tongmenghui in 1905, raising support among overseas Chinese for repeated uprisings against the Qing. After the Wuchang uprising of 1911 he became provisional president in January 1912 but soon yielded to Yuan Shikai. He reorganised the Kuomintang in the 1920s with Soviet help and an alliance with the Communists, and died of cancer in 1925.

His Three Principles of the People, nationalism, democracy and the people's livelihood, combined Western political models with Chinese concerns. He is honoured as 'Father of the Nation' in Taiwan and as the 'forerunner of the revolution' in the People's Republic, one of the few figures revered on both sides of the Taiwan Strait.

## Key histories & documents

### Three Principles of the People (Sanmin zhuyi) (1924)

- **Date:** 1924
- **Type:** Political lectures
- **Written / held:** Delivered as lectures in Guangzhou, January–August 1924
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Three%20Principles%20of%20the%20People%20%28Sanmin%20zhuyi%29%20Sun%20Yat-sen)

Sun had outlined the three principles since 1905; in 1924, with his manuscripts lost in an earlier rebellion, he gave sixteen lectures setting them out in full, leaving the last principle incomplete at his death. Nationalism called for unity of the Chinese and an end to foreign domination; democracy combined popular rights with a government of five branches, adding examination and control to the executive, legislative and judicial; livelihood proposed equalisation of land rights and regulation of capital. The principles are cited in the constitution of the Republic of China, open its national anthem, and were also accepted, with different emphasis, by the Chinese Communist Party during the alliances of the 1920s and 1930s.
