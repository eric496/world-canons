## Biography

Rai San'yō was the son of the Confucian scholar Rai Shunsui of the Hiroshima domain. As a young man he deserted the domain and was confined at home for years, during which he began his history. Disinherited, he settled in Kyoto as an independent teacher, poet and calligrapher, celebrated for his Chinese verse and his circle of literati friends.

The Nihon gaishi, finished around 1827 and presented to the former chief councillor Matsudaira Sadanobu, tells the history of the warrior houses from the Taira and Minamoto to the Tokugawa in vivid classical Chinese. Its praise of loyalty to the emperor made it a favourite of the loyalist samurai of the 1850s and 1860s, and it remained a school text well into the Meiji era.

- **Date:** c. 1827; printed 1836–37
- **Type:** Narrative history (22 volumes)
- **Written / held:** Written in Kyoto; first printed in Osaka and Kyoto
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Unofficial%20History%20of%20Japan%20%28Nihon%20gaishi%29%20Rai%20San%27y%C5%8D)

Organised around the great families, from the Taira and Minamoto through the Hōjō, Kusunoki, Ashikaga, Oda and Toyotomi to the Tokugawa, the Nihon gaishi is written in vigorous classical Chinese with frequent comments by the author. It praises warriors who served the emperor faithfully, such as Kusunoki Masashige, and quietly frames the shogunate as delegated imperial authority. Circulated in manuscript and then printed, it became essential reading for the young samurai of the loyalist movement of the 1850s and 1860s and a school text in the Meiji era. Modern historians regard it as literature rather than critical history.
