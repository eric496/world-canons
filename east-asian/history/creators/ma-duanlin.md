# Ma Duanlin

*馬端臨*

**1254 – 1323** · Chinese  
Born: Leping, Jiangxi · Died: Leping, Jiangxi  
Traditions & eras: [Song comprehensive & institutional history](../movements/song-comprehensive-history.md)  

> Scholar of the Song–Yuan transition whose Comprehensive Examination of Literature (Wenxian tongkao) is the fullest encyclopaedic history of Chinese institutions.

## Biography

Ma Duanlin was the son of Ma Tingluan, a Southern Song chancellor. After the Mongol conquest of the Song in 1279 he declined high office, served only as a local teacher and academy head, and devoted some twenty years to his great compilation, completed around 1307 and printed in 1322.

The Wenxian tongkao extends Du You's Tongdian of the Tang, organising the history of institutions from antiquity to the Song under twenty-four headings such as land tax, currency, population, examinations, rites, military affairs, bibliography and foreign peoples. Ma distinguished 'documents' (wen) from 'discussions' (xian) and added his own critical notes. Its emphasis on long-term institutional change has made it a favourite of modern economic and social historians.

## Key histories & documents

### Comprehensive Examination of Literature (Wenxian tongkao) (c. 1307; printed 1322)

- **Date:** c. 1307; printed 1322
- **Type:** Institutional encyclopaedia (348 chapters)
- **Written / held:** Compiled in Leping, Jiangxi; printed under the Yuan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Comprehensive%20Examination%20of%20Literature%20%28Wenxian%20tongkao%29%20Ma%20Duanlin)

Ma Duanlin built on Du You's Tongdian of 801 and Zheng Qiao's Tongzhi of the twelfth century, the other two of the 'Three Comprehensives' (santong). His categories run from land tax, currency, population, labour service, markets and tribute through schools, examinations, offices, rites, music, military affairs and punishments to bibliography, imperial genealogy, geography and foreign peoples. Under each he arranged the documentary record (wen) and the discussions of officials and scholars (xian), then gave his own analysis. His preface argues that while political events are unique, institutions develop continuously and must be studied over the long term. The work was continued under the Ming and Qing and remains a standard reference for Chinese economic and administrative history.
