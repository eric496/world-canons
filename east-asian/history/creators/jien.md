# Jien

## Biography

Jien was a son of the regent Fujiwara no Tadamichi and younger brother of the regent Kujō Kanezane. He entered the Buddhist priesthood as a child and was four times appointed head (zasu) of the Tendai school at Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei. He was also one of the finest waka poets of his day, well represented in the Shin Kokinshū and the Hyakunin Isshu.

Writing around 1220, as tension grew between the retired emperor Go-Toba and the Kamakura shogunate, Jien argued that history unfolds according to a principle (dōri) that changes with the age, and that in a degenerate era cooperation between the Fujiwara regents, the court and the warriors was right. The Gukanshō is written in Japanese with kana, unusual for serious history at the time, so that more readers could understand it.

## Key histories & documents

### Jottings of a Fool (Gukanshō) (c. 1220)

- **Date:** c. 1220
- **Type:** Interpretive history (7 chapters)
- **Written / held:** Written in Kyoto or on Mount Hiei on the eve of the Jōkyū War
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jottings%20of%20a%20Fool%20%28Gukansh%C5%8D%29%20Jien)

The Gukanshō opens with a chronological table of Chinese and Japanese rulers, then narrates Japanese history from the first emperor, Jinmu, to Jien's own day, with growing detail on the twelfth-century wars between the Taira and Minamoto. Jien explains the past as the working out of principles that change with each age, within the Buddhist scheme of decline, and argues that in his own time the court, the Fujiwara regents and the warriors of Kamakura must cooperate. Written in Japanese with katakana rather than classical Chinese, so that it would be understood, it is also an implicit warning against the plans of the retired emperor Go-Toba, whose rising against Kamakura in 1221 failed. It is valued as an insider's view of court politics and as a pioneering work of historical reflection.
