# Joseon historiographers

*朝鮮 史官*

**1392 – 1910** · Korean (Joseon)  
Born: Hanseong (Seoul) · Died:   
Traditions & eras: [Joseon Veritable Records & court diaries](../movements/joseon-records.md)  

> The court historians (sagwan) of the Joseon dynasty, who recorded the king's words and deeds and compiled the Annals of the Joseon Dynasty.

## Biography

Joseon maintained a corps of historians in the Office for Annals Compilation and the Office of Royal Decrees. Young officials selected for integrity attended every royal audience and wrote down what was said and done in draft records (sacho), which they kept at home and which even the king was not permitted to read. After each king's death a compilation office used the drafts, government records and the Royal Secretariat diaries to produce the annals of the reign.

Their independence was not absolute: drafts were sometimes revised in factional disputes, and the purge of 1498 began when a historian's draft was read as criticism of King Sejo. Yet the tradition produced one of the longest and most detailed continuous official records of any dynasty.

## Key histories & documents

### Annals of the Joseon Dynasty (Joseon wangjo sillok) (1413–1865)

<sub>Volumes of the Annals of the Joseon Dynasty with their storage case Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Joseon_Wangjo_Sillok_and_its_case_in_museum.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1413–1865
- **Type:** Veritable records (1,893 gwon)
- **Written / held:** Compiled in Hanseong (Seoul); copies kept in mountain archives (Jeongjoksan, Taebaeksan, Odaesan, Jeoksangsan); now in Seoul National University and other repositories
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Annals%20of%20the%20Joseon%20Dynasty%20%28Joseon%20wangjo%20sillok%29%20Joseon%20historiographers)

After each king's death a compilation office gathered the draft records of the court historians, the Royal Secretariat diaries and government documents and produced the annals of the reign, which were printed in a few copies and sealed in archives; the drafts were then washed out so that the paper could be reused and the historians protected. The annals cover politics, diplomacy, economy, natural phenomena, rituals and the arts in great detail, from King Taejo to King Cheoljong. Only one set survived the Japanese invasions of 1592, and copies were remade and dispersed to remote mountain archives; one set taken to Tokyo in the colonial period was largely destroyed in the 1923 earthquake, and surviving volumes were returned to Korea in 2006. The records of the last two reigns, compiled under Japanese rule, are not usually counted. The annals were registered in UNESCO's Memory of the World in 1997.
