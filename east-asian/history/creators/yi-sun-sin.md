# Yi Sun-sin

*李舜臣*

**1545 – 1598** · Korean (Joseon)  
Born: Hanseong (Seoul) · Died: Noryang Strait (killed in battle)  
Traditions & eras: [Joseon Veritable Records & court diaries](../movements/joseon-records.md)  

> Joseon admiral who defeated the Japanese navy repeatedly during the Imjin War and kept the War Diary (Nanjung ilgi).

## Biography

Yi Sun-sin passed the military examination in 1576 and served on the northern frontier before being appointed naval commander of Left Jeolla province in 1591, where he prepared his fleet, including the armoured 'turtle ships'. After the Japanese invasion of 1592 he won a series of victories, including Hansan Island, that cut Japanese sea supply lines. Falsely accused and stripped of command in 1597, he was reinstated after the Joseon fleet was destroyed and won the battle of Myeongnyang with thirteen ships. He was killed at Noryang in 1598, in the war's last battle.

His war diaries record battles, weather, the illness of his mother, grief at his son's death and frustration with court politics in plain, often moving entries. He is Korea's foremost national hero, and the diaries are one of the most personal documents of the war.

- **Date:** 1592–1598
- **Type:** Personal war diary
- **Written / held:** Kept aboard ship and in camp; autograph manuscripts at Hyeonchungsa shrine, Asan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=War%20Diary%20%28Nanjung%20ilgi%29%20Yi%20Sun-sin)

Yi Sun-sin kept the diary from the start of the Japanese invasion in 1592 until shortly before his death at Noryang in November 1598. The surviving autograph volumes record naval preparations, the battles of Okpo, Hansan, Myeongnyang and others, weather and sea conditions, discipline, supplies, relations with Ming allies and quarrels with rival commanders and the court. They also record his worry for his mother, his grief at her death and at the killing of his son Yi Myeon, and his illness and sleeplessness. The title, meaning 'diary in the midst of war', was given when it was edited in 1795. It is a Korean National Treasure and was inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World in 2013.
