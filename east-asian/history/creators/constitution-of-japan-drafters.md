# Drafters of the Constitution of Japan

*日本国憲法起草者*

**1946 – 1946** · American and Japanese  
Born: Tokyo · Died:   
Traditions & eras: [Postwar constitutions](../movements/postwar-documents.md)  

> The officials of the Allied occupation's Government Section, Japanese cabinet officials and members of the Imperial Diet who together produced the Constitution of Japan of 1946.

## Biography

In February 1946, after a Japanese government draft was judged too close to the Meiji Constitution, General MacArthur's Government Section, led by Courtney Whitney and Charles Kades, prepared a model draft in about a week; Beate Sirota was among those who wrote its rights articles. The cabinet of Shidehara Kijūrō, with the minister Matsumoto Jōji and the official Satō Tatsuo, reworked it into Japanese, and the Diet elected in April 1946, the first with women voters, debated and amended it.

The degree to which the constitution was imposed or freely adopted has been argued over ever since. Its supporters stress its long popular acceptance and the Japanese contributions made in the Diet, including the expansion of social rights; critics point to its drafting under occupation. It has never been amended.

## Key histories & documents

### Constitution of Japan (Nihonkoku kenpō) (1946)

- **Date:** 1946
- **Type:** Constitution
- **Written / held:** Promulgated in Tokyo, 3 November 1946; in force 3 May 1947; original in the National Archives of Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Constitution%20of%20Japan%20%28Nihonkoku%20kenp%C5%8D%29%20Drafters%20of%20the%20Constitution%20of%20Japan)

Adopted as a formal amendment to the Meiji Constitution, the Constitution of Japan makes the emperor a symbol of the state and of the unity of the people, deriving his position from the will of the people, with whom sovereignty resides. It guarantees civil liberties, equality of the sexes, academic freedom and social rights, establishes parliamentary cabinet government and an independent judiciary, and in Article 9 renounces war as a sovereign right and the maintenance of armed forces for that purpose. The interpretation of Article 9, under which Japan has maintained Self-Defence Forces since 1954, has been debated ever since, as has whether the constitution should be revised. It has never been amended, making it one of the oldest unamended constitutions in the world.
