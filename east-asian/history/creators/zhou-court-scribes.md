# Zhou court scribes

*周代史官*

**c. 11th century BC – c. 3rd century BC** · Chinese  
Born: Royal and state courts of Zhou China · Died:   
Traditions & eras: [Zhou classics & annals](../movements/zhou-classics.md)  

> The anonymous scribes, archivists and later editors of the Zhou royal and state courts, whose speeches, decrees and records were gathered into the Book of Documents.

## Biography

Early Chinese courts employed scribes (shi, the word that later came to mean 'history' and 'historian') who kept records of rituals, decrees, speeches and events. Bronze inscriptions of the Western Zhou show them reading out royal appointments, and later texts describe a 'scribe of the left' who recorded words and a 'scribe of the right' who recorded deeds. Their archives, repeatedly copied, edited and supplemented during the Spring and Autumn and Warring States periods, lie behind the oldest Chinese historical texts.

The Book of Documents (Shangshu) is the most important product of this tradition. Its chapters present the speeches and proclamations of sage kings and Zhou founders, and they were read for two thousand years as the model of good government. Modern scholarship sees the collection as layered: a few chapters may preserve genuine Western Zhou material, many were composed later in the voice of ancient rulers, and some were forged centuries afterward.

## Key histories & documents

### Book of Documents (Shujing / Shangshu) (c. 1000–300 BC)

[![Book of Documents (Shujing / Shangshu)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ed/Guwen_Shangshu_TNM.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Guwen_Shangshu_TNM.jpg)

<sub>Early manuscript of the Old Text Shangshu (Tokyo National Museum) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Guwen_Shangshu_TNM.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1000–300 BC
- **Type:** Collection of speeches and decrees
- **Written / held:** Compiled in Zhou China; earliest excavated chapters on Warring States bamboo slips (Tsinghua University)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Book%20of%20Documents%20%28Shujing%20/%20Shangshu%29%20Zhou%20court%20scribes)

The Book of Documents is one of the Five Classics of Confucianism. Its chapters, arranged by dynasty, present the words of rulers and ministers at decisive moments, such as the Duke of Zhou's addresses justifying the overthrow of the Shang by the 'Mandate of Heaven'. Its textual history is unusually tangled: much was lost in the Qin burning of books, a 'New Text' version of twenty-eight or twenty-nine chapters survived through the Han scholar Fu Sheng, and an expanded 'Old Text' version appeared in the fourth century AD. The Qing scholar Yan Ruoqu demonstrated that the additional Old Text chapters were later forgeries, and the bamboo manuscripts acquired by Tsinghua University in 2008 have added previously unknown 'documents' style texts. Despite these layers, the book shaped Chinese political thought for two millennia.
