# Authors of the Nine Chapters

*九章算術作者*

**c. 200 BC – c. AD 100** · Chinese  
Born: China · Died: China  
Eras & fields: [Han mathematics](../movements/han-mathematics.md)  

> The anonymous Han scholars and officials who compiled the Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art, the foundation text of East Asian mathematics.

## Biography

The Nine Chapters grew out of administrative mathematics of the Qin and early Han; bamboo-strip books such as the Suanshushu of about 186 BC contain similar problems. Its final form is usually dated to the first century BC or AD. Later tradition linked it with the Han officials Zhang Cang and Geng Shouchang, but this cannot be confirmed.

The 246 problems, each with an answer and a general method, cover land measure, proportion, square and cube roots, volumes, taxation, excess and deficit, and the right triangle. Chapter eight solves systems of linear equations by an elimination method identical to Gaussian elimination and handles negative numbers. With Liu Hui's commentary it was the core of mathematical teaching in China, Korea and Japan for over a thousand years.

- **Date:** c. 1st century BC–AD
- **Type:** Mathematical problem book
- **Published / where:** China; Liu Hui's commentary 263; printed 1084
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Nine%20Chapters%20on%20the%20Mathematical%20Art%20Authors%20of%20the%20Nine%20Chapters)

Jiuzhang Suanshu is a collection of 246 practical problems, each with an answer and a general procedure, arranged in nine chapters: field measurement, millet and rice exchange, distribution by proportion, short width, construction works, fair levies, excess and deficit, rectangular arrays and right triangles. Its methods include adding fractions, finding square and cube roots, computing volumes of solids, the rule of false position and, in the chapter fangcheng, solving systems of linear equations by elimination on a rectangular array of counting rods, with rules for positive and negative numbers. The text gives no proofs; Liu Hui's commentary of 263 supplied justifications. It shaped mathematical teaching in China for over a millennium and was studied in Korea and Japan; the Tang made it the chief text of the mathematics examination.
