# Zeng Gongliang

*曾公亮*

**999 – 1078** · Chinese  
Born: Jinjiang, Quanzhou (Fujian) · Died: Kaifeng  
Eras & fields: [Gunpowder & the compass](../movements/gunpowder-compass.md), [Song science & engineering](../movements/song-science.md)  

> Song chancellor who led the compilation of the Wujing Zongyao, the military encyclopedia that contains the earliest printed gunpowder formulas.

## Biography

Zeng Gongliang passed the examinations in 1024 and served in high office under Emperors Renzong, Yingzong and Shenzong, becoming chancellor. Together with Ding Du he compiled by imperial order the Complete Essentials for the Military Classics, finished in 1044.

The encyclopedia covers strategy, fortification, ships and weapons, including three recipes for gunpowder combining saltpetre, sulphur and charcoal with other ingredients for incendiary bombs and smoke. It also describes a 'south-pointing fish' of magnetised iron for finding direction.

## Key works & inventions

### Wujing Zongyao (1044)

[![Wujing Zongyao](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c2/Chinese_Gunpowder_Formula.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chinese_Gunpowder_Formula.JPG)

<sub>Gunpowder formula in a Ming edition of the Wujing Zongyao Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chinese_Gunpowder_Formula.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1044
- **Type:** Military encyclopedia
- **Published / where:** Kaifeng, Song China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Wujing%20Zongyao%20Zeng%20Gongliang)

Commissioned by Emperor Renzong after a defeat by the Western Xia, the Wujing Zongyao was compiled by Zeng Gongliang, Ding Du and others and completed in 1044. Its forty chapters cover strategy, organisation, fortification, naval warfare, signalling and weapons, with many illustrations of crossbows, siege engines, ships and flamethrowers. Three recipes describe gunpowder for an incendiary ball, a poison-smoke ball and a thorn-studded bomb, with saltpetre making up roughly half the mixture. The book also explains how to make a thin iron leaf in the shape of a fish magnetic by heating and quenching it in the earth's field, and float it on water to find south. The surviving editions are Ming reprints, and some illustrations may have been redrawn.
