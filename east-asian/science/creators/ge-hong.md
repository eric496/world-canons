## Biography

Ge Hong came from a southern gentry family with a tradition of Daoist learning. After service as a minor military officer during the civil wars of the Jin, he withdrew to pursue alchemy and, by tradition, died on Mount Luofu near Guangzhou while preparing elixirs.

His Baopuzi ('He Who Embraces Simplicity') describes the making of elixirs from cinnabar, mercury, gold and other substances, mixing practical chemistry with the quest for immortality. His Handbook of Prescriptions for Emergencies recommends soaking sweet wormwood, qinghao, in water and drinking the juice for intermittent fevers; this passage led Tu Youyou to artemisinin in 1972.

## Key works & inventions

### Baopuzi (c. 317)

- **Date:** c. 317
- **Type:** Alchemical and philosophical treatise
- **Published / where:** Jiankang, Eastern Jin
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Baopuzi%20Ge%20Hong)

The inner chapters of the Baopuzi, finished around 317, argue that immortality can be attained and describe how: by breathing exercises, diet, sexual practice, talismans and above all by elixirs made from cinnabar, gold, mercury and other minerals refined in sealed crucibles over long periods. Ge Hong quotes recipes from older alchemical scriptures and describes experiments such as the transformation of cinnabar into mercury and back. The outer chapters discuss politics and morality in Confucian terms. With his medical handbook, it is the main source for early Chinese alchemy and its practical chemistry.
