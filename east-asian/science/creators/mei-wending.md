# Mei Wending

*梅文鼎*

**1633 – 1721** · Chinese  
Born: Xuancheng, Anhui · Died: Xuancheng, Anhui  
Eras & fields: [Jesuit science in China](../movements/jesuit-science.md)  

> Early Qing astronomer and mathematician who mastered both Chinese and Western methods and argued that Western learning had Chinese roots.

## Biography

Mei Wending was a private scholar from Anhui who never held office but became famous for his more than eighty works on mathematics and astronomy. In 1705 the Kangxi Emperor received him on the imperial boat, and his grandson Mei Juecheng worked on the court's mathematical encyclopedia.

He explained Western trigonometry, geometry and planetary theory in Chinese terms and revived interest in older Chinese mathematics. His view that Western science had come from ancient China, widespread in his time, helped make it acceptable to Confucian scholars. He is considered the leading Chinese mathematician of the seventeenth century.

## Key works & inventions

### Lisuan Quanshu (1723)

- **Date:** 1723
- **Type:** Collected works on astronomy and mathematics
- **Published / where:** Qing China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Lisuan%20Quanshu%20Mei%20Wending)

Mei Wending wrote some eighty works, many circulated in manuscript; after his death they were edited and printed in 1723 as Lisuan Quanshu, and later rearranged by his grandson Mei Juecheng as Meishi Congshu Jiyao. They explain Western plane and spherical trigonometry, geometry and planetary models clearly in Chinese terms, compare them with traditional methods, and recover forgotten Chinese techniques. Mei held that Western astronomy descended from ancient Chinese knowledge carried abroad, an idea that made it easier for scholars to accept. His books shaped the evidential scholars of the Qing and the mathematics studied in late Joseon Korea.
