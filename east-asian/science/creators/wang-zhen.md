# Wang Zhen

*王禎*

**fl. 1290–1333 – fl. 1333** · Chinese  
Born: Dongping, Shandong · Died: China  
Eras & fields: [Printing in Song, Goryeo & Japan](../movements/song-printing.md), [Song science & engineering](../movements/song-science.md)  

> Yuan official and agronomist whose Nong Shu (1313) is an illustrated encyclopedia of farming tools and who improved wooden movable type.

## Biography

Wang Zhen served as a county magistrate in Anhui and Jiangxi in the 1290s, and encouraged farming improvements among the people in his charge.

His Book of Agriculture describes crops and illustrates some three hundred agricultural tools and machines, including water-powered mills, bellows and spinning machines. In an appendix he explains how he had about 60,000 wooden type cut and set them from revolving tables arranged by rhyme, printing a local gazetteer in 1298.

## Key works & inventions

### Nong Shu and wooden movable type (1298–1313)

[![Nong Shu and wooden movable type](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/16/Chinese_movable_type_1313-ce.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chinese_movable_type_1313-ce.png)

<sub>Wang Zhen's revolving type tables, illustration from the Nong Shu, 1313 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chinese_movable_type_1313-ce.png).</sub>

- **Date:** 1298–1313
- **Type:** Agricultural treatise
- **Published / where:** Yuan China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Nong%20Shu%20and%20wooden%20movable%20type%20Wang%20Zhen)

Wang Zhen's Nong Shu, completed in 1313, combines a general treatise on farming in north and south China, an account of individual crops, and a long illustrated section on implements: ploughs, harrows, seed drills, irrigation wheels, water-powered trip-hammers, mills and bellows, and machines for silk and hemp. It is one of the main sources for Chinese engineering before modern times. Appended to it is his essay on printing with wooden movable type, describing how he had more than 60,000 characters carved, sorted them on two large revolving tables by rhyme, and printed a gazetteer of Jingde county in 1298 in less than a month. It is the earliest detailed description of a working movable-type printing shop.
