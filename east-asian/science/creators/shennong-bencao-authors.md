# Authors of the Shennong Bencao Jing

*神農本草經作者*

**c. 100 BC – c. AD 200** · Chinese  
Born: China · Died: China  
Eras & fields: [Classical Chinese medicine](../movements/classical-chinese-medicine.md), [Chinese alchemy & pharmacology](../movements/chinese-alchemy.md)  

> The unknown compilers of the Divine Farmer's Materia Medica, the oldest Chinese pharmacopoeia, attributed to the mythical inventor of agriculture Shennong.

## Biography

The Shennong Bencao Jing was compiled in the Eastern Han from the knowledge of herbalists and physicians and credited to Shennong, the Divine Farmer, who was said to have tasted every plant to learn its properties. The original is lost; the text survives through quotations in later works, above all Tao Hongjing's edition of about 500.

It lists 365 drugs, mostly plants but also minerals and animal products, arranged into three classes: superior drugs that nourish life, middling ones that strengthen, and inferior ones that cure disease but are toxic. This framework of drugs ranked by effect and toxicity began the long bencao tradition that runs to Li Shizhen.

## Key works & inventions

### Shennong Bencao Jing (c. 1st–2nd century)

- **Date:** c. 1st–2nd century
- **Type:** Materia medica
- **Published / where:** China; reconstructed from quotations
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Shennong%20Bencao%20Jing%20Authors%20of%20the%20Shennong%20Bencao%20Jing)

The Shennong Bencao Jing is the oldest work in the bencao tradition of Chinese pharmacology. It lists 365 drugs, one for each day of the year, of which some 250 are plants, the rest animals and minerals, and divides them into superior, middle and inferior classes according to whether they nourish life, strengthen the body or attack disease. For each it gives the taste, the 'temperature' and the conditions it treats, and it states rules for combining drugs. The original text is lost; around 500 Tao Hongjing edited it with additions, and later pharmacopoeias absorbed it, so modern versions are reconstructions. Its scheme of grading drugs by toxicity and effect shaped all later Chinese materia medica.
