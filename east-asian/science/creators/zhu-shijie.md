# Zhu Shijie

*朱世傑*

**c. 1249 – c. 1314** · Chinese  
Born: Yanshan (Beijing area) · Died: China  
Eras & fields: [Song–Yuan mathematics](../movements/song-yuan-mathematics.md)  

> Yuan mathematician whose Jade Mirror of the Four Unknowns (1303) solved systems of polynomial equations in up to four unknowns, the summit of Chinese traditional algebra.

## Biography

Zhu Shijie spent some twenty years as a travelling teacher of mathematics across Yuan China. He published an introductory textbook in 1299, which was lost in China but preserved in Korea and Japan, where it influenced Seki Takakazu.

His Siyuan Yujian of 1303 extended the tianyuan method to four unknowns, eliminating variables to reach a single equation, and summed finite series. Its opening diagram shows the arithmetical triangle to the eighth power. After the Yuan, Chinese algebra declined, and the book was rediscovered only in the nineteenth century.

## Key works & inventions

### Jade Mirror of the Four Unknowns (1303)

- **Date:** 1303
- **Type:** Mathematical treatise
- **Published / where:** Yuan China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jade%20Mirror%20of%20the%20Four%20Unknowns%20Zhu%20Shijie)

The Siyuan Yujian presents 288 problems solved by the 'method of four elements', in which up to four unknowns, named heaven, earth, man and matter, are arranged around a central constant term on the counting board, so that each square holds the coefficient of a product of powers. Zhu Shijie eliminates unknowns step by step to reach a single equation in one unknown, which he solves numerically, and he also sums finite series, such as piles of stacked balls, using what are now called finite differences. The book opens with the arithmetical triangle, labelled the 'ancient method'. Little understood under the Ming, it was recovered in the nineteenth century and studied by both Chinese and Western historians of mathematics.
