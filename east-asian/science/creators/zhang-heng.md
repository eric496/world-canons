# Zhang Heng

*張衡*

**78 – 139** · Chinese  
Born: Nanyang, Han China · Died: Luoyang  
Eras & fields: [Paper & Han technology](../movements/han-technology.md), [Early astronomy & calendars](../movements/early-astronomy.md)  

> Han astronomer, mathematician, engineer and poet who built a water-driven armillary sphere and the first known seismoscope.

## Biography

Zhang Heng was born to a gentry family in Nanyang and made his name first as a writer of rhapsodies on the Han capitals. He twice served as Grand Astrologer, head of the office of astronomy and the calendar, and later held provincial posts before returning to the court.

He described the universe as an egg with the earth as its yolk, catalogued some 2,500 stars, gave a value for π, and built a celestial globe turned by water power. His seismoscope of 132 was said to drop a ball from a dragon's mouth into a toad's to show the direction of a distant earthquake. His works are lost and known only from Han histories, but he remains the emblem of Chinese scientific genius, and a lunar crater and an asteroid bear his name.

### Water-powered armillary sphere (c. 125)

- **Date:** c. 125
- **Type:** Astronomical instrument
- **Published / where:** Luoyang, Han China (lost)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Water-powered%20armillary%20sphere%20Zhang%20Heng)

Zhang Heng, as Grand Astrologer, built an armillary or celestial globe whose rings represented the equator, the ecliptic and the horizon, and connected it to an inflow water clock so that it rotated in time with the heavens; observers inside could call out to those on the platform outside when a star rose or set. His writings on it survive only in fragments. The device began a long Chinese tradition of water-driven astronomical machines that led through Yi Xing to Su Song's clock tower.

### Seismoscope (132)

[![Seismoscope](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ae/EastHanSeismograph.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:EastHanSeismograph.JPG)

<sub>Modern reconstruction of Zhang Heng's seismoscope Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:EastHanSeismograph.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 132
- **Type:** Scientific instrument
- **Published / where:** Luoyang, Han China (lost)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Seismoscope%20Zhang%20Heng)

According to the History of the Later Han, in 132 Zhang Heng presented to the court a bronze vessel like a wine jar, about two metres across, with eight dragons around its body facing the directions of the compass, each holding a ball above an open-mouthed toad. When an earthquake occurred, a mechanism inside, probably a pendulum, released one ball, indicating the direction of the shock. The history tells that once a ball fell when no tremor was felt in Luoyang, and days later a messenger reported an earthquake in Longxi to the west. The instrument is lost and its interior is not described, so modern reconstructions differ; it is nonetheless recognised as the first known seismoscope.
