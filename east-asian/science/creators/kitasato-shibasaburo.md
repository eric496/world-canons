# Kitasato Shibasaburō

*北里柴三郎*

**1853 – 1931** · Japanese  
Born: Oguni, Higo (Kumamoto) · Died: Tokyo  
Eras & fields: [Meiji and modern Japanese science](../movements/meiji-science.md)  

> Bacteriologist who grew the tetanus bacillus in pure culture and with Emil von Behring developed antitoxin serum therapy in 1890.

## Biography

Kitasato studied medicine at Kumamoto and Tokyo and in 1885 went to Berlin to work under Robert Koch. There he obtained a pure culture of the tetanus bacillus in 1889, and with Emil von Behring showed in 1890 that serum from immunised animals neutralised the toxins of tetanus and diphtheria.

Returning to Japan in 1892, he founded the Institute for Infectious Diseases, later the Kitasato Institute, and in 1894 in Hong Kong identified the plague bacterium at the same time as Alexandre Yersin. He trained many researchers, including Shiga and Noguchi, and founded Keiō University's medical school. He appears on the 1,000-yen note issued from 2024.

### Tetanus antitoxin and serum therapy (1889–1890)

- **Date:** 1889–1890
- **Type:** Bacteriological research
- **Published / where:** Robert Koch's Institute of Hygiene, Berlin
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Tetanus%20antitoxin%20and%20serum%20therapy%20Kitasato%20Shibasabur%C5%8D)

Working in Koch's laboratory, Kitasato in 1889 grew Clostridium tetani in pure culture by heating mixed cultures to kill other bacteria and growing the spores without oxygen, and showed that it produced a toxin. In December 1890 he and Emil von Behring published a paper showing that the blood serum of animals immunised with small doses of tetanus toxin protected other animals from the disease; Behring published similar work on diphtheria. Serum therapy became the first effective treatment for these infections and opened the field of immunology. Behring alone received the first Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1901, an omission that has often been discussed.

### Identification of the plague bacillus (1894)

- **Date:** 1894
- **Type:** Bacteriological research
- **Published / where:** Hong Kong
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Identification%20of%20the%20plague%20bacillus%20Kitasato%20Shibasabur%C5%8D)

When plague broke out in Hong Kong in 1894, the Japanese government sent Kitasato with a team; he arrived on 12 June, a few days before the Swiss-French bacteriologist Yersin, and reported a bacillus in the blood of victims. Yersin, working separately, isolated the organism from buboes and described it more accurately, and the bacterium was later named Yersinia pestis. Kitasato's description contained confusions, possibly from contamination, and the priority has been debated; for some decades the organism was credited to both men. The episode marked Japan's arrival in international bacteriology.
