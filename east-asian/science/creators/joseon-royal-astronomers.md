# Joseon royal astronomers

*書雲觀*

**1392 – 1910** · Korean  
Born: Hanseong (Seoul) · Died: Hanseong (Seoul)  
Eras & fields: [Joseon science under Sejong](../movements/joseon-science.md)  

> The astronomers of the Joseon Office for Observing Clouds (Seoun-gwan), later the Gwansang-gam, who compiled calendars, kept sky records and carved the great star chart of 1395.

## Biography

The royal astronomical office of Joseon continued that of Goryeo. Its officials observed the sky day and night, recording eclipses, comets, meteors and weather, and prepared the annual calendar. The star chart Cheonsang Yeolcha Bunyajido was carved in 1395 under King Taejo by a team of twelve astronomers, with an inscription by the scholar Gwon Geun.

Their observation logs, partly preserved in the Annals of the Joseon Dynasty and the records of the office, are among the richest sources for historical astronomy, including observations of the supernova of 1604.

## Key works & inventions

### Cheonsang Yeolcha Bunyajido (1395)

[![Cheonsang Yeolcha Bunyajido](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/Cheonsang.jpg/1280px-Cheonsang.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cheonsang.jpg)

<sub>Rubbing of the Cheonsang Yeolcha Bunyajido star chart Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cheonsang.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1395
- **Type:** Star chart carved in stone
- **Published / where:** Hanseong (Seoul), Joseon; now National Palace Museum of Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Cheonsang%20Yeolcha%20Bunyajido%20Joseon%20royal%20astronomers)

According to its inscription by Gwon Geun, the chart was carved for King Taejo, founder of Joseon, from a rubbing of an old Goguryeo star map whose stone had been lost in a river, corrected with new observations by the royal astronomers. The stele, about two metres tall, shows a circular map of the sky centred on the north pole, with the 28 lunar lodges, the equator and the ecliptic, and some 1,467 stars, followed by texts on the lodges, the twelve stations and the stars culminating at dusk and dawn in each season. It legitimised the new dynasty by showing its alignment with heaven. A second carving was made under King Sukjong in 1687. Both stones survive in Seoul, and rubbings and printed copies spread widely; it appears on the 10,000-won note.
