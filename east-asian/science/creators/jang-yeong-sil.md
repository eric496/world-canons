# Jang Yeong-sil

*蔣英實*

**c. 1390 – after 1442** · Korean  
Born: Dongnae (Busan) · Died: Korea  
Eras & fields: [Joseon science under Sejong](../movements/joseon-science.md)  

> Joseon engineer, born a government slave, who built King Sejong's self-striking water clock, rain gauges and astronomical instruments.

## Biography

Jang Yeong-sil was the son of a Chinese father and a Korean kisaeng mother, and was born with the status of a government slave in Dongnae. His skill came to the notice of King Taejong and then Sejong, who sent him to study instruments in Ming China, freed him and raised him to official rank.

He built the Jagyeongnu, a water clock with automatic time-signalling figures (1434), the Okru, an astronomical clock, armillary spheres and sundials, and is linked with the cheugugi rain gauge of 1441. In 1442 a royal palanquin he oversaw broke, and he was dismissed and disappears from the records. He is now a national hero of Korean science.

### Jagyeongnu (1434)

[![Jagyeongnu](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/67/BoRuGak_Jagyeongnu.JPG/1280px-BoRuGak_Jagyeongnu.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:BoRuGak_Jagyeongnu.JPG)

<sub>The surviving vessels of the Borugak Jagyeongnu of 1536, Deoksu Palace, Seoul Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:BoRuGak_Jagyeongnu.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1434
- **Type:** Automatic water clock
- **Published / where:** Gyeongbokgung, Hanseong (Seoul), Joseon
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jagyeongnu%20Jang%20Yeong-sil)

Built by Jang Yeong-sil and Kim Bin at Sejong's command and installed in 1434 in the Borugak pavilion of Gyeongbok Palace, the Jagyeongnu was the official timekeeper of Joseon. Water flowed from large vessels into two cylinders in which floats rose, pushing rods that released copper balls at set intervals. The balls rolled down channels to trip a mechanism of levers and further balls that made three wooden figures strike a bell for the double-hours and a drum and gong for the night watches, while figures holding time-tablets appeared. The design drew on Chinese and Islamic water clocks. The original was destroyed, and a version made in 1536 survives in part at Deoksu Palace; full working reconstructions have been built in Seoul.

### Angbu Ilgu (1434)

- **Date:** 1434
- **Type:** Sundial
- **Published / where:** Hanseong (Seoul), Joseon
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Angbu%20Ilgu%20Jang%20Yeong-sil)

The Angbu Ilgu is a hemispherical bronze sundial with a pointed gnomon aimed at the celestial pole; the shadow of its tip falls on a grid of seven vertical hour lines and thirteen curving lines for the solar terms of the year. Sejong placed such sundials in public streets of Seoul in 1434, with the hours marked by animal figures of the zodiac so that people who could not read could tell the time. They were made by Jang Yeong-sil and other court craftsmen, building on Yuan models. Several later examples from the seventeenth to nineteenth centuries survive, and it is one of the symbols of Joseon science.
