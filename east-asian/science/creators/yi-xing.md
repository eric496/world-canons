## Biography

Zhang Sui, who took the Buddhist name Yi Xing, was a monk of the esoteric school and a mathematician summoned to court by Emperor Xuanzong to reform the calendar. His Dayan calendar of 728 was considered one of the best of the Tang.

He organised a survey of the length of the noon shadow and the altitude of the pole star at stations stretching some 2,500 km, the first measurement of a length of meridian arc in China, which disproved the old belief that the shadow changed by one inch per thousand li. With the engineer Liang Lingzan he built a water-driven celestial globe whose mechanism is often regarded as the first escapement in clockwork.

## Key works & inventions

### Dayan calendar and meridian survey (724–728)

- **Date:** 724–728
- **Type:** Calendar and geodetic survey
- **Published / where:** Tang China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dayan%20calendar%20and%20meridian%20survey%20Yi%20Xing)

Between 723 and 726 Yi Xing and the astronomer Nangong Yue organised teams who measured the summer-solstice noon shadow of a standard gnomon and the altitude of the pole star at stations from Jiaozhou in modern Vietnam to the Mongolian steppe. The results showed that the old rule of one inch of shadow per thousand li was false, and gave a value for a degree of meridian arc of about 130 km, too long but a remarkable first measurement. With the data and new observations Yi Xing compiled the Dayan calendar, finished at his death in 727 and issued in 729, which was praised as the model of Tang calendrical science and adopted in Japan.
