## Biography

Wang Xi, styled Shuhe, served as imperial physician under the Wei and Jin dynasties. He gathered the scattered chapters of Zhang Zhongjing's writings and arranged them into the form that survives.

His Maijing, or Pulse Classic, systematised the reading of the pulse at three positions on each wrist into twenty-four pulse types. It was carried to Korea, Japan, Tibet and the Islamic world, and pulse-taking remains the signature of East Asian diagnosis.

## Key works & inventions

### Pulse Classic (Maijing) (c. 280)

- **Date:** c. 280
- **Type:** Medical treatise
- **Published / where:** China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pulse%20Classic%20%28Maijing%29%20Wang%20Shuhe)

Wang Shuhe's Maijing, in ten chapters, gathered earlier teaching on the pulse into a method: the physician feels the radial pulse at three positions on each wrist, at three depths, each related to particular organs, and distinguishes twenty-four types such as floating, sinking, slippery and choppy. The book links these types to diseases and prognoses, drawing on the Inner Classic, the Classic of Difficulties and Zhang Zhongjing. It became a required text of the Tang medical bureau, was carried to Korea and Japan, and passed in translation through Persia, where a version appears in the fourteenth-century compilation of Rashid al-Din.
