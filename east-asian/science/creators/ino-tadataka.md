> Merchant turned surveyor who from 1800 to 1816 walked the coasts of Japan and produced the first accurate maps of the country.

<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Inō Tadataka · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ino_Tadataka.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Inō Tadataka was adopted into a sake-brewing and merchant family in Sawara and ran it successfully for thirty years. At fifty he retired to Edo to study astronomy with Takahashi Yoshitoki, an expert in Western calendrical science.

To measure the length of a degree of latitude he won shogunal permission to survey Ezo (Hokkaidō) in 1800, and over ten expeditions he and his team measured the coasts and main roads by pacing, chains and astronomical observation. The completed maps were finished by his pupils in 1821. They were so accurate that they were used into the twentieth century.

## Key works & inventions

### Maps of the coasts of Japan (1800–1821)

[![Maps of the coasts of Japan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Ino_Tadataka_map_%28daizu%2C%2C_Atsumi-hanto%29.jpg/1280px-Ino_Tadataka_map_%28daizu%2C%2C_Atsumi-hanto%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ino_Tadataka_map_%28daizu%2C%2C_Atsumi-hanto%29.jpg)

<sub>Large-scale Inō map of the Atsumi peninsula Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ino_Tadataka_map_%28daizu%2C%2C_Atsumi-hanto%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1800–1821
- **Type:** Survey maps
- **Published / where:** Edo, Japan; Tokyo National Museum and other collections
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Maps%20of%20the%20coasts%20of%20Japan%20In%C5%8D%20Tadataka)

Between 1800 and 1816 Inō and his teams walked some 35,000 km in ten expeditions, measuring distances with ropes, chains and counted paces, directions with magnetic compasses and latitudes by observing stars. The final maps, completed by his pupils in 1821 after his death, consisted of 214 sheets at about 1:36,000, 8 at 1:216,000 and 3 at 1:432,000, showing coastlines, roads, towns and landmarks with striking accuracy. Kept secret by the shogunate, they became famous when Siebold was caught with a copy in 1828; British surveyors in 1861 adopted them rather than complete their own. The government relied on them until the 1920s, and full sets of copies have been rediscovered in Japan and the United States.
