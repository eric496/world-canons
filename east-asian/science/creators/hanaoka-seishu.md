> Japanese surgeon who in 1804 performed the first known operation under general anaesthesia, removing a breast cancer using his herbal formula tsūsensan.

<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Hanaoka Seishū · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:HanaokaSeishu.gif)</sub>

## Biography

Hanaoka Seishū studied Chinese medicine and Dutch-style surgery in Kyoto and returned to practise in his village in Kii province. Inspired by the legend of Hua Tuo, he spent years developing an oral anaesthetic from datura (Korean morning glory), aconite and other herbs. His mother and wife volunteered for the tests; his mother died and his wife lost her sight.

On 13 October 1804 he removed a breast tumour from a sixty-year-old woman, Kan Aiya, under the anaesthetic, four decades before ether was used in Boston. He went on to perform over 150 such operations and trained more than a thousand students, combining Chinese and Western medicine, but his methods were kept secret and did not reach the West.

## Key works & inventions

### General anaesthesia with tsūsensan (1804)

[![General anaesthesia with tsūsensan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d2/Image_from_%22Surgical_Casebook%22_by_Hanaoka_Seishu.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Image_from_%22Surgical_Casebook%22_by_Hanaoka_Seishu.jpg)

<sub>Illustration from a surgical casebook of the Hanaoka school Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Image_from_%22Surgical_Casebook%22_by_Hanaoka_Seishu.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1804
- **Type:** Surgical operation
- **Published / where:** Hirayama, Kii province, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=General%20anaesthesia%20with%20ts%C5%ABsensan%20Hanaoka%20Seish%C5%AB)

Hanaoka Seishū's tsūsensan, also called mafutsusan, was a decoction of six or more herbs, chiefly the datura plant chōsen-asagao and aconite, whose alkaloids produce deep unconsciousness. After years of animal tests and trials on his mother and wife, he gave it to Kan Aiya, a sixty-year-old woman with breast cancer, and excised the tumour while she was unconscious; she died some months later, probably of the disease. Hanaoka performed many more operations for breast cancer, as well as other surgery, and recorded them with illustrations. Because his school kept the formula secret and Japan was closed, the work was unknown in the West, where ether anaesthesia was demonstrated in 1846. It is now recognised by surgical and anaesthesiology societies worldwide.
