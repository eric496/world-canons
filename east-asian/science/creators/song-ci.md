# Song Ci

*宋慈*

**1186 – 1249** · Chinese  
Born: Jianyang, Fujian · Died: Guangzhou  
Eras & fields: [Song science & engineering](../movements/song-science.md)  

> Southern Song judge whose Collected Cases of Injustice Rectified (1247) is the world's first systematic book of forensic medicine.

## Biography

Song Ci passed the examinations in 1217 and served as a judicial intendant in several provinces, where he was responsible for reviewing criminal cases, including deaths.

His Xi Yuan Ji Lu brings together methods for examining corpses: distinguishing drowning from death before immersion, strangling from hanging, and recognising wounds and poisons. It became the official handbook of coroners in China for six hundred years and was translated into Korean, Japanese and, in the nineteenth century, European languages.

## Key works & inventions

### Collected Cases of Injustice Rectified (1247)

- **Date:** 1247
- **Type:** Forensic handbook
- **Published / where:** Hunan, Southern Song China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Collected%20Cases%20of%20Injustice%20Rectified%20Song%20Ci)

Drawing on earlier case collections and his own experience as a judicial commissioner, Song Ci wrote the Xi Yuan Ji Lu in 1247 to guide officials who had to examine deaths. It explains how to conduct an inquest, how to distinguish suicide, murder and accident, how to recognise wounds made before and after death, signs of drowning, burning, hanging and strangulation, and the effects of poisons, and how to reveal bruises with vinegar and heated pits. Some of its methods, such as testing for bloodstains, have been tested by modern scientists, others are unreliable. Expanded by later editions, it remained the official coroners' manual of China until the twentieth century and was translated into Korean, Japanese, Dutch, French, German and English.
