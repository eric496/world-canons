# Kim Jeong-ho

*金正浩*

**c. 1804 – c. 1866** · Korean  
Born: Hwanghae province (probably) · Died: Korea  
Eras & fields: [Silhak and late Joseon science](../movements/silhak-science.md)  

> Joseon cartographer who produced the Daedong Yeojido (1861), the largest and most accurate traditional map of Korea.

## Biography

Kim Jeong-ho was of humble origin, and very little reliable information about his life survives; the popular story that he walked the length of Korea many times and was imprisoned by the regent is a later legend. He worked in Seoul with scholar friends who gave him access to government maps.

After earlier maps and a geographical gazetteer, he published the Daedong Yeojido in 1861, printed from woodblocks he carved himself. It divides the country into 22 folding strips that join into a map nearly 7 metres tall, with a uniform grid scale and symbols for roads, rivers, fortresses and post stations.

## Key works & inventions

### Daedong Yeojido (1861)

[![Daedong Yeojido](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Daedongyeojido-full.jpg/1280px-Daedongyeojido-full.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Daedongyeojido-full.jpg)

<sub>The Daedong Yeojido assembled Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Daedongyeojido-full.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1861
- **Type:** Woodblock-printed map
- **Published / where:** Hanseong (Seoul), Joseon
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Daedong%20Yeojido%20Kim%20Jeong-ho)

The Daedong Yeojido was printed in 1861 from 60 woodblocks, some of which survive in the National Museum of Korea. It divides Korea from north to south into 22 strips, each folded like a screen into a book, which together form a map at a scale of about 1:160,000. Kim used a uniform grid, marked roads with dots at every ten li so that distances could be read, and used a legend of symbols for towns, fortresses, post stations, beacons and warehouses, while drawing mountains as continuous ridges. Built on earlier government and private maps and on his own gazetteer, it is the most accurate and practical traditional map of Korea.
