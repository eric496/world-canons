# Zu Chongzhi

*祖沖之*

**429 – 500** · Chinese  
Born: Jiankang (Nanjing) · Died: Jiankang (Nanjing)  
Eras & fields: [Paper & Han technology](../movements/han-technology.md), [Han mathematics](../movements/han-mathematics.md)  

> Mathematician and astronomer of the Southern Dynasties who placed π between 3.1415926 and 3.1415927, a record that stood for nearly a thousand years.

## Biography

Zu Chongzhi came from a family of court astronomers and served the Liu Song and Southern Qi dynasties in the south. He drew up the Daming calendar, the first in China to take account of the precession of the equinoxes, and defended it at court against conservative critics; it was adopted only after his death.

His mathematical book Zhui Shu is lost, but the Sui dynastic history records his bounds for π and his fractions 22/7 and 355/113, the latter accurate to six decimal places. With his son Zu Geng he found the volume of a sphere using a principle equivalent to Cavalieri's. He was also said to have built a south-pointing chariot and a paddle-wheel boat.

## Key works & inventions

### Zu Chongzhi's calculation of π (c. 480)

- **Date:** c. 480
- **Type:** Mathematical result
- **Published / where:** Southern Qi, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Zu%20Chongzhi%27s%20calculation%20of%20%CF%80%20Zu%20Chongzhi)

The Sui dynastic history records that Zu Chongzhi showed that π lies between 3.1415926 and 3.1415927, and gave 22/7 as an 'approximate ratio' and 355/113 as a 'close ratio'. His book Zhui Shu, which explained his methods, was lost by the Song, but he probably extended Liu Hui's method of inscribed polygons to one of 12,288 or 24,576 sides, a huge labour on the counting board. The fraction 355/113 was not found again in Europe until the sixteenth century, and his decimal bounds were not bettered until Jamshid al-Kashi in 1424. In China the fraction is known as the Zu ratio.
