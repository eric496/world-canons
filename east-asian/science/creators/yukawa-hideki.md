# Yukawa Hideki

*湯川秀樹*

**1907 – 1981** · Japanese  
Born: Tokyo · Died: Kyoto  
Eras & fields: [Meiji and modern Japanese science](../movements/meiji-science.md), [Science since 1945](../movements/contemporary-science.md)  

> Theoretical physicist who predicted the meson as the carrier of the nuclear force in 1935 and in 1949 became the first Japanese Nobel laureate.

## Biography

Yukawa grew up in Kyoto, son of a geologist, and studied physics at Kyoto Imperial University, where he and Tomonaga Shin'ichirō were classmates. He taught at Osaka and Kyoto, and after the war spent several years at Princeton and Columbia before returning to Kyoto to direct a new institute of theoretical physics.

In 1935, aged 27, he proposed that protons and neutrons are held together by exchanging a new particle about two hundred times heavier than the electron. The pion was found in cosmic rays in 1947, and he received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1949. He became an advocate of nuclear disarmament and signed the Russell–Einstein Manifesto.

## Key works & inventions

### Meson theory of nuclear forces (1935)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Nobel Prize →](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/1949/yukawa/facts/)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1935
- **Type:** Theoretical physics paper
- **Published / where:** Osaka Imperial University; Proceedings of the Physico-Mathematical Society of Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Meson%20theory%20of%20nuclear%20forces%20Yukawa%20Hideki)

In his 1935 paper 'On the Interaction of Elementary Particles. I', Yukawa reasoned that just as the electromagnetic force is carried by photons, the strong force binding protons and neutrons should be carried by a field whose quanta have mass, since the force acts only over very short distances. From the range of nuclear forces he estimated the mass of the quantum at around 200 electron masses; the potential he introduced is still called the Yukawa potential. A particle of about that mass found in cosmic rays in 1937, the muon, turned out not to interact strongly, but the pion, discovered by Cecil Powell's group in 1947, fitted the prediction. Yukawa received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1949. The idea of force-carrying particles became central to modern particle physics.
