# Ferdinand Verbiest

*南懷仁*

> Flemish Jesuit who regained the astronomical bureau for the Jesuits in 1669 and built the bronze instruments of the Beijing Ancient Observatory.

<sub>Portrait: Chinese portrait of Ferdinand Verbiest · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%E5%8D%97%E5%85%AC%E6%87%B7%E4%BB%81%E5%83%8F.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Verbiest arrived in China in 1659 and assisted Schall in Beijing. Imprisoned with him during the anti-Jesuit trial, he won a series of public tests of astronomical prediction against his opponents in 1668–69, and the Kangxi Emperor appointed him director of the astronomical bureau.

He designed six large bronze instruments, an armillary sphere, ecliptic and equatorial armillaries, a celestial globe, an azimuth instrument and a sextant, installed on the Beijing observatory in 1674. He also taught the emperor mathematics, cast cannon, made a world map and built what may have been a small steam-propelled cart.

## Key works & inventions

### Instruments of the Beijing Ancient Observatory (1673–1674)

[![Instruments of the Beijing Ancient Observatory](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1c/Ancient_Beijing_observatory_10.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ancient_Beijing_observatory_10.jpg)

<sub>Verbiest's bronze instruments on the Beijing Ancient Observatory Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ancient_Beijing_observatory_10.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1673–1674
- **Type:** Astronomical instruments
- **Published / where:** Beijing Ancient Observatory
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Instruments%20of%20the%20Beijing%20Ancient%20Observatory%20Ferdinand%20Verbiest)

In 1669 the Kangxi Emperor ordered new instruments for the observatory on the city wall of Beijing, and Verbiest designed six on the pattern of those of Tycho Brahe: an ecliptic armillary sphere, an equatorial armillary sphere, an altazimuth, a quadrant, a sextant and a celestial globe, cast in bronze with dragons and clouds in Chinese style and installed in 1674. He described them in an illustrated work printed in Beijing. Later instruments were added in the eighteenth century. Several were taken to Germany and France after the Boxer War of 1900 and returned after the First World War; they can still be seen on the observatory platform.
