# Zhu Zaiyu

*朱載堉*

**1536 – 1611** · Chinese  
Born: Huaiqing, Henan · Died: Huaiqing, Henan  
Eras & fields: [Ming technology & natural history](../movements/ming-science.md)  
Also in: [Music & Theatre](../../music/creators/zhu-zaiyu.md)  

> Ming prince who in 1584 gave the first exact mathematical solution for equal temperament, dividing the octave by the twelfth root of two.

## Biography

Zhu Zaiyu was heir to a Ming princely house, but after his father was disgraced he lived for years in a hut outside the palace walls, studying music, mathematics and the calendar. He later gave up his title to devote himself to scholarship.

In his New Account of the Science of the Pitch-pipes he calculated string and pipe lengths for twelve equal semitones to some twenty-five digits, using an 81-row abacus. Whether his result reached Europe, where Simon Stevin and Mersenne worked on the same problem, is debated. He also wrote on calendars and dance.

## Key works & inventions

### Exact equal temperament (1584)

- **Date:** 1584
- **Type:** Mathematical acoustics
- **Published / where:** Ming China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Exact%20equal%20temperament%20Zhu%20Zaiyu)

For two thousand years Chinese music theory generated its twelve pitches by cycles of fifths, which never return exactly to the starting note. In his Lülü Jingyi of 1584 and later Yuelü Quanshu, the Ming prince Zhu Zaiyu showed that dividing the octave into twelve equal semitones requires each pipe or string to be shorter than the last by the twelfth root of two, and calculated the lengths to twenty-five digits by extracting square and cube roots on a large abacus. He also built a set of tuned pipes and corrected lengths for the end effect of pipes. Simon Stevin reached the same idea in Europe at about the same time; whether either knew of the other is debated.
