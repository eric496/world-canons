# Bi Sheng

*畢昇*

**c. 970 – c. 1051** · Chinese  
Born: Yingshan, Hubei (probably) · Died: China  
Eras & fields: [Printing in Song, Goryeo & Japan](../movements/song-printing.md)  

> Song commoner who invented movable type, of baked clay, in the 1040s; he is known only from Shen Kuo's description.

## Biography

Bi Sheng was a commoner, perhaps an artisan, of whom almost nothing is known. Shen Kuo, writing a few decades later in the Dream Pool Essays, says that in the Qingli reign (1041–48) Bi Sheng made type from clay. A gravestone found in Hubei in 1990 may be his, but the identification is debated.

Each character was carved in soft clay and hardened by fire; the type was set in an iron frame on a bed of resin, wax and paper ash, warmed and pressed flat, and two frames were used in turn for speed. The method was not widely adopted in China, where woodblocks suited a script of thousands of characters, but it was the starting point for wooden type and Korean metal type.

## Key works & inventions

### Movable type (c. 1040)

- **Date:** c. 1040
- **Type:** Printing invention
- **Published / where:** Song China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Movable%20type%20Bi%20Sheng)

Woodblock printing, used in China since the seventh or eighth century, required carving a whole block for every page. Bi Sheng instead made a separate piece for each character in clay, hardening them by fire. He placed the types side by side in an iron frame on an iron plate coated with pine resin, wax and paper ash, warmed it to soften the coating, pressed the type flat with a board and printed; while one frame printed, another was set. Common characters had twenty or more copies. Shen Kuo, who describes it, says that for a few copies it was not fast, but for hundreds or thousands it was very quick. No books printed with his type survive, and in China wooden type, used by Wang Zhen, and later bronze type remained a secondary method; in Korea movable metal type became central.
