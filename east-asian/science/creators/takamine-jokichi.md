# Takamine Jōkichi

*高峰譲吉*

**1854 – 1922** · Japanese  
Born: Takaoka, Etchū (Toyama) · Died: New York  
Eras & fields: [Meiji and modern Japanese science](../movements/meiji-science.md)  

> Chemist who isolated the enzyme preparation Taka-diastase and in 1901 purified adrenaline, the first hormone to be isolated in crystalline form.

## Biography

Takamine studied chemistry in Tokyo and Glasgow and worked for the Japanese government before settling in the United States. He developed Taka-diastase, a digestive enzyme from the koji mould used in sake brewing, patented in 1894.

In 1900–01, with his assistant Uenaka Keizō, he purified adrenaline from adrenal glands, a discovery whose priority was later disputed with John Jacob Abel. He also helped found the RIKEN research institute in Tokyo and gave the cherry trees planted in Washington in 1912.

## Key works & inventions

### Isolation of adrenaline (1901)

- **Date:** 1901
- **Type:** Biochemical discovery
- **Published / where:** Takamine's laboratory, New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Isolation%20of%20adrenaline%20Takamine%20J%C5%8Dkichi)

Extracts of the adrenal gland were known to raise blood pressure, and John Jacob Abel at Johns Hopkins had obtained an impure derivative he called epinephrine. In 1900 Takamine's assistant Uenaka Keizō succeeded in crystallising the active substance, and in 1901 Takamine patented and published it as adrenalin; it was the first hormone isolated in pure form. Marketed by Parke-Davis, it was soon used to stop bleeding in surgery and to treat asthma. Abel disputed the priority, and in the United States the name epinephrine was long preferred, while Uenaka's laboratory notebook later supported the Japanese claim.
