# Liu Hui

*劉徽*

**c. 225 – c. 295** · Chinese  
Born: Zibo, Shandong (probably) · Died: China  
Eras & fields: [Han mathematics](../movements/han-mathematics.md)  

> Mathematician of the Wei kingdom whose commentary on the Nine Chapters (263) added proofs and computed π by inscribed polygons.

## Biography

Almost nothing is known of Liu Hui's life except that he completed his commentary on the Nine Chapters in 263, in the Wei kingdom of the Three Kingdoms period. He also wrote the Sea Island Mathematical Manual, on surveying heights and distances with poles and sightlines.

Liu Hui explained why the methods of the Nine Chapters work, using dissections of figures and a limit argument close to the method of exhaustion. Doubling the sides of a polygon inscribed in a circle to 3,072, he obtained π as 3.14159, and he found the volume of a pyramid by an infinite process. He is regarded as the greatest mathematician of ancient China.

## Key works & inventions

### Liu Hui's commentary and π by polygons (263)

- **Date:** 263
- **Type:** Mathematical commentary
- **Published / where:** Wei kingdom, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Liu%20Hui%27s%20commentary%20and%20%CF%80%20by%20polygons%20Liu%20Hui)

In 263 Liu Hui completed a commentary on the Nine Chapters that explained the reasoning behind each procedure, using cut-and-rearrange dissections of plane and solid figures, which he described as pieces of coloured paper or blocks. For the area of the circle he inscribed a hexagon and repeatedly doubled its sides, arguing that 'cut after cut', the polygon finally coincides with the circle, and obtained 3.14 and then 3.1416. His treatment of the volume of a pyramid used a limiting process, and his appendix on surveying became the separate Sea Island Mathematical Manual. The commentary was printed with the Nine Chapters and is the earliest surviving Chinese mathematical writing to argue for its results.
