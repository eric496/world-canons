# Noguchi Hideyo

*野口英世*

**1876 – 1928** · Japanese  
Born: Inawashiro, Fukushima · Died: Accra, Gold Coast  
Eras & fields: [Meiji and modern Japanese science](../movements/meiji-science.md)  

> Bacteriologist at the Rockefeller Institute who showed in 1913 that syphilis bacteria cause general paresis, and died researching yellow fever in Africa.

## Biography

Noguchi grew up poor in rural Fukushima; a burn in infancy crippled his left hand, and surgery on it inspired him to study medicine. He worked at Kitasato's institute and in 1900 moved to the United States, joining the Rockefeller Institute in New York in 1904.

He demonstrated Treponema pallidum in the brains of patients with general paresis, proving the syphilitic cause of this form of insanity. Several of his other claims, including a bacterial cause of yellow fever, proved wrong. He died of yellow fever in Accra while investigating it. Admired in Japan as a model of perseverance, he appears on the 1,000-yen note issued 2004–24.

## Key works & inventions

### Spirochaete in general paresis (1913)

- **Date:** 1913
- **Type:** Medical research
- **Published / where:** Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research, New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Spirochaete%20in%20general%20paresis%20Noguchi%20Hideyo)

General paresis, a progressive dementia with paralysis, filled asylums in the nineteenth century, and a link with past syphilis was suspected but unproven. In 1913 Noguchi and Joseph W. Moore published their finding of the syphilis spirochaete Treponema pallidum in the brain tissue of patients who had died of paresis, and Noguchi also found it in the spinal cord in tabes dorsalis. The discovery confirmed that these were late forms of syphilis and was one of the clearest demonstrations of an infectious cause of a mental illness. Several other claims by Noguchi were later shown to be wrong, but this one has stood.
