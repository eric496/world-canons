# Shang royal diviners

*商代貞人*

**c. 1250 BC – c. 1046 BC** · Chinese  
Born: Anyang, Henan · Died: Anyang, Henan  
Eras & fields: [Early astronomy & calendars](../movements/early-astronomy.md)  
Also in: [Religion](../../religion/creators/shang-diviners.md)  

> The anonymous diviners of the late Shang kings, whose inscribed oracle bones are the oldest Chinese writing and contain the earliest records of eclipses and the sixty-day calendar.

## Biography

At the Shang capital near modern Anyang, specialists put the king's questions to his ancestors by applying heat to prepared ox shoulder-blades and turtle shells and reading the cracks. From about 1250 BC the question, and sometimes the outcome, was carved on the bone. Over 150,000 fragments survive, many naming the diviner who cut them.

Among the questions about harvests, war and illness are notes of lunar and solar eclipses, a 'new star' and weather, all dated in the sixty-day ganzhi cycle still used in East Asian calendars. Astronomers have used them to test calculations of the earth's rotation over three millennia, though the reading of individual records is often debated.

## Key works & inventions

### Oracle-bone eclipse and star records (c. 1250–1050 BC)

- **Date:** c. 1250–1050 BC
- **Type:** Inscribed divination records
- **Published / where:** Anyang, Henan; fragments in many collections
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Oracle-bone%20eclipse%20and%20star%20records%20Shang%20royal%20diviners)

Among the tens of thousands of inscribed bones and shells excavated since 1899 at the late Shang capital of Yin, near Anyang, a few dozen refer to eclipses of the moon and sun, to stars and to unusual weather, usually as omens on which the king sought advice. They are dated by the ganzhi cycle of sixty days that the Shang used continuously, and are the earliest evidence for that count. One famous inscription mentions a 'great new star' beside the asterism Huo, sometimes read as a nova. Because the dates can be correlated with eclipse calculations, the records have been used both to fix Shang chronology and to measure the slowing of the earth's rotation, though the readings of particular characters remain debated.
