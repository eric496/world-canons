**c. 300 BC – c. AD 100** · Chinese  
Born: China · Died: China

> The unknown compilers of the Zhoubi Suanjing, the oldest Chinese book of mathematical astronomy, which uses the gnomon and the right-angled triangle to measure the heavens.

## Biography

The Zhoubi Suanjing ('Mathematical Classic of the Zhou Gnomon') was put together over several centuries and reached its present form under the Han, probably by about the first century BC. It is framed as dialogues, including one between the Duke of Zhou and a figure named Shang Gao, but no author is known.

The book explains the 'canopy heaven' cosmology, in which a round sky covers a square earth, and uses shadows cast by an eight-foot gnomon, with right-triangle reasoning, to calculate the distance and size of the sun. Its famous diagram demonstrates the relationship known in the West as Pythagoras' theorem, called in China the gougu rule.

- **Date:** c. 100 BC
- **Type:** Mathematical astronomy
- **Published / where:** China; earliest printed edition 1213
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Zhoubi%20Suanjing%20Authors%20of%20the%20Zhoubi%20Suanjing)

The Zhoubi Suanjing, 'Mathematical Classic of the Zhou Gnomon', is a short text compiled under the Western Han from older material. It sets out the gaitian or 'canopy heaven' cosmology, in which the heavens are a dome rotating over a flat earth, and computes the distance of the sun from the length of the noon shadow of an eight-foot gnomon at different places, assuming that the shadow changes by one inch for every thousand li. Its first section, a dialogue between the Duke of Zhou and Shang Gao, contains the gougu rule for right triangles and the famous 'hypotenuse diagram' that demonstrates it for the 3-4-5 triangle. With commentaries by Zhao Shuang and later Li Chunfeng, it became one of the Ten Computational Canons of the Tang examinations.
