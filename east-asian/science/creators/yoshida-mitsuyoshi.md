# Yoshida Mitsuyoshi

*吉田光由*

**1598 – 1672** · Japanese  
Born: Saga, Kyoto · Died: Kyoto  
Eras & fields: [Wasan: Edo mathematics](../movements/wasan.md)  

> Kyoto mathematician whose Jinkōki (1627), a popular arithmetic book on the soroban, launched the mathematical culture of Edo Japan.

## Biography

Yoshida Mitsuyoshi belonged to the Suminokura family of Kyoto merchants and engineers, who ran river transport and overseas trade. He studied the Chinese mathematical text Suanfa Tongzong of Cheng Dawei.

His Jinkōki adapted it for Japanese readers, teaching the soroban, measures, currency exchange and practical problems with illustrations, and became one of the best-selling books of the Edo period. Later editions ended with unsolved problems for readers, starting the custom of challenge problems that drove wasan forward.

## Key works & inventions

### Jinkōki (1627)

[![Jinkōki](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/67/Yoshida_Soroban.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yoshida_Soroban.jpg)

<sub>Illustration of the soroban from the Jinkōki Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yoshida_Soroban.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1627
- **Type:** Arithmetic textbook
- **Published / where:** Kyoto, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jink%C5%8Dki%20Yoshida%20Mitsuyoshi)

Based on the Chinese Suanfa Tongzong (1592) of Cheng Dawei, the Jinkōki taught multiplication and division on the soroban, units of measure, the exchange of gold, silver and copper coins, interest, surveying, the calculation of rice and the volume of timber, with lively pictures and puzzles such as the multiplying of mice. It sold so well that it was pirated and revised for two centuries, and its name became a byword for an arithmetic book. In the 1641 edition Yoshida added twelve problems without answers, challenging readers; solving and posing such problems, idai keishō, became the engine of wasan.
