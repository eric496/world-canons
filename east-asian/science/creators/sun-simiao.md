## Biography

Sun Simiao was famous as a youth for his learning and lived, by tradition, past one hundred. He repeatedly declined official posts under the Sui and Tang, preferring to practise medicine and Daoist cultivation, though he spent time at court.

His two great compendia, Beiji Qianjin Yaofang (652) and its supplement (682), contain over 6,000 prescriptions and give special attention to women's and children's medicine, nutrition and deficiency diseases. Their opening essay on 'the absolute sincerity of the great physician' asked doctors to treat rich and poor alike. He was deified in popular religion as the medicine king, and an early gunpowder-like formula has been linked, uncertainly, to his alchemical writings.

## Key works & inventions

### Prescriptions Worth a Thousand Gold (652)

- **Date:** 652
- **Type:** Medical encyclopedia
- **Published / where:** Tang China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Prescriptions%20Worth%20a%20Thousand%20Gold%20Sun%20Simiao)

Sun Simiao named his book on the principle that a human life is worth more than a thousand pieces of gold. In thirty chapters it treats gynaecology and obstetrics first, then paediatrics, then internal medicine, acupuncture, diet and longevity, with over 5,000 prescriptions; a supplement of 682 adds more than 2,000 others. It describes goitre treated with seaweed and thyroid tissue and night blindness treated with liver, and opens with 'On the Absolute Sincerity of Great Physicians', a statement of medical ethics still quoted in Chinese medical schools. Both works were central texts of later Chinese and Japanese medicine.
