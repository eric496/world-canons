# Ogata Kōan

*緒方洪庵*

**1810 – 1863** · Japanese  
Born: Ashimori, Bitchū (Okayama) · Died: Edo  
Eras & fields: [Rangaku: Dutch learning](../movements/rangaku.md)  

> Osaka physician and teacher whose school, the Tekijuku, trained a generation of modernisers, and who led smallpox vaccination in Japan.

## Biography

Ogata Kōan studied Dutch medicine in Osaka, Edo and Nagasaki and in 1838 opened the Tekijuku in Osaka, where some three thousand students passed through, among them Fukuzawa Yukichi and Ōmura Masujirō.

He founded a vaccination centre in 1849 to spread cowpox inoculation against smallpox, wrote on cholera during the 1858 epidemic, and translated a German pathology. In 1862 he was called to Edo to head the shogunate's Western medical institute.

## Key works & inventions

### Smallpox vaccination in Osaka (1849)

- **Date:** 1849
- **Type:** Public health campaign
- **Published / where:** Osaka, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Smallpox%20vaccination%20in%20Osaka%20Ogata%20K%C5%8Dan)

Cowpox vaccine first reached Japan successfully in 1849, when the Dutch physician Otto Mohnike in Nagasaki obtained viable lymph from Batavia. Within months Ogata Kōan and the merchant-physicians of Osaka opened the Jotōkan to vaccinate children free of charge, and Kōan sent vaccine to branch stations in many domains, overcoming fear and local resistance. In 1858 the shogunate recognised the centre officially, and the vaccination institute in Edo founded in the same year became the root of the University of Tokyo's medical faculty. The campaign was one of the first large public-health efforts in Japan.
