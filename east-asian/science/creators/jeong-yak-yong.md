# Jeong Yak-yong

*丁若鏞*

**1762 – 1836** · Korean  
Born: Namyangju, Gyeonggi · Died: Namyangju, Gyeonggi  
Eras & fields: [Silhak and late Joseon science](../movements/silhak-science.md)  
Also in: [Architecture](../../architecture/creators/jeong-yak-yong.md), [Philosophy](../../philosophy/creators/jeong-yak-yong.md)  

> Greatest scholar of Korean Silhak, who designed cranes for the Hwaseong fortress and wrote on smallpox vaccination, government and the classics.

## Biography

Jeong Yak-yong, known as Dasan, served King Jeongjo as a trusted official and in 1792 designed the construction methods for the fortress of Hwaseong at Suwon, including a crane using pulleys that is said to have saved much labour. After Jeongjo's death he was exiled for eighteen years for his links with Catholicism.

In exile he wrote some five hundred volumes on the classics, administration, law, geography and medicine, including a treatise on smallpox with an account of variolation and, later, vaccination. His practical, reforming learning made him the central figure of the Silhak tradition.

## Key works & inventions

### Geojunggi crane (1792)

- **Date:** 1792
- **Type:** Lifting machine
- **Published / where:** Hwaseong fortress, Suwon, Joseon
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Geojunggi%20crane%20Jeong%20Yak-yong)

When King Jeongjo planned the fortress of Hwaseong at Suwon, he gave Jeong Yak-yong the Qing encyclopedia Gujin Tushu Jicheng, which reproduced a Jesuit book of Western machines. Jeong designed a crane, the geojunggi, using a set of upper and lower pulleys with two winches, with which a few men could lift heavy stones, and other devices for moving loads. The construction record, the Hwaseong Seongyeok Uigwe of 1801, illustrates the crane and says the machines saved some 40,000 strings of cash. The fortress was finished in 1796 in under three years and is now a World Heritage Site.
