## Biography

Hiraga Gennai was a low-ranking samurai who studied herbal medicine in Nagasaki and Edo, organised exhibitions of natural products, prospected for mines and made a thermometer and asbestos cloth. He was also a popular satirical writer and introduced Western-style oil painting to Akita domain artists.

In 1776 he restored a broken Dutch electrostatic generator and showed it to amazed audiences. A brilliant but restless figure, he died in prison after killing a man in a quarrel. He is remembered as an emblem of Edo curiosity about the Western sciences.

## Key works & inventions

### Erekiteru (1776)

[![Erekiteru](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/Elekiter_replica.jpg/1280px-Elekiter_replica.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Elekiter_replica.jpg)

<sub>Replica of an Edo-period erekiteru Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Elekiter_replica.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1776
- **Type:** Electrostatic generator
- **Published / where:** Edo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Erekiteru%20Hiraga%20Gennai)

The elektriciteit, rendered in Japanese as erekiteru, was a box-shaped friction machine in which a crank turned a glass cylinder against a pad, generating static electricity that could be drawn off in sparks; Dutch traders had brought such devices as curiosities and for supposed medical use. Hiraga Gennai obtained a broken one in Nagasaki and after several years of work restored it in Edo in 1776, demonstrating sparks and shocks to paying visitors and patrons. He did not understand electrical theory, and the machine was mainly a spectacle, but it inspired later Japanese experimenters. One surviving erekiteru attributed to him is in the Postal Museum Japan.
