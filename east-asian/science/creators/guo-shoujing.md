# Guo Shoujing

*郭守敬*

**1231 – 1316** · Chinese  
Born: Xingtai, Hebei · Died: Dadu (Beijing)  
Eras & fields: [Song–Yuan mathematics](../movements/song-yuan-mathematics.md), [Early astronomy & calendars](../movements/early-astronomy.md)  

> Yuan astronomer, engineer and hydraulic expert who built new instruments and created the Shoushi calendar of 1281, used in modified form for over 360 years.

## Biography

Guo Shoujing was trained by his grandfather and by the scholars around Liu Bingzhong. He first served Khubilai Khan as a hydraulic engineer, restoring canals in the north, and in 1276 joined the commission to reform the calendar.

He designed some twenty instruments, including the simplified armillary (jianyi) and a 40-foot gnomon, and directed observations at many stations. The resulting Shoushi calendar fixed the tropical year at 365.2425 days, the same value as the later Gregorian calendar, and used a form of spherical trigonometry and finite differences. He also completed the canal that linked Beijing to the Grand Canal.

- **Date:** 1281
- **Type:** Astronomical calendar
- **Published / where:** Dadu (Beijing), Yuan China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Shoushi%20calendar%20Guo%20Shoujing)

After the Mongol conquest Khubilai Khan ordered a new calendar, and a commission led by Wang Xun and Guo Shoujing worked from 1276 to 1280. Guo designed new bronze instruments, including the simplified armillary and an enlarged gnomon with a pinhole shadow-definer that measured the solstice shadow precisely, and observations were taken at twenty-seven stations across the empire. The resulting Shoushi calendar, promulgated in 1281, adopted a tropical year of 365.2425 days and used third-order interpolation for the motions of the sun and moon and a form of spherical trigonometry. Renamed the Datong calendar under the Ming, it was used until 1644, and Korea's Chiljeongsan was adapted from it.
