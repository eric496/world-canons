# Jia Sixie

*賈思勰*

**5th–6th century – 6th century** · Chinese  
Born: Yidu, Shandong · Died: China  
Eras & fields: [Paper & Han technology](../movements/han-technology.md)  

> Official of the Northern Wei whose Qimin Yaoshu is the oldest complete Chinese agricultural treatise.

## Biography

Little is known of Jia Sixie beyond his book, in which he describes himself as a former governor of Gaoyang commandery in modern Shandong. He wrote it between about 533 and 544 under the Northern Wei, drawing on farmers, older books and his own experiments.

His Essential Techniques for the Welfare of the People covers grain crops, vegetables, fruit trees, sericulture, animal husbandry, and food processing including fermentation, brewing and soy sauce. Its methodical, observational approach set the model for the many later Chinese, Korean and Japanese farming manuals.

## Key works & inventions

### Qimin Yaoshu (c. 544)

[![Qimin Yaoshu](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1b/Qimin_yaoshu.gif)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Qimin_yaoshu.gif)

<sub>Page of an early edition of the Qimin Yaoshu Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Qimin_yaoshu.gif).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 544
- **Type:** Agricultural treatise
- **Published / where:** Northern Wei, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Qimin%20Yaoshu%20Jia%20Sixie)

Jia Sixie's book, in ten chapters and ninety-two sections, describes the cultivation of grains, legumes, vegetables and fruit, tree grafting, the raising of silkworms, horses, cattle, sheep and fish, and the making of vinegar, wine, soy sauce, pickles and other foods, mostly for the dry north of China. It records crop rotation, green manuring and the selection of seed, quotes about 160 earlier works, many otherwise lost, and adds the author's own observations. It is a key source for Chinese agricultural and culinary history and the model for later official farming manuals.
