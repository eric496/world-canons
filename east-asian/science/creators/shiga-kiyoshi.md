# Shiga Kiyoshi

*志賀潔*

**1871 – 1957** · Japanese  
Born: Sendai · Died: Sendai  
Eras & fields: [Meiji and modern Japanese science](../movements/meiji-science.md)  

> Bacteriologist who discovered the dysentery bacillus, named Shigella after him, in 1897.

## Biography

Shiga Kiyoshi graduated from the Imperial University in Tokyo in 1896 and joined Kitasato's institute. During a large epidemic in 1897 he isolated the bacterium that causes bacillary dysentery.

He later worked with Paul Ehrlich in Frankfurt on chemotherapy and served as head of the medical college in colonial Seoul. The genus Shigella and the Shiga toxin carry his name.

## Key works & inventions

### Discovery of the dysentery bacillus (1897)

- **Date:** 1897
- **Type:** Bacteriological research
- **Published / where:** Institute for Infectious Diseases, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Discovery%20of%20the%20dysentery%20bacillus%20Shiga%20Kiyoshi)

In 1897 an epidemic of dysentery in Japan affected over 90,000 people and killed more than a fifth of them. Working at Kitasato's institute, Shiga Kiyoshi examined patients' stools and identified a bacillus that was agglutinated by the serum of patients but not of healthy people, showing that it was the cause. He published in Japanese in 1897 and in German in 1898. The genus Shigella was named after him, and the potent toxin produced by S. dysenteriae is called Shiga toxin. He went on to develop a vaccine and to work on chemotherapy with Paul Ehrlich.
