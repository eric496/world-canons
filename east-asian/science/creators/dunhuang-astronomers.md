# Tang astronomers of the Dunhuang star chart

*敦煌星圖作者*

**7th century – 7th century** · Chinese  
Born: China · Died: China  
Eras & fields: [Sui–Tang science & medicine](../movements/sui-tang-science.md), [Early astronomy & calendars](../movements/early-astronomy.md)  

> The unknown Tang astronomers who drew the Dunhuang star chart, the oldest known complete star atlas from any civilisation.

## Biography

The Dunhuang star chart was found in the sealed 'Library Cave' at Dunhuang in 1900 and taken to London by Aurel Stein in 1907. A brief note on it has led some scholars to associate it with the astronomer Li Chunfeng (602–670), but this is debated, and it was probably copied by a scribe from an existing map.

The scroll maps some 1,300 stars in 257 asterisms, following the three schools of Chinese star lore distinguished by colour, in a projection that anticipates the cylindrical one. It shows the systematic star cataloguing of the Tang astronomical bureau.

## Key works & inventions

### Dunhuang Star Chart (c. 650–700)

[![Dunhuang Star Chart](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/Dunhuang_star_map.jpg/1280px-Dunhuang_star_map.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dunhuang_star_map.jpg)

<sub>Part of the Dunhuang star chart, British Library Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dunhuang_star_map.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 650–700
- **Type:** Star atlas on paper
- **Published / where:** Dunhuang, Tang China; now British Library, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dunhuang%20Star%20Chart%20Tang%20astronomers%20of%20the%20Dunhuang%20star%20chart)

The Dunhuang star chart is a paper scroll found in the Mogao 'Library Cave', sealed around 1000, and now in the British Library. After a section of cloud divinations it maps the whole sky visible from China in thirteen panels: twelve strips along the equator, one for each Jupiter station, drawn in a projection similar to the cylindrical, and a circular map of the polar region. About 1,300 stars are shown in 257 asterisms, coloured red, black and yellow according to the three ancient star catalogues of Shi Shen, Gan De and Wu Xian. Dating by the script and taboo characters points to the late seventh century. Its accuracy shows that it was copied from careful astronomical originals.
