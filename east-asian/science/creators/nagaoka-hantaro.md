# Nagaoka Hantarō

*長岡半太郎*

**1865 – 1950** · Japanese  
Born: Ōmura, Hizen (Nagasaki) · Died: Tokyo  
Eras & fields: [Meiji and modern Japanese science](../movements/meiji-science.md)  

> Physicist who in 1903–04 proposed a 'Saturnian' model of the atom, with electrons orbiting a heavy positive centre.

## Biography

Nagaoka studied physics at the Imperial University in Tokyo and in Germany under Boltzmann and others, becoming professor in Tokyo in 1901 and later first president of Osaka Imperial University.

His Saturnian model pictured electrons in a ring around a massive central charge, like the rings of Saturn. It proved unstable and was set aside, but Rutherford cited it when he proposed the nuclear atom in 1911. Nagaoka also worked on magnetostriction and spectroscopy and trained the first generation of Japanese physicists.

## Key works & inventions

### Saturnian model of the atom (1904)

- **Date:** 1904
- **Type:** Physical theory
- **Published / where:** Tokyo; published in Philosophical Magazine
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Saturnian%20model%20of%20the%20atom%20Nagaoka%20Hantar%C5%8D)

In a lecture in Tokyo in 1903 and a paper in the Philosophical Magazine in 1904, Nagaoka rejected J. J. Thomson's model of electrons embedded in a sphere of positive charge, proposing instead a large positive centre with electrons revolving in a ring around it, by analogy with Maxwell's analysis of the stability of Saturn's rings. He used the model to explain spectral lines and radioactivity. It faced the objection that orbiting electrons should radiate their energy and collapse, and he abandoned it, but in 1911 Ernest Rutherford cited Nagaoka when proposing the nuclear atom from scattering experiments.
