# Li Ye

*李冶*

**1192 – 1279** · Chinese  
Born: Daxing (Beijing) · Died: Fenglong Mountain, Hebei  
Eras & fields: [Song–Yuan mathematics](../movements/song-yuan-mathematics.md)  

> Jin and Yuan scholar whose Sea Mirror of Circle Measurements (1248) is the earliest surviving work using the tianyuan method of algebra.

## Biography

Li Ye, originally Li Zhi, passed the Jin examinations and was a prefect when the Mongols overran the Jin in 1232. He lived in poverty and retirement in the north, teaching, and declined most offers of office from Khubilai Khan.

His Ceyuan Haijing treats problems of circles inscribed in triangles through some 170 problems, setting up equations with a single unknown, the 'celestial element', on the counting board, with a symbol for negative numbers. It shows the northern school of algebra that Zhu Shijie would extend to four unknowns.

## Key works & inventions

### Sea Mirror of Circle Measurements (1248)

<sub>The 'diagram of the round town' that opens the Ceyuan Haijing Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%E5%9C%86%E5%9F%8E%E5%9B%BE%E5%BC%8F.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1248
- **Type:** Mathematical treatise
- **Published / where:** Northern China under the Mongols
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sea%20Mirror%20of%20Circle%20Measurements%20Li%20Ye)

The Ceyuan Haijing begins with a single diagram, a right triangle with a circle inscribed and many smaller triangles, and then poses 170 problems about the diameter of a circular city from distances walked by people leaving its gates. Li Ye sets up each problem by the tianyuan shu, the 'method of the celestial element', in which the coefficients of an equation in one unknown are written in a vertical column of counting-rod numerals, with negatives marked by a slanted stroke and zero by a circle. He solves equations up to the sixth degree. The book and his later Yigu Yanduan are the principal sources for northern Chinese algebra of the thirteenth century.
