# Johann Adam Schall von Bell

*湯若望*

**1592 – 1666** · German  
Born: Cologne · Died: Beijing  
Eras & fields: [Jesuit science in China](../movements/jesuit-science.md)  

> German Jesuit astronomer who worked on the Chongzhen calendar reform and became director of the Imperial Astronomical Bureau under the early Qing.

<sub>Portrait: Johann Adam Schall von Bell in Chinese official dress, engraving · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Adam_Schall.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Schall entered the Jesuit order in Rome and arrived in China in 1622. He joined Xu Guangqi's calendar bureau in Beijing in 1630, wrote on the telescope and cast cannon for the Ming. When the Manchus took Beijing in 1644 he presented his calendar to the new Qing court, which adopted it as the Shixian calendar and made him head of the astronomical bureau.

He became a confidant of the young Shunzhi Emperor, but in 1664 the scholar Yang Guangxian accused him of treason and false astronomy, and he was condemned to death. He was released after an earthquake struck Beijing but died soon after. His calendar, based on Tychonic astronomy, remained the basis of the Chinese calendar.

## Key works & inventions

### Shixian calendar (1645)

- **Date:** 1645
- **Type:** Astronomical calendar
- **Published / where:** Beijing, Qing China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Shixian%20calendar%20Johann%20Adam%20Schall%20von%20Bell)

When the Manchus entered Beijing in 1644, Schall offered the new rulers the calendar prepared under Xu Guangqi, and demonstrated its accuracy with an eclipse prediction. It was promulgated for 1645 as the Shixian li, 'Calendar of Temporal Models', with Schall as head of the Imperial Astronomical Bureau. Its foreign origin became the target of Yang Guangxian's attacks in 1664, but after Verbiest's tests the Kangxi Emperor restored it. With further revisions incorporating Kepler's ellipses in the eighteenth century, it was the basis of the official Chinese calendar until 1912, and it reached Korea, where it was adopted in 1653.
