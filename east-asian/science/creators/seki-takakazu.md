# Seki Takakazu

*関孝和*

<a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Seki.jpeg"><img align="right" width="240" src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0f/Seki.jpeg" alt="Seki Takakazu" title="Traditional portrait of Seki Takakazu"></a>

**c. 1642 – 1708** · Japanese  
Born: Fujioka, Kōzuke or Edo · Died: Edo  
Eras & fields: [Wasan: Edo mathematics](../movements/wasan.md)  

> The great mathematician of Edo Japan, who developed determinants, the theory of equations and methods for areas and arcs, and founded the Seki school.

<sub>Portrait: Traditional portrait of Seki Takakazu · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Seki.jpeg)</sub>

## Biography

Seki Takakazu was born into a samurai family and adopted by the Seki family; he served the Kōfu domain as an accountant and then the shogunate. Largely self-taught from Chinese books, including Zhu Shijie's primer, he published only one book in his lifetime.

His Hatsubi Sanpō (1674) solved the fifteen problems left by an earlier writer, using a written algebraic notation, endan-jutsu, that freed Japanese mathematics from the counting board. His manuscripts treat elimination by what are now called determinants, before Leibniz, Bernoulli numbers, and the length of arcs. Revered as sansei, the 'sage of calculation', he founded a school that dominated wasan.

### Hatsubi Sanpō (1674)

[![Hatsubi Sanpō](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/Hatsubi_Sanpou.jpg/1280px-Hatsubi_Sanpou.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hatsubi_Sanpou.jpg)

<sub>Page of the Hatsubi Sanpō, 1674 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hatsubi_Sanpou.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1674
- **Type:** Mathematical treatise
- **Published / where:** Edo, Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Hatsubi%20Sanp%C5%8D%20Seki%20Takakazu)

In 1671 Sawaguchi Kazuyuki's Kokon Sanpōki ended with fifteen problems that seemed to require equations beyond the counting-board method of tengen jutsu, adopted from Zhu Shijie and Li Ye. Seki's Hatsubi Sanpō of 1674 solved all of them. Instead of rod numerals on a board, he wrote coefficients and unknowns on paper beside vertical lines, a notation later called bōsho-hō or endan-jutsu, which allowed equations with several unknowns and literal coefficients to be manipulated. Because it gave only the final equations without explanation, his pupil Takebe Katahiro published a commentary in 1685. It marks the beginning of a distinctively Japanese algebra.

### Katsuyō Sanpō (1712)

[![Katsuyō Sanpō](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Seki_Kowa_Katsuyo_Sampo_Bernoulli_numbers.png/1280px-Seki_Kowa_Katsuyo_Sampo_Bernoulli_numbers.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Seki_Kowa_Katsuyo_Sampo_Bernoulli_numbers.png)

<sub>Page of the Katsuyō Sanpō with the table of Bernoulli numbers Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Seki_Kowa_Katsuyo_Sampo_Bernoulli_numbers.png).</sub>

- **Date:** 1712
- **Type:** Mathematical treatise
- **Published / where:** Edo, Japan (published posthumously)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Katsuy%C5%8D%20Sanp%C5%8D%20Seki%20Takakazu)

Four years after Seki's death, his pupils Araki Murahide and Ōtaka Yoshimaro edited and printed his writings as Katsuyō Sanpō. It includes his work on sums of powers of integers, in which he gave a table of the coefficients now called Bernoulli numbers, printed a year before, and independently of, Jakob Bernoulli's Ars Conjectandi of 1713; work on regular polygons; and the calculation of π and arc length by series. His other manuscripts, circulated within the school, treat determinants, which he introduced in 1683, before Leibniz.
