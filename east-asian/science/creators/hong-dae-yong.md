## Biography

Hong Dae-yong came from a noble family and studied Neo-Confucianism, but turned to mathematics, music and astronomy, building a private observatory at his home. In 1765–66 he accompanied an embassy to Beijing, where he talked with Chinese scholars and visited the Jesuit astronomers and their observatory.

In his dialogue Uisan Mundap he argued that the earth is a rotating sphere, that there is no centre to an infinite universe, and that no nation can claim to be the centre of the world, a challenge to China-centred thinking. His mathematical book Juhae Suyong collected Chinese and Western methods. He is a leading figure of the Bukhak, or Northern Learning, school.

## Key works & inventions

### Uisan Mundap (c. 1766)

- **Date:** c. 1766
- **Type:** Philosophical and cosmological dialogue
- **Published / where:** Joseon Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Uisan%20Mundap%20Hong%20Dae-yong)

Written after Hong's journey to Beijing, the Uisan Mundap is a dialogue between a conventional scholar, Heo Ja, and a recluse, Silong, on Mount Yiwulü in Manchuria. Silong argues that the earth is a sphere that spins once a day, that there is no up and down in space, that the universe has no centre and holds countless worlds, and that since every country sees itself as the centre, the distinction between civilised China and barbarian others has no basis. Hong drew on Jesuit astronomy but went beyond it; the rotation of the earth was not taught by the Jesuits in China. It circulated in manuscript and became known widely only in the twentieth century.
