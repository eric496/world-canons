# Liu An

*劉安*

**c. 179 BC – 122 BC** · Chinese  
Born: Han China · Died: Shouchun, Huainan  
Schools & eras: [Yin-Yang & Han Cosmology](../movements/yin-yang-han.md)  

> King of Huainan, grandson of the founding Han emperor, who sponsored the Huainanzi, a grand synthesis of early Chinese thought.

## Biography

Liu An was a grandson of Liu Bang, founder of the Han dynasty, and inherited the kingdom of Huainan in 164 BC. At his court in Shouchun he gathered scholars, writers and practitioners of esoteric arts, and in 139 BC presented the book they had produced to his nephew, Emperor Wu. He was later accused of plotting rebellion and took his own life in 122 BC; legend instead says he ascended to heaven as an immortal, and credits him with inventing tofu.

The Huainanzi, compiled under his patronage, gathers cosmology, astronomy, geography, military strategy and political advice into a single framework grounded in the Dao and in yin-yang correlations. It is the key document of the Huang-Lao style of Daoist statecraft that flourished in the early Han, just before Emperor Wu's court turned to Confucian orthodoxy.

- **Date:** 139 BC
- **Type:** Syncretic treatise
- **Written / first published:** Court of Huainan; presented to Emperor Wu
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Huainanzi%20Liu%20An)

The Huainanzi ('Masters of Huainan') was written by scholars at the court of Liu An, King of Huainan, and presented to Emperor Wu in 139 BC. Its chapters move from the origin of the cosmos out of formless undifferentiation, through astronomy, topography and the seasonal ordinances, to human nature, military strategy, government and the uses of history, ending with a summary of the whole. It offers the ruler a comprehensive guide to ordering the world in tune with the Dao, drawing on the Laozi and Zhuangzi but also on Confucian, Mohist and Legalist sources. It is the most complete statement of early Han Huang-Lao thought and a treasure house of early Chinese myth, including the story of the old man at the frontier who lost his horse.
