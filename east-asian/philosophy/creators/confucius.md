## Biography

Kong Qiu, honoured as Kongzi or Kong Fuzi ('Master Kong'), was born in 551 BC into a family of minor nobility in the small state of Lu, in present-day Shandong. Orphaned young and poor, he worked in minor posts, educated himself in the rites and texts of the early Zhou, and gathered students from every background, an unusual step at the time. He may have held office in Lu around 500 BC; disappointed, he spent years travelling between states seeking a ruler who would put his ideas into practice, then returned home to teach. He died in 479 BC. Later tradition credited him with editing the Five Classics, a claim modern scholars treat with great caution.

Confucius taught that social order depends on moral cultivation: the junzi, or exemplary person, cultivates ren (humaneness), practises li (ritual propriety), and governs by virtue and example rather than force. Filial piety, the correct use of names and lifelong learning are recurring themes. Our main source, the Analects, was compiled by later generations of disciples, so the historical Confucius is partly a reconstruction. Under the Han his teaching became state orthodoxy, and through Neo-Confucianism it shaped government, family and schooling in China, Korea, Japan and Vietnam into the modern age.

### Spring and Autumn Annals (Chunqiu) (traditionally edited c. 480 BC)

[![Spring and Autumn Annals (Chunqiu)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6e/Spring_and_Autumn_Annals_annoted.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Spring_and_Autumn_Annals_annoted.JPG)

<sub>Annotated edition of the Spring and Autumn Annals Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Spring_and_Autumn_Annals_annoted.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** traditionally edited c. 480 BC
- **Type:** Chronicle
- **Written / first published:** State of Lu
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Spring%20and%20Autumn%20Annals%20%28Chunqiu%29%20Confucius)

The Chunqiu records, in entries of a few characters each, the events of the state of Lu over 242 years: accessions, deaths, treaties, battles and eclipses. Mencius claimed that Confucius made it, and that wrongdoers trembled at its verdicts, so it was read as a coded moral history in which the choice of a word passed judgement. Three commentaries grew up around it, the narrative Zuozhuan and the question-and-answer Gongyang and Guliang; Dong Zhongshu built his political theory on the Gongyang. Modern scholars doubt that Confucius had much hand in it, but it remained one of the Five Classics and gave its name to the Spring and Autumn period.

### Analects (Lunyu) (compiled c. 475–200 BC)

[![Analects (Lunyu)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/84/Rongo_Analects_02.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rongo_Analects_02.jpg)

<sub>Page of a Japanese edition of the Analects (Rongo) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rongo_Analects_02.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** compiled c. 475–200 BC
- **Type:** Recorded sayings
- **Written / first published:** Lu and other states; received text fixed in the Han
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Analects%20%28Lunyu%29%20Confucius)

The Analects is a collection of about five hundred brief passages in twenty 'books', mostly sayings of Confucius and exchanges with his students, compiled by successive generations of disciples. It sketches the exemplary person (junzi), who loves learning, practises ren (humaneness) and li (ritual propriety), serves parents with filial piety and governs by moral example; in one famous saying the Master describes his own life as a progression from setting his heart on learning at fifteen to following his heart's desire without transgressing at seventy. Scholars debate how the layers of the text were formed; a version on bamboo slips from a Han tomb at Dingzhou (buried 55 BC) is among the earliest witnesses. The Analects was memorised by schoolchildren throughout East Asia for two millennia, became one of Zhu Xi's Four Books, and is the most influential text on ethics in the region.
