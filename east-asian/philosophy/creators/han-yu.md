## Biography

Han Yu was orphaned young and raised by his elder brother's family. He passed the jinshi examination in 792 and had a stormy official career; in 819 his Memorial on the Bone of the Buddha, protesting the emperor's reverence for a relic, nearly cost him his life, and he was banished to Chaozhou in the far south. Recalled, he rose to vice-minister in the capital before his death in 824.

As a writer Han Yu led the 'ancient prose' movement, rejecting ornate parallel prose for the plain style of the classics; he is one of the Eight Masters of Tang and Song prose. His essay Yuan Dao argued that the true Way had been handed down from the sage-kings to Confucius and Mencius and then lost, a theory of the 'transmission of the Way' that Song Neo-Confucians adopted. He also raised Mencius and the Great Learning to the centre of Confucian learning.

## Key key texts

### On the Origin of the Way (Yuan Dao) (c. 805)

- **Date:** c. 805
- **Type:** Essay
- **Written / first published:** Tang China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20the%20Origin%20of%20the%20Way%20%28Yuan%20Dao%29%20Han%20Yu)

In Yuan Dao Han Yu defines the Way by its Confucian content, humaneness and righteousness, and attacks Buddhism and Daoism for withdrawing from family and state and for producing idle monks who live on others' labour. He quotes the Great Learning, then little regarded, to show that the Confucian path joins inner cultivation to ordering the world, and claims that the true Way was handed down from the sage-kings Yao and Shun through King Wen and the Duke of Zhou to Confucius and Mencius, after which it was lost. Song Neo-Confucians adopted this idea of the transmission of the Way (daotong) and placed themselves at its revival.
