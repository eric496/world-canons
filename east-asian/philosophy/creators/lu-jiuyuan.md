## Biography

Lu Jiuyuan, known as Xiangshan after the mountain where he taught, was the youngest of six brothers in a large Jiangxi family that lived communally. He passed the jinshi in 1172. At the Goose Lake meeting of 1175, arranged by Lü Zuqian, he and his brother debated Zhu Xi, accusing him of fragmenting learning into endless study. He taught crowds of students in the mountains and died in office as prefect of Jingmen in 1193.

For Lu, 'the universe is my mind, and my mind is the universe': principle is not to be sought outside in things or books but discovered by establishing the great foundation of one's own original mind, which Mencius had described. Study should serve moral self-realisation, not the reverse. Wang Yangming took up his teaching three centuries later, and the two names define the Lu-Wang school.

## Key key texts

### Goose Lake Debate and Letters of Lu Jiuyuan (1175–1193)

- **Date:** 1175–1193
- **Type:** Letters and recorded sayings
- **Written / first published:** Jiangxi; collected in the Complete Works of Lu Xiangshan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Goose%20Lake%20Debate%20and%20Letters%20of%20Lu%20Jiuyuan%20Lu%20Jiuyuan)

Lu Jiuyuan wrote no treatise; his thought survives in letters, poems, lectures and recorded sayings gathered after his death into the Complete Works of Lu Xiangshan. At the Goose Lake Temple in 1175 he and his brother recited poems contrasting their 'simple and easy' method of establishing the original mind with Zhu Xi's 'fragmented' study of texts. In letters of 1188–89 he argued with Zhu over the opening of Zhou Dunyi's diagram, claiming that 'non-polar' was Daoist. The sayings contain his declaration that the universe is his mind. These materials define the Lu side of the greatest debate in Neo-Confucianism.
