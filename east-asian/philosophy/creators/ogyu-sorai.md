# Ogyū Sorai

*荻生徂徠*

**1666 – 1728** · Japanese  
Born: Edo · Died: Edo  
Schools & eras: [Edo Confucianism](../movements/edo-confucianism.md)  

> The most influential Confucian of Edo Japan, who held that the Way consists of the rites and institutions created by the ancient kings.

<sub>Portrait: Edo-period portrait of Ogyū Sorai · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ogyuh_Sorai.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Ogyū Sorai was the son of a physician to the future shogun Tokugawa Tsunayoshi; when his father was exiled, Sorai grew up in rural Kazusa, where he observed the lives of farmers. Returning to Edo, he served from 1696 the powerful chamberlain Yanagisawa Yoshiyasu, and later advised the shogun Tokugawa Yoshimune on policy. He founded the Ken'en school and died in Edo in 1728.

Sorai's method of 'ancient words and phrases' (kobunjigaku) demanded reading the classics in the language of their own time. He concluded that the Way is not a moral principle in nature or the mind but the concrete system of ritual, music, law and government invented by the early kings to bring peace to the realm. This separation of politics from personal morality, and his practical proposals in works such as Seidan, made him a key figure in what the political scientist Maruyama Masao described as the emergence of modern political thought in Japan.

### Distinguishing the Way (Bendō) (1717)

- **Date:** 1717
- **Type:** Philosophical essay
- **Written / first published:** Edo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Distinguishing%20the%20Way%20%28Bend%C5%8D%29%20Ogy%C5%AB%20Sorai)

In the Bendō and its companion Benmei (Distinguishing Names), both completed in 1717, Sorai attacked Song Neo-Confucians and Itō Jinsai alike for misunderstanding the ancient language of the classics. The Way of the sages, he argued, is a collective name for the rites, music, punishments and government that the early kings invented to pacify the realm; it was not found in nature or in the mind but made, and its purpose is the peace and welfare of the people rather than personal cultivation. Individuals contribute by developing their particular talents in service of that order. The work is the key text of Sorai's school and of what Maruyama Masao read as the separation of politics from ethics in Tokugawa thought.

### Discourse on Government (Seidan) (c. 1726)

- **Date:** c. 1726
- **Type:** Policy memorial
- **Written / first published:** Edo; submitted to the shogun Tokugawa Yoshimune
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Discourse%20on%20Government%20%28Seidan%29%20Ogy%C5%AB%20Sorai)

Written privately for the shogun Tokugawa Yoshimune, the Seidan diagnoses the ills of Tokugawa society: samurai living like 'travellers in an inn' in castle towns, dependent on merchants and money, and a population drifting out of control. Sorai proposes returning samurai to the land, strict household registration, sumptuary laws and the reestablishment of institutions in the spirit of the ancient kings. The text circulated in manuscript and was printed only much later. It shows how Sorai's philosophy of the Way as institutions translated into concrete statecraft, and is a key source for Edo social history.
