# Mou Zongsan

*牟宗三*

**1909 – 1995** · Chinese  
Born: Qixia, Shandong · Died: Taipei  
Schools & eras: [New Confucianism](../movements/new-confucianism.md)  

> Leading New Confucian of the postwar era, who read Confucian moral metaphysics through and against Kant.

## Biography

Mou Zongsan studied philosophy at Peking University, where he became a disciple of Xiong Shili. He left the mainland in 1949 and taught in Taiwan and from 1960 in Hong Kong, at the University of Hong Kong and then New Asia College of the Chinese University of Hong Kong. In 1958 he co-signed, with Tang Junyi, Xu Fuguan and Zhang Junmai, a manifesto on Chinese culture addressed to the world. He died in Taipei in 1995.

Mou argued that Confucianism contains a 'moral metaphysics' in which the moral mind, through what he called intellectual intuition, grasps ultimate reality, something Kant had denied to human beings. He translated Kant's three Critiques into Chinese, reclassified the Song-Ming schools in his Mind and Nature, and wrote major studies of Wei-Jin and Buddhist thought. He is the most influential figure of contemporary New Confucianism.

### Mind and Nature (Xinti yu xingti) (1968–1969)

- **Date:** 1968–1969
- **Type:** Philosophical study
- **Written / first published:** Taipei: Zhengzhong shuju
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Mind%20and%20Nature%20%28Xinti%20yu%20xingti%29%20Mou%20Zongsan)

In this massive study Mou Zongsan reads the Song and Ming Confucians as builders of a 'moral metaphysics' in which the moral mind is identical with the creative reality of the universe. He argues that the main line ran through Zhou Dunyi, Zhang Zai, Cheng Hao, Hu Hong, Liu Zongzhou, and Lu Jiuyuan and Wang Yangming, while Cheng Yi and Zhu Xi, by separating principle from mind, made principle static and formed a 'side branch' that nonetheless became orthodoxy. The thesis has been much debated, but the work changed the study of Neo-Confucianism and is the centrepiece of Mou's philosophy.

### Phenomenon and the Thing-in-Itself (Xianxiang yu wuzishen) (1975)

- **Date:** 1975
- **Type:** Philosophical treatise
- **Written / first published:** Taipei: Student Book Co.
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Phenomenon%20and%20the%20Thing-in-Itself%20%28Xianxiang%20yu%20wuzishen%29%20Mou%20Zongsan)

Kant held that human beings know only phenomena, because only an infinite mind could have intellectual intuition of things in themselves. In this book Mou Zongsan argues that Confucian, Daoist and Buddhist traditions all claim that the human mind can become infinite, through the moral mind, the Daoist mind of emptiness or the Buddhist mind of wisdom, and so can know things in themselves. He builds a 'two-level ontology' of an attached, phenomenal level and an unattached, noumenal one, and describes how the moral mind 'self-negates' to create the cognitive subject needed for science and democracy. It is the core of Mou's mature system and the most discussed work of contemporary New Confucian philosophy.
