# Choe Je-u

*崔濟愚*

**1824 – 1864** · Korean  
Born: Gyeongju · Died: Daegu  
Schools & eras: [Reform & Enlightenment](../movements/modern-reform.md)  

> Founder of Donghak ('Eastern Learning'), which taught that Heaven dwells in every person and later inspired the peasant revolution of 1894.

## Biography

Choe Je-u, known as Suun, was born near Gyeongju to a declining yangban family; as the son of a remarried widow he was barred from office. After years of wandering and seeking, in 1860 he reported a revelation from the Lord of Heaven and began teaching Donghak, Eastern Learning, as a native answer to Western Learning, that is, Catholicism. The movement spread quickly among peasants, and the government arrested him as a heretic; he was executed in Daegu in 1864.

Choe taught that each person bears the Lord of Heaven within (sicheonju), a doctrine later developed by his successors into the principle that the human being is Heaven (innaecheon). Combining Confucian, Buddhist, Daoist and folk elements with a call for social renewal, Donghak fuelled the Donghak Peasant Revolution of 1894 and survives as the Cheondogyo religion.

## Key key texts

### Great Scripture of Eastern Learning (Donggyeong daejeon) (written 1860–1863; printed 1880)

- **Date:** written 1860–1863; printed 1880
- **Type:** Religious-philosophical scripture
- **Written / first published:** Gyeongju; compiled and printed by Choe Si-hyeong
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Great%20Scripture%20of%20Eastern%20Learning%20%28Donggyeong%20daejeon%29%20Choe%20Je-u)

The Donggyeong daejeon gathers the essays Choe Je-u wrote in classical Chinese between his revelation of 1860 and his arrest, among them 'Discussion of Learning' (Nonhak mun), which explains why his teaching is 'Eastern Learning' rather than Western Learning, and the twenty-one-character incantation. Its central idea is sicheonju, 'bearing the Lord of Heaven within', which implied the equal dignity of every person irrespective of class. His successor Choe Si-hyeong compiled and printed it in 1880, together with the vernacular songs of the Yongdam yusa. It is the scripture of Donghak and a key text of nineteenth-century Korean thought.
