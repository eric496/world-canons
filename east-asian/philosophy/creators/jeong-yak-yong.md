# Jeong Yak-yong

*丁若鏞*

**1762 – 1836** · Korean  
Born: Gwangju, Gyeonggi (now Namyangju) · Died: Namyangju  
Schools & eras: [Silhak (Practical Learning)](../movements/silhak.md)  

> Dasan, the great synthesiser of Silhak, who wrote some five hundred volumes on the classics, administration and reform during eighteen years of exile.

## Biography

Jeong Yak-yong, known by his pen name Dasan, passed the civil examination in 1789 and became a favourite of King Jeongjo, for whom he designed cranes and other devices used in building Hwaseong Fortress at Suwon. Through his family he came into contact with Catholic books from China. After Jeongjo's death the persecution of 1801 killed one of his brothers and sent Dasan into exile at Gangjin in the far south, where for eighteen years he studied and wrote with local students. Released in 1818, he spent his last years at his family home near Seoul.

Dasan reread the Confucian classics against Zhu Xi, treating human nature as a preference for the good rather than an implanted principle, and emphasising moral choice and a personal Heaven, perhaps under Catholic influence. His Mongmin simseo (Admonitions on Governing the People), Gyeongse yupyo and Heumheum sinseo set out detailed plans for fair administration, land reform and criminal justice. He is regarded as the culmination of Korean practical learning.

### Design for Good Government (Gyeongse yupyo) (1817)

- **Date:** 1817
- **Type:** Political treatise
- **Written / first published:** Gangjin, Jeolla (in exile)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Design%20for%20Good%20Government%20%28Gyeongse%20yupyo%29%20Jeong%20Yak-yong)

Originally titled 'Bangnye chobon', the Gyeongse yupyo uses the Rites of Zhou as a model for the complete reorganisation of the Joseon government: the structure of ministries, the examination and appointment of officials, a well-field land system adapted to Korean conditions, fair taxation and military reform. Jeong Yak-yong left it unfinished but it is the most ambitious institutional plan of the Silhak movement. With the Mongmin simseo on local government and the Heumheum sinseo on criminal justice, it forms the trilogy of Dasan's practical writings.

### Admonitions on Governing the People (Mongmin simseo) (1818)

- **Date:** 1818
- **Type:** Administrative treatise
- **Written / first published:** Gangjin, Jeolla (in exile)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Admonitions%20on%20Governing%20the%20People%20%28Mongmin%20simseo%29%20Jeong%20Yak-yong)

Written at the end of Jeong Yak-yong's eighteen years of exile in Gangjin, the Mongmin simseo is addressed to the local magistrates who directly governed the Korean people. Its twelve parts, from taking up office to leaving it, cover personal integrity, care for the elderly and destitute, taxes, corvée, justice, rites, the military, public works and famine relief, drawing on Chinese and Korean precedent and on Dasan's own observations of abuses by clerks and officials. Its title calls it the 'heart's book' because the exiled author could only write what he could not carry out. It became a classic of Korean public administration and is still read by officials today.
