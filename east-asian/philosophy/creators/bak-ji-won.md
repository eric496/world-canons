# Bak Ji-won

*朴趾源*

**1737 – 1805** · Korean  
Born: Hanyang (Seoul) · Died: Hanyang  
Schools & eras: [Silhak (Practical Learning)](../movements/silhak.md)  
Also in: [Literature](../../literature/creators/bak-ji-won.md)  

> Writer of the Northern Learning school who urged Joseon to learn technology and commerce from Qing China, and satirised the yangban class.

## Biography

Bak Ji-won, also romanised Pak Chiwŏn and known as Yeonam, came from a distinguished yangban family in Seoul but long declined to sit the examinations. In 1780 he accompanied a relative on an embassy to the Qing emperor's birthday celebrations, travelling to Beijing and the summer palace at Jehol (Chengde). His account of the journey, the Jehol Diary, made him famous. He later held provincial posts and died in 1805.

Bak and friends such as Bak Je-ga and Hong Dae-yong formed the 'Northern Learning' (Bukhak) circle, which argued that Korea should stop despising the Manchu Qing and adopt its carts, bricks, commerce and techniques for the people's welfare. His satirical stories, such as The Tale of a Yangban and Master Heo, mocked idle aristocrats and are classics of Korean literature.

## Key key texts

### Jehol Diary (Yeolha ilgi) (1780s)

- **Date:** 1780s
- **Type:** Travel diary and essays
- **Written / first published:** Written after the 1780 embassy to Beijing and Jehol
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jehol%20Diary%20%28Yeolha%20ilgi%29%20Bak%20Ji-won)

In 1780 Bak Ji-won travelled with a Joseon embassy to congratulate the Qianlong Emperor on his seventieth birthday, going to Beijing and then to the summer palace at Jehol (Rehe, now Chengde). His account, written in a lively, unorthodox classical style, describes Chinese carts, kilns, bricks, markets and buildings, conversations with Chinese scholars and Tibetan lamas, and argues that Korea should learn from the Qing rather than despising them as barbarians. It also contains the satirical tales 'Master Heo' and 'The Tiger's Rebuke'. Its style was condemned by King Jeongjo, but the book became the classic of the Northern Learning school and of Korean travel writing.
