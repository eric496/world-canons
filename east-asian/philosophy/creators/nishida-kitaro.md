# Nishida Kitarō

*西田幾多郎*

**1870 – 1945** · Japanese  
Born: Unoke, Ishikawa · Died: Kamakura  
Schools & eras: [Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md)  

> Founder of the Kyoto School, who joined Zen insight to Western philosophy in theories of pure experience and the logic of place.

## Biography

Nishida Kitarō was born in 1870 in a village near Kanazawa. At the Fourth Higher School he befriended D. T. Suzuki, and he studied philosophy at Tokyo Imperial University as a special student. For years he taught at schools in Kanazawa while practising Zen meditation intensively. An Inquiry into the Good (1911) made his name, and from 1910 he taught at Kyoto Imperial University, where he trained the thinkers later called the Kyoto School. He retired in 1928 and continued writing until his death in Kamakura in June 1945.

Nishida began from 'pure experience', the immediate state before the split between subject and object, and later developed the 'logic of basho' (place), in which beings are located ultimately in an 'absolute nothingness' that is not an object at all. His late essays on the 'contradictory self-identity' of the world and on religion engaged Hegel, Leibniz and Christianity alongside Buddhism. His writings on national culture in the 1930s and 1940s remain the subject of debate.

### An Inquiry into the Good (Zen no kenkyū) (1911)

- **Date:** 1911
- **Type:** Philosophical treatise
- **Written / first published:** Tokyo: Kōdōkan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=An%20Inquiry%20into%20the%20Good%20%28Zen%20no%20kenky%C5%AB%29%20Nishida%20Kitar%C5%8D)

Written while Nishida was teaching in Kanazawa and published in 1911, An Inquiry into the Good begins from pure experience, the direct awareness of, for example, a colour or a sound before one judges that 'I' perceive 'it'. From there Nishida argues that reality is a single self-developing system of consciousness, that the good is the realisation of the self's true personality in unity with that reality, and that religion is the deepest form of this unity, which he linked to God. He drew on William James, Wundt, Hegel and his own Zen practice. Often called the first original work of modern Japanese philosophy, it was widely read by students and remains Nishida's most accessible book.

### Place (Basho) (1926)

- **Date:** 1926
- **Type:** Philosophical essay
- **Written / first published:** Tokyo: in the journal Tetsugaku kenkyū
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Place%20%28Basho%29%20Nishida%20Kitar%C5%8D)

Published in 1926 and later included in From the Acting to the Seeing, 'Basho' turned Nishida from the psychology of experience to logic. Aristotle had defined substance as what is a subject and never a predicate; Nishida reverses the perspective and asks about the 'place' in which subjects and predicates, knower and known, are located. Physical things are located in a field of consciousness, which is in turn located in deeper places, until one reaches a place of 'absolute nothingness' that cannot itself be objectified. This logic of basho became the core of Nishida's mature philosophy and the defining idea of the Kyoto School.
