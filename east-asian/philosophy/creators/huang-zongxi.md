# Huang Zongxi

*黃宗羲*

**1610 – 1695** · Chinese  
Born: Yuyao, Zhejiang · Died: Yuyao  
Schools & eras: [Ming–Qing Critique & Evidential Learning](../movements/evidential-learning.md)  

> Ming loyalist scholar whose Waiting for the Dawn criticised autocracy, and who wrote the first great history of Ming philosophy.

<sub>Portrait: Traditional portrait of Huang Zongxi · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Huang_Zong-Xi.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Huang Zongxi's father, a censor, died in prison in 1626 after denouncing the eunuch Wei Zhongxian; the young Huang famously confronted his father's persecutors in court. He studied with Liu Zongzhou, the last great Ming Confucian, and after the Manchu conquest of 1644 fought for years in the Southern Ming resistance. Thereafter he lived as a private scholar, refusing invitations to serve the Qing.

In Waiting for the Dawn (Mingyi daifang lu, 1663) he argued that the ruler exists for the people, not the people for the ruler, and proposed restoring the chief minister's power, an independent role for schools as forums of public opinion, and law that serves the whole realm. His Records of Ming Scholars (Mingru xue'an) set a new standard for intellectual history. Late Qing reformers reprinted his political work as a native argument for constitutional government.

### Waiting for the Dawn (Mingyi daifang lu) (1663)

- **Date:** 1663
- **Type:** Political treatise
- **Written / first published:** Zhejiang
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Waiting%20for%20the%20Dawn%20%28Mingyi%20daifang%20lu%29%20Huang%20Zongxi)

Writing in 1663 after two decades of failed resistance to the Qing, Huang Zongxi set out a plan for a future ruler, the title alluding to a hexagram of the Book of Changes in which light is hidden, waiting to be called on. He argues that the ancients regarded the realm as master and the ruler as guest, but later rulers treated the realm as private property; that law should be for the whole realm, not for the dynasty; that the chief minister should be restored as a check on the throne; and that schools and academies should judge right and wrong for the court. Proposals on land, taxation, the military and eunuchs follow. Banned under the Qing, the book was printed by late Qing reformers as a Chinese charter of democratic ideas.

### Records of Ming Scholars (Mingru xue'an) (1676)

- **Date:** 1676
- **Type:** Intellectual history
- **Written / first published:** Zhejiang
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Records%20of%20Ming%20Scholars%20%28Mingru%20xue%27an%29%20Huang%20Zongxi)

Huang Zongxi's Mingru xue'an surveys more than two hundred Ming thinkers, grouping them by lineage and region, with a critical introduction to each school, a biography of each scholar and excerpts from his writings. It gives special attention to Wang Yangming and his followers, whom Huang divided into regional schools, and ends with his own teacher Liu Zongzhou. Huang aimed to show how each scholar had reached his own insight. The work created the genre of the 'scholarly case' history; Huang began a similar history of Song and Yuan Confucians that was completed by others, and the book remains the starting point for the study of Ming philosophy.
