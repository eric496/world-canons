## Biography

Li Zhi, also known as Zhuowu, was born in the port city of Quanzhou to a family with Muslim merchant ancestry. He passed the provincial examination and served for about twenty years in minor posts before resigning in 1580. He then lived in a Buddhist temple at Macheng in Hubei, shaved his head while refusing to become a monk, taught women as well as men, and wrote with scandalous frankness. Arrested in 1602 on charges of spreading dangerous ideas, he cut his own throat in prison.

Developing the left wing of Wang Yangming's school, Li Zhi held that the 'childlike mind' (tongxin), the genuine feeling one has before received opinion takes over, is the source of truth and good writing. He praised vernacular fiction and drama, reassessed historical villains, and defended self-interest as natural. His books were repeatedly banned but widely read, and modern Chinese and Japanese intellectuals rediscovered him as a critic of authority.

## Key key texts

### A Book to Burn (Fenshu) (1590)

- **Date:** 1590
- **Type:** Essays and letters
- **Written / first published:** Macheng, Hubei
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Book%20to%20Burn%20%28Fenshu%29%20Li%20Zhi)

Li Zhi called this collection A Book to Burn because, he wrote, it was bound to give offence. Published in 1590, it gathers his letters to friends and critics, notably the official Geng Dingxiang, together with essays and poems. In 'On the Childlike Mind' he argues that the genuine mind one has before received doctrines take over is the source of all good writing, and that the classics, compiled from fragments by disciples, cannot be taken as eternal truth; elsewhere he defends the abilities of women and praises vernacular fiction and drama. Banned after his death in 1602 and again under the Qing, it circulated nonetheless, and a sequel, A Book to Keep Hidden, contains his iconoclastic historical judgements.
