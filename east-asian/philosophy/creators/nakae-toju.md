## Biography

Nakae Tōju served as a retainer of the Ōzu domain on Shikoku but in 1634 abandoned his post, without permission, to return to Ōmi and care for his mother. There he lived as a village teacher, earning a reputation for sincerity that made him a legend as the 'sage of Ōmi'. He died young in 1648; among his students was Kumazawa Banzan.

Beginning as a follower of Zhu Xi, Tōju was drawn to Wang Yangming's teaching of innate knowing, which seemed to him to put sagehood within everyone's reach. He made filial piety a cosmic principle that unites the individual with the universe, and in his Okina mondō explained Confucian ethics in simple Japanese for ordinary people and for women as well as samurai.

## Key key texts

### Dialogue with an Old Man (Okina mondō) (1640s)

- **Date:** 1640s
- **Type:** Moral dialogue
- **Written / first published:** Ogawa, Ōmi
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dialogue%20with%20an%20Old%20Man%20%28Okina%20mond%C5%8D%29%20Nakae%20T%C5%8Dju)

In the Okina mondō a disciple questions an old teacher on the meaning of learning and the Way. Nakae Tōju answers that filial piety is not only duty to parents but the virtue that unites a person with heaven, earth and the 'Great Lord on High', the source of all life, and that learning means cultivating the mind rather than reading books. Written in plain Japanese and addressed to warriors, farmers and women alike, it adapts Confucian teaching to circumstances, a point Tōju stressed in the idea of acting according to time, place and status. Printed after his death, it made him one of the best-loved Confucians of Japan and an early exponent of Wang Yangming's learning.
