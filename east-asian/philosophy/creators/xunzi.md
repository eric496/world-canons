# Xunzi

*荀子*

**c. 310 BC – c. 235 BC** · Chinese  
Born: State of Zhao · Died: Lanling, State of Chu  
Schools & eras: [Classical Confucianism](../movements/classical-confucianism.md)  

> The most systematic of the early Confucians, who argued that human nature is bad and goodness comes from ritual, teachers and deliberate effort.

<sub>Portrait: Later imagined portrait of Xunzi · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Portrait_of_Xun_Zi.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Xun Kuang, honoured as Xunzi or Xun Qing, was born in the state of Zhao in the late fourth century BC. He was three times head of the libation ritual at the Jixia academy in Qi, the most famous gathering of scholars of the age, visited the state of Qin, and ended his career as magistrate of Lanling in Chu, where he died. Among his students were Han Fei and Li Si, the future chancellor of Qin.

Xunzi rejected Mencius: people are born with desires that, unchecked, lead to strife, and goodness is the product of conscious effort (wei) guided by ritual and teachers, as wood is straightened on a frame. He treated Heaven as the regular course of nature, not a moral will, and wrote on language, the correct use of names, music and the dangers of mental obsession. His careful essays were influential in the Han; later Neo-Confucians ranked him below Mencius, but modern scholars admire him as a philosopher.

### Xunzi (c. 3rd century BC)

[![Xunzi](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e6/Ancient_prose_from_the_Xunzi%2C_in_seal_script.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ancient_prose_from_the_Xunzi%2C_in_seal_script.jpg)

<sub>A passage from the Xunzi written in seal script Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ancient_prose_from_the_Xunzi%2C_in_seal_script.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 3rd century BC
- **Type:** Philosophical essays
- **Written / first published:** China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Xunzi%20Xunzi)

Unlike earlier Chinese texts, the Xunzi consists mainly of extended essays, many probably written by Xunzi himself. 'An Exhortation to Learning' opens with the image of indigo blue that is bluer than the plant it comes from; 'A Discussion of Heaven' treats heaven as the regular course of nature indifferent to human pleas; 'Rectifying Names' analyses how names are fixed by convention; 'Dispelling Obsession' warns against minds fixed on one side of a question; and the chapter claiming that human nature is bad argues that goodness is the product of deliberate effort. Liu Xiang edited the text in the first century BC and Yang Liang wrote its first commentary in 818. Long overshadowed by the Mencius, it is now valued as the most systematic work of classical Confucianism.

### Human Nature Is Bad (Xing'e) (c. 3rd century BC)

- **Date:** c. 3rd century BC
- **Type:** Philosophical essay (chapter 23 of the Xunzi)
- **Written / first published:** China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Human%20Nature%20Is%20Bad%20%28Xing%27e%29%20Xunzi)

In this chapter Xunzi argues that human beings are born loving profit and envying others, so that following their nature leads to strife; goodness is the result of wei, deliberate effort, as a warped board must be steamed and pressed to become straight. The sages created ritual and moral norms through such effort, and ordinary people can become sages by accumulating it. He examines Mencius's claims point by point. Although Neo-Confucians rejected it as heterodox, the chapter is one of the classic statements of the view that morality is a human construction, and it remains central to debates on Confucian ethics.
