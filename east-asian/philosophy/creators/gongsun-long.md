## Biography

Gongsun Long came from the state of Zhao and was a retainer of the Lord of Pingyuan, a prince of Zhao, in the third century BC. Anecdotes record him urging rulers toward disarmament and debating the Confucian Kong Chuan, a descendant of Confucius. He belonged to the bianzhe, the disputers, whom later historians grouped as the School of Names.

A short book in his name survives, though some chapters may be later. Its White Horse dialogue argues that 'white horse' and 'horse' do not pick out the same thing, since one names a colour and a shape and the other only a shape; other chapters treat hardness and whiteness as separable and discuss pointing and reference. Ancient critics dismissed such arguments as sophistry; modern scholars read them as serious, if contested, work in the philosophy of language.

## Key key texts

### White Horse Dialogue (Baima lun) (c. 3rd century BC)

- **Date:** c. 3rd century BC
- **Type:** Philosophical dialogue
- **Written / first published:** State of Zhao; preserved in the Gongsun Longzi
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=White%20Horse%20Dialogue%20%28Baima%20lun%29%20Gongsun%20Long)

The most famous chapter of the surviving Gongsun Longzi is a dialogue between an objector and a defender of the thesis that a white horse is not a horse. The defender argues that 'horse' names a shape and 'white' a colour, that if you ask for a horse a yellow or black one will do but not if you ask for a white horse, and so the two names cannot be the same. Interpreters have read the argument as a point about the logic of mass nouns, about classes and their members, about the meanings of compound terms, or as pure sophistry. Some scholars think the text was reworked after Gongsun Long's time. It is the best-known product of the School of Names and a touchstone in the debate over whether early China had logic.
