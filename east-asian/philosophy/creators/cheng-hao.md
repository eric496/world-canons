## Biography

Cheng Hao, known as Mingdao, studied with Zhou Dunyi as a boy together with his brother Cheng Yi. He passed the jinshi in 1057 and served as a local official and briefly at court, where he opposed the reforms of Wang Anshi. Students remembered his warmth and gentle manner; he died in 1085, shortly after the anti-reform party returned to power.

His sayings, preserved with his brother's in the Recorded Sayings of the two Chengs, describe ren (humaneness) as feeling oneself one body with all things, so that lacking humaneness is like numbness in one's limbs. He emphasised quiet sincerity and an intuitive grasp of principle over bookish investigation, which is why later scholars have seen in him a forerunner of the Lu-Wang school of mind as much as of Zhu Xi.

## Key key texts

### On Understanding Humaneness (Shiren pian) (c. 1080)

- **Date:** c. 1080
- **Type:** Recorded teaching
- **Written / first published:** Song China; preserved in the Recorded Sayings of the two Chengs
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20Understanding%20Humaneness%20%28Shiren%20pian%29%20Cheng%20Hao)

This short passage, recorded by Cheng Hao's disciple Lü Dalin, became the classic statement of the elder Cheng's thought. One who is humane forms one body with heaven, earth and all things; righteousness, propriety, wisdom and faithfulness are all aspects of this humaneness. Once one has understood it, one needs only to preserve it with sincerity and reverence, without anxious searching or forcing. Cheng Hao elsewhere compares lack of humaneness to paralysis of the limbs, which do not feel as part of oneself. The text was a touchstone for later scholars of the School of Mind and is often contrasted with Cheng Yi's emphasis on investigation.
