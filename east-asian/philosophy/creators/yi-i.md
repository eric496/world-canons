# Yi I

*李珥*

**1536 – 1584** · Korean  
Born: Gangneung, Gangwon · Died: Hanyang (Seoul)  
Schools & eras: [Korean Neo-Confucianism](../movements/korean-neo-confucianism.md)  

> Yulgok, statesman and the other great Joseon Neo-Confucian, who held that only material force issues and principle rides upon it.

## Biography

Yi I, known as Yulgok, was born in 1536 in Gangneung; his mother Sin Saimdang was a celebrated painter and poet. After her death he spent a year in a Buddhist monastery, then returned to Confucianism, and is said to have taken first place in nine civil examinations. He held high office, including minister of personnel and of war, and pressed for reforms of taxation and the army. He died in 1584. Mother and son both appear on modern Korean banknotes.

Yulgok answered Toegye that principle does not itself move: in every feeling material force issues and principle 'rides' on it, so that the four moral feelings are the good part of the seven. His Essentials of the Studies of the Sages (Seonghak jibyo), presented to King Seonjo in 1575, reorganises Neo-Confucian learning for a ruler, and his Essential Secrets for Removing Ignorance became a standard primer. His followers formed the Giho school, rivals of Toegye's.

### Letters to Seong Hon on Principle and Material Force (1572)

- **Date:** 1572
- **Type:** Philosophical correspondence
- **Written / first published:** Paju and Hanyang
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Letters%20to%20Seong%20Hon%20on%20Principle%20and%20Material%20Force%20Yi%20I)

In 1572 Seong Hon (Ugye) wrote to his friend Yi I asking whether Toegye's view of the four beginnings and seven emotions was correct. In nine replies Yulgok argued that there is only one way in which feelings arise: material force issues and principle rides upon it. The four beginnings are not a separate source but the good part of the seven emotions; principle is universal and pervasive, while qi is particular and limiting, a formula (igi tong guk) that summed up his philosophy. The letters founded the Giho school of Yulgok's followers, which debated Toegye's Yeongnam school for the rest of the Joseon dynasty.

### Essentials of the Studies of the Sages (Seonghak jibyo) (1575)

- **Date:** 1575
- **Type:** Anthology with commentary
- **Written / first published:** Hanyang; presented to King Seonjo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Essentials%20of%20the%20Studies%20of%20the%20Sages%20%28Seonghak%20jibyo%29%20Yi%20I)

Yi I presented the Seonghak jibyo to King Seonjo in 1575 as a more practical and Korean alternative to the Chinese Extended Meaning of the Great Learning by Zhen Dexiu. In five parts, following the structure of the Great Learning, it gathers passages from the classics and the Song masters with Yulgok's commentary, moving from the ruler's self-cultivation, through the regulation of his family, to governing and the transmission of the Way. It reflects Yulgok's conviction that moral learning must issue in institutional reform, and became a standard text in the education of later Joseon kings.

### Essential Secrets for Removing Ignorance (Gyeongmong yogyeol) (1577)

- **Date:** 1577
- **Type:** Primer
- **Written / first published:** Haeju, Hwanghae
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Essential%20Secrets%20for%20Removing%20Ignorance%20%28Gyeongmong%20yogyeol%29%20Yi%20I)

Written in 1577 while Yi I was living in Haeju, the Gyeongmong yogyeol was intended for young students who had just begun to learn. In ten chapters it urges them to set their will on becoming sages, to change old habits, to keep their bodies and minds in order, to read the classics in a prescribed sequence, and to serve their parents, conduct funerals and sacrifices, manage the household and deal with others correctly. Simple and practical, it became one of the most widely used primers in Joseon schools, alongside the Elementary Learning. An autograph manuscript with an inkstone associated with Yulgok is preserved at Ojukheon in Gangneung.
