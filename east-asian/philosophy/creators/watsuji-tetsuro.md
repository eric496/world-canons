# Watsuji Tetsurō

*和辻哲郎*

**1889 – 1960** · Japanese  
Born: Himeji, Hyōgo · Died: Tokyo  
Schools & eras: [Kyoto School](../movements/kyoto-school.md)  

> Philosopher of ethics and culture who described human existence as 'betweenness' and wrote on climate, Japanese art and Dōgen.

## Biography

Watsuji Tetsurō was the son of a country doctor near Himeji. He studied philosophy at Tokyo Imperial University and wrote early books on Nietzsche and Kierkegaard, then turned to Japanese culture with a study of the temples of Nara (Koji junrei, 1919) and an essay that rescued Dōgen for modern readers. He taught at Kyoto Imperial University from 1925 and held the chair of ethics at Tokyo from 1934 to 1949. His studies in Germany in 1927–28 brought him into contact with Heidegger's Being and Time.

Against Heidegger's focus on the individual and time, Watsuji argued in Fūdo (1935) that human beings exist in space and climate, and in his three-volume Ethics (1937–49) that a person (ningen) is fundamentally 'between' people, an individual and a social being at once. His works on Japanese ethical thought and culture were widely read; his accounts of the emperor and the state remain contested.

### Climate and Culture (Fūdo) (1935)

- **Date:** 1935
- **Type:** Philosophical anthropology
- **Written / first published:** Tokyo: Iwanami Shoten
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Climate%20and%20Culture%20%28F%C5%ABdo%29%20Watsuji%20Tetsur%C5%8D)

Fūdo grew out of Watsuji's sea voyage to Europe in 1927 and his reading of Heidegger's Being and Time, which he felt neglected the spatial and social dimension of existence. Human beings, he argued, discover themselves through fūdo, the climate and environment in which they live; the cold we feel is not merely a physical state but a way in which we already exist together outside ourselves. He distinguishes three types: the monsoon of South and East Asia, receptive and resigned; the desert, combative and submissive to a single god; and the European meadow, regular and rational, with a special analysis of Japan. The typology has been criticised as essentialist, but the book founded a philosophy of environment and is still widely read.

### Ethics (Rinrigaku) (1937–1949)

- **Date:** 1937–1949
- **Type:** Philosophical treatise
- **Written / first published:** Tokyo: Iwanami Shoten
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ethics%20%28Rinrigaku%29%20Watsuji%20Tetsur%C5%8D)

Watsuji built his ethics on the Japanese word ningen, written with characters meaning 'person' and 'between', to argue that a human being is at once an individual and a member of relationships, and that ethics is the study of the 'betweenness' (aidagara) in which we exist. Individuality arises by negating the community and the community by negating the individual, in a continuing double negation that ultimately expresses emptiness. The volumes trace this structure through family, kin, local community, economy, culture and the state. Its account of the state was criticised after the war and revised by Watsuji; the work is the most systematic ethical philosophy of modern Japan.
