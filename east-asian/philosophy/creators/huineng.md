# Huineng

*慧能*

**638 – 713** · Chinese  
Born: Xinzhou (Guangdong) · Died: Caoxi, Shaozhou  
Schools & eras: [Chan, Seon & Zen](../movements/chan-zen.md)  

> The Sixth Patriarch of Chan, hero of the Platform Sutra and the doctrine of sudden awakening; how much of his story is history is debated.

<sub>Portrait: Liang Kai (attributed), The Sixth Patriarch Cutting Bamboo, Southern Song, Tokyo National Museum · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Sixth_Patriarch_Cutting_the_Bamboo.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

According to the Platform Sutra, Huineng was an illiterate woodcutter from the far south who heard the Diamond Sutra recited in the market, walked north to the monastery of the Fifth Patriarch Hongren, and was put to work pounding rice. When Hongren asked his monks for a verse expressing their understanding, Huineng's reply outshone that of the senior monk Shenxiu, and Hongren secretly passed him the robe of succession. He later taught for decades at Caoxi in Guangdong and died in 713.

Scholars since Hu Shi have shown that this story was shaped by the polemics of Huineng's self-proclaimed heir Shenhui, who in the 730s attacked Shenxiu's 'Northern School' as gradualist; the earliest version of the Platform Sutra, found at Dunhuang, dates from after that controversy. Whatever the historical Huineng taught, the figure of the Sixth Patriarch came to embody Chan's message: Buddha-nature is present in everyone, awakening is sudden, and meditation and wisdom are one. All later Chan, Seon and Zen lineages trace themselves to him.

## Key key texts

### Platform Sutra of the Sixth Patriarch (Liuzu tanjing) (c. 780 (Dunhuang version))

[![Platform Sutra of the Sixth Patriarch (Liuzu tanjing)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Double_page_from_the_Korean_print_of_%22The_Sixth_Patriarch%27s_Dharma_Jewel_Platform_Sutra%22.jpg/1280px-Double_page_from_the_Korean_print_of_%22The_Sixth_Patriarch%27s_Dharma_Jewel_Platform_Sutra%22.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Double_page_from_the_Korean_print_of_%22The_Sixth_Patriarch%27s_Dharma_Jewel_Platform_Sutra%22.jpg)

<sub>Double page from a Korean woodblock print of the Platform Sutra Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Double_page_from_the_Korean_print_of_%22The_Sixth_Patriarch%27s_Dharma_Jewel_Platform_Sutra%22.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 780 (Dunhuang version)
- **Type:** Sermon and autobiography
- **Written / first published:** China; earliest manuscript from Dunhuang
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Platform%20Sutra%20of%20the%20Sixth%20Patriarch%20%28Liuzu%20tanjing%29%20Huineng)

The Platform Sutra presents itself as Huineng's sermon from a platform at Dafan temple in Shaozhou, beginning with his autobiography: the illiterate woodcutter, the verse contest with Shenxiu, and the secret transmission of the robe. It teaches that the original nature is pure, that meditation and wisdom are one as a lamp and its light, and that awakening is sudden. It is the only Chinese Buddhist text to be called a sutra, a title otherwise reserved for words of the Buddha. The earliest text, found at Dunhuang, dates from around 780 and shows the influence of Shenhui's campaign for Southern Chan; later editions, especially Zongbao's of 1291, expanded it greatly. It became the founding scripture of Chan, Seon and Zen.
