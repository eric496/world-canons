## Biography

Meng Ke, known as Mengzi, was born around 372 BC in the small state of Zou, near Confucius's home. Tradition says his widowed mother moved house three times to give him a good environment, and that he studied with followers of Confucius's grandson Zisi. Like Confucius he travelled among the courts of the Warring States, holding a post at Qi and debating with King Hui of Liang (Wei) and King Xuan of Qi, before retiring to teach. The book named after him records these conversations.

Mencius argued that everyone has four 'sprouts' of goodness, compassion, shame, deference and the sense of right and wrong, which cultivation can grow into full virtue; he illustrates the point with a child about to fall into a well. He defended Confucian ethics against Mozi's impartial love and Yang Zhu's egoism, and taught that a ruler who abuses the people forfeits Heaven's mandate. Neo-Confucians made his book one of the Four Books, and his view of human nature became Confucian orthodoxy.

## Key key texts

### Mencius (Mengzi) (c. 300 BC)

[![Mencius (Mengzi)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f6/Jiatai_Era_Mencius_title_page.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jiatai_Era_Mencius_title_page.jpg)

<sub>Title page of a Southern Song edition of the Mencius, Jiatai era (1201–04) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jiatai_Era_Mencius_title_page.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 300 BC
- **Type:** Dialogues and sayings
- **Written / first published:** China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Mencius%20%28Mengzi%29%20Mencius)

The Mencius, in seven books each divided in two, records the philosopher's audiences with rulers, debates with rivals and sayings; it may have been compiled by his disciples, perhaps with his participation. It contains the classic arguments for the goodness of human nature: anyone who suddenly sees a child about to fall into a well feels alarm and compassion, the 'sprout' of humaneness. Mencius debates the philosopher Gaozi on whether nature is like water that can flow either way, mocks the farmer of Song who pulled his sprouts to help them grow, and justifies removing a tyrant. Zhu Xi made it one of the Four Books, and its political ideas were cited by reformers and critics of autocracy into the modern age.
