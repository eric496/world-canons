# Hu Shi

*胡適*

**1891 – 1962** · Chinese  
Born: Shanghai · Died: Taipei  
Schools & eras: [Reform & Enlightenment](../movements/modern-reform.md)  

> May Fourth liberal and student of John Dewey who championed vernacular literature, pragmatism and the scientific study of China's past.

## Biography

Hu Shi studied at Cornell and then at Columbia under John Dewey. In 1917 his essay proposing reforms of literature, published in New Youth, helped launch the vernacular literature movement, and the same year he became a professor at Peking University. He was a leading voice of the New Culture and May Fourth era, served as Chinese ambassador to the United States from 1938 to 1942 and as president of Peking University after the war, and from 1958 headed the Academia Sinica in Taipei, where he died in 1962.

Hu applied Dewey's experimental method to Chinese thought: 'bold hypotheses, careful verification'. His Outline of the History of Chinese Philosophy (1919) was the first modern history of the subject, treating the ancient masters as philosophers rather than sages, and his research on the Dunhuang manuscripts reshaped the history of Chan. He argued for gradual liberal reform against both revolutionary Marxism and cultural conservatism.

## Key key texts

### Outline of the History of Chinese Philosophy (Zhongguo zhexue shi dagang) (1919)

- **Date:** 1919
- **Type:** History of philosophy
- **Written / first published:** Shanghai: Commercial Press
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Outline%20of%20the%20History%20of%20Chinese%20Philosophy%20%28Zhongguo%20zhexue%20shi%20dagang%29%20Hu%20Shi)

Based on Hu Shi's Columbia dissertation and lectures at Peking University, this first volume, covering the period up to the Qin, began not with the legendary sage-kings but with the Book of Odes as historical evidence, and treated Confucius, Mozi, the later Mohists and Zhuangzi as philosophers whose arguments could be analysed and compared with Western logic. It gave special attention to logical method and to the Mohist canons. Cai Yuanpei's preface praised its critical approach. Hu never completed the later volumes, but the book established the discipline of Chinese philosophy in modern universities, and its method was soon followed and challenged by Feng Youlan.
