## Biography

Dong Zhongshu was an expert on the Gongyang commentary to the Spring and Autumn Annals, appointed an erudite under Emperor Jing. In about 134 BC he answered Emperor Wu's questions with three famous memorials, recommending that the state promote the Confucian classics alone; the imperial academy for the Five Classics founded soon after in 124 BC is often linked to his advice. He served as minister to two kingdoms and later retired to write.

Dong developed a theory of resonance between Heaven and humanity, in which the emperor mediates between the cosmos and the people, and floods, eclipses and other portents are Heaven's warnings against misrule. He read the Spring and Autumn Annals as a coded set of judgements by Confucius. The Chunqiu fanlu, transmitted under his name, is partly of later date. His synthesis shaped imperial Confucianism for centuries.

- **Date:** 2nd century BC (partly later)
- **Type:** Political-cosmological treatise
- **Written / first published:** Han China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Luxuriant%20Dew%20of%20the%20Spring%20and%20Autumn%20Annals%20%28Chunqiu%20fanlu%29%20Dong%20Zhongshu)

The Chunqiu fanlu consists of eighty-two chapters, three of them lost, attributed to Dong Zhongshu, though scholars think parts were written by his followers in later centuries. The first section interprets the Spring and Autumn Annals in the Gongyang tradition, finding in its wording Confucius's judgements on rulers; the rest develops a cosmology in which Heaven, Earth and humanity correspond, the ruler links the three as the character for king (wang) links three horizontal strokes, yin and yang and the Five Phases govern the seasons and the state, and natural anomalies signal Heaven's displeasure. The book became the main source for Han imperial Confucianism and for later theories of the mandate of Heaven.
