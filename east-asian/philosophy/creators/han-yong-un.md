# Han Yong-un

*韓龍雲*

**1879 – 1944** · Korean  
Born: Hongseong, Chungcheong · Died: Seoul  
Schools & eras: [Reform & Enlightenment](../movements/modern-reform.md)  

> Buddhist monk, reformer and poet, a signer of the 1919 Declaration of Independence, who called for a modern, socially engaged Korean Buddhism.

## Biography

Han Yong-un, known by his Buddhist name Manhae, took part as a youth in the turmoil of the 1890s and became a monk at Baekdamsa in the Seorak mountains around 1905. He travelled in Japan, where he studied Buddhism and observed its modernisation, and opposed plans to merge Korean Buddhism with a Japanese sect. One of the thirty-three signatories of the March First Declaration of Independence of 1919, he was imprisoned for three years. He died in Seoul in 1944, the year before liberation.

In On the Reform of Korean Buddhism (1913) he called for Buddhism to leave the mountains for the cities, educate its monks, allow them to marry, and serve society, drawing on ideas of equality and freedom. His poetry collection The Silence of Love (1926) is a classic of modern Korean literature.

## Key key texts

### On the Reform of Korean Buddhism (Joseon bulgyo yusin non) (1913)

- **Date:** 1913
- **Type:** Reform treatise
- **Written / first published:** Seoul (written at Baekdamsa, 1910)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20the%20Reform%20of%20Korean%20Buddhism%20%28Joseon%20bulgyo%20yusin%20non%29%20Han%20Yong-un)

Written in 1910 and published in 1913, the treatise argues that Buddhism's core values are equality and saving all beings, which accord with modern ideas of freedom and progress, but that Korean Buddhism had decayed in remote mountain monasteries under centuries of Joseon repression. Han calls for modern education of monks, simplified rituals, the relocation of temples to cities, the abolition of superstitious practices, economic self-reliance and, controversially, permission for monks to marry. Influenced by Liang Qichao's writings and by Japanese Buddhist modernisation, it is the founding document of modern Korean Buddhist reform.
