# Kang Youwei

*康有為*

**1858 – 1927** · Chinese  
Born: Nanhai, Guangdong · Died: Qingdao  
Schools & eras: [Reform & Enlightenment](../movements/modern-reform.md)  

> Reformer who reinterpreted Confucius as a prophet of progress, led the Hundred Days' Reform of 1898, and imagined a utopian 'Great Unity'.

## Biography

Kang Youwei was born into a scholar-official family near Guangzhou. Visits to British Hong Kong and treaty-port Shanghai convinced him that China must reform, and he began petitioning the throne in 1888. With his student Liang Qichao he organised examination candidates in 1895 to protest the treaty ending the war with Japan. In 1898 the Guangxu Emperor adopted his programme in the Hundred Days' Reform, until Empress Dowager Cixi's coup ended it; Kang fled abroad and spent sixteen years in exile, founding the Protect the Emperor Society. After 1912 he remained a monarchist and supported the brief restoration of 1917.

Kang read the Confucian classics through the New Text tradition, arguing that the 'Old Text' versions were forgeries and that Confucius had been a reformer who invented the ideal antiquity to justify change. History, he held, moves through three ages toward Great Peace. His Book of the Great Unity (Datong shu) imagined a future world government without nations, classes, private property or the family, a vision so radical he kept it largely unpublished in his lifetime.

### A Study of Confucius as a Reformer (Kongzi gaizhi kao) (1897)

- **Date:** 1897
- **Type:** Classical scholarship
- **Written / first published:** Shanghai
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Study%20of%20Confucius%20as%20a%20Reformer%20%28Kongzi%20gaizhi%20kao%29%20Kang%20Youwei)

Building on his earlier attack on the Old Text classics as forgeries by the Han scholar Liu Xin, Kang Youwei argued in this book that Confucius, like the other masters of the Warring States, had created an idealised antiquity of sage-kings in order to justify changing the institutions of his own day. Confucius thus became a prophet of progress and a founder of a religion, and Kang read the Gongyang theory of three ages as a forecast of advance toward democracy. Published on the eve of the 1898 reforms, the book was banned and its printing blocks burned; it shook traditional confidence in the classics and opened the way to the 'doubting antiquity' movement of the 1920s.

### Book of the Great Unity (Datong shu) (drafted c. 1884–1902; published 1935)

- **Date:** drafted c. 1884–1902; published 1935
- **Type:** Utopian treatise
- **Written / first published:** Written partly in exile in India; published posthumously in Shanghai
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Book%20of%20the%20Great%20Unity%20%28Datong%20shu%29%20Kang%20Youwei)

Kang Youwei claimed to have begun this book in 1884 and wrote most of it in Darjeeling around 1901–02; parts were published in 1913 but the whole appeared only in 1935, after his death. Inspired by the Book of Rites' account of an age of Great Unity, Buddhist compassion and Western utopian thought, it surveys the sufferings of humanity and traces them to boundaries: of nations, classes, races, sexes, families, property and species. In the coming age, Kang predicts, these will dissolve: a world parliament will govern, marriage will be replaced by renewable contracts, children raised in public institutions and the dead cremated in public. Mao Zedong cited the book, noting that Kang had not found the road to the Great Unity.
