## Biography

Gu Yanwu was born to a gentry family in Kunshan near Suzhou. After the Manchu conquest his adoptive mother starved herself to death rather than live under the Qing, enjoining him never to serve the new dynasty, and he took part in the resistance. From 1657 he travelled for over twenty years across northern China, studying geography, inscriptions, economics and defence, and refused all offers of office.

Gu blamed the fall of the Ming on scholars who chattered about mind and nature while the state decayed, and called for learning that is broad, grounded in evidence and useful to the world. His Record of Daily Knowledge and his studies of ancient phonology made textual evidence the measure of interpretation and are regarded as the starting point of Qing kaozheng scholarship. A line paraphrased from him, that every ordinary person bears responsibility for the fate of the realm, became a modern patriotic slogan.

## Key key texts

### Record of Daily Knowledge (Rizhi lu) (1670; expanded to 1682)

- **Date:** 1670; expanded to 1682
- **Type:** Scholarly notes
- **Written / first published:** Northern China; first printed 1670
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Record%20of%20Daily%20Knowledge%20%28Rizhi%20lu%29%20Gu%20Yanwu)

Gu Yanwu took his title from the Analects phrase about knowing each day what one lacks. Over some thirty years he recorded findings on the classics, government, economics, geography, rites and language, testing each claim against texts, inscriptions and his own observation during his travels. The notes show his conviction that learning must be grounded in evidence and useful to the world, and they contain his famous distinction between the fall of a dynasty and the fall of 'all under heaven', the moral order for which everyone is responsible. Published in an expanded thirty-two-chapter edition after his death, it was the model for Qing kaozheng scholarship.
