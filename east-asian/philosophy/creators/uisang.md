## Biography

Uisang was born into an aristocratic family of Silla and became a monk in his youth. With Wonhyo he set out for China, and in 661 he reached Tang, where he studied for nearly ten years with the Huayan master Zhiyan alongside Fazang, who remained his friend and corresponded with him. Returning around 670, he founded Buseoksa and other temples as centres of Hwaeom teaching and trained many disciples. He died in 702.

His one major surviving work, the Diagram of the Dharma Realm of the One Vehicle of Hwaeom (668), arranges a poem of 210 characters along a single path that winds through a square seal, showing that 'one is all and all is one'. Uisang's austere, practice-centred Hwaeom became the dominant doctrinal tradition of Korean Buddhism.

## Key key texts

### Diagram of the Dharma Realm of the One Vehicle of Hwaeom (Hwaeom ilseung beopgye do) (668)

- **Date:** 668
- **Type:** Diagram-poem with commentary
- **Written / first published:** Tang China (Zhixiang monastery, Mount Zhongnan); presented to Zhiyan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Diagram%20of%20the%20Dharma%20Realm%20of%20the%20One%20Vehicle%20of%20Hwaeom%20%28Hwaeom%20ilseung%20beopgye%20do%29%20Uisang)

Uisang composed this work in 668 while studying with Zhiyan in China and presented it to his teacher shortly before Zhiyan's death. It is a poem of thirty lines of seven characters, arranged on a single red line that winds through a square grid in fifty-four turns, beginning and ending at the centre with the character for dharma and the character for Buddha. Its verses state that the dharma-nature is perfectly interfused, that one is in all and all is in one, and that a single mote of dust contains the ten directions. Uisang added an explanatory commentary. Recited and copied in Korean monasteries ever since, the diagram is the founding document of Korean Hwaeom and a striking union of poetry, image and philosophy.
