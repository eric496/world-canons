# Shinran

## Biography

Shinran trained for twenty years as a Tendai monk on Mount Hiei, then in 1201 became a disciple of Hōnen, who taught reliance on the recitation of Amida Buddha's name. When Hōnen's movement was suppressed in 1207 Shinran was laicised and exiled to Echigo; he married, and later spent some twenty years teaching in the Kantō region before returning to Kyoto, where he wrote his major works and died in 1263.

Shinran radicalised Hōnen's teaching: since no one can attain awakening by their own power, salvation depends wholly on the 'other power' of Amida's vow, received in the moment of entrusting (shinjin), and even recitation is an expression of gratitude, not a means. The famous claim that if even good people are saved, how much more the wicked, appears in the Tannishō. His honest reflection on human limitation has attracted modern philosophers, notably Tanabe Hajime.

### Teaching, Practice, Faith and Realisation (Kyōgyōshinshō) (c. 1224–1247)

[![Teaching, Practice, Faith and Realisation (Kyōgyōshinshō)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/41/Kyogyoshinsho.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kyogyoshinsho.jpg)

<sub>Manuscript of the Kyōgyōshinshō Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kyogyoshinsho.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1224–1247
- **Type:** Doctrinal treatise
- **Written / first published:** Kantō and Kyoto
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Teaching%2C%20Practice%2C%20Faith%20and%20Realisation%20%28Ky%C5%8Dgy%C5%8Dshinsh%C5%8D%29%20Shinran)

The six-fascicle Kyōgyōshinshō, properly Ken jōdo shinjitsu kyōgyōshō monrui, was begun, according to the True Pure Land tradition, around 1224 in the Kantō and revised until late in Shinran's life. It assembles passages from sutras and from Indian, Chinese and Japanese masters, with Shinran's interpretations, to show that the true teaching is the Larger Sutra of Immeasurable Life, the true practice is saying Amida's name, the true faith is the entrusting given by Amida, and the true realisation is birth in the Pure Land and nirvana. Shinran rereads classical Chinese passages so that the agent of faith becomes Amida rather than the believer. An autograph manuscript at Higashi Honganji is a National Treasure. It is the foundational text of Jōdo Shinshū, the largest Buddhist denomination in Japan.

### A Record in Lament of Divergences (Tannishō) (c. 1290)

- **Date:** c. 1290
- **Type:** Recorded sayings
- **Written / first published:** Compiled by Yuien
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Record%20in%20Lament%20of%20Divergences%20%28Tannish%C5%8D%29%20Shinran)

The Tannishō was written about thirty years after Shinran's death, probably by his disciple Yuien, who 'laments' the divergences from his master's teaching that had arisen among followers. Its first part records sayings of Shinran in vivid Japanese, including his declaration that he does not know whether the nembutsu leads to the Pure Land or to hell, and the paradox that if even good people attain birth, how much more the evil person; the second part corrects specific errors. The fifteenth-century Shin leader Rennyo restricted access to it; rediscovered in the Meiji era by Kiyozawa Manshi, it became one of the most read religious texts of modern Japan and attracted philosophers such as Nishida and Tanabe.
