<sub>Portrait: Fazang as a Huayan patriarch, Japanese print, 13th century · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fazang%2C_buddhist_Monk%2C_Japanese_print%2C_13th_century.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Fazang was born in Chang'an to a family of Sogdian descent and studied with the Huayan master Zhiyan. He also joined Xuanzang's translation bureau for a time and later worked on the new translation of the Avatamsaka (Huayan) Sutra sponsored by Empress Wu Zetian, who gave him the title Xianshou. He lectured on the sutra many times, enjoyed the patronage of several Tang rulers, and died in 712.

Fazang built the Huayan doctrine of the dharma-realm of unobstructed interpenetration: every phenomenon depends on and contains every other, as each jewel in Indra's net reflects all the others. He made the idea concrete for the empress with a golden lion, in which gold and lion shape are inseparable, and, in another story, with mirrors surrounding a Buddha image. His classification of teachings and his metaphysics of whole and part influenced Chan, Korean Hwaeom through his friend Uisang, and Song Neo-Confucianism.

### Treatise on the Five Teachings of Huayan (Huayan wujiao zhang) (c. 670s)

- **Date:** c. 670s
- **Type:** Doctrinal treatise
- **Written / first published:** Chang'an
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Treatise%20on%20the%20Five%20Teachings%20of%20Huayan%20%28Huayan%20wujiao%20zhang%29%20Fazang)

In this treatise Fazang ranks the Buddhist teachings into five levels, from the Hīnayāna through early and advanced Mahāyāna and the sudden teaching to the perfect teaching of the Avatamsaka Sutra, and expounds the latter's metaphysics: the six characteristics of whole and part, the ten mysterious gates of dependent origination, and the interpenetration of all things in the dharma-realm. It gave Huayan its definitive doctrinal form, was studied in Korean Hwaeom and Japanese Kegon, and is one of the most demanding works of East Asian Buddhist philosophy.

### Treatise on the Golden Lion (Jin shizi zhang) (c. 700)

- **Date:** c. 700
- **Type:** Philosophical essay
- **Written / first published:** Chang'an
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Treatise%20on%20the%20Golden%20Lion%20%28Jin%20shizi%20zhang%29%20Fazang)

According to tradition, when Empress Wu Zetian found the Huayan teaching hard to follow, Fazang pointed to a golden lion guarding the palace. The gold stands for principle, emptiness without fixed nature, and the lion shape for phenomena arising through conditions; neither exists without the other, and every hair of the lion contains the whole lion and all its other hairs. In ten brief sections the essay runs through the main Huayan doctrines, the six characteristics, the ten mysteries and the interpenetration of principle and phenomena. Its brevity made it the most widely read introduction to Huayan, and Song Neo-Confucians absorbed its relation of one principle and many things.
