# Wonhyo

*元曉*

**617 – 686** · Korean  
Born: Amnyang, Silla · Died: Hyeolsa, Silla  
Schools & eras: [Buddhist Scholastic Schools](../movements/buddhist-schools.md)  
Also in: [Religion](../../religion/creators/wonhyo.md)  

> Silla monk and the most prolific Buddhist writer of Korea, who sought to harmonise the disputes between doctrinal schools.

## Biography

Wonhyo was born in 617 in the kingdom of Silla and became a monk as a young man. Twice he set out for Tang China with his friend Uisang; on the second attempt, the story goes, he sheltered in the dark in what he took for a cave and drank sweet water from a gourd, then saw at dawn that it was a tomb and the water had stood in a skull, and realised that all things arise from the mind, so there was no need to travel. He stayed in Silla, later left the monastic order after fathering a son, the scholar Seol Chong, with a royal princess, and travelled among the common people singing and dancing to spread Pure Land devotion. He died in 686.

Wonhyo wrote around eighty works, of which some twenty survive. His commentaries on the Awakening of Faith in the Mahāyāna and the Vajrasamādhi Sutra were read in China and Japan, and his Ten Approaches to the Reconciliation of Doctrinal Controversy (Simmun hwajaeng non) argued that apparently opposed teachings are partial views of a single 'one mind'. This principle of hwajaeng, harmonising disputes, has been seen as the hallmark of Korean Buddhist thought.

### Ten Approaches to the Reconciliation of Doctrinal Controversy (Simmun hwajaeng non) (c. 660s)

- **Date:** c. 660s
- **Type:** Doctrinal treatise
- **Written / first published:** Silla; survives in fragments
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ten%20Approaches%20to%20the%20Reconciliation%20of%20Doctrinal%20Controversy%20%28Simmun%20hwajaeng%20non%29%20Wonhyo)

In this treatise, now surviving only in fragments discovered on woodblocks at Haeinsa, Wonhyo took up ten disputed questions of his time, such as whether all beings possess buddha-nature, whether emptiness and existence conflict, and whether the self and the dharmas are real, and showed that each side was correct from its own standpoint and mistaken only in denying the other. The method of hwajaeng, harmonising disputes, made his work a touchstone for later Korean Buddhists, and modern scholars have taken it as the distinctive character of Korean Buddhism, though how far it differs from Chinese doctrinal classification is debated.

### Commentary on the Awakening of Faith (Daeseung gisillon so) (c. 660s–680s)

- **Date:** c. 660s–680s
- **Type:** Doctrinal commentary
- **Written / first published:** Silla
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Commentary%20on%20the%20Awakening%20of%20Faith%20%28Daeseung%20gisillon%20so%29%20Wonhyo)

The Awakening of Faith in the Mahāyāna, a sixth-century treatise probably composed in China, teaches that one mind has two aspects, suchness and arising-and-ceasing, and so unites the doctrines of buddha-nature and of the storehouse consciousness. Wonhyo wrote two commentaries on it, the Gisillon so and the Byeolgi, arguing that the text resolves the conflict between Madhyamaka emptiness and Yogācāra analysis, which are like the two sides of one coin. Fazang drew on Wonhyo's work in his own celebrated commentary, and Wonhyo's was among the 'three great commentaries' on the treatise in East Asia. It is the clearest statement of the one-mind philosophy that underlies his whole work.

### Exposition of the Vajrasamādhi Sutra (Geumgang sammaegyeong non) (c. 680s)

- **Date:** c. 680s
- **Type:** Doctrinal commentary
- **Written / first published:** Silla
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Exposition%20of%20the%20Vajrasam%C4%81dhi%20Sutra%20%28Geumgang%20sammaegyeong%20non%29%20Wonhyo)

The Vajrasamādhi Sutra was probably composed in East Asia in the seventh century, and some scholars argue it was written in Silla itself. According to Wonhyo's biography in the Song Biographies of Eminent Monks, a dragon king gave the sutra to the Silla court and only Wonhyo could explain it; he lectured on it riding on an ox, writing his commentary between its horns. Uniquely for a commentary, it was given the title non, 'treatise', normally reserved for works by bodhisattvas. Wonhyo uses the sutra to integrate meditation with doctrine around the theme of the 'contemplation practice of a single taste'. It is often considered his most mature work.
