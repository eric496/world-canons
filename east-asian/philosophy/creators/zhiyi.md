# Zhiyi

*智顗*

**538 – 597** · Chinese  
Born: Huarong, Jingzhou (Hunan) · Died: Mount Tiantai  
Schools & eras: [Buddhist Scholastic Schools](../movements/buddhist-schools.md)  

> Founder in all but name of the Tiantai school, who organised the whole of Buddhist teaching around the Lotus Sutra and a doctrine of threefold truth.

## Biography

Zhiyi was born in 538 into an official family in central China and became a monk at seventeen after the fall of the Liang dynasty ruined his family. He studied meditation under Huisi, lectured to large audiences in the southern capital Jiankang, and in 575 withdrew to Mount Tiantai in Zhejiang, which gave his school its name. Later he enjoyed the patronage of the Sui prince Yang Guang, the future Emperor Yang, and conferred the bodhisattva precepts on him. He died in 597.

Zhiyi's major works, written down from his lectures by his disciple Guanding, classify the Buddha's teachings into periods and methods, culminating in the Lotus Sutra, and join doctrine to a detailed programme of meditation. His teaching of the threefold truth holds that every phenomenon is at once empty, provisionally real and the middle, and that 'the three thousand worlds are contained in a single moment of thought'. Tiantai, carried to Japan by Saichō as Tendai, became the matrix of much Japanese Buddhism.

### Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sutra (Fahua xuanyi) (593)

- **Date:** 593
- **Type:** Doctrinal commentary
- **Written / first published:** Jade Spring Monastery, Jingzhou; recorded by Guanding
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Profound%20Meaning%20of%20the%20Lotus%20Sutra%20%28Fahua%20xuanyi%29%20Zhiyi)

Lectured in 593 and written down by Guanding, the Fahua xuanyi expounds the five characters of the Lotus Sutra's title at enormous length, using them to set out the Tiantai system: the 'five periods and eight teachings' that arrange the Buddha's sermons into a progression culminating in the Lotus, and the idea that the 'wonderful dharma' is the perfect, all-inclusive truth beyond relative distinctions. This scheme of doctrinal classification (panjiao) was one of the great achievements of Chinese Buddhism, a way of reconciling a vast and contradictory scriptural inheritance, and was adopted in Japan and Korea.

### Great Calming and Contemplation (Mohe zhiguan) (594)

- **Date:** 594
- **Type:** Treatise on meditation
- **Written / first published:** Jade Spring Monastery, Jingzhou; recorded by Guanding
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Great%20Calming%20and%20Contemplation%20%28Mohe%20zhiguan%29%20Zhiyi)

The Mohe zhiguan was delivered as lectures in 594 at Yuquan monastery and edited by Zhiyi's disciple Guanding. It lays out a complete path of practice, the four kinds of samādhi, preparatory disciplines and ten modes of contemplating objects such as the aggregates, illness, karma and demons, grounded in the teaching that the mind in each moment contains all three thousand realms of existence and that every phenomenon is at once empty, provisional and the middle. With the Profound Meaning of the Lotus Sutra and the Words and Phrases of the Lotus Sutra it forms the three great works of Tiantai. It became the core meditative text of Japanese Tendai on Mount Hiei.
