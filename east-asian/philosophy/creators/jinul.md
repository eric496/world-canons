## Biography

Jinul, also known by his title Bojo Guksa, was born in 1158 and ordained at eight. He passed the monastic examination in 1182 but, dismayed by the worldliness of the Goryeo clergy, called on fellow monks to form a retreat community devoted to meditation and wisdom. After years of solitary study, during which reading the Platform Sutra, a Huayan commentary and the sayings of Dahui each brought him awakening, he established the community at Suseonsa on Mount Songgwang, now Songgwangsa, in 1200. He died there in 1210.

Jinul argued that one must first awaken suddenly to the nature of one's own mind as Buddha, and then cultivate gradually to remove ingrained habits, drawing on the Chinese master Zongmi. He reconciled Seon with Hwaeom doctrine and adopted Dahui's method of contemplating a single word, the hwadu. His synthesis remains the foundation of the Jogye Order, the main school of Korean Buddhism.

### Secrets on Cultivating the Mind (Susim gyeol) (c. 1203–1205)

- **Date:** c. 1203–1205
- **Type:** Meditation treatise
- **Written / first published:** Suseonsa, Mount Songgwang
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Secrets%20on%20Cultivating%20the%20Mind%20%28Susim%20gyeol%29%20Jinul)

Written in the form of questions and answers, the Susim gyeol explains that the mind of every person is Buddha and that one must look within rather than seek outside. Awakening to this is sudden, like realising one's mistake on waking; but long-accumulated habits remain, so it must be followed by gradual cultivation, as ice that is water by nature still needs time to melt. Jinul describes this cultivation as the balanced practice of samādhi and prajñā, and answers objections from those who think practice unnecessary after awakening. It remains a standard text for Korean monks and lay practitioners and was translated by Robert Buswell.

### Excerpts from the Dharma Collection with Personal Notes (Beopjip byeolhaeng nok jeoryo byeongip sagi) (1209)

- **Date:** 1209
- **Type:** Doctrinal treatise
- **Written / first published:** Suseonsa, Mount Songgwang
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Excerpts%20from%20the%20Dharma%20Collection%20with%20Personal%20Notes%20%28Beopjip%20byeolhaeng%20nok%20jeoryo%20byeongip%20sagi%29%20Jinul)

In this major treatise Jinul quotes and comments on the Chinese master Zongmi's comparison of the Chan lineages, accepting his argument that understanding must precede practice and that awakening is sudden while cultivation is gradual. He then goes beyond Zongmi, concluding with Dahui's 'shortcut' method of investigating a hwadu, a critical phrase from a Chan dialogue, as the means to cut through conceptual understanding. The work became part of the Korean monastic curriculum and fixed the Korean Seon synthesis of doctrinal study, gradual cultivation and kanhwa meditation.
