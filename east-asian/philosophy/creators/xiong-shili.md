# Xiong Shili

*熊十力*

**1885 – 1968** · Chinese  
Born: Huanggang, Hubei · Died: Shanghai  
Schools & eras: [New Confucianism](../movements/new-confucianism.md)  

> Revolutionary turned philosopher, whose New Treatise on Consciousness-Only rebuilt Confucian metaphysics from a critique of Buddhism.

## Biography

Xiong Shili took part in the 1911 revolution as a young soldier before turning to scholarship. He studied Yogācāra Buddhism under Ouyang Jingwu at the Inner Learning Institute in Nanjing and from 1922 taught at Peking University. He remained in mainland China after 1949, and died in Shanghai in 1968 during the Cultural Revolution.

In his New Treatise on Consciousness-Only (1932, later revised) Xiong rejected the Yogācāra theory he had studied and argued, drawing on the Book of Changes, that reality is ceaseless creative transformation, with substance and function inseparable. His students Mou Zongsan, Tang Junyi and Xu Fuguan became the leading New Confucians of the next generation.

## Key key texts

### New Treatise on Consciousness-Only (Xin weishi lun) (1932; vernacular edition 1944)

- **Date:** 1932; vernacular edition 1944
- **Type:** Metaphysical treatise
- **Written / first published:** Hangzhou (classical edition)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=New%20Treatise%20on%20Consciousness-Only%20%28Xin%20weishi%20lun%29%20Xiong%20Shili)

After years of studying Yogācāra Buddhism, Xiong Shili came to reject its theory of seeds and stored consciousness, and in this book, published in classical Chinese in 1932 and in an enlarged vernacular version in 1944, he built his own system. Reality is an original substance that exists only in its functioning, a constant 'closing and opening' that produces the world; the mind that grasps this is the original mind of the Confucians, and the Book of Changes, not Buddhism, expresses it best. His former Buddhist teachers wrote rebuttals, provoking a major debate. The book founded the metaphysical strand of New Confucianism.
