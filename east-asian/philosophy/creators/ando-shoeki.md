# Andō Shōeki

*安藤昌益*

**1703 – 1762** · Japanese  
Born: Akita domain (Dewa) · Died: Ōdate  
Schools & eras: [Edo Confucianism](../movements/edo-confucianism.md)  

> Rural physician whose radical writings, rediscovered only in 1899, condemned all rulers, scholars and priests as idle eaters of farmers' labour.

## Biography

Little is known of Andō Shōeki's life. He seems to have been born in northern Japan, practised medicine in the castle town of Hachinohe in the 1740s and 1750s, and returned late in life to his native village near Ōdate, where he died in 1762. His manuscripts, unknown during the Edo period, were discovered in 1899 by the educator Kano Kōkichi; much of the main manuscript was later lost in the Great Kantō earthquake of 1923.

In Shizen shin'eidō (The Way of Nature and Labour) Shōeki imagined an original 'world of nature' in which everyone farms and all are equal, and argued that the sages of China, the Buddha and Japan's rulers had invented hierarchy so they could live off others' work. His criticism of every established teaching has been compared to European utopian and anarchist thought; the Canadian historian E. H. Norman made him known in the West.

## Key key texts

### The Way of Nature and Labour (Shizen shin'eidō) (c. 1752–1762)

[![The Way of Nature and Labour (Shizen shin'eidō)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/10/Ando_Shoeki_-_Dc-shizen.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ando_Shoeki_-_Dc-shizen.png)

<sub>Page of Andō Shōeki's Shizen shin'eidō Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ando_Shoeki_-_Dc-shizen.png).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1752–1762
- **Type:** Philosophical treatise
- **Written / first published:** Hachinohe, Mutsu; manuscript mostly lost in 1923
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Way%20of%20Nature%20and%20Labour%20%28Shizen%20shin%27eid%C5%8D%29%20And%C5%8D%20Sh%C5%8Deki)

The Shizen shin'eidō survives in a three-fascicle printed version of 1753 and fragments of a manuscript of about a hundred fascicles, most of which was destroyed in the fire after the 1923 earthquake. Shōeki describes nature as a self-moving process of advancing and retreating qi, in which men and women, heaven and earth, form complementary pairs without hierarchy. In the original 'world of nature' everyone farmed; then sages, Buddhas and rulers created the 'world of law', with its classes and doctrines, in order to eat without working. He prescribes 'direct cultivation' by all. Unknown until 1899, the work has been read as a precursor of anarchism and ecological thought.
