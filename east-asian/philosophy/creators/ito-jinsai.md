## Biography

Itō Jinsai was born into a Kyoto merchant family and refused to become a physician, as his family wished, in order to study Confucianism. After years of intense study of Zhu Xi and a long breakdown, he concluded that Song metaphysics distorted the original teaching. In 1662 he opened a private school, the Kogidō ('Hall of Ancient Meaning'), across from his home by the Horikawa; it attracted thousands of students and was run by his son Tōgai and their descendants into the twentieth century.

Jinsai argued that the Analects and the Mencius must be read in their own terms. In his Gomō jigi (The Meaning of Terms in the Analects and Mencius) he redefined key words so that the Way is the everyday path of human relations, ren is loving care, and the world is a living process of qi rather than the expression of a static principle. His philological approach anticipated Ogyū Sorai and the Kokugaku scholars.

### The Meaning of Terms in the Analects and Mencius (Gomō jigi) (1683; printed 1705)

- **Date:** 1683; printed 1705
- **Type:** Philosophical lexicon
- **Written / first published:** Kogidō, Kyoto
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Meaning%20of%20Terms%20in%20the%20Analects%20and%20Mencius%20%28Gom%C5%8D%20jigi%29%20It%C5%8D%20Jinsai)

Jinsai drafted the Gomō jigi in 1683 and revised it throughout his life; it was printed in the year of his death. In thirty entries, on terms such as the Way of heaven, fate, the Way, principle, virtue, humaneness, the mind, nature and loyalty, he argues from the Analects and the Mencius that Song scholars had imported Buddhist and Daoist ideas of quiescence and an abstract principle. For Jinsai the universe is a single living, generative qi; the Way is the path of everyday human relations; and humaneness is love. The book anticipated by a century Dai Zhen's similar project in China, and it set the method of 'ancient learning' (kogaku) in Japan.

### Boy's Questions (Dōjimon) (c. 1690s; printed 1707)

- **Date:** c. 1690s; printed 1707
- **Type:** Catechism
- **Written / first published:** Kogidō, Kyoto
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Boy%27s%20Questions%20%28D%C5%8Djimon%29%20It%C5%8D%20Jinsai)

The Dōjimon presents Jinsai's teaching as a series of questions from a young student and the master's answers, in three fascicles. It returns to his central themes: that the Analects is the supreme book in the universe, that the Way lies in the everyday relations of ruler and subject, parent and child, husband and wife, sibling and friend, and that humaneness is essentially love and lies close at hand. It insists that learning should cultivate practical virtue rather than speculation on human nature. Published two years after his death, it is the most accessible statement of his mature thought.
