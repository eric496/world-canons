# Uemura Shōen

*上村松園*

**1875 – 1949** · Japanese  
Born: Kyoto · Died: Nara  
Movements & schools: [Nihonga and yōga in modern Japan](../movements/nihonga-yoga.md)  

> Kyoto nihonga painter of bijinga (pictures of beautiful women), the first woman to receive Japan's Order of Culture.

## Biography

Uemura Tsune was born in Kyoto, where her widowed mother ran a tea shop and encouraged her to paint. She studied with Suzuki Shōnen, Kōno Bairei and Takeuchi Seihō, and her work won prizes at national exhibitions from her teens. She raised her son, the painter Uemura Shōkō, as a single mother in a male-dominated art world. In 1948 she became the first woman awarded the Order of Culture.

Shōen painted women in historical and contemporary dress with fine line and soft colour, aiming, as she put it, to show women of pure and refined feeling rather than mere prettiness. Works such as Flame (1918), with its jealous ghost from Noh drama, and Jo no mai (1936) are among the masterpieces of modern nihonga.

## Key artworks

### Jo no mai (1936)

[![Jo no mai](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/Jo-no-mai_by_Uemura_Shoen.jpg/1280px-Jo-no-mai_by_Uemura_Shoen.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jo-no-mai_by_Uemura_Shoen.jpg)

<sub>Uemura Shōen, Jo no mai, 1936 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jo-no-mai_by_Uemura_Shoen.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1936
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** Tokyo University of the Arts Museum
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jo%20no%20mai%20Uemura%20Sh%C5%8Den)

Jo no mai is the slow introductory dance of a Noh play. Uemura Shōen shows a young woman in a vermilion kimono with a long-sleeved robe, standing still with her fan held forward, her eyes cast down, at the moment of starting the dance. The pure line, refined colours and controlled posture express what Shōen called the dignity and spiritual purity of a woman. It was shown at the government exhibition of 1936 and bought by the government. It is an Important Cultural Property.
