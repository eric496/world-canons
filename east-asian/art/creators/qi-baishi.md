# Qi Baishi

*齊白石*

**1864 – 1957** · Chinese  
Born: Xiangtan, Hunan · Died: Beijing  
Movements & schools: [Shanghai school and modern Chinese ink](../movements/shanghai-school.md)  

> Carpenter turned painter, the best-loved Chinese painter of the twentieth century, famous for shrimps, crabs, insects and flowers in vivid ink and colour.

## Biography

Qi Baishi was born to a poor peasant family in Hunan and worked as a carpenter and woodcarver before learning to paint, write poetry and carve seals in his twenties. He travelled across China between 1902 and 1909 and settled in Beijing in 1917. There, encouraged by the painter Chen Shizeng, he reformed his style around the age of sixty, after Xu Wei, Bada Shanren and Wu Changshuo. He taught at the Beiping Art School, where Xu Beihong supported him, and after 1949 he was honoured by the People's Republic, becoming president of the Chinese Artists Association in 1953.

Qi Baishi painted familiar things, shrimps, crabs, frogs, chicks, cabbages, lotuses and tools, with bold ink and bright colour and a humour that reached a wide audience. His shrimps, painted with a few translucent strokes, became his signature. He was also a prolific seal carver and poet. His works now set records at auction. Because he died in 1957, his works are not reproduced here.

## Key artworks

### Shrimps (1940s)

- **Date:** 1940s
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink on paper
- **Where to see it:** Beijing Fine Art Academy, National Art Museum of China and other collections (many versions)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Shrimps%20Qi%20Baishi)
- **Links:** [Qi Baishi on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qi_Baishi)

Qi Baishi kept shrimps in a bowl on his desk to observe them and painted them for decades, gradually simplifying his method. A few pale, wet strokes form the body and its segments, a darker spot the head, and fine dry lines the long antennae and legs, so that the creatures seem transparent and alive in water that is never painted. He said that he had changed the way he painted shrimps several times until he reached the form he wanted in his seventies. His shrimps became immensely popular and are among the best-known images in modern Chinese art. Because Qi Baishi died in 1957, his paintings are not reproduced here.
