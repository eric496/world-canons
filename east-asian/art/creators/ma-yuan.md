# Ma Yuan

*馬遠*

**c. 1160 – c. 1225** · Chinese  
Born: Qiantang (Hangzhou) · Died: Hangzhou  
Movements & schools: [Song academy & literati painting](../movements/song-academy-literati.md)  

> Southern Song court painter whose 'one-corner' compositions of misty space and figures in landscape defined the Ma–Xia school.

## Biography

Ma Yuan came from a family that produced painters for five generations, beginning with his great-grandfather Ma Fen in Huizong's academy. He served as painter-in-attendance at the Southern Song court in Hangzhou under the emperors Guangzong and Ningzong, and members of the imperial family, including Empress Yang, wrote poems on his paintings. His son Ma Lin was also a court painter.

Ma Yuan concentrated the landscape into one corner of the picture, leaving large areas of empty silk to suggest mist, water and distance, so that he was nicknamed One-Corner Ma. His paintings, often small album leaves and fans, pair a scholar or poet with a gnarled pine or plum under a moon, and his twelve studies of water in Beijing are virtuoso exercises in line. Ming critics disparaged his style as professional, but in Japan the Ma–Xia manner was the main model for Muromachi ink painting.

### Walking on a Mountain Path in Spring (early 13th century)

[![Walking on a Mountain Path in Spring](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Ma_Yuan_Walking_on_Path_in_Spring.jpg/1280px-Ma_Yuan_Walking_on_Path_in_Spring.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ma_Yuan_Walking_on_Path_in_Spring.jpg)

<sub>Ma Yuan, Walking on a Mountain Path in Spring Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ma_Yuan_Walking_on_Path_in_Spring.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** early 13th century
- **Type:** Album leaf, ink and colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** National Palace Museum, Taipei
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Walking%20on%20a%20Mountain%20Path%20in%20Spring%20Ma%20Yuan)

In the lower left corner a scholar in a long robe walks along a path with a servant carrying a qin zither, brushing against willow branches. Two birds perch and one flies away into empty space. The rest of the leaf is almost bare silk, suggesting misty spring air. On the right are two lines of poetry written by Emperor Ningzong, saying that the wild flowers dance when the sleeves brush past them and the hidden birds do not sing at the sight of people. The composition shows how Ma Yuan concentrated forms in one corner to create a poetic mood, and the union of image and court poetry typical of Southern Song academy painting.

### Water (twelve album leaves) (c. 1222)

[![Water (twelve album leaves)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9f/Ma_Yuan_-_Water_Album_-_Clouds_Rising_from_the_Green_Sea.jpg/1280px-Ma_Yuan_-_Water_Album_-_Clouds_Rising_from_the_Green_Sea.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ma_Yuan_-_Water_Album_-_Clouds_Rising_from_the_Green_Sea.jpg)

<sub>Ma Yuan, Clouds Rising from the Green Sea, from the Water album Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ma_Yuan_-_Water_Album_-_Clouds_Rising_from_the_Green_Sea.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1222
- **Type:** Twelve leaves mounted as a handscroll, ink and colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** Palace Museum, Beijing
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Water%20%28twelve%20album%20leaves%29%20Ma%20Yuan)

Each of the twelve leaves shows water in a different condition, with a title written by Empress Yang: waves on the Yellow River, autumn waters of Dongting Lake, clouds rising from the green sea, layered waves, a gentle breeze on a lake. Apart from a little land in one or two leaves, they are made almost only of ink lines, thick and thin, curling, breaking and repeating, that suggest movement and texture. The series is a virtuoso display of brushwork and close observation of nature. It is one of the few works by Ma Yuan that do not include figures, and shows how far Song painters could push a single subject.
