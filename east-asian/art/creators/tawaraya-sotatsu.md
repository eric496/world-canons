# Tawaraya Sōtatsu

*俵屋宗達*

**c. 1570 – c. 1640** · Japanese  
Born: Kyoto · Died: ?  
Movements & schools: [Momoyama and Edo decorative painting (Kanō and Rinpa)](../movements/kano-rinpa.md)  

> Kyoto painter who founded the Rinpa style with Hon'ami Kōetsu, best known for the Wind God and Thunder God screens.

## Biography

Little is documented about Sōtatsu's life. He ran a painting shop, the Tawaraya, in Kyoto, producing decorated fans, paper and screens, and he restored old Heian narrative scrolls, which gave him close knowledge of yamato-e. He collaborated with Hon'ami Kōetsu, painting underdrawings for his calligraphy, and later received the honorary rank of hokkyō. His workshop continued after his death under his followers.

Sōtatsu combined the themes and colours of Heian court art with daring, simplified designs, large flat areas of gold, and the tarashikomi technique of dropping wet ink or colour onto wet paint to create pooling effects. His Wind God and Thunder God screens in Kennin-ji were later copied by Ogata Kōrin and Sakai Hōitsu, a chain of homage that defines the Rinpa tradition.

## Key artworks

### Wind God and Thunder God (Fūjin Raijin-zu) (early 17th century)

[![Wind God and Thunder God (Fūjin Raijin-zu)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/37/Wind_God_and_Thunder_God_Screens_by_Tawaraya_Sotatsu_hi-res.png/1280px-Wind_God_and_Thunder_God_Screens_by_Tawaraya_Sotatsu_hi-res.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wind_God_and_Thunder_God_Screens_by_Tawaraya_Sotatsu_hi-res.png)

<sub>Tawaraya Sōtatsu, Wind God and Thunder God Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wind_God_and_Thunder_God_Screens_by_Tawaraya_Sotatsu_hi-res.png).</sub>

- **Date:** early 17th century
- **Type:** Pair of two-panel folding screens, ink, colour and gold on paper
- **Where to see it:** Kennin-ji, Kyoto (on deposit at Kyoto National Museum)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Wind%20God%20and%20Thunder%20God%20%28F%C5%ABjin%20Raijin-zu%29%20Tawaraya%20S%C5%8Dtatsu)

On the right screen the green-skinned Wind God rushes in, holding a billowing bag of wind; on the left the white Thunder God beats a ring of drums. Both ride dark clouds painted with tarashikomi, the pooling technique Sōtatsu favoured, and both are pushed to the outer edges, so that the vast empty gold between them seems charged with energy. The figures derive from Buddhist guardian imagery and perhaps from a Kamakura scroll Sōtatsu restored. The screens, which belong to the Zen temple Kennin-ji, are a National Treasure. Ogata Kōrin copied them, and Sakai Hōitsu copied Kōrin's version, making the composition the signature of the Rinpa lineage.
