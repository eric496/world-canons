## Biography

Wen Zhengming failed the provincial examinations ten times. In 1523 he was recommended for a post at the Hanlin Academy in Beijing, but after three unhappy years he resigned and returned to Suzhou, where he spent the rest of his long life painting, writing and teaching. His sons, nephews and many pupils carried on his style, and his friendship circle included Tang Yin and the calligrapher Zhu Yunming.

Wen Zhengming painted in both fine, detailed styles and broader manners, favouring scholars in gardens, old trees and cypresses, and the scenery of Suzhou. His album of the Garden of the Humble Administrator records one of the city's great gardens. He was also among the finest calligraphers of the Ming. His refined, sometimes austere art became the model of the Suzhou gentleman's taste.

## Key artworks

### Garden of the Humble Administrator (album) (1533)

[![Garden of the Humble Administrator (album)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Garden_of_the_Inept_Administrator_-_leaf_aa.jpg/1280px-Garden_of_the_Inept_Administrator_-_leaf_aa.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Garden_of_the_Inept_Administrator_-_leaf_aa.jpg)

<sub>A leaf from Wen Zhengming's garden album, Metropolitan Museum of Art Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Garden_of_the_Inept_Administrator_-_leaf_aa.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1533
- **Type:** Album of thirty-one leaves, ink on paper
- **Where to see it:** Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Garden%20of%20the%20Humble%20Administrator%20%28album%29%20Wen%20Zhengming)

The Humble Administrator's Garden in Suzhou was created in the early sixteenth century by Wang Xianchen, a retired official, whose name for it alluded to a text about living simply. Wen Zhengming, a friend of the owner, wrote an account of the garden and painted it in an album in 1533, picturing thirty-one sites, pavilions, bridges, ponds, rocks and groves, each with a poem in his calligraphy. The paintings are quiet and spare, showing a scholar at leisure in each setting. The album is both a work of literati art and a record of a garden that, much changed, still survives and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
