# Wu Daozi

*吳道子*

**c. 680 – c. 760** · Chinese  
Born: Yangdi, Henan · Died: ?  
Movements & schools: [Tang painting & sancai](../movements/tang-art.md)  

> The Tang painter celebrated in Chinese tradition as the Sage of Painting, famous for his temple murals; none of his work survives with certainty.

## Biography

Wu Daozi was born around 680 in Yangdi in Henan to a poor family and was trained first as a calligrapher before turning to painting. Emperor Xuanzong brought him to court, where he worked for decades. Sources say he painted more than three hundred murals in Buddhist and Daoist temples in Chang'an and Luoyang, working at great speed with sweeping, swelling and tapering lines. A famous legend tells that he painted a landscape for the emperor, clapped his hands, walked into a cave in the picture and vanished.

Later writers ranked Wu Daozi the greatest painter of the Tang, praising the vitality of his brushwork and figures whose robes seemed to move in the wind. Almost all his murals were destroyed in the persecution of Buddhism in the 840s and in later wars. His style is known through stone engravings, through works painted in his manner, such as the Eighty-seven Celestials scroll in Beijing, which some attribute to him, and through the many Daoist and Buddhist figure painters who claimed his lineage.

## Key artworks

### Eighty-seven Celestials (8th–11th century (attributed))

[![Eighty-seven Celestials](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Eighty-seven_Celestials.jpg/1280px-Eighty-seven_Celestials.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Eighty-seven_Celestials.jpg)

<sub>Eighty-seven Celestials (detail) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Eighty-seven_Celestials.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 8th–11th century (attributed)
- **Type:** Handscroll, ink on silk
- **Where to see it:** Xu Beihong Memorial Museum, Beijing
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Eighty-seven%20Celestials%20Wu%20Daozi)

This unpainted scroll, nearly three metres long, shows a procession of eighty-seven Daoist immortals and attendants crossing a bridge and walking past railings, carrying banners, flowers and instruments. It is drawn entirely in ink line (baimiao) with rhythmic, flowing strokes that suggest a draft for a temple mural. The painter Xu Beihong bought it in Hong Kong in 1937 and attributed it to Wu Daozi, though many scholars date it to the Song and connect it with the eleventh-century painter Wu Zongyuan. It was briefly stolen during the war with Japan and later recovered. Whatever its author, it is the best surviving example of the Tang-style figure line associated with Wu's school.
