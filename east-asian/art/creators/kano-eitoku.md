# Kanō Eitoku

*狩野永徳*

**1543 – 1590** · Japanese  
Born: Kyoto · Died: Kyoto  
Movements & schools: [Momoyama and Edo decorative painting (Kanō and Rinpa)](../movements/kano-rinpa.md)  

> Leading painter of the Momoyama period, who created the bold, large-scale style of gold screens for the castles of Oda Nobunaga and Toyotomi Hideyoshi.

## Biography

Kanō Eitoku was the grandson of Kanō Motonobu, who had made the Kanō family the leading painters in Kyoto. Eitoku was trained by his grandfather and by the age of about twenty was painting important commissions, including the Rakuchū rakugai zu screens of Kyoto, which Oda Nobunaga presented to the warlord Uesugi Kenshin. He decorated Nobunaga's Azuchi Castle and later Hideyoshi's Osaka Castle and Jurakudai palace, directing large workshops. He died suddenly in 1590, apparently from overwork.

Eitoku developed a monumental manner suited to vast castle interiors: giant trees and rocks painted with forceful brushwork against shining gold leaf, as in the Cypress Trees screen in the Tokyo National Museum. Almost all his castle paintings perished with the buildings, but his style defined the Kanō school, which served as official painters to the Tokugawa shoguns until the nineteenth century.

### Scenes in and around Kyoto (Uesugi screens) (c. 1565)

[![Scenes in and around Kyoto (Uesugi screens)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Kan%C5%8D_Eitoku_-_Rakuch%C5%AB_rakugai_zu_%28Uesugi%29_-_right_screen.jpg/1280px-Kan%C5%8D_Eitoku_-_Rakuch%C5%AB_rakugai_zu_%28Uesugi%29_-_right_screen.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kan%C5%8D_Eitoku_-_Rakuch%C5%AB_rakugai_zu_%28Uesugi%29_-_right_screen.jpg)

<sub>Uesugi Rakuchū rakugai zu, right screen Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kan%C5%8D_Eitoku_-_Rakuch%C5%AB_rakugai_zu_%28Uesugi%29_-_right_screen.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1565
- **Type:** Pair of six-panel folding screens, ink, colour and gold on paper
- **Where to see it:** Yonezawa City Uesugi Museum, Yamagata
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Scenes%20in%20and%20around%20Kyoto%20%28Uesugi%20screens%29%20Kan%C5%8D%20Eitoku)

Screens of scenes in and around the capital (rakuchū rakugai zu) were a popular genre in the sixteenth century, showing Kyoto's palaces, temples, streets and festivals seen from above through gaps in golden clouds. This pair, painted by the young Kanō Eitoku, is the most celebrated. According to tradition it was commissioned by the shogun Ashikaga Yoshiteru and later presented by Oda Nobunaga to Uesugi Kenshin in 1574. It includes the shogun's palace, the Gion festival procession, merchants, priests, samurai and townspeople at work and play, about 2,500 figures in all. It is a key historical record of Kyoto and a National Treasure.

- **Date:** 1590
- **Type:** Eight-panel folding screen, ink, colour and gold on paper
- **Where to see it:** Tokyo National Museum
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Cypress%20Trees%20Kan%C5%8D%20Eitoku)

This screen was originally a set of sliding doors, probably for the palace of Prince Hachijō no Miya Toshihito in Kyoto, built in 1590, the year of Eitoku's death; it was later remounted as a screen. A huge cypress tree twists across the panels, its trunk and branches thrusting out of the frame, with blue water and rocks below and gold clouds and ground behind. The scale and energy of the tree express the power and ambition of the Momoyama warlords whose castles Eitoku decorated. Though some scholars attribute it partly to his workshop, it is regarded as the most important surviving work in his style and is a National Treasure.
