## Biography

Dong Qichang passed the highest examination in 1589 and had a long career in government, rising to be Minister of Rites in Nanjing, though he withdrew from office several times during factional struggles. He was also a powerful landowner, and in 1616 a mob angered by his family's behaviour burned his house in Songjiang. He was the foremost connoisseur and collector of his day and wrote inscriptions on many of the most famous old paintings.

Dong Qichang's theory, borrowing the terms of Chan Buddhism, opposed a Southern school of scholar-amateurs, from Wang Wei through Dong Yuan to the Four Yuan Masters, to a Northern school of professionals and academicians. Painters should transform the old masters, not copy nature. His own landscapes, such as The Qingbian Mountains of 1617 in Cleveland, rearrange forms in abstract, almost geometric masses. His ideas governed the Orthodox school of the Qing and still shape how Chinese painting is studied.

## Key artworks

### The Qingbian Mountains (1617)

[![The Qingbian Mountains](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bb/Dong_Qichang._The_Qingbian_Mountains_1617_Cleveland_Museum_of_Art.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dong_Qichang._The_Qingbian_Mountains_1617_Cleveland_Museum_of_Art.jpg)

<sub>Dong Qichang, The Qingbian Mountains, 1617 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dong_Qichang._The_Qingbian_Mountains_1617_Cleveland_Museum_of_Art.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1617
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink on paper
- **Where to see it:** Cleveland Museum of Art
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Qingbian%20Mountains%20Dong%20Qichang)

In this large scroll, painted in 1617, Dong Qichang took up the subject of Wang Meng's famous Dwelling in the Qingbian Mountains, which he had praised, and the style of the tenth-century master Dong Yuan. But he did not copy them: the mountains are built from repeated, clearly outlined blocks that tilt and interlock without a consistent ground plane, and their volumes are flattened into patterns of light and dark. Dong's inscription says that he wished to vie with the old masters. The painting illustrates his idea that a painter should transform rather than imitate the ancients. It is one of the most important Chinese paintings in the West.
