# Tanaka Atsuko

*田中敦子*

**1932 – 2005** · Japanese  
Born: Osaka · Died: Nara  
Movements & schools: [Postwar avant-gardes](../movements/postwar-avant-garde.md)  

> Gutai artist whose Electric Dress (1956), a costume of flashing coloured light bulbs, is a landmark of postwar performance and installation art.

## Biography

Tanaka Atsuko studied in Kyoto and Osaka and joined the Gutai Art Association, founded by Yoshihara Jirō in Ashiya in 1954, in 1955. She took part in Gutai's outdoor exhibitions and stage events, making works with a cloth stretched to move in the wind and a bell work that rang in sequence through a gallery. She left the group in 1965 with her husband, the painter Kanayama Akira, and continued painting in Nara until her death in 2005.

Electric Dress, a wearable sculpture of painted light bulbs and tubes that she wore at the second Gutai exhibition in 1956, linked the body, technology and painting. In later decades she painted networks of coloured circles and lines derived from its wiring. Her works are still under copyright.

## Key artworks

### Electric Dress (1956)

- **Date:** 1956
- **Type:** Painted light bulbs, electric cords and controller (performance and installation)
- **Where to see it:** Remade versions in several museums
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Electric%20Dress%20Tanaka%20Atsuko)
- **Links:** [Atsuko Tanaka on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atsuko_Tanaka_(artist))

Inspired by the neon advertising signs of Osaka, Tanaka Atsuko made a dress of about two hundred light bulbs and tubes painted in bright colours and wired to flash in sequence. She wore it in public at the second Gutai exhibition, describing the risk of electric shock and the heat as part of the experience. The work fused painting, sculpture, performance and technology, and anticipated later installation and body art. Tanaka later painted large canvases of circles connected by lines, based on the dress's wiring diagrams. The original was rebuilt in later decades for exhibitions. Her works are under copyright and are not reproduced here.
