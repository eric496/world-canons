# Kanō Hōgai

*狩野芳崖*

**1828 – 1888** · Japanese  
Born: Chōfu, Nagato province · Died: Tokyo  
Movements & schools: [Nihonga and yōga in modern Japan](../movements/nihonga-yoga.md)  

> Last great painter trained in the Kanō school, who with Fenollosa and Okakura helped create modern nihonga; painted Merciful Mother Kannon.

## Biography

Hōgai was the son of a Kanō painter in the service of the Chōfu domain and trained in Edo at the Kobikichō Kanō studio. After the Meiji Restoration of 1868 the domain system collapsed and the market for Kanō painting vanished, leaving him in poverty, painting pottery for export. In the early 1880s the American scholar Ernest Fenollosa and Okakura Kakuzō discovered his work and enlisted him in their campaign to revive Japanese painting, and he was appointed to teach at the new Tokyo School of Fine Arts, but died in 1888 before it opened.

Hōgai combined Kanō brushwork with Western ideas of light, colour and space. His last painting, Merciful Mother Kannon (Hibo Kannon), in which a luminous Kannon pours the water of life onto a baby in a bubble, became an icon of the new nihonga and is an Important Cultural Property.

## Key artworks

### Merciful Mother Kannon (Hibo Kannon) (1888)

- **Date:** 1888
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** Tokyo University of the Arts Museum
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Merciful%20Mother%20Kannon%20%28Hibo%20Kannon%29%20Kan%C5%8D%20H%C5%8Dgai)

Kanō Hōgai painted Hibo Kannon in the last months of his life, finishing it just before his death in 1888. The bodhisattva of compassion, Kannon, floats in a golden mist above a dark mountainous landscape, pouring water from a vase onto a newborn child enclosed in a transparent sphere, who looks up at her. The figure combines traditional Buddhist imagery with Western ideas of light and atmosphere and Renaissance images of the Madonna, which Fenollosa had shown Hōgai. The colours are vivid and the space deep. The painting was hailed as the model for the new Japanese painting that Fenollosa and Okakura promoted, and it is an Important Cultural Property.
