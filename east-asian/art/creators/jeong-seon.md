# Jeong Seon

*정선 (鄭敾)*

**1676 – 1759** · Korean  
Born: Hanyang (Seoul) · Died: Hanyang  
Movements & schools: [Joseon painting and ceramics](../movements/joseon-painting.md)  

> Painter who created true-view landscape painting (jingyeong sansu), depicting the real mountains of Korea rather than idealised Chinese scenery.

## Biography

Jeong Seon, known by his pen name Gyeomjae, was born to an impoverished yangban family in Seoul and grew up near Mount Inwang. He studied Neo-Confucianism with the circle of Kim Chang-hyeop, whose members promoted a culture rooted in Korea. Through their recommendation he entered government service and held local posts, painting all his life. He travelled several times to the Diamond Mountains (Geumgangsan), which became his great subject.

Jeong Seon painted real Korean places with a mixture of Chinese Southern school techniques and inventions of his own, such as the vertical, crystalline strokes for granite peaks and wet dotting for forested hills. His Inwangsan after Rain (1751) and Complete View of the Diamond Mountains (1734) are National Treasures. He made Korean landscape a subject in its own right and inspired a generation of true-view painters.

### Complete View of the Diamond Mountains (Geumgang jeondo) (1734)

[![Complete View of the Diamond Mountains (Geumgang jeondo)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Geumgangjeondo.jpg/1280px-Geumgangjeondo.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Geumgangjeondo.jpg)

<sub>Jeong Seon, Complete View of the Diamond Mountains, 1734 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Geumgangjeondo.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1734
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink and light colour on paper
- **Where to see it:** Leeum Museum of Art, Seoul
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Complete%20View%20of%20the%20Diamond%20Mountains%20%28Geumgang%20jeondo%29%20Jeong%20Seon)

The Diamond Mountains in present-day North Korea were famous for their many granite pinnacles, and Jeong Seon travelled there several times. In this painting he shows the whole inner range as if from above, in a round, almost cosmic composition: the sharp white peaks on the right are drawn with vertical lines, while the earthy, forested hills on the left are rendered with soft, wet dots. The two halves have been read as yin and yang. An inscription describes the mountain range. Rather than following a Chinese model, the work captures the character of a real Korean landscape. It is National Treasure No. 217.

### Inwangsan after Rain (Inwang jesaekdo) (1751)

[![Inwangsan after Rain (Inwang jesaekdo)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Inwangjesaekdo.jpg/1280px-Inwangjesaekdo.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Inwangjesaekdo.jpg)

<sub>Jeong Seon, Inwangsan after Rain, 1751 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Inwangjesaekdo.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1751
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink on paper
- **Where to see it:** National Museum of Korea, Seoul
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Inwangsan%20after%20Rain%20%28Inwang%20jesaekdo%29%20Jeong%20Seon)

Mount Inwang rises on the western side of Seoul, and Jeong Seon grew up beneath it. In the summer of 1751, after a week of rain, he painted the mountain as it emerged from the clouds. The great bare granite cliffs are painted with heavy, dark ink in broad horizontal strokes, contrasted with mist rising from the valleys and pines and houses at the base. Many scholars believe it was painted for his lifelong friend, the poet Yi Byeong-yeon, who was gravely ill and died days later, and see it as a prayer for his recovery. Long in the Samsung collection, it was donated to the National Museum of Korea in 2021. It is a National Treasure and the most famous true-view landscape.
