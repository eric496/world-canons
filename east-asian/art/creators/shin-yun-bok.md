# Shin Yun-bok

*신윤복 (申潤福)*

**1758 – c. 1813** · Korean  
Movements & schools: [Joseon painting and ceramics](../movements/joseon-painting.md)  

> Late Joseon painter known as Hyewon, famous for elegant, colourful scenes of romance, courtesans and the leisure of the aristocracy.

## Biography

Shin Yun-bok was the son of the court painter Shin Han-pyeong. Almost nothing about his life is documented; according to a common but unverified story he was expelled from the royal painting bureau for painting improper subjects. His dates are uncertain.

Unlike Kim Hong-do's rustic scenes, Shin Yun-bok painted the urban world of yangban men and gisaeng entertainers: picnics, moonlit meetings, the Dano festival with women bathing and swinging, with fine line, bright colour and detailed settings. His album in the Kansong Art Museum is a National Treasure, and his Portrait of a Beauty is one of the best-known Korean paintings.

### Portrait of a Beauty (Miindo) (early 19th century)

[![Portrait of a Beauty (Miindo)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/69/Hyewon-Miindo.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hyewon-Miindo.jpg)

<sub>Shin Yun-bok, Portrait of a Beauty Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hyewon-Miindo.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** early 19th century
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink and colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** Kansong Art Museum, Seoul
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Portrait%20of%20a%20Beauty%20%28Miindo%29%20Shin%20Yun-bok)

A young woman, perhaps a gisaeng entertainer, stands facing the viewer, her head with its large braided wig bowed slightly, holding a tassel of a norigae ornament. Her short jacket and full indigo skirt are rendered with delicate lines and soft colour, and her expression is shy and thoughtful. Shin Yun-bok's inscription says that he painted the spring feelings in her heart. Women were rarely the subject of full portraits in Joseon painting, which makes the work unusual. It is in the Kansong Art Museum, founded by the collector Jeon Hyeong-pil, and is a Treasure of Korea.

### Lovers under the Moon (Wolha jeongin) (early 19th century)

[![Lovers under the Moon (Wolha jeongin)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/Hyewon-Wolha.jeongin-3.jpg/1280px-Hyewon-Wolha.jeongin-3.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hyewon-Wolha.jeongin-3.jpg)

<sub>Shin Yun-bok, Lovers under the Moon Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hyewon-Wolha.jeongin-3.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** early 19th century
- **Type:** Album leaf, ink and colour on paper (from Hyewon's album of genre paintings)
- **Where to see it:** Kansong Art Museum, Seoul
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Lovers%20under%20the%20Moon%20%28Wolha%20jeongin%29%20Shin%20Yun-bok)

A young man with a lantern and a woman in a long veil-like cloak meet beside a wall in a quiet alley at night, under a thin moon. The inscription says that at midnight under a dim moon only the two of them know their hearts. The scene belongs to Shin Yun-bok's album of thirty genre paintings, National Treasure No. 135, which shows the amusements, flirtations and festivals of the Seoul elite and gisaeng. The fine line, bright colours and settings in gardens and streets distinguish his elegant style from the rustic humour of Kim Hong-do.
