# Giuseppe Castiglione

*郎世寧*

**1688 – 1766** · Italian  
Born: Milan · Died: Beijing  
Movements & schools: [Qing painting: Orthodox masters and Individualists](../movements/qing-painting.md)  

> Italian Jesuit brother who served three Qing emperors as court painter under the name Lang Shining, blending European realism with Chinese painting.

## Biography

Giuseppe Castiglione trained as a painter in Milan and joined the Society of Jesus as a lay brother in 1707. He arrived in China in 1715 and was taken into service at the court of the Kangxi Emperor, then worked for Yongzheng and above all Qianlong, for more than fifty years in all. He painted portraits of the emperor and his consorts, horses, dogs, flowers and great records of hunts and ceremonies, trained Chinese court painters in perspective, and helped design the European-style palaces of the Yuanmingyuan. He died in Beijing in 1766 and was buried in the Jesuit cemetery there.

Castiglione developed a hybrid style acceptable to the court: European modelling, perspective and anatomy, but on silk with Chinese pigments, with little cast shadow and with traditional formats. His One Hundred Horses of 1728 and portraits of Qianlong are central works of Qing court art. Chinese literati critics dismissed such work as mere craft, but it is now seen as one of the most remarkable meetings of European and Chinese art.

### One Hundred Horses (1728)

[![One Hundred Horses](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/One_Hundred_Horses_%28part1%29.jpg/1280px-One_Hundred_Horses_%28part1%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:One_Hundred_Horses_%28part1%29.jpg)

<sub>Castiglione, One Hundred Horses (section) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:One_Hundred_Horses_%28part1%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1728
- **Type:** Handscroll, ink and colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** National Palace Museum, Taipei
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=One%20Hundred%20Horses%20Giuseppe%20Castiglione)

Castiglione painted this long handscroll for the Yongzheng Emperor, and a preparatory drawing for it is in the Metropolitan Museum. It shows a herd of a hundred horses with grooms in an open landscape of willows and pines, some grazing, some rolling on the ground or being driven to a river. The horses are modelled in light and shade with European anatomy and foreshortening, and the landscape recedes towards a low horizon in one-point perspective, but there are no strong cast shadows and the painting is on silk in Chinese pigments, so that it could please Chinese eyes. The work is a classic example of the hybrid court style of the eighteenth century and became one of Castiglione's most famous paintings.

### The Qianlong Emperor in Ceremonial Armour on Horseback (1739 or 1758)

[![The Qianlong Emperor in Ceremonial Armour on Horseback](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/The_Qianlong_Emperor_in_Ceremonial_Armour_on_Horseback.jpg/1280px-The_Qianlong_Emperor_in_Ceremonial_Armour_on_Horseback.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Qianlong_Emperor_in_Ceremonial_Armour_on_Horseback.jpg)

<sub>Castiglione, The Qianlong Emperor in Ceremonial Armour on Horseback Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Qianlong_Emperor_in_Ceremonial_Armour_on_Horseback.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1739 or 1758
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink and colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** Palace Museum, Beijing
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Qianlong%20Emperor%20in%20Ceremonial%20Armour%20on%20Horseback%20Giuseppe%20Castiglione)

This large portrait shows the Qianlong Emperor in the armour he wore for the grand review of troops at Nanyuan, riding a prancing white horse in a landscape, holding a bow. The emperor's face is painted frontally with soft European modelling but no strong shadows, in accordance with Chinese taste, and the details of armour and harness are exact. The date is debated, with 1739 and 1758 both proposed. The image has become one of the best-known portraits of a Chinese emperor and represents the Manchu ideal of the ruler as a warrior on horseback, central to Qianlong's self-image.
