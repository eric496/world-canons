# Josetsu

*如拙*

**fl. c. 1405 – c. 1423** · Japanese  
Born: ? · Died: Kyoto  
Movements & schools: [Muromachi ink painting](../movements/muromachi-ink.md)  

> Zen monk-painter at Shōkoku-ji in Kyoto, regarded as the founder of Muromachi ink painting; painted Catching a Catfish with a Gourd.

## Biography

Josetsu was a monk of Shōkoku-ji, the great Zen temple in Kyoto patronised by the Ashikaga shoguns. He may have been of Chinese origin, but almost nothing about his life is documented. He is remembered as the teacher of Tenshō Shūbun, who in turn taught or influenced Sesshū, so that he stands at the head of the Japanese ink painting lineage.

His one secure work, Catching a Catfish with a Gourd in Taizō-in, was commissioned by the shogun Ashikaga Yoshimochi and is topped with inscriptions by thirty-one Zen monks. It shows a man trying to catch a slippery catfish with a smooth gourd, a Zen riddle about the impossibility of grasping the mind by ordinary means, painted in a soft, Southern Song-inspired ink manner.

## Key artworks

### Catching a Catfish with a Gourd (Hyōnen-zu) (c. 1413)

[![Catching a Catfish with a Gourd (Hyōnen-zu)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Hy%C3%B4nen_zu_by_Josetsu.jpg/1280px-Hy%C3%B4nen_zu_by_Josetsu.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hy%C3%B4nen_zu_by_Josetsu.jpg)

<sub>Josetsu, Catching a Catfish with a Gourd Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hy%C3%B4nen_zu_by_Josetsu.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1413
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink and light colour on paper
- **Where to see it:** Taizō-in, Myōshin-ji, Kyoto
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Catching%20a%20Catfish%20with%20a%20Gourd%20%28Hy%C5%8Dnen-zu%29%20Josetsu)

The shogun Ashikaga Yoshimochi commissioned this painting, originally for a small standing screen, and had thirty-one Zen monks of Kyoto compose verses in answer to the question it poses: how can one catch a catfish with a gourd? A man stands on a riverbank holding a gourd while a catfish swims in the stream, with bamboo and misty mountains around. The riddle, like a kōan, points to the impossibility of grasping the mind through reason. The painting adapts Southern Song ink techniques in a soft, misty manner and is the founding work of Muromachi ink painting. It is a National Treasure.
