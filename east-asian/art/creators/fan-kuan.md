# Fan Kuan

*范寬*

**c. 950 – c. 1032** · Chinese  
Born: Huayuan, Shaanxi · Died: ?  
Movements & schools: [Monumental landscape (Five Dynasties & Northern Song)](../movements/song-landscape.md)  

> Northern Song landscape painter whose Travellers among Mountains and Streams is the supreme example of the monumental landscape.

## Biography

Fan Kuan, whose personal name may have been Fan Zhongzheng, lived in the late tenth and early eleventh centuries in Shaanxi. Song writers describe him as a man of easy-going, unconventional manners who loved wine and the Way (Dao), and say he lived in the Zhongnan and Taihua mountains to study nature. He began by imitating the earlier master Li Cheng but is reported to have concluded that it was better to learn from nature than from other men, and better still to learn from the heart.

His only universally accepted work, Travellers among Mountains and Streams in the National Palace Museum, Taipei, shows a sheer central mountain rising over a tiny mule train, built from countless short, dense raindrop texture strokes. Its signature was discovered hidden in the foliage only in 1958. The painting embodies the Northern Song vision of a vast, ordered natural world in which human beings are small, and it shaped the idea of the monumental landscape for all later Chinese painters.

## Key artworks

### Travellers among Mountains and Streams (early 11th century)

[![Travellers among Mountains and Streams](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Fan_Kuan_-_Travelers_Among_Mountains_and_Streams_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Fan_Kuan_-_Travelers_Among_Mountains_and_Streams_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fan_Kuan_-_Travelers_Among_Mountains_and_Streams_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Fan Kuan, Travellers among Mountains and Streams Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fan_Kuan_-_Travelers_Among_Mountains_and_Streams_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** early 11th century
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink and light colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** National Palace Museum, Taipei
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Travellers%20among%20Mountains%20and%20Streams%20Fan%20Kuan)

Nearly two metres high, the painting is divided into three planes: boulders in the foreground, a wooded hill with a temple roof and a mule train of travellers in the middle, and in the back a vast cliff, with a thin waterfall at its right, filling most of the picture. A band of mist separates the mountain from the foreground, so that it seems both near and immeasurably far. Fan Kuan built the rock faces with thousands of short texture strokes, called raindrop strokes, that give a sense of solid, gritty stone. The travellers are almost invisible, stressing the smallness of humans in the order of nature. The painting was long attributed to him, and in 1958 his signature was found hidden among the leaves. It has inspired painters for nearly a thousand years.
