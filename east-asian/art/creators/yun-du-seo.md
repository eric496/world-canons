## Biography

Yun Du-seo was born into the eminent Haenam Yun family and passed the lower civil examination in 1693, but because his faction, the Southerners, was out of power he never held office. He lived in Seoul and later on the family estate in Haenam, studying geography, astronomy, mathematics and painting in the spirit of practical learning (silhak). His son Yun Deok-hui and grandson Yun Yong were also painters.

Yun Du-seo painted horses, figures and early genre scenes of ordinary people working, which anticipated the genre painting of Kim Hong-do. His Self-portrait, owned by his descendants in Haenam and designated a National Treasure, shows only his face and beard, staring at the viewer with extraordinary intensity, painted in fine lines.

## Key artworks

### Self-portrait (c. 1710)

[![Self-portrait](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/Yun_Du-seo.jpg/1280px-Yun_Du-seo.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yun_Du-seo.jpg)

<sub>Yun Du-seo, Self-portrait Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yun_Du-seo.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1710
- **Type:** Ink and light colour on paper
- **Where to see it:** Haenam Yun family collection (Nogudang), Haenam
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Self-portrait%20Yun%20Du-seo)

Yun Du-seo's self-portrait shows his face alone, frontal, with intense, staring eyes and a large beard painted hair by hair, floating on the paper without a body. Infrared and X-ray studies have shown that ears and the outline of a robe and body were originally drawn in faint lines that have since faded, so its striking appearance is partly due to time. The work reflects Joseon portraiture's ideal of conveying the spirit through exact likeness. Painted by a scholar barred from office, it has been read as a gaze of self-scrutiny. It is National Treasure No. 240.
