## Biography

Ni Zan was born to a wealthy landowning family in Wuxi and lived as a cultivated gentleman, collecting books, art and antiquities in his Qingbige pavilion. Many stories tell of his fastidiousness. In the 1350s, amid rebellion and heavy taxation, he gave away much of his property and spent about twenty years travelling by boat on the lakes of the Yangzi delta, staying with friends and painting for them.

Ni Zan's landscapes are built from a nearly unvarying formula: a near shore with a few thin trees and an empty pavilion, a wide stretch of blank water, and low distant hills, drawn with dry, pale ink. They were seen as the perfect expression of the noble recluse's detachment, and owning or imitating a Ni Zan became a mark of taste. The Rongxi Studio of 1372 in Taipei is one of his best-known works.

## Key artworks

### The Rongxi Studio (1372)

[![The Rongxi Studio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/09/Ni_Zan._The_Rongxi_Studio.1372._74%2C7x35%2C3._National_Palace_Museum%2C_Taipei.jpg/1280px-Ni_Zan._The_Rongxi_Studio.1372._74%2C7x35%2C3._National_Palace_Museum%2C_Taipei.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ni_Zan._The_Rongxi_Studio.1372._74%2C7x35%2C3._National_Palace_Museum%2C_Taipei.jpg)

<sub>Ni Zan, The Rongxi Studio, 1372 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ni_Zan._The_Rongxi_Studio.1372._74%2C7x35%2C3._National_Palace_Museum%2C_Taipei.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1372
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink on paper
- **Where to see it:** National Palace Museum, Taipei
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Rongxi%20Studio%20Ni%20Zan)

In the foreground a few slender trees rise from rocks beside an empty thatched pavilion; across a wide expanse of blank paper, representing water, are low distant hills. The ink is pale and dry, applied with a slanted brush in sideways strokes. Ni Zan painted it for a physician called Rengzhong, adding a poem describing it as the studio of his friend; the name, Rongxi Studio, means a studio just big enough to fit one's knees, an image of contentment with little. The absence of people and the restraint of the composition expressed for later scholars the purity and detachment of the recluse. The painting is one of his most famous and was much copied.
