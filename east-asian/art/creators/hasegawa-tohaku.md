# Hasegawa Tōhaku

*長谷川等伯*

**1539 – 1610** · Japanese  
Born: Nanao, Noto province · Died: Edo  
Movements & schools: [Momoyama and Edo decorative painting (Kanō and Rinpa)](../movements/kano-rinpa.md), [Muromachi ink painting](../movements/muromachi-ink.md)  

> Momoyama painter and rival of the Kanō school, master of gold-leaf screens and of the misty ink masterpiece Pine Trees.

## Biography

Tōhaku was born in Nanao in Noto province and was adopted into a family of dyers; he began as a painter of Buddhist images. Around 1571 he moved to Kyoto, where he associated with the tea master Sen no Rikyū and studied the Chinese paintings in Zen temple collections. He founded the Hasegawa school, competed with the Kanō for major commissions, and in 1592 painted screens with his son Kyūzō for Hideyoshi's memorial temple for his dead son, now in Chishaku-in. He died in Edo shortly after travelling there at the invitation of Tokugawa Ieyasu.

Tōhaku called himself the fifth-generation follower of Sesshū and admired the ink paintings of Muqi. His Pine Trees screens in the Tokyo National Museum, in which pines emerge from and disappear into mist, are considered the masterpiece of Japanese ink painting, while the Chishaku-in maple and cherry panels show his mastery of gold and colour.

## Key artworks

### Pine Trees (Shōrin-zu byōbu) (late 16th century)

[![Pine Trees (Shōrin-zu byōbu)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Hasegawa_Tohaku_-_Pine_Trees_%28Sh%C5%8Drin-zu_by%C5%8Dbu%29_-_right_hand_screen.jpg/1280px-Hasegawa_Tohaku_-_Pine_Trees_%28Sh%C5%8Drin-zu_by%C5%8Dbu%29_-_right_hand_screen.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hasegawa_Tohaku_-_Pine_Trees_%28Sh%C5%8Drin-zu_by%C5%8Dbu%29_-_right_hand_screen.jpg)

<sub>Hasegawa Tōhaku, Pine Trees, right screen Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hasegawa_Tohaku_-_Pine_Trees_%28Sh%C5%8Drin-zu_by%C5%8Dbu%29_-_right_hand_screen.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** late 16th century
- **Type:** Pair of six-panel folding screens, ink on paper
- **Where to see it:** Tokyo National Museum
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pine%20Trees%20%28Sh%C5%8Drin-zu%20by%C5%8Dbu%29%20Hasegawa%20T%C5%8Dhaku)

On two six-panel screens, pine trees stand in clusters across empty paper, some dark and close, others pale and fading, so that mist seems to fill the space between them, with a faint mountain in the distance. Tōhaku painted them with a rough brush, perhaps made of straw, in swift wet and dry strokes. The paper is relatively coarse, and the panels seem to have been arranged in an unusual order, leading some scholars to suggest they were preliminary drafts. The screens echo the misty landscapes of Muqi, whom Tōhaku admired, but evoke a Japanese coastal landscape. They are a National Treasure and often called the masterpiece of Japanese ink painting.
