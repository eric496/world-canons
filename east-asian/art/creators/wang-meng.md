# Wang Meng

*王蒙*

**c. 1308 – 1385** · Chinese  
Born: Huzhou, Zhejiang · Died: Nanjing (in prison)  
Movements & schools: [Yuan literati painting](../movements/yuan-literati.md)  

> Youngest of the Four Masters of the Yuan, a grandson of Zhao Mengfu, known for dense, writhing landscapes such as Dwelling in the Qingbian Mountains.

## Biography

Wang Meng was the son of Zhao Mengfu's daughter Zhao Yong and the scholar Wang Guoqi. He held minor posts under the Yuan and lived for some years as a recluse in the Yellow Crane Mountain near Hangzhou. After the founding of the Ming he served as a local official in Shandong, but he was implicated in the purge following the fall of the chief minister Hu Weiyong in 1380 and died in prison in 1385.

Wang Meng's landscapes are crowded with mountains that twist and heave, covered with ox-hair texture strokes and dotted vegetation, leaving little empty space. Dwelling in the Qingbian Mountains of 1366 in the Shanghai Museum was called by Dong Qichang the finest painting in the world. His restless energy contrasts with the calm of Ni Zan and influenced later painters including Shen Zhou and Wang Hui.

## Key artworks

### Dwelling in the Qingbian Mountains (1366)

- **Date:** 1366
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink on paper
- **Where to see it:** Shanghai Museum
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dwelling%20in%20the%20Qingbian%20Mountains%20Wang%20Meng)

The Qingbian (Bian) mountains near Wang Meng's home of Huzhou were the site of the retreat of his cousin, and in 1366, as the Yuan dynasty was collapsing in war, he painted this tall landscape. Mountains rise in writhing, rippling masses to the top of the paper, covered with ox-hair and unravelled-rope texture strokes and thick dots of vegetation; a tiny scholar sits in a hut in a fold of the hills. The crowding and agitation of the forms have been read as expressing the anxiety of the times. Dong Qichang praised it in an inscription in 1616. It is one of the masterpieces of Yuan painting.
