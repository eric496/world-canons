# Goryeo Buddhist painters

*고려 불화 화가*

**c. 1270 – c. 1392** · Korean  
Born: Gaegyeong (Kaesong), Korea · Died: ?  
Movements & schools: [Song and Goryeo ceramics](../movements/song-goryeo-ceramics.md)  

> The court and professional painters of late Goryeo who made luxurious Buddhist paintings in gold and mineral colours, most of which survive in Japan.

## Biography

Buddhism was the state religion of the Goryeo dynasty (918–1392), and in the late thirteenth and fourteenth centuries, under Mongol Yuan overlordship, the royal family and aristocracy commissioned devotional paintings on silk for temples and private chapels. The painters were usually anonymous professionals or court painters, though a few, such as Seo Gubang and Kim U-mun, are known from inscriptions. Subjects include Amitabha welcoming souls to the Pure Land, Ksitigarbha, and above all the Water-Moon Avalokiteshvara.

Goryeo Buddhist paintings are celebrated for their refinement: translucent veils painted over brilliant robes, fine gold line and patterning, and colours applied partly from the back of the silk. About 160 survive, most of them in Japanese temples and museums, where they arrived through trade, gifts and the looting of the sixteenth-century invasions; only a handful remain in Korea. Their rediscovery in the late twentieth century made them a focus of Korean art history.

## Key artworks

### Water-Moon Avalokiteshvara (c. 1310–1350)

[![Water-Moon Avalokiteshvara](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fa/Goryeo-Avalokiteshvara-1310-kagami_Jinjya_Temple.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Goryeo-Avalokiteshvara-1310-kagami_Jinjya_Temple.jpg)

<sub>Water-Moon Avalokiteshvara, 1310, Kagami Shrine, Karatsu Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Goryeo-Avalokiteshvara-1310-kagami_Jinjya_Temple.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1310–1350
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink, colour and gold on silk
- **Where to see it:** Kagami Shrine, Karatsu (on deposit at Saga Prefectural Museum), and other collections in Japan and Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Water-Moon%20Avalokiteshvara%20Goryeo%20Buddhist%20painters)

Goryeo Buddhist paintings, made mainly in the fourteenth century for the court and aristocracy, are among the most luxurious of East Asian religious images, and about 160 survive, most of them in Japan. The Water-Moon Avalokiteshvara shows the bodhisattva seated on a rocky ledge by the sea on Mount Potalaka, with a willow branch in a vase, bamboo behind, and the pilgrim boy Sudhana below asking for guidance. The figure wears a translucent white veil painted over brilliant red, green and gold, and the patterns are painted in gold line with extraordinary finesse. The paintings were made by professional painters, sometimes named in inscriptions, such as Seo Gubang. They express the devout luxury of the late Goryeo elite.
