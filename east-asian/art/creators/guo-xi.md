# Guo Xi

*郭熙*

**c. 1020 – c. 1090** · Chinese  
Born: Wenxian, Henan · Died: ?  
Movements & schools: [Monumental landscape (Five Dynasties & Northern Song)](../movements/song-landscape.md)  

> Leading landscape painter at the court of Emperor Shenzong and author of an influential treatise on landscape; painted Early Spring (1072).

## Biography

Guo Xi came from Wenxian in Henan and became the favourite court painter of Emperor Shenzong (r. 1067–85), who had his large landscapes hung in palace halls and government offices. His son Guo Si compiled his teachings as The Lofty Message of Forests and Streams (Linquan gaozhi), one of the most important texts on Chinese landscape painting. After Shenzong's death, Emperor Huizong preferred other styles, and Guo Xi's paintings are said to have been used to wipe tables in the palace.

Guo Xi's treatise explains that landscapes allow the viewer to wander in the mountains while staying at home, and describes three kinds of distance: high, deep and level. It also notes that mountains change with the seasons and the time of day. His Early Spring, dated 1072, shows a mountain dissolving in mist, with twisting crab-claw trees, and uses several viewpoints in a single painting. Through the treatise and his style he influenced landscape painting across China, Korea and Japan.

## Key artworks

### Early Spring (1072)

<sub>Guo Xi, Early Spring, 1072 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Guo_Xi_-_Early_Spring_%28large%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1072
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink and light colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** National Palace Museum, Taipei
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Early%20Spring%20Guo%20Xi)

Signed and dated in the year 1072, Early Spring was painted while Guo Xi was court painter to Emperor Shenzong. A central mountain rises in an S-curve, built of swelling forms that seem to move, with twisting, claw-like trees, waterfalls, pavilions and small figures of fishermen and travellers. Guo Xi uses shifting viewpoints, the high, deep and level distances described in his treatise, so the eye can wander from the foreground to the summit. Washes of mist suggest the moist air of early spring and the awakening of nature. The painting is one of the few securely dated Northern Song masterpieces and a classic of Chinese landscape.
