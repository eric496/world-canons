## Biography

Wang Xizhi came from the powerful Wang clan of Langya, which moved south when northern China fell to invaders in the early fourth century. He served the Eastern Jin dynasty as an official, eventually as general of the right army and governor of Kuaiji (Shaoxing), before retiring to a life of Daoist pursuits. In 353 he hosted a gathering of friends at the Orchid Pavilion near Kuaiji, where they drank wine and composed poems; his preface to the collection became legendary. Stories tell of his love of geese, whose curving necks were said to inspire his brushwork.

Wang Xizhi's running and cursive scripts combined elegance and strength with a natural, unforced rhythm, and they became the classical model for all later calligraphy. Emperor Taizong of Tang collected his work passionately and, according to tradition, was buried with the original Orchid Pavilion Preface. No undisputed original survives; his writing is known through tracing copies and stone-engraved rubbings, and calligraphers in China, Korea and Japan have copied these for over 1,500 years. His son Wang Xianzhi was also a celebrated calligrapher.

## Key artworks

### Preface to the Poems Composed at the Orchid Pavilion (Lantingji Xu) (353 (original lost; Tang copies))

[![Preface to the Poems Composed at the Orchid Pavilion (Lantingji Xu)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1e/XingshuLantingxv.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:XingshuLantingxv.jpg)

<sub>Tang tracing copy of the Lantingji Xu Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:XingshuLantingxv.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 353 (original lost; Tang copies)
- **Type:** Calligraphy in running script; handscroll copies
- **Where to see it:** Palace Museum, Beijing (Shenlong copy)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Preface%20to%20the%20Poems%20Composed%20at%20the%20Orchid%20Pavilion%20%28Lantingji%20Xu%29%20Wang%20Xizhi)

On the third day of the third month of 353, Wang Xizhi and forty-one friends gathered at the Orchid Pavilion near Shaoxing for a spring purification festival, floating wine cups on a winding stream and composing poems. Wang wrote a preface to the collection, 324 characters reflecting on the pleasure of the day and the brevity of life. Written spontaneously and with corrections, the manuscript was treasured by his descendants until, according to tradition, Emperor Taizong of Tang obtained it and had it buried with him. Taizong ordered court calligraphers to make copies, and the version known as the Shenlong copy, perhaps by Feng Chengsu, is thought closest to the original. The text has been copied by calligraphers ever since, and the gathering itself became a favourite subject of painting.
