# Liang Kai

*梁楷*

**c. 1140 – c. 1210** · Chinese  
Born: Shandong · Died: ?  
Movements & schools: [Song academy & literati painting](../movements/song-academy-literati.md)  

> Southern Song painter who left the court academy and painted in an abbreviated, spontaneous ink style, as in the Immortal in Splashed Ink.

## Biography

Liang Kai was a painter-in-attendance at the academy in Hangzhou in the early thirteenth century and is said to have been awarded the golden belt, which he hung up in the academy before leaving. He was nicknamed Madman Liang for his free behaviour and liking for wine, and he seems to have associated with Chan monks after leaving court.

His early works are careful and detailed, but he is famous for his abbreviated brush (jianbi) paintings, in which a figure is suggested with a few wet strokes, as in the Immortal in Splashed Ink and the portrait of Li Bai Strolling and Chanting, both in Tokyo. Like Muqi, he was more admired in Japan than in China, and his works became treasures of Japanese collections.

## Key artworks

### Immortal in Splashed Ink (early 13th century)

[![Immortal in Splashed Ink](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/84/Liang_Kai_-_Immortal_in_Splashed_Ink.jpg/1280px-Liang_Kai_-_Immortal_in_Splashed_Ink.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Liang_Kai_-_Immortal_in_Splashed_Ink.jpg)

<sub>Liang Kai, Immortal in Splashed Ink, Tokyo National Museum Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Liang_Kai_-_Immortal_in_Splashed_Ink.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** early 13th century
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink on paper
- **Where to see it:** Tokyo National Museum
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Immortal%20in%20Splashed%20Ink%20Liang%20Kai)

A plump, bald figure with drooping eyebrows shuffles forward, his robe painted with a handful of sweeping, wet strokes and his face with a few touches of fine line. The figure, perhaps a Daoist immortal or an eccentric, seems both comic and wise. The method, called splashed ink or abbreviated brush, relied on speed and confidence and was linked with Chan Buddhist ideas of sudden enlightenment. Liang Kai had left the court academy, and his free work was little appreciated by Chinese critics but highly valued in Japan, where this scroll is a National Treasure.
