# Wu Changshuo

*吳昌碩*

**1844 – 1927** · Chinese  
Born: Anji, Zhejiang · Died: Shanghai  
Movements & schools: [Shanghai school and modern Chinese ink](../movements/shanghai-school.md)  

> Painter, calligrapher and seal carver who brought the archaic strength of stone and bronze inscriptions into flower painting; a leader of the late Shanghai school.

## Biography

Wu Changshuo (born Wu Junqing) grew up in a scholarly family in Zhejiang. The Taiping war killed most of his family and drove him into years of wandering. He worked as a minor official and private secretary, studied ancient inscriptions and seal carving, and in his forties began to paint seriously, encouraged by Ren Bonian. He settled in Shanghai, where he became the dominant figure in the art world, and in 1904 he became the first president of the Xiling Seal Engravers' Society in Hangzhou.

Wu Changshuo painted plum blossoms, chrysanthemums, wisteria and rocks with the thick, rough brush of a calligrapher who had studied the Stone Drum inscriptions, using strong colour and dense inscriptions. His fusion of poetry, calligraphy, painting and seal carving made him the last great master of the literati ideal, and his influence extended to Qi Baishi, Pan Tianshou and many Japanese collectors and artists.

## Key artworks

### Plum Blossoms (early 20th century)

[![Plum Blossoms](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Wu_Changshuo_-_Red_plum_blossoms_and_rock_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Wu_Changshuo_-_Red_plum_blossoms_and_rock_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wu_Changshuo_-_Red_plum_blossoms_and_rock_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Wu Changshuo, Red Plum Blossoms and Rock Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wu_Changshuo_-_Red_plum_blossoms_and_rock_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** early 20th century
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink and colour on paper
- **Where to see it:** Shanghai Museum and other collections
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Plum%20Blossoms%20Wu%20Changshuo)

Plum blossom, which flowers in late winter, was the emblem of the scholar's endurance, and Wu Changshuo painted it throughout his career. Branches cross the paper diagonally, drawn with thick, dry strokes like characters in the ancient Stone Drum script he had copied for decades, and the blossoms are dotted in pink or red with dark centres. The composition is balanced by a block of inscription and red seal impressions he carved himself. The result unites poetry, calligraphy, painting and seal carving in one work, the ideal of the literati tradition, with a new bold force and bright colour that appealed to the Shanghai public.
