# Hon'ami Kōetsu

*本阿弥光悦*

**1558 – 1637** · Japanese  
Born: Kyoto · Died: Takagamine, Kyoto  
Movements & schools: [Momoyama and Edo decorative painting (Kanō and Rinpa)](../movements/kano-rinpa.md)  

> Calligrapher, potter and lacquer designer from a family of sword connoisseurs, co-founder with Sōtatsu of the Rinpa style.

## Biography

Kōetsu was born into the Hon'ami family, which polished and appraised swords for the shoguns and the great warrior families. He became famous as one of the Three Brushes of the Kan'ei era for his calligraphy, made tea bowls in raku ware, and designed lacquer and printed books. In 1615 Tokugawa Ieyasu granted him land at Takagamine in north Kyoto, where he founded an artistic community of craftsmen bound together by the Nichiren Buddhist faith.

Kōetsu's collaborations with Tawaraya Sōtatsu, scrolls in which his calligraphy of classical poems flows over Sōtatsu's gold and silver paintings of deer, cranes and flowers, revived the courtly aesthetic of the Heian period in a bold modern form. He is regarded as one of the founders of Rinpa, and his tea bowls, such as Fujisan, are National Treasures.

## Key artworks

### Crane Scroll (Tsuru shita-e waka kan) (early 17th century)

- **Date:** early 17th century
- **Type:** Handscroll, ink, gold and silver on paper
- **Where to see it:** Kyoto National Museum
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Crane%20Scroll%20%28Tsuru%20shita-e%20waka%20kan%29%20Hon%27ami%20K%C5%8Detsu)

In this collaboration, Tawaraya Sōtatsu painted flocks of cranes in gold and silver pigments across a long scroll of paper, starting with birds standing on the shore, then rising, flying in long lines and finally landing again. Over this underpainting Hon'ami Kōetsu wrote thirty-six waka poems by the Thirty-six Immortal Poets of the Heian period, his characters swelling and thinning in rhythm with the flight of the birds. The partnership revived the Heian practice of writing poetry on decorated paper but with a new boldness of scale and abstraction. The scroll, nearly fourteen metres long, is an Important Cultural Property and a founding work of Rinpa.
