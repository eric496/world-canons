# Park Soo-keun

*박수근 (朴壽根)*

**1914 – 1965** · Korean  
Born: Yanggu, Gangwon · Died: Seoul  
Movements & schools: [Postwar avant-gardes](../movements/postwar-avant-garde.md)  

> Self-taught Korean painter of ordinary people, women and children, painted in a thick granite-like texture that recalls ancient stone carvings.

## Biography

Park Soo-keun grew up in poverty in Gangwon province and was largely self-taught; he decided to become a painter after seeing a reproduction of Millet's Angelus. He exhibited at the Joseon Art Exhibition from 1932. After the Korean War, during which he fled south, he lived in Seoul working for the US Army post exchange painting portraits, and sold paintings mainly to foreigners. He died poor in 1965.

Park's paintings show women carrying babies or selling vegetables, children and bare trees in muted earth tones with a heavily layered, rough surface inspired by Silla stone reliefs. They capture the hardship and dignity of postwar Korean life, and he is now regarded as one of the most beloved Korean painters. His works are under copyright.

## Key artworks

### The Washing Place (1950s)

- **Date:** 1950s
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Private collection
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Washing%20Place%20Park%20Soo-keun)

After the Korean War, Park Soo-keun painted the lives of ordinary people around him, especially women at work. In The Washing Place a row of women kneel beside a stream, beating and rinsing laundry. The figures are flattened into simple outlines and set in a shallow space, and the surface is built up of many layers of thick paint, scraped and dried, until it resembles granite, which Park linked to the reliefs of ancient Korean stone carving. The muted palette and the dignity of the women give the painting a quiet, timeless quality. A version of the subject set an auction record for Korean art in 2007. Park's works are under copyright and are not reproduced here.
