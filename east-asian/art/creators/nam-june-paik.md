# Nam June Paik

*백남준 (白南準)*

**1932 – 2006** · Korean-American  
Born: Seoul · Died: Miami  
Movements & schools: [Postwar avant-gardes](../movements/postwar-avant-garde.md)  

> Korean-born artist regarded as the founder of video art, a member of Fluxus, whose works include TV Buddha and vast multi-monitor installations.

## Biography

Nam June Paik was born in Seoul to a wealthy family that fled the Korean War to Hong Kong and Japan. He studied aesthetics at the University of Tokyo and music in Germany, where he met John Cage and joined the Fluxus movement. In 1963 he showed altered television sets in Wuppertal, and after moving to New York in 1964 he used one of the first portable video cameras, working with the cellist Charlotte Moorman and engineers on video synthesisers. He died in Miami in 2006.

Paik foresaw the importance of television and electronic networks, coining the phrase electronic superhighway. TV Buddha (1974), in which a statue of the Buddha watches its own image on a monitor, is a meditation on technology and contemplation. His works are under copyright.

## Key artworks

### TV Buddha (1974)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Stedelijk Museum Amsterdam →](https://www.stedelijk.nl)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1974
- **Type:** Video installation: Buddha statue, monitor and closed-circuit camera
- **Where to see it:** Stedelijk Museum, Amsterdam
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=TV%20Buddha%20Nam%20June%20Paik)

Nam June Paik first made TV Buddha in 1974 for an exhibition in New York, reportedly to fill a gap in the show. An old seated Buddha statue faces a television monitor, and a closed-circuit video camera behind the monitor films the statue, so that the Buddha sits watching its own image in an endless present. The work brings together Eastern contemplation and Western technology, the ancient and the electronic, and the narcissism of watching oneself on screen. Paik made many later versions. It is one of the defining works of video art. Paik's works are under copyright and are not reproduced here.
