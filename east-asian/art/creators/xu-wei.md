# Xu Wei

*徐渭*

**1521 – 1593** · Chinese  
Born: Shaoxing, Zhejiang · Died: Shaoxing  
Movements & schools: [Ming painting & porcelain](../movements/ming-painting-porcelain.md)  

> Painter, calligrapher, playwright and poet whose wild ink paintings of flowers and grapes founded the freely expressive xieyi tradition.

## Biography

Xu Wei was born in Shaoxing to a declining family and failed the provincial examinations eight times. He served as secretary to Hu Zongxian, a general fighting coastal pirates, but after Hu's arrest in 1565 he fell into severe mental illness, tried several times to kill himself and killed his wife, for which he spent seven years in prison. In later life he lived in poverty, selling paintings and writing.

Xu Wei wrote plays and poems and claimed that his calligraphy came first, his poetry second, his prose third and his painting last. His flowers, grapes and bamboo, painted in splashed, running ink with wild calligraphic energy, broke with the restraint of the Wu school. He was a major influence on Bada Shanren, Shitao, the Yangzhou eccentrics, Wu Changshuo and Qi Baishi, who said he wished he could have ground ink for Xu Wei.

## Key artworks

### Ink Grapes (late 16th century)

[![Ink Grapes](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/41/Xu_Wei_Grapes.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Xu_Wei_Grapes.jpg)

<sub>Xu Wei, Ink Grapes Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Xu_Wei_Grapes.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** late 16th century
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink on paper
- **Where to see it:** Palace Museum, Beijing
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ink%20Grapes%20Xu%20Wei)

A vine hangs down the paper in sweeping, wet strokes, with grapes made of blots of ink in different tones and leaves that blur into each other. Xu Wei wrote a poem at the top, in a rough, tilting hand, saying that he is half his life poor and old, standing alone in his study in the evening wind, and that his bright pearls have nowhere to be sold and are thrown among wild vines. The painting merges calligraphy, poetry and image into a single expression of frustration and genius. It became a model for the freely expressive xieyi style of later painters.
