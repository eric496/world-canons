# Hishikawa Moronobu

*菱川師宣*

**c. 1618 – 1694** · Japanese  
Born: Hota, Awa province (Chiba) · Died: Edo  
Movements & schools: [Ukiyo-e](../movements/ukiyo-e.md)  

> Edo painter and book illustrator regarded as the founder of ukiyo-e, who turned the woodblock illustration into an independent art.

<sub>Portrait: Self-portrait · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Self-portrait_by_Hishikawa_Moronobu.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Moronobu was the son of a textile embroiderer and dyer in Awa province on the Bōsō peninsula, and he learned design in his father's trade. He moved to Edo around the 1660s, studied Tosa and Kanō painting, and became the most prolific book illustrator of his time, producing more than a hundred illustrated books on the pleasure quarters, kabuki, famous places and erotic subjects.

Moronobu gave the illustration a strong, flowing black line and began issuing single-sheet prints not tied to a text, which made him the first great ukiyo-e master. He also painted handscrolls and screens of the pleasure districts. His painting Beauty Looking Back, in the Tokyo National Museum, created the type of the elegantly turning courtesan that later artists repeated for two centuries.

## Key artworks

### Beauty Looking Back (Mikaeri bijin-zu) (late 17th century)

- **Date:** late 17th century
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink and colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** Tokyo National Museum
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Beauty%20Looking%20Back%20%28Mikaeri%20bijin-zu%29%20Hishikawa%20Moronobu)

A woman walks to the left, then turns her head to look back, revealing the fashionable obi tied in a high knot and the floral pattern of her bright red kimono. The pose shows off the clothes and the elegant line of her body. The painting is unsigned except for a seal and an inscription reading 'Hishikawa Moronobu', which some scholars think may be of a pupil. As a painting, not a print, it was more expensive and made for a wealthy client. The image became one of the icons of Japanese art and appeared on a famous postage stamp of 1948.
