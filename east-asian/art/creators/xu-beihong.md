# Xu Beihong

*徐悲鴻*

**1895 – 1953** · Chinese  
Born: Yixing, Jiangsu · Died: Beijing  
Movements & schools: [Shanghai school and modern Chinese ink](../movements/shanghai-school.md)  

> Paris-trained painter who championed Western realism and academic drawing in Chinese art education; best known for galloping horses in ink.

## Biography

Xu Beihong was the son of a village painter and teacher in Jiangsu. He moved to Shanghai, studied briefly in Tokyo, and in 1919 went to Paris on a government scholarship, studying at the École nationale supérieure des Beaux-Arts and travelling in Europe until 1927. Back in China he taught in Nanjing and Beijing, organised exhibitions abroad to raise funds during the war with Japan, and after 1949 became president of the Central Academy of Fine Arts.

Xu Beihong believed Chinese painting had declined since the Ming and should be revived with Western realism, anatomy and drawing from life. He painted large historical subjects in oil and ink, but is best known for horses in ink, painted with broad, wet strokes and accurate anatomy. Through his teaching, realism became the foundation of art education in the People's Republic.

## Key artworks

### Horses in ink (1940s)

[![Horses in ink](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cd/Horse_Standing_by_Xu_Beihong%2C_1940.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Horse_Standing_by_Xu_Beihong%2C_1940.jpg)

<sub>Xu Beihong, Horse Standing, 1940 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Horse_Standing_by_Xu_Beihong%2C_1940.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1940s
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink on paper
- **Where to see it:** Xu Beihong Memorial Museum, Beijing (and other versions)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Horses%20in%20ink%20Xu%20Beihong)

Xu Beihong painted horses throughout his life, using his knowledge of anatomy from drawing in Paris and observation of horses in the Central Asian borderlands and in Singapore. In his galloping horses the animal runs with flying mane and tail, often seen in steep foreshortening, the bones and muscles indicated with a few broad, wet strokes and the hooves with sharp touches. During the war against Japan these spirited animals were seen as symbols of the nation's will to resist. They became the most famous of all his works and models for later ink painters.
