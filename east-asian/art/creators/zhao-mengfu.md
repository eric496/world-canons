## Biography

Zhao Mengfu was a descendant of the founder of the Song dynasty. After the Mongol conquest he lived in retirement in Huzhou until 1286, when he accepted a summons to serve the Yuan court of Khubilai Khan, a decision that later loyalist critics held against him. He rose to high office in the Hanlin Academy. His wife Guan Daosheng was a noted painter and calligrapher of bamboo.

Zhao Mengfu argued that painting must have the spirit of antiquity (guyi) and turned back from Southern Song styles to Tang and Five Dynasties masters. He painted horses, figures, bamboo and landscapes, and his Autumn Colors on the Qiao and Hua Mountains of 1295 is a turning point toward the scholarly landscape. His calligraphy, in all scripts, became a standard model for centuries, and his insistence that calligraphy and painting share the same brushwork shaped the Yuan literati tradition.

## Key artworks

### Autumn Colors on the Qiao and Hua Mountains (1295)

- **Date:** 1295
- **Type:** Handscroll, ink and colour on paper
- **Where to see it:** National Palace Museum, Taipei
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Autumn%20Colors%20on%20the%20Qiao%20and%20Hua%20Mountains%20Zhao%20Mengfu)

Zhao Mengfu painted this short scroll after returning from service in the north, for his friend Zhou Mi, whose family came from Shandong but who had never been there. It shows two mountains near Jinan, the pointed Mount Hua and the rounded Mount Qiao, across a flat marshy plain with trees, houses, fishermen and goats. The style deliberately looks back to Tang and Five Dynasties painters such as Dong Yuan, with simple forms, archaic perspective and restrained colour, rather than to the dramatic manner of the Southern Song. It embodies Zhao's idea of the spirit of antiquity and opened the way for the Four Yuan Masters. The Qianlong Emperor added several inscriptions.
