# Li Tang

*李唐*

**c. 1050 – c. 1130** · Chinese  
Born: Heyang, Henan · Died: Lin'an (Hangzhou)  
Movements & schools: [Monumental landscape (Five Dynasties & Northern Song)](../movements/song-landscape.md), [Song academy & literati painting](../movements/song-academy-literati.md)  

> Song court painter who bridged the Northern and Southern Song, moving from monumental landscape to the axe-cut strokes and close-up views of the Southern Song academy.

## Biography

Li Tang served in Emperor Huizong's painting academy in Kaifeng. When the Jurchen captured the capital in 1127 he fled south, and according to tradition he was found by bandits who discovered his painting materials and let him go. He rejoined the court of Emperor Gaozong in Hangzhou, where he was made head of the reconstituted academy despite his great age.

His Whispering Pines in the Mountains, dated 1124 and now in the National Palace Museum, Taipei, still follows the monumental Northern Song type. In his later works the composition moves closer and the strokes broaden into the large axe-cut texture strokes that became characteristic of Southern Song painting. He was the teacher, directly or through his followers, of the Ma–Xia school, and is the essential link between the two halves of the Song tradition.

## Key artworks

### Whispering Pines in the Mountains (1124)

[![Whispering Pines in the Mountains](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f3/Li_Tang_-_Wind_in_Pines_Among_a_Myriad_Valleys.jpg/1280px-Li_Tang_-_Wind_in_Pines_Among_a_Myriad_Valleys.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Li_Tang_-_Wind_in_Pines_Among_a_Myriad_Valleys.jpg)

<sub>Li Tang, Whispering Pines in the Mountains, 1124 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Li_Tang_-_Wind_in_Pines_Among_a_Myriad_Valleys.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1124
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink and colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** National Palace Museum, Taipei
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Whispering%20Pines%20in%20the%20Mountains%20Li%20Tang)

A tall central peak rises behind a group of tall pine trees, with a waterfall, a stream and a small figure seated by the water. Li Tang's signature and the date are written on the distant peak. The painting still follows the monumental tradition of Fan Kuan, but the rocks are modelled with broader, blockier axe-cut strokes and the composition is more compact. Three years later Kaifeng fell to the Jurchen and Li Tang fled south, where his style evolved further, so this work is an important document of the transition from Northern to Southern Song painting.
