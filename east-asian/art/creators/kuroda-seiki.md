# Kuroda Seiki

*黒田清輝*

**1866 – 1924** · Japanese  
Born: Kagoshima · Died: Tokyo  
Movements & schools: [Nihonga and yōga in modern Japan](../movements/nihonga-yoga.md)  

> Founder of modern Western-style painting (yōga) in Japan, who brought plein-air Impressionist light back from Paris; painted Lakeside (1897).

## Biography

Kuroda was born in Kagoshima into a samurai family of the Satsuma domain and adopted by his uncle, a viscount. In 1884 he went to Paris to study law but turned to painting, studying with the academic painter Raphaël Collin and working at Grez-sur-Loing. Returning in 1893, he founded the Hakubakai society and in 1896 became the head of the new Western painting department at the Tokyo School of Fine Arts. He also served in the House of Peers.

Kuroda introduced the bright palette and outdoor light of French plein-air painting, sometimes called the purple school for its violet shadows. His nude Morning Toilette caused a scandal when shown in Kyoto in 1895. Lakeside, a portrait of his future wife at Lake Ashi, is one of the best-loved Japanese oil paintings, and through his teaching he shaped Japanese yōga for decades.

## Key artworks

### Lakeside (Kohan) (1897)

<sub>Kuroda Seiki, Lakeside, 1897 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kuroda-seiki-kohan00-6-1b.jpeg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1897
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Kuroda Memorial Hall, Tokyo National Museum, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Lakeside%20%28Kohan%29%20Kuroda%20Seiki)

In the summer of 1897 Kuroda stayed at Hakone and painted Kaneko Teruko, who later became his wife, seated on a rock beside Lake Ashi with a round fan, the water and mountains behind her in pale blue. The painting uses the light palette and soft, natural light of the French plein-air painting he had learned from Raphaël Collin, but the subject, pose and mood are quietly Japanese. It was shown at the Hakubakai exhibition and later at the Paris Exposition of 1900. It is an Important Cultural Property and appeared on a Japanese postage stamp.
