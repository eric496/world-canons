## Biography

Shitao, born Zhu Ruoji, was a descendant of the Ming royal house. In 1645 his father was killed in a power struggle among Ming loyalists, and the infant was saved by a servant and raised as a Buddhist monk. He travelled widely, lived in Xuancheng and Nanjing, twice met the Kangxi Emperor on his southern tours, and spent three years in Beijing seeking patronage. Late in life he left the Buddhist order and settled in Yangzhou as a professional painter.

Shitao's paintings range widely, from wet, spontaneous landscapes and orchids to detailed albums of real places such as Mount Huang. His treatise Remarks on Painting (Huayu lu) centres on the single brushstroke (yihua) and rejects slavish imitation of the ancients. He influenced the Yangzhou eccentrics and was taken up in the twentieth century as a forerunner of modern individualism; Zhang Daqian forged his works so well that some still hang in museums.

## Key artworks

### Reminiscences of Qinhuai River (c. 1695)

[![Reminiscences of Qinhuai River](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9f/Shitao-Qinhuai.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shitao-Qinhuai.jpg)

<sub>Shitao, leaf from Reminiscences of Qinhuai River Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shitao-Qinhuai.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1695
- **Type:** Album of eight leaves, ink and colour on paper
- **Where to see it:** Cleveland Museum of Art
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Reminiscences%20of%20Qinhuai%20River%20Shitao)

Shitao lived in Nanjing for several years in the 1680s, and this album recalls places along the Qinhuai River and around the city in eight leaves, each with a poem. The most famous shows a small figure in a boat bowing to a great overhanging cliff that seems to lean towards him, a humorous image of the bond between the artist and nature. Other leaves show plum blossoms, river villages and rocks in fresh colour and free, varied brushwork. The album shows Shitao's insistence, in his treatise on painting, on finding his own method and expressing direct experience rather than copying the old masters.
