# Jōchō

*定朝*

**c. 1000 – 1057** · Japanese  
Born: Kyoto · Died: Kyoto  
Movements & schools: [Buddhist sculpture and cave temples](../movements/buddhist-sculpture.md), [Yamato-e and narrative handscrolls](../movements/yamato-e-emaki.md)  

> Heian Buddhist sculptor who perfected the joined-wood technique and the serene, gentle style seen in the Amida Buddha of the Byōdō-in.

## Biography

Jōchō was the son or pupil of the sculptor Kōshō and headed a large workshop in Kyoto serving the Fujiwara regents and the imperial court. He made images for the temple Hōjō-ji built by Fujiwara no Michinaga, and in 1022 became the first sculptor to receive the Buddhist rank of hokkyō, raising the status of sculptors in Japan. His only surviving work is the Amida Nyorai in the Phoenix Hall of the Byōdō-in at Uji, completed in 1053.

Jōchō refined yosegi-zukuri, the technique of carving a statue from several hollowed blocks of wood and joining them, which allowed large, light sculptures to be produced by teams. His calm, round-faced Amida with shallow, rhythmic drapery became the standard for Buddhist images for the next century and a half, known as the Jōchō style, and his descendants founded the main lineages of Japanese Buddhist sculptors, including the Kei school of Unkei.

## Key artworks

### Amida Nyorai of the Phoenix Hall, Byōdō-in (1053)

- **Date:** 1053
- **Type:** Gilded wood, joined-block technique
- **Where to see it:** Phoenix Hall, Byōdō-in, Uji, Kyoto
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Amida%20Nyorai%20of%20the%20Phoenix%20Hall%2C%20By%C5%8Dd%C5%8D-in%20J%C5%8Dch%C5%8D)

In 1052 the regent Fujiwara no Yorimichi turned his villa at Uji into the temple Byōdō-in, and the next year dedicated the Phoenix Hall, whose wings and reflection in a pond were meant to evoke Amida's palace in the Pure Land. Inside sits the Amida Buddha by Jōchō, nearly three metres high, made of joined blocks of cypress wood, covered in lacquer and gold leaf. His calm round face, shallow-carved robes and relaxed posture express the promise of salvation that made Pure Land devotion popular in an age that believed the world was in decline. Above him on the walls fly fifty-two small wooden bodhisattvas playing instruments and dancing on clouds. The hall appears on the Japanese ten-yen coin.
