# Zhang Daqian

*張大千*

**1899 – 1983** · Chinese  
Born: Neijiang, Sichuan · Died: Taipei  
Movements & schools: [Shanghai school and modern Chinese ink](../movements/shanghai-school.md), [Postwar avant-gardes](../movements/postwar-avant-garde.md)  

> Prodigious traditional painter, collector and master forger who in later life invented a splashed-colour style close to abstraction.

## Biography

Zhang Daqian (Chang Dai-chien) was born in Sichuan and studied textile dyeing in Kyoto before training as a painter in Shanghai. He made his name in the 1920s and 30s as a brilliant imitator of Shitao and other old masters, producing forgeries that entered major collections. From 1941 to 1943 he spent more than two years copying the Buddhist murals at Dunhuang. After 1949 he left China, living in India, Argentina, Brazil and California, before settling in Taipei in 1978, where he died.

In the 1960s, partly because of failing eyesight, he developed splashed ink and colour (pomo pocai), pouring pigment on paper and then adding details, which was compared to Abstract Expressionism. He met Picasso in 1956. Zhang Daqian bridged the old literati world and the international art market, and his paintings are among the most expensive by any Chinese artist.

## Key artworks

### Splashed-colour landscapes (1960s–1983)

> 🖼️ **[View it at National Palace Museum, Taipei →](https://www.npm.gov.tw)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1960s–1983
- **Type:** Ink and colour on paper or silk
- **Where to see it:** National Palace Museum, Taipei, and other collections
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Splashed-colour%20landscapes%20Zhang%20Daqian)

From the early 1960s, while living in Brazil and California and suffering from failing eyesight, Zhang Daqian developed the technique of splashed ink and colour (pomo pocai). He poured ink and brilliant mineral blues and greens onto paper, tilting it so that they flowed and pooled, then added a few houses, trees or boats with a fine brush to turn the abstract forms into misty mountains. The method drew on the Tang painter Wang Mo's legendary splashed ink and on the blue-and-green tradition, but the effect recalled Abstract Expressionism. His last great work, the huge Panorama of Mount Lu (1981–83), is in the National Palace Museum, Taipei. Because Zhang died in 1983, these works are not reproduced here.
