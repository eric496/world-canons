# Cave-temple sculptors and painters

*石窟造像畫工*

**c. 366 – c. 1300** · Chinese  
Born: Northern China along the Silk Road · Died: ?  
Movements & schools: [Buddhist sculpture and cave temples](../movements/buddhist-sculpture.md)  

> The anonymous monks, sculptors and painters who carved and painted the Buddhist cave temples of Dunhuang, Yungang and Longmen.

## Biography

From the fourth century, Buddhist communities along the Silk Road began cutting temples into cliffs, following Indian and Central Asian models. At Dunhuang, at the western edge of China proper, the Mogao caves were painted and sculpted in clay over about a thousand years. Under the Northern Wei dynasty, colossal stone Buddhas were carved at Yungang near the capital Pingcheng from around 460, and after the move of the capital to Luoyang in 494 work moved to Longmen, where it continued through the Tang. Donors included emperors and empresses, monks, local officials and ordinary believers, whose names are often recorded in inscriptions, but the carvers and painters themselves are almost never named.

Their work traces the sinicisation of Buddhist art: the early Yungang Buddhas recall Gandhara and Gupta India, while later figures wear Chinese robes and have slender bodies, and the Tang Buddhas of Longmen are full, calm and majestic. The Dunhuang murals preserve the best surviving record of Tang painting, including paradise scenes, landscapes and portraits of donors. The caves were damaged by later iconoclasm and in the twentieth century by looting, and many sculptures are now in museums abroad.

### Colossal Buddha of Yungang Cave 20 (c. 460–470)

[![Colossal Buddha of Yungang Cave 20](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/91/Great.Buddha.Datong.jpg/1280px-Great.Buddha.Datong.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Great.Buddha.Datong.jpg)

<sub>Colossal seated Buddha, Yungang Grottoes Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Great.Buddha.Datong.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 460–470
- **Type:** Sandstone, carved in the cliff
- **Where to see it:** Yungang Grottoes, Datong, Shanxi
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Colossal%20Buddha%20of%20Yungang%20Cave%2020%20Cave-temple%20sculptors%20and%20painters)

Around 460 the monk Tanyao persuaded the Northern Wei emperor Wencheng to sponsor five great caves at Wuzhou Mountain near the capital Pingcheng, today Datong, in part as atonement for an earlier persecution of Buddhism. Each cave held a colossal Buddha, and tradition links the five to the dynasty's emperors. The front wall of Cave 20 collapsed long ago, leaving its seated Buddha open to view. The broad shoulders, long ears, almond eyes and stylised robe draped over one shoulder show Central Asian and Indian influence, filtered through the Turkic-speaking Tuoba rulers. The Yungang site eventually contained over 250 caves and some 51,000 images, and it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Vairocana Buddha at Fengxian Temple, Longmen (672–675)

[![Vairocana Buddha at Fengxian Temple, Longmen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/57/Ancient_Buddhist_Grottoes_at_Longmen-_Fengxian_Temple%2C_Colossal_Statue_of_Vairocana.jpg/1280px-Ancient_Buddhist_Grottoes_at_Longmen-_Fengxian_Temple%2C_Colossal_Statue_of_Vairocana.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ancient_Buddhist_Grottoes_at_Longmen-_Fengxian_Temple%2C_Colossal_Statue_of_Vairocana.jpg)

<sub>Vairocana Buddha, Fengxian Temple, Longmen Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ancient_Buddhist_Grottoes_at_Longmen-_Fengxian_Temple%2C_Colossal_Statue_of_Vairocana.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 672–675
- **Type:** Limestone, carved in the cliff
- **Where to see it:** Longmen Grottoes, Luoyang, Henan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Vairocana%20Buddha%20at%20Fengxian%20Temple%2C%20Longmen%20Cave-temple%20sculptors%20and%20painters)

Longmen, south of Luoyang where the Yi River runs between two limestone cliffs, holds more than 2,000 caves and niches carved from the late fifth to the eighth century. The largest group, the open-air Fengxian Temple, was completed in 675. An inscription records that Empress Wu, later the only woman to rule China in her own name, contributed money from her cosmetics allowance. The central Vairocana, the cosmic Buddha of the Huayan school, is about 17 metres high and is flanked by disciples, bodhisattvas, heavenly kings and fierce guardians. Its calm, full face and soft, rounded form represent the mature Tang style, which spread to Korea and Japan. Popular tradition says the Buddha's face was modelled on the empress herself.

- **Date:** 4th–14th century
- **Type:** Mineral pigments on plaster; painted clay sculpture
- **Where to see it:** Mogao Caves, Dunhuang, Gansu
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Murals%20of%20the%20Mogao%20Caves%20Cave-temple%20sculptors%20and%20painters)

According to an inscription, the monk Lezun began cutting the first cave at Mogao in 366, and for a thousand years monks, merchants and officials sponsored new caves in the cliff near the oasis of Dunhuang, a meeting point of Chinese, Central Asian and Indian cultures. The walls are painted with Jataka tales of the Buddha's past lives, huge paradise scenes of the Pure Land with palaces and dancers, landscapes, portraits of donors and scenes of daily life, while clay sculptures fill the niches. Because almost all Tang painting elsewhere has been lost, the murals are the best evidence for it. In 1900 a sealed library cave was found, and many manuscripts and paintings were taken abroad by Aurel Stein, Paul Pelliot and others. Mogao is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
