# Ren Bonian

*任伯年*

**1840 – 1896** · Chinese  
Born: Shanyin (Shaoxing), Zhejiang · Died: Shanghai  
Movements & schools: [Shanghai school and modern Chinese ink](../movements/shanghai-school.md)  

> The most versatile painter of the Shanghai school, famous for lively figures, birds and flowers and for his portraits of fellow artists.

## Biography

Ren Yi, better known by his courtesy name Bonian, was the son of a portrait painter in Zhejiang. During the Taiping Rebellion he is said to have served as a flag bearer in the Taiping army. He then studied with the painters Ren Xiong and Ren Xun and in 1868 settled in Shanghai, which was becoming China's commercial capital. There he sold paintings to merchants and the new urban public with great success. He died in 1896, apparently weakened by opium addiction.

Ren Bonian worked quickly in a fresh, bright style that combined the outline techniques of earlier masters with boneless washes and even touches of Western sketching, which he may have learned from Jesuit-trained artists in Shanghai. His subjects included popular legends, birds and flowers, and portraits of friends such as Wu Changshuo. He is regarded as the founder of the modern Shanghai style and taught or influenced many later painters.

## Key artworks

### Portrait of Wu Changshuo (1880s)

- **Date:** 1880s
- **Type:** Hanging scroll, ink and colour on paper
- **Where to see it:** Zhejiang Provincial Museum and other collections (several portraits)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Portrait%20of%20Wu%20Changshuo%20Ren%20Bonian)

Ren Bonian painted his younger friend Wu Changshuo several times in the 1880s, in moods ranging from formal to humorous: seated in the summer heat with bared chest, holding a hoe as a poor scholar, or dressed in an official's robes. The faces are carefully modelled, sometimes with light shading that may reflect Western influence, while the bodies and clothes are drawn with quick, calligraphic strokes. Wu often added long inscriptions, and the portraits record the friendship between the two leading figures of the Shanghai art world. They are among Ren Bonian's most personal works and show his gift for character.
