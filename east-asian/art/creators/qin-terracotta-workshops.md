# Qin terracotta workshops

*秦俑工匠*

**c. 246 BC – c. 206 BC** · Chinese  
Born: Qin state, Shaanxi · Died: ?  
Movements & schools: [Qin and Han tomb art](../movements/qin-han-tomb-art.md)  

> The thousands of potters, sculptors and painters who made the Terracotta Army for the tomb of the First Emperor of Qin.

## Biography

When King Zheng of Qin came to the throne in 246 BC, work began on his tomb near modern Xi'an; after he unified China in 221 BC and took the title First Emperor (Qin Shi Huang), the project grew to employ a vast labour force. The terracotta figures were made in government workshops using a modular system: legs, torsos, arms and heads were moulded or built from coils of clay and assembled, then individual faces, hair and armour were modelled by hand. Many figures carry stamped or incised names of foremen and workshops, a form of quality control, so we know the names of some supervisors even though the sculptors are anonymous.

After firing, the figures were coated with lacquer and painted in bright colours, most of which flaked away on excavation. The result combines mass production with remarkable variety, so that no two soldiers seem identical. The workshops also made bronze chariots, birds and acrobats for the tomb complex. Their work was forgotten until farmers digging a well found fragments in 1974; it has since become the most famous archaeological discovery in China.

## Key artworks

### Terracotta Army (c. 210 BC)

[![Terracotta Army](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/88/51714-Terracota-Army.jpg/1280px-51714-Terracota-Army.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:51714-Terracota-Army.jpg)

<sub>Soldiers in Pit 1 of the Terracotta Army Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:51714-Terracota-Army.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 210 BC
- **Type:** Painted terracotta figures
- **Where to see it:** Mausoleum of the First Qin Emperor, Lintong, Xi'an
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Terracotta%20Army%20Qin%20terracotta%20workshops)

In 1974 farmers digging a well near Xi'an found fragments of clay figures, and archaeologists uncovered three pits east of the burial mound of Qin Shi Huang, who died in 210 BC. Pit 1 alone holds an army of an estimated six thousand soldiers in battle formation, with archers, infantry, chariots and officers; in total about eight thousand figures are thought to have been buried. They were made in workshops from moulded parts and individually finished heads, painted in bright colours and armed with real bronze weapons, many of which were looted soon after the emperor's death, when the pits were burned. The army reflects the emperor's belief that his power would continue in the afterlife. Excavation continues, and the emperor's tomb itself has not been opened.
