# Wang Ximeng

*王希孟*

**1096 – c. 1119** · Chinese  
Movements & schools: [Song academy & literati painting](../movements/song-academy-literati.md)  

> Young painter at Emperor Huizong's court who, at about eighteen, painted the huge blue-and-green handscroll A Thousand Li of Rivers and Mountains.

## Biography

Wang Ximeng is known from a single painting and from a colophon by the minister Cai Jing attached to it. According to Cai Jing, he was a student at the imperial academy who was taught by Emperor Huizong himself, and he completed the scroll in 1113, when he was eighteen. He is believed to have died young, perhaps in his early twenties, and his name is recorded only by later writers.

A Thousand Li of Rivers and Mountains, nearly twelve metres long, is painted in the blue-and-green style with mineral azurite and malachite pigments, revived from Tang models. It shows an idealised panorama of lakes, mountains, villages and travellers. Long kept in the imperial collection, it is now in the Palace Museum, Beijing, where its rare exhibitions draw huge crowds.

- **Date:** 1113
- **Type:** Handscroll, ink and colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** Palace Museum, Beijing
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Thousand%20Li%20of%20Rivers%20and%20Mountains%20Wang%20Ximeng)

The scroll is about 12 metres long and shows a vast landscape of mountains, lakes, rivers and bridges, with villages, temples, mills, fishermen and travellers. It is painted in the blue-and-green style, with layers of azurite and malachite over ink and ochre, an archaic manner associated with the Tang. According to the colophon by Cai Jing, Huizong personally guided the young Wang Ximeng, who finished the work in half a year in 1113 and died soon after. The brilliant mineral colours have survived remarkably well. The painting is one of the most celebrated in Chinese art and has inspired modern dance and films.
