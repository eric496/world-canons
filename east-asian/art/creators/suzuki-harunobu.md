# Suzuki Harunobu

*鈴木春信*

**c. 1725 – 1770** · Japanese  
Born: Edo · Died: Edo  
Movements & schools: [Ukiyo-e](../movements/ukiyo-e.md)  

> Ukiyo-e master who in 1765 pioneered the full-colour brocade print (nishiki-e), filled with slender, doll-like young lovers and poetic allusions.

## Biography

Little is known of Harunobu's early life. He was working as a print designer in Edo by about 1760. In 1765 wealthy amateur poets commissioned calendar prints (egoyomi) for exchange at new year parties, and Harunobu and his engravers and printers used them to develop a technique of printing with many separate colour blocks, registered with marks called kentō. The result was the nishiki-e, or brocade picture, which quickly became the standard ukiyo-e print.

In the five years before his death in 1770 Harunobu designed hundreds of prints. His delicate young women and youths, often barely distinguishable, appear in domestic scenes and in parodies (mitate) of classical poems and legends. He also portrayed real local beauties such as the teahouse girl Kasamori Osen, starting a fashion that Utamaro later developed.

## Key artworks

### Girl Viewing Plum Blossoms at Night (c. 1766)

[![Girl Viewing Plum Blossoms at Night](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/13/Suzuki_Harunobu_-_Woman_Admiring_Plum_Blossoms_at_Night.jpg/1280px-Suzuki_Harunobu_-_Woman_Admiring_Plum_Blossoms_at_Night.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Suzuki_Harunobu_-_Woman_Admiring_Plum_Blossoms_at_Night.jpg)

<sub>Suzuki Harunobu, Girl Viewing Plum Blossoms at Night Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Suzuki_Harunobu_-_Woman_Admiring_Plum_Blossoms_at_Night.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1766
- **Type:** Polychrome woodblock print (nishiki-e)
- **Where to see it:** Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Girl%20Viewing%20Plum%20Blossoms%20at%20Night%20Suzuki%20Harunobu)

On a veranda, a young woman leans forward with a paper lantern to see a branch of white plum blossom in the night, while a maid looks on. The background is a uniform black, an unusual and difficult effect in woodblock printing, and the blossoms are left in the white of the paper with blind-embossing for texture. Harunobu's figures are slender and delicate, and the scene evokes classical poems about plum blossoms whose scent is noticed at night. It belongs to the first years of the full-colour brocade print, which Harunobu pioneered in 1765.
