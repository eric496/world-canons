# Xia Gui

*夏珪*

**c. 1180 – c. 1230** · Chinese  
Born: Qiantang (Hangzhou) · Died: ?  
Movements & schools: [Song academy & literati painting](../movements/song-academy-literati.md)  

> Southern Song court painter, partner of Ma Yuan in the Ma–Xia school, celebrated for the ink handscroll Pure and Remote Views of Streams and Mountains.

## Biography

Xia Gui served as painter-in-attendance at the Southern Song court under Emperor Ningzong in the early thirteenth century and was awarded the golden belt, a high honour. Little is known of his life beyond his court service. He painted mostly in ink alone, and his works include long handscrolls and small album leaves.

Xia Gui pushed Ma Yuan's style further, with wet, abbreviated strokes, sharply angled brushwork and landscapes that dissolve into mist. His long scroll in the National Palace Museum, Taipei, unfolds like a journey through river country. With Ma Yuan he was a major model for Japanese painters such as Sesshū and the Kanō school.

## Key artworks

### Pure and Remote Views of Streams and Mountains (early 13th century)

<sub>Xia Gui, Pure and Remote Views of Streams and Mountains (detail) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Xia_Gui%2C_Streams_and_Mountains_with_a_Clear_Distant_View%2C_detail.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** early 13th century
- **Type:** Handscroll, ink on paper
- **Where to see it:** National Palace Museum, Taipei
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pure%20and%20Remote%20Views%20of%20Streams%20and%20Mountains%20Xia%20Gui)

The scroll unfolds as a journey: rocky cliffs, a river with boats, a village, a bridge, mountains half lost in mist, and travellers on paths. Xia Gui used only ink on paper, with strong, angular axe-cut strokes for the rocks and wet, splashed washes for the distance, alternating dense passages with open space. The work has the rhythm of a piece of music, with crescendos and pauses. It shows the dramatic, compressed style of the Southern Song academy at its most powerful and was a model for Japanese ink painters such as Sesshū.
