# Utagawa Kuniyoshi

*歌川国芳*

**1797 – 1861** · Japanese  
Born: Edo · Died: Edo  
Movements & schools: [Ukiyo-e](../movements/ukiyo-e.md)  

> Late ukiyo-e master of warrior prints, ghosts, monsters, cats and witty satire.

## Biography

Kuniyoshi was the son of a silk dyer in Edo and joined the studio of Utagawa Toyokuni around 1811. His early career was a struggle until 1827, when his series of heroes of the Chinese novel Water Margin (Suikoden) caused a craze for warrior prints (musha-e) and tattoos based on them. He went on to design thousands of prints and had many pupils, among them Tsukioka Yoshitoshi.

Kuniyoshi's work is inventive and often humorous: giant skeletons and sea monsters spread across triptychs, cats imitate kabuki actors, and faces are built from human bodies. During the Tenpō reforms of the 1840s, which banned prints of actors and courtesans, he evaded censorship with satirical allegories. He also experimented with Western-style shading and perspective.

## Key artworks

### Takiyasha the Witch and the Skeleton Spectre (c. 1844)

[![Takiyasha the Witch and the Skeleton Spectre](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cf/Takiyasha_the_Witch_and_the_Skeleton_Spectre%2C_by_Utagawa_Kuniyoshi.jpg/1280px-Takiyasha_the_Witch_and_the_Skeleton_Spectre%2C_by_Utagawa_Kuniyoshi.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Takiyasha_the_Witch_and_the_Skeleton_Spectre%2C_by_Utagawa_Kuniyoshi.jpg)

<sub>Kuniyoshi, Takiyasha the Witch and the Skeleton Spectre Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Takiyasha_the_Witch_and_the_Skeleton_Spectre%2C_by_Utagawa_Kuniyoshi.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1844
- **Type:** Triptych of polychrome woodblock prints
- **Where to see it:** Victoria and Albert Museum, London, and other collections
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Takiyasha%20the%20Witch%20and%20the%20Skeleton%20Spectre%20Utagawa%20Kuniyoshi)

The print illustrates a story of Princess Takiyasha, daughter of the tenth-century rebel Taira no Masakado, who lived on in his ruined palace at Sōma and used sorcery to summon spirits. Here she recites a spell from a scroll on the left, while a gigantic skeleton leans over a broken bamboo screen across two sheets of the triptych to attack the warrior Ōya no Mitsukuni and his companion, who are sent to destroy her. Kuniyoshi drew the skeleton with an accuracy that suggests he had studied Western anatomy books. The scale and drama of the composition are typical of his inventive warrior and ghost prints.
