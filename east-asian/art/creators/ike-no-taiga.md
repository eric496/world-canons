## Biography

Ike no Taiga was born in Kyoto, the son of a poor family, and was a child prodigy at calligraphy. He ran a fan shop as a young man and learned Chinese painting from books, imported woodblock manuals and the Ōbaku Zen monks of Uji. He travelled widely, climbing Mount Fuji and other mountains, and married the painter and poet Gyokuran. He was famous for his unworldliness and good nature.

Taiga drew on Chinese literati models but painted with a light, playful freedom of his own, using finger painting and broad, rhythmic dotting. In 1771 he and Yosa Buson painted an album illustrating poems by the Chinese writer Li Yu on the joys of country life, Taiga taking the Ten Conveniences and Buson the Ten Pleasures; it is a National Treasure. With Buson he defined Japanese nanga.

## Key artworks

### Ten Conveniences and Ten Pleasures (Jūben jūgi-zu) (1771)

- **Date:** 1771
- **Type:** Album of twenty leaves, ink and colour on paper (with Yosa Buson)
- **Where to see it:** Kawabata Yasunari Memorial Foundation, Kamakura
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ten%20Conveniences%20and%20Ten%20Pleasures%20%28J%C5%ABben%20j%C5%ABgi-zu%29%20Ike%20no%20Taiga)

The Chinese playwright Li Yu wrote poems on the ten conveniences and ten pleasures of his country retreat, and in 1771 a patron commissioned the two leading Japanese literati painters to illustrate them. Ike no Taiga painted the conveniences, such as farming, fishing, drawing water and walking, in a light, humorous style with rounded forms and dots, while Yosa Buson, also the great haiku poet, painted the pleasures of the seasons and weather in a more atmospheric, poetic manner. The album, owned by the novelist Kawabata Yasunari, is a National Treasure and shows how Japanese painters reinvented Chinese literati ideals.
