# Qiu Ying

*仇英*

**c. 1494 – c. 1552** · Chinese  
Born: Taicang, Jiangsu · Died: Suzhou  
Movements & schools: [Ming painting & porcelain](../movements/ming-painting-porcelain.md)  

> Professional painter of humble origins, one of the Four Masters of the Ming, celebrated for meticulous figures, palaces and blue-and-green landscapes.

## Biography

Qiu Ying was born in Taicang and trained as a lacquer craftsman before moving to Suzhou, where he studied painting, probably under Zhou Chen. Unlike the scholar-painters, he worked on commission and lived for long periods in the households of rich collectors such as Xiang Yuanbian, where he copied and studied old masterpieces. He was admired by Wen Zhengming and his circle despite his lack of literary training.

Qiu Ying was a master of the fine-line (gongbi) style and of the blue-and-green landscape, and he excelled at court ladies, garden scenes and historical subjects. Spring Morning in the Han Palace, in Taipei, is a long, jewel-like procession of palace life. His version of Along the River During the Qingming Festival was widely copied, and many paintings are falsely signed with his name.

## Key artworks

### Spring Morning in the Han Palace (c. 1540)

[![Spring Morning in the Han Palace](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/Qiu_Ying_Spring_morning_in_the_Han_palace.jpg/1280px-Qiu_Ying_Spring_morning_in_the_Han_palace.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Qiu_Ying_Spring_morning_in_the_Han_palace.jpg)

<sub>Qiu Ying, Spring Morning in the Han Palace (section) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Qiu_Ying_Spring_morning_in_the_Han_palace.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1540
- **Type:** Handscroll, ink and colour on silk
- **Where to see it:** National Palace Museum, Taipei
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Spring%20Morning%20in%20the%20Han%20Palace%20Qiu%20Ying)

Nearly six metres long, the scroll imagines life in the women's quarters of the Han dynasty palace on a spring morning. More than a hundred women and attendants dress, play the qin and pipa, play chess, dance, read, feed peacocks and pose for a portrait painter, an allusion to the story of Wang Zhaojun, who refused to bribe the court painter. The palace buildings, trees and rocks are painted with meticulous ruled lines and brilliant colour. Qiu Ying, a professional painter, made it for a wealthy patron. It was later in the Qing imperial collection and became a model for court painters.
