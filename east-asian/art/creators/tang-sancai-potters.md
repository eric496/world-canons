# Tang sancai potters

*唐三彩陶工*

**c. 680 – c. 760** · Chinese  
Born: Gongyi (Henan) and Xi'an · Died: ?  
Movements & schools: [Tang painting & sancai](../movements/tang-art.md)  

> The potters who made glazed earthenware tomb figures and vessels in the three-colour (sancai) style for the Tang aristocracy.

## Biography

Sancai, meaning three colours, refers to lead-glazed earthenware in amber, green and cream, often with blue from cobalt, made mainly between the late seventh and mid-eighth centuries. Kilns at Gongyi in Henan and near Chang'an (Xi'an) produced it for burial: figures of horses, camels, grooms, guardians, court ladies and merchants from Central Asia, as well as vessels. The figures were mould-made in parts and assembled; glazes were allowed to run and mingle in the firing, giving the characteristic streaked effect.

These objects record the cosmopolitan world of the Tang, with its Sogdian traders, Bactrian camels and prized Central Asian horses. Sumptuary rules limited the size and number of figures by rank, and production declined after the An Lushan rebellion of 755. Similar wares were made in Liao China, in Silla Korea and in Nara Japan, and sancai tomb figures became sought-after by collectors after railway building uncovered tombs in the early twentieth century.

## Key artworks

### Sancai camel carrying musicians (c. 723)

[![Sancai camel carrying musicians](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4a/Sancai-glazed_foreign_musicians_on_camel._723_CE.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sancai-glazed_foreign_musicians_on_camel._723_CE.jpg)

<sub>Sancai camel with musicians, 723, National Museum of China Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sancai-glazed_foreign_musicians_on_camel._723_CE.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 723
- **Type:** Glazed earthenware tomb figure
- **Where to see it:** National Museum of China, Beijing
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sancai%20camel%20carrying%20musicians%20Tang%20sancai%20potters)

This figure, about 58 centimetres high, was excavated in 1957 from the tomb of Xianyu Tinghui, a general who died in 723, in the western suburbs of Xi'an. A two-humped camel stands with its head thrown back, carrying on a rug-covered platform seven musicians playing lutes, flutes and pipes, mostly bearded foreigners, and a dancer in the middle. The camel and the figures were moulded separately, painted with amber, green and cream glazes that run together, and fired at a low temperature. It shows the Silk Road world of the Tang capital, where Central Asian music and dance were highly fashionable. It is one of the masterpieces of sancai and of Tang tomb sculpture.
