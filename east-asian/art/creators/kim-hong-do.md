# Kim Hong-do

*김홍도 (金弘道)*

**1745 – c. 1806** · Korean  
Movements & schools: [Joseon painting and ceramics](../movements/joseon-painting.md)  

> Joseon court painter, master of genre scenes of ordinary Korean life as well as landscapes and Daoist immortals.

## Biography

Kim Hong-do, known as Danwon, studied as a youth with the scholar-painter Kang Se-hwang and entered the royal painting bureau. He painted royal portraits for King Yeongjo and King Jeongjo, who greatly favoured him, and in 1789 was sent with an embassy to Japan to make maps of Tsushima. He served briefly as a county magistrate. His later life was difficult, and the date of his death is uncertain.

Kim Hong-do's album of genre paintings in the National Museum of Korea shows wrestlers, dancers, blacksmiths, a village school and farmers at work in quick, humorous sketches without background, full of observation of gesture. He was equally skilled in landscape and figure painting, and he is regarded with Jeong Seon and Shin Yun-bok as one of the three great masters of late Joseon painting.

## Key artworks

### Album of Genre Paintings (Danwon pungsokdo cheop) (late 18th century)

[![Album of Genre Paintings (Danwon pungsokdo cheop)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/Danwon_Ssireum.jpg/1280px-Danwon_Ssireum.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Danwon_Ssireum.jpg)

<sub>Ssireum (wrestling), from Kim Hong-do's album Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Danwon_Ssireum.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** late 18th century
- **Type:** Album of 25 leaves, ink and light colour on paper
- **Where to see it:** National Museum of Korea, Seoul
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Album%20of%20Genre%20Paintings%20%28Danwon%20pungsokdo%20cheop%29%20Kim%20Hong-do)

Each leaf of the album shows a scene of ordinary life without background: a ring of spectators watching two men wrestle (ssireum), a boy crying in front of his teacher while classmates laugh, a smithy, women washing clothes, men threshing grain, a dancer and musicians. Kim Hong-do drew with quick, confident outlines and light colour, and he had an eye for gesture, humour and composition, arranging figures in circles or diagonals. The scenes reflect the interest in real Korean life of eighteenth-century Joseon culture. The attribution of every leaf to Kim himself has been questioned, but the album, Treasure No. 527, is one of the most beloved works of Korean art.
