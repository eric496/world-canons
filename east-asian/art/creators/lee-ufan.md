# Lee Ufan

*이우환 (李禹煥)*

**1936** · Korean  
Born: Haman, South Gyeongsang · Died: ?  
Movements & schools: [Postwar avant-gardes](../movements/postwar-avant-garde.md)  

> Korean painter, sculptor and philosopher, a leading theorist of Japan's Mono-ha movement, known for the From Point and From Line series.

## Biography

Lee Ufan studied painting briefly in Seoul and moved to Japan in 1956, where he studied philosophy at Nihon University. In the late 1960s he became the principal theorist of Mono-ha (School of Things), writing essays on encounter and relation, and making works of stones, steel plates and glass. He has taught at Tama Art University and lived between Japan, Korea and Paris. A museum devoted to him on Naoshima opened in 2010.

Lee's paintings of the 1970s, From Point and From Line, consist of marks repeated as the brush runs out of pigment, recording time and the act of painting; his later Correspondence and Dialogue works reduce this to a few strokes. He was also associated with Korean Dansaekhwa. His works are under copyright.

## Key artworks

### From Line (1970s)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Lee Ufan Museum, Benesse Art Site Naoshima →](https://benesse-artsite.jp)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1970s
- **Type:** Series of paintings, pigment with glue on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Lee Ufan Museum, Naoshima, and many collections
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=From%20Line%20Lee%20Ufan)

In the From Line series, begun in 1973, Lee Ufan loaded a brush with mineral pigment mixed with animal glue and drew it down the canvas from top to bottom until the paint ran out, then repeated the action beside it. Each line fades from dark to pale, and the canvas fills with parallel strokes like a slow rhythm. The paintings, together with the From Point series, reflect Lee's theory, developed in the context of Mono-ha, of art as an encounter between the artist's act, the material and the space. They are also associated with Korean Dansaekhwa. His works are under copyright and are not reproduced here.
