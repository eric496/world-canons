# Yayoi Kusama

*草間彌生*

**1929** · Japanese  
Born: Matsumoto, Nagano · Died: ?  
Movements & schools: [Postwar avant-gardes](../movements/postwar-avant-garde.md)  

> Japanese artist of polka dots, mirrored infinity rooms and Infinity Net paintings, a figure of the New York avant-garde of the 1960s.

## Biography

Kusama was born in Matsumoto to a family of seed merchants. She has described hallucinations from childhood of patterns covering everything around her. After studying nihonga in Kyoto she moved to New York in 1958, where she painted vast Infinity Nets, made soft sculptures covered with phalluses, staged naked happenings and created the first mirrored infinity environments. She returned to Japan in 1973 and since 1977 has lived by choice in a psychiatric hospital in Tokyo, working in a studio nearby.

Kusama's art of repetition and accumulation, which she links to self-obliteration, anticipated Minimalism, Pop and installation art. Long overlooked, she was rediscovered from the late 1980s and represented Japan at the Venice Biennale in 1993, becoming one of the most popular living artists. Her works are under copyright.

## Key artworks

### Infinity Nets (1958 onwards)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Yayoi Kusama Museum, Tokyo →](https://yayoikusamamuseum.jp)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1958 onwards
- **Type:** Series of oil paintings on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Many collections
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Infinity%20Nets%20Yayoi%20Kusama)

Soon after arriving in New York in 1958, Kusama began painting very large canvases, some more than ten metres long, entirely covered with tiny loops of white paint over a darker ground, repeated obsessively for hours at a time. The nets have no centre or edge and seem to extend beyond the canvas. When shown in 1959 they were praised by critics including Donald Judd and were seen as anticipating Minimalism. Kusama connects the repetition with her hallucinations and the idea of self-obliteration. She has returned to the Infinity Nets throughout her career. Her works are under copyright and are not reproduced here.
