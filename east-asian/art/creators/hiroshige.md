# Utagawa Hiroshige

> Ukiyo-e master of landscape, whose Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō and One Hundred Famous Views of Edo capture travel, weather and seasons with poetic feeling.

<sub>Portrait: Utagawa Kunisada, memorial portrait of Hiroshige, 1858 · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Memorial_Portrait_of_Utagawa_Hiroshige_%285765350019%29.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Hiroshige, born Andō Tokutarō, was the son of a fire warden of the shogun's fire brigade in Edo, and inherited his father's post in 1809. He joined the studio of Utagawa Toyohiro and produced actor prints and pictures of women before turning to landscape around 1830. In 1832, according to tradition, he travelled along the Tōkaidō highway with an official delegation to Kyoto; the resulting series of The Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō, published from 1833, made him famous. He produced thousands of prints, became a Buddhist monk in 1856, and died in the cholera epidemic of 1858.

Where Hokusai's landscapes are dramatic and structured, Hiroshige's are lyrical, with rain, snow, mist and moonlight, travellers and local people, and subtle colour gradations (bokashi). His late One Hundred Famous Views of Edo, in vertical format with bold close-up foregrounds, was copied in oil by Van Gogh and influenced Whistler and the Impressionists.

### The Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō (1833–1834)

[![The Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Utagawa_Hiroshige_%28the_first%29_-_Shono_from_the_Fifty-three_Stations_on_Tokaido_Highway%2C_Hoeido_version_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Utagawa_Hiroshige_%28the_first%29_-_Shono_from_the_Fifty-three_Stations_on_Tokaido_Highway%2C_Hoeido_version_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Utagawa_Hiroshige_%28the_first%29_-_Shono_from_the_Fifty-three_Stations_on_Tokaido_Highway%2C_Hoeido_version_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Hiroshige, Shōno: Driving Rain Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Utagawa_Hiroshige_%28the_first%29_-_Shono_from_the_Fifty-three_Stations_on_Tokaido_Highway%2C_Hoeido_version_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1833–1834
- **Type:** Series of 55 polychrome woodblock prints
- **Where to see it:** Many collections
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Fifty-three%20Stations%20of%20the%20T%C5%8Dkaid%C5%8D%20Utagawa%20Hiroshige)

The Tōkaidō, the main highway along the Pacific coast between Edo and Kyoto, had fifty-three post stations, and travel guides and prints of it were popular. Hiroshige's series, published by Hōeidō and Tsuruya, includes the starting point Nihonbashi, the fifty-three stations and Kyoto, 55 prints in all. They show travellers, porters, palanquins, inns and local people in all kinds of weather: driving rain at Shōno, snow at Kanbara, wind at Yokkaichi. The scenes combine accurate observation with lyrical atmosphere. The series sold in huge numbers, launching the craze for landscape prints, and Hiroshige later made many other Tōkaidō series.

### Sudden Shower over Shin-Ōhashi Bridge and Atake (1857)

[![Sudden Shower over Shin-Ōhashi Bridge and Atake](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Utagawa_Hiroshige_-_Evening_Shower_at_Atake_and_the_Great_Bridge.jpg/1280px-Utagawa_Hiroshige_-_Evening_Shower_at_Atake_and_the_Great_Bridge.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Utagawa_Hiroshige_-_Evening_Shower_at_Atake_and_the_Great_Bridge.jpg)

<sub>Hiroshige, Sudden Shower over Shin-Ōhashi Bridge and Atake Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Utagawa_Hiroshige_-_Evening_Shower_at_Atake_and_the_Great_Bridge.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1857
- **Type:** Polychrome woodblock print
- **Where to see it:** Many impressions, including the Brooklyn Museum and Metropolitan Museum of Art
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sudden%20Shower%20over%20Shin-%C5%8Chashi%20Bridge%20and%20Atake%20Utagawa%20Hiroshige)

This print belongs to One Hundred Famous Views of Edo, Hiroshige's last great series, published from 1856 to 1859. A wooden bridge over the Sumida River crosses the picture diagonally, seen from above, while figures with umbrellas and straw coats hurry across it. The rain falls in fine lines of two angles, cut by the block carver with great precision, and dark clouds are printed with graded colour. A raft is poled along the grey river and the far shore of Atake fades in the rain. Van Gogh painted a copy of it in 1887, and its composition influenced Western painters' handling of weather and space.

### Plum Park in Kameido (1857)

[![Plum Park in Kameido](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/De_pruimenboomgaard_te_Kameido-Rijksmuseum_RP-P-1956-743.jpeg/1280px-De_pruimenboomgaard_te_Kameido-Rijksmuseum_RP-P-1956-743.jpeg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:De_pruimenboomgaard_te_Kameido-Rijksmuseum_RP-P-1956-743.jpeg)

<sub>Hiroshige, Plum Park in Kameido, Rijksmuseum Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:De_pruimenboomgaard_te_Kameido-Rijksmuseum_RP-P-1956-743.jpeg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1857
- **Type:** Polychrome woodblock print
- **Where to see it:** Many impressions, including the Brooklyn Museum
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Plum%20Park%20in%20Kameido%20Utagawa%20Hiroshige)

Also from One Hundred Famous Views of Edo, the print shows a famous plum garden at Kameido, east of the city, whose old tree called the Sleeping Dragon Plum was a celebrated sight. Hiroshige placed a gnarled branch of the tree across the front of the picture, cut off by the frame, and showed the visitors small behind it under a red sky. This bold use of an enlarged foreground, a device used throughout the series, was new to European viewers. Van Gogh's copy of 1887, painted with a border of invented Japanese characters, is in the Van Gogh Museum.
