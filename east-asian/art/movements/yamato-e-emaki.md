# Yamato-e and narrative handscrolls (大和絵・絵巻)

*c. 900–1333*

Era: [Song, Heian & Goryeo](../../periods/song-heian-goryeo.md)

From the Heian period Japanese painters developed yamato-e, painting of Japanese subjects in a Japanese manner, as opposed to kara-e on Chinese themes. Its greatest achievement is the emakimono, the narrative handscroll, from the courtly Tale of Genji scrolls with their roofless rooms and stylised faces to the comic animals of the Chōjū-giga and the battle scenes of the Heiji scrolls. In Kamakura Japan, sculptors of the Kei school such as Unkei gave Buddhist statues a new muscular realism.
