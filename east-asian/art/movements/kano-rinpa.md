# Momoyama and Edo decorative painting (Kanō and Rinpa) (狩野派・琳派)

*c. 1550–1850*

Era: [Ming, Muromachi & Early Joseon](../../periods/ming-muromachi-joseon.md)

The warlords who reunified Japan built castles whose rooms were lined with sliding doors and folding screens on gold leaf. The Kanō school, led by Kanō Eitoku, supplied bold, monumental pines and cypresses, while Hasegawa Tōhaku could paint both gold screens and the misty ink Pine Trees. In Kyoto, Hon'ami Kōetsu and Tawaraya Sōtatsu created the style later called Rinpa after Ogata Kōrin: flat, daring designs drawn from courtly literature, revived in Edo by Sakai Hōitsu.
