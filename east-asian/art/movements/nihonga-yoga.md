# Nihonga and yōga in modern Japan (日本画・洋画)

*1868–1945*

Era: [Revolution & Modernisation](../../periods/east-asian-modern.md)

After the Meiji Restoration Japanese artists had to choose between Western oil painting (yōga), taught by teachers such as Kuroda Seiki who had studied in Paris, and a modernised Japanese painting (nihonga) championed by Okakura Kakuzō and Ernest Fenollosa. Nihonga painters such as Kanō Hōgai, Yokoyama Taikan and Uemura Shōen used mineral pigments and silk but absorbed Western ideas of space and atmosphere.
