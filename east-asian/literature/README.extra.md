## Notes on this section

- **Scope.** Poetry, prose, drama and fiction of China, Japan and Korea from the Book of Songs to the late twentieth century. Historical writing (Sima Qian) and philosophical classics (Confucius, Zhuangzi) belong to other sections; Han Yu, Pak Ji-won (`bak-ji-won`), Han Yong-un and Su Shi share their entries with the philosophy and art sections.
- **Names and romanization.** Pinyin for Chinese, Hepburn with macrons for Japanese, Revised Romanization for Korean, family name first. Original script is given for every author, movement and work. Works are listed under an English title, often with the romanized original.
- **Attribution.** Anonymous or collective works are listed under honest group entries (the poets of the Book of Songs, the Han Music Bureau poets, the compilers of the Kojiki, the biwa storytellers of the Heike, anonymous Heian authors, hyangga poets, pansori singers). Traditional attributions such as Shi Nai'an for Water Margin, Wu Cheng'en for Journey to the West and Heo Gyun for Hong Gildong are flagged as debated.
- **Translations and quotations.** Most English translations of these texts are still in copyright, so poems are paraphrased rather than quoted.
- **Pictures.** Manuscripts, early printed editions, illustrated handscrolls, calligraphy and ukiyo-e prints from Wikimedia Commons. Portraits are paintings, prints or a postage stamp, never photographs; most portraits of pre-modern writers are later imagined likenesses. Works by authors who died less than about seventy years ago (Lao She, Kawabata, Mishima, Ōe and others) are not illustrated.
