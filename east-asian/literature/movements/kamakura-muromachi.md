# War tales, recluses & Noh (軍記物語 · 隠者文学 · 能)

*c. 1185–1600*

Era: [Song, Heian & Goryeo](../../periods/song-heian-goryeo.md)

As warriors took power in Japan, literature turned to impermanence. Blind biwa-playing monks chanted the war tales of the Taira and Minamoto; Buddhist recluses such as Kamo no Chōmei and Yoshida Kenkō wrote short essays from their huts; and the Shin Kokinshū pursued a melancholy, suggestive beauty. In the Muromachi period Zeami turned sarugaku into Noh, a masked dance-drama of ghosts and memory guided by the ideal of yūgen.
