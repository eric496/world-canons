# Book of Songs & Songs of Chu (詩經 · 楚辭)

*c. 1000–200 BC*

Era: [Bronze Age & the Hundred Schools](../../periods/early-dynasties.md)

Chinese poetry begins with the Shijing, a Zhou-dynasty anthology of 305 songs from court, temple and countryside, mostly in four-character lines, which Confucius was said to have edited and which became a Confucian classic. From the southern state of Chu came a different voice: the Chuci, long shamanistic and allegorical poems of exile and spirit journeys traditionally linked to Qu Yuan. Together the northern 'airs' and the southern 'songs' became the twin sources of the whole tradition.
