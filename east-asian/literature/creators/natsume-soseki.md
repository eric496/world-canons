# Natsume Sōseki

*夏目漱石*

**1867 – 1916** · Japanese  
Born: Edo (Tokyo) · Died: Tokyo  
Movements & eras: [Meiji to early Shōwa modern literature](../movements/meiji-modern.md)  

> The most beloved modern Japanese novelist, author of I Am a Cat, Botchan and Kokoro.

## Biography

Born Natsume Kinnosuke in Edo the year before the Meiji Restoration, Sōseki was given up for adoption and later returned to his family. He studied English literature at Tokyo Imperial University, taught in Matsuyama and Kumamoto, and spent two miserable years in London (1900–02) on a government scholarship. Back in Tokyo, he lectured at the university and in 1905 began serialising I Am a Cat; in 1907 he left academia to write full-time for the Asahi newspaper. He suffered from stomach ulcers and died at forty-nine.

Sōseki moved from comic satire to psychological novels about loneliness, egoism and the cost of modernisation for individuals caught between Japanese and Western values, culminating in Kokoro and the unfinished Light and Darkness. He mentored a circle of younger writers, including Akutagawa, and his portrait appeared on the 1,000-yen note from 1984 to 2004.

### I Am a Cat (1905–06)

[![I Am a Cat](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/I_AM_A_CAT_by_K._Ando.jpg/1280px-I_AM_A_CAT_by_K._Ando.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:I_AM_A_CAT_by_K._Ando.jpg)

<sub>Title page of the 1906 English translation by K. Andō Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:I_AM_A_CAT_by_K._Ando.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1905–06
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Hototogisu magazine, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=I%20Am%20a%20Cat%20Natsume%20S%C5%8Dseki)

The novel began as a short comic piece read to Masaoka Shiki's circle and published in the haiku magazine Hototogisu in 1905; its success led Sōseki to expand it into eleven instalments. The narrator, a cat that has no name yet, as its first sentence announces, lives in the house of the teacher Kushami, a thinly disguised self-portrait, and observes with lofty disdain the conversations of his master and his friends, the aesthete Meitei and the scientist Kangetsu, as well as the vulgarity of the rich businessman next door. Through the cat's eyes Sōseki satirises the pretensions of Meiji intellectuals and the new money culture. It made him famous overnight and is still one of the most popular Japanese novels.

### Botchan (1906)

[![Botchan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Manuscripts_of_%22Botchan%22.jpg/1280px-Manuscripts_of_%22Botchan%22.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Manuscripts_of_%22Botchan%22.jpg)

<sub>Natsume Sōseki's manuscript of Botchan Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Manuscripts_of_%22Botchan%22.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1906
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Hototogisu magazine, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Botchan%20Natsume%20S%C5%8Dseki)

The narrator, a reckless and honest young man from Tokyo nicknamed Botchan ('young master') by Kiyo, the old family servant who dotes on him, takes a job teaching mathematics at a middle school in a provincial town on Shikoku, modelled on Matsuyama where Sōseki taught. He is tormented by his pupils and becomes caught up in the intrigues of the staff, whom he nicknames Red Shirt, the scheming head teacher, his toady Clown, and the upright Porcupine, his ally. After the two teachers beat up Red Shirt and Clown, Botchan resigns and returns to Tokyo to live with Kiyo. Written in a lively first-person colloquial style, it is one of the most widely read Japanese novels.

### Kokoro (1914)

[![Kokoro](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/51/Natsume_S%C3%B4seki_Umschlag_%22Kokoro%22.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Natsume_S%C3%B4seki_Umschlag_%22Kokoro%22.jpg)

<sub>Cover of the first edition, 1914 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Natsume_S%C3%B4seki_Umschlag_%22Kokoro%22.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1914
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Asahi Shimbun, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Kokoro%20Natsume%20S%C5%8Dseki)

In the first two parts, a student narrator befriends an older man he calls Sensei, who lives quietly with his wife, visits a grave each month and seems to carry a burden; the student then returns home to his dying father as Emperor Meiji dies and General Nogi follows him in death. The third part is Sensei's testament, a letter explaining that as a student he and his friend K both loved the daughter of their landlady; Sensei secretly asked for her hand, and K killed himself. Haunted by guilt and by the egoism he discovered in himself, Sensei has decided to die with the passing of the Meiji era. A study of loneliness and the moral costs of modern individualism, it is Sōseki's most famous novel and a staple of Japanese school reading.
