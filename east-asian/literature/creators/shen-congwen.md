# Shen Congwen

*沈從文*

**1902 – 1988** · Chinese  
Born: Fenghuang, Hunan · Died: Beijing  
Movements & eras: [May Fourth & modern Chinese literature](../movements/may-fourth-modern.md)  

> Lyrical writer of western Hunan's river towns and their people, author of Border Town.

## Biography

Shen Congwen grew up in a garrison town in western Hunan among Han, Miao and Tujia peoples and served as a teenage soldier before going to Beijing in 1923 to become a writer, with little formal schooling. He taught at universities in Qingdao, Wuhan and Kunming and edited literary supplements. Criticised by leftist writers as reactionary, he gave up fiction after 1949, attempted suicide, and became a respected historian of Chinese costume at the Palace Museum.

His stories and novellas, above all Border Town (1934), portray the people of the Hunan borderlands with a gentle lyricism and an affection for the natural and the unspoiled, set against the corruption of the city. Ignored in the People's Republic for decades, he was rediscovered in the 1980s and is now ranked among the finest Chinese stylists of the century.

## Key works

### Border Town (1934)

- **Date:** 1934
- **Type:** Novella
- **Written / first published:** Shanghai, 1934
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Border%20Town%20Shen%20Congwen)

In the small town of Chadong on the border of Hunan and Sichuan, an old ferryman lives with his granddaughter Cuicui. Two brothers, sons of a local boat owner, both fall in love with her: the elder proposes through a go-between, the younger by singing love songs across the river at night. Misunderstandings, pride and silence lead to the elder brother's drowning, the younger brother's departure and the grandfather's death in a storm; Cuicui is left waiting at the ferry for someone who may or may not return. Written in a lyrical, understated style, the novella evokes a world of natural goodness and local customs, such as the Dragon Boat Festival, that Shen saw as threatened by modernity. It is among the most beloved works of modern Chinese literature.
