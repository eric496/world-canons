# Lao She

*老舍*

**1899 – 1966** · Chinese  
Born: Beijing · Died: Beijing  
Movements & eras: [May Fourth & modern Chinese literature](../movements/may-fourth-modern.md)  

> Novelist and playwright of Beijing life, author of Rickshaw Boy and Teahouse.

## Biography

Lao She, the pen name of Shu Qingchun, was born into a poor Manchu family in Beijing; his father, a palace guard, was killed during the Boxer crisis of 1900. He became a teacher, then taught Chinese at the School of Oriental Studies in London from 1924 to 1929, where he read Dickens and wrote his first novels. During the war against Japan he led the national writers' association. He returned from a stay in the United States in 1949 and wrote plays for the new regime, but at the start of the Cultural Revolution he was beaten by Red Guards and was found drowned the following day.

Lao She's humour, colloquial Beijing dialect and sympathy for the poor made him one of the most popular modern Chinese writers. Rickshaw Boy, about a puller's struggle and fall, is his best-known novel, and his play Teahouse, spanning fifty years of history in one Beijing teahouse, is a classic of Chinese spoken drama.

### Rickshaw Boy (1937)

- **Date:** 1937
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Serialised in Yuzhou feng, 1936–37
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Rickshaw%20Boy%20Lao%20She)

Xiangzi, a strong, honest young peasant who has come to Beijing, has one dream: to own a rickshaw. He saves for three years and buys one, only to have it seized by soldiers; he escapes with three camels, earning the nickname Camel. His savings are stolen, he is tricked into marriage by Huniu, the daughter of a rickshaw-garage owner, who dies in childbirth, and the girl he loves is sold into prostitution and kills herself. By the end Xiangzi has become a cynical, degraded loafer. Lao She's realistic, humorous and compassionate portrait of the Beijing poor, in its lively local dialect, made it a classic. A bowdlerised English translation of 1945 with a happy ending was a bestseller in America.

### Teahouse (1957)

- **Date:** 1957
- **Type:** Spoken drama
- **Written / first published:** Beijing People's Art Theatre, 1958
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Teahouse%20Lao%20She)

Teahouse's three acts are set in the Yutai teahouse in 1898, after the failure of the Hundred Days' Reform; around 1918, in the warlord era; and in the late 1940s, under the Nationalists. Its owner, Wang Lifa, tries to adapt and survive, while dozens of characters, from Manchu bannermen and eunuchs to informers, refugees and gangsters, pass through. In the final scene three old men scatter paper money for their own funeral. Lao She's play, first staged in 1958 in a famous production by Jiao Juyin, is regarded as the masterpiece of Chinese spoken drama (huaju), and the Beijing People's Art Theatre still performs it.
