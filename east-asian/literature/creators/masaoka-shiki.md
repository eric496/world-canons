# Masaoka Shiki

*正岡子規*

**1867 – 1902** · Japanese  
Born: Matsuyama, Iyo Province · Died: Tokyo  
Movements & eras: [Meiji to early Shōwa modern literature](../movements/meiji-modern.md)  

> Poet and critic who reformed haiku and tanka for the modern age.

## Biography

Born in Matsuyama, Shiki went to Tokyo, where he befriended Natsume Sōseki at university. He left without a degree to work for the newspaper Nippon. Tuberculosis, contracted after a stint as a war correspondent in 1895, confined him to bed for his last seven years, during which he wrote prolifically.

Shiki coined the term haiku for the independent verse, criticised the stale conventions of his day and praised Buson above Bashō, advocating shasei, 'sketching from life'. He also revived the tanka through his Negishi poetry circle, and his bed-ridden diaries are classics.

## Key works

### A Sickbed Six Feet Long (1902)

- **Date:** 1902
- **Type:** Diary essays
- **Written / first published:** Nippon newspaper, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Sickbed%20Six%20Feet%20Long%20Masaoka%20Shiki)

From May 1902 Shiki, bedridden with spinal tuberculosis and in great pain, published a short piece almost every day in the newspaper Nippon under this title, referring to the bed that had become his whole world. The 127 instalments discuss haiku and painting, food, memories, plants in his garden and the realities of his illness, in a frank, humorous and unsentimental voice that reflects his ideal of shasei, describing life as it is. The last appeared two days before his death in September 1902. Together with his other sickbed diaries, it is regarded as a classic of modern Japanese prose.
