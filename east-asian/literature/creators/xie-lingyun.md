## Biography

Xie Lingyun was heir to one of the greatest aristocratic clans of the Eastern Jin and inherited the title Duke of Kangle. When the Liu Song dynasty replaced the Jin in 420 his rank was reduced, and he served unhappily in provincial posts, preferring to explore mountains with hundreds of retainers, cutting trails and building estates. He was a serious Buddhist and wrote on sudden enlightenment. Repeatedly accused of arrogance and finally of rebellion, he was exiled to Guangzhou and executed there.

In dense, parallel couplets full of precise observation and rare words, Xie described the climbs, rivers, cliffs and changing light of the south-eastern hills, often ending with a philosophical reflection. Although later readers found him laboured beside Tao Yuanming, he established nature itself, rather than nature as backdrop, as a central subject of Chinese poetry, preparing the way for Wang Wei and the Tang.

## Key works

### Climbing the Tower by the Pond (423)

- **Date:** 423
- **Type:** Poem
- **Written / first published:** Yongjia (Wenzhou), Zhejiang
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Climbing%20the%20Tower%20by%20the%20Pond%20Xie%20Lingyun)

Written while Xie served unhappily as governor of Yongjia, after his demotion from court, the poem begins by contrasting the dragon hidden in the depths and the high-flying goose, images of the recluse and the official, with his own failure to be either. Bedridden through the winter, he finally opens the window and climbs the tower, seeing the changed season: spring grasses on the pond and the songs of birds altered among the willows. That couplet was admired as one of the most natural lines in Chinese poetry, and Xie himself reportedly said it came to him in a dream. The poem ends by resolving that retirement need not wait for old age. It shows his typical structure of narrative, landscape and reflection in parallel couplets.
