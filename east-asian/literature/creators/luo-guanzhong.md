# Luo Guanzhong

*羅貫中*

**c. 1330 – c. 1400** · Chinese  
Born: Taiyuan, Shanxi (or Dongping, Shandong) · Died:   
Movements & eras: [Ming novels & chuanqi drama](../movements/ming-novel-drama.md)  

> Late Yuan–early Ming writer traditionally credited with Romance of the Three Kingdoms and a share in Water Margin.

## Biography

Luo Guanzhong is a shadowy figure. A brief note by a contemporary describes him as a solitary man who wrote plays and moved restlessly during the wars at the end of the Yuan; legend links him to the rebel Zhang Shicheng. His home town is given variously as Taiyuan or Dongping. He is traditionally said to have been a pupil of Shi Nai'an.

Several novels are attributed to him, but his name is firmly attached to Romance of the Three Kingdoms, earliest printed in 1522, which reshaped centuries of history and storytelling into a coherent epic. How much of the surviving text is his, and his role in Water Margin, are debated.

- **Date:** 14th century; printed 1522
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** China; earliest edition Beijing, 1522
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Romance%20of%20the%20Three%20Kingdoms%20Luo%20Guanzhong)

Based on Chen Shou's third-century history and centuries of storytelling and drama, the novel covers about a century from the Yellow Turban rebellion of 184 to the reunification of China under the Jin in 280. It begins with the oath of brotherhood sworn in a peach garden by Liu Bei, Guan Yu and Zhang Fei, and follows their struggle against the cunning Cao Cao and the southern state of Wu, culminating in the battle of Red Cliff and the long campaigns of the strategist Zhuge Liang. Written in a simplified classical Chinese, it blends history with legend, about seven parts fact to three parts fiction in the traditional estimate. The standard 120-chapter version was edited by Mao Lun and Mao Zonggang around 1680. One of the Four Classic Novels, it has shaped popular ideas of loyalty, strategy and heroism across East Asia: Guan Yu became a god of war and wealth, and the story lives on in opera, comics and video games.
