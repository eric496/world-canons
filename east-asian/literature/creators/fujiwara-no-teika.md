## Biography

Teika (or Sadaie) was the son of the poet Fujiwara no Shunzei. He was a leading poet at the court of Retired Emperor Go-Toba, who appointed him one of the compilers of the Shin Kokinshū (1205), and later compiled the Shin Chokusenshū alone. His diary, Meigetsuki, covers more than fifty years of court life.

Teika developed an ideal of ethereal, suggestive beauty (yōen and ushin) and wrote influential treatises on poetry. His copies and editions of classical texts, including The Tale of Genji, preserved them for later ages. The anthology of a hundred poets each represented by one poem, associated with his villa at Ogura, became the basis of a card game still played at New Year.

- **Date:** 1205
- **Type:** Poetry anthology
- **Written / first published:** Kyoto, court of Retired Emperor Go-Toba
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Shin%20Kokin%20Wakash%C5%AB%20Fujiwara%20no%20Teika)

Ordered by Retired Emperor Go-Toba, who took an active part in its selection, the New Collection of Ancient and Modern Poems was compiled by Fujiwara no Teika, Fujiwara no Ietaka, Jakuren and others, with about 2,000 poems in twenty books. It includes many recent poets, above all Saigyō, Jien, Shunzei, Teika and Princess Shikishi, and older poems. Its style favours symbolic, dreamlike imagery, allusion to earlier poems (honkadori) and moods of loneliness and yūgen, mysterious depth, as in the three famous 'autumn evening' poems by Jakuren, Saigyō and Teika. Go-Toba continued revising it in exile on the Oki Islands. Regarded with the Man'yōshū and Kokinshū as one of the three great anthologies, it shaped Noh, linked verse and the tea ceremony.

### Hyakunin Isshu (c. 1235)

[![Hyakunin Isshu](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/04/Hyakuninisshu_001.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hyakuninisshu_001.jpg)

<sub>Emperor Tenji's poem in an Edo-period illustrated edition Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hyakuninisshu_001.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1235
- **Type:** Poetry anthology
- **Written / first published:** Ogura, near Kyoto
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Hyakunin%20Isshu%20Fujiwara%20no%20Teika)

The Ogura Hyakunin Isshu is traditionally said to have been selected by Fujiwara no Teika to decorate sliding doors at the Ogura villa of his son's father-in-law. It contains one tanka by each of a hundred poets, roughly in chronological order from Emperor Tenji in the seventh century to Retired Emperors Go-Toba and Juntoku in the thirteenth, including Ono no Komachi, Ariwara no Narihira, Ki no Tsurayuki, Murasaki Shikibu, Sei Shōnagon and Saigyō. Teika's authorship has been questioned, though most scholars accept it. Printed in illustrated editions in the Edo period and used for the card game uta-garuta, still played at the New Year and in competitive form, it became the most widely known collection of classical Japanese poetry.
