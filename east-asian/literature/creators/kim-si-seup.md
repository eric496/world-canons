## Biography

Kim Si-seup was a celebrated prodigy said to have been praised by King Sejong at five. When Prince Suyang usurped the throne from his nephew in 1455 (becoming King Sejo), Kim burned his books, became a Buddhist monk and spent most of his life wandering, as one of the 'Six Loyal Subjects Who Lived'. He lived for some years at Geumo-san near Gyeongju, where he wrote his tales.

His Geumo sinhwa, five tales in classical Chinese modelled on the Ming collection New Tales Under the Lamplight, tell of love between the living and ghosts and of journeys to other worlds. They are regarded as the beginning of Korean prose fiction. He also left some 2,200 poems and philosophical writings.

## Key works

### New Stories of the Golden Turtle (Geumo sinhwa) (c. 1465–70)

- **Date:** c. 1465–70
- **Type:** Tale collection
- **Written / first published:** Geumo-san, Gyeongju
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=New%20Stories%20of%20the%20Golden%20Turtle%20%28Geumo%20sinhwa%29%20Kim%20Si-seup)

Written by Kim Si-seup while living as a monk at Mount Geumo near Gyeongju, the collection contains five tales modelled on Qu You's Ming collection New Tales Under the Lamplight but set in Korea. In The Tale of Manboksa Temple, a lonely scholar plays a game of dice with the Buddha to win a wife, and spends nights with a beautiful woman who turns out to be the ghost of a girl killed in the Japanese pirate raids; in another, a young man's wife is killed in war and returns to him as a ghost for a few years. Others describe a visit to the underworld and to a dragon palace. Combining love, grief and implicit criticism of the times, the tales are counted the first Korean novels. Lost in Korea, the work survived in Japanese editions and was rediscovered in the twentieth century.
