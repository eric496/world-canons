## Biography

Almost nothing certain is known about Ono no Komachi beyond her poems; she was probably a lady-in-waiting at the court in the mid-ninth century. Ki no Tsurayuki named her among the Six Poetic Immortals in the Kokinshū preface, comparing her poems to a beautiful woman troubled by illness.

Some eighteen poems in the Kokinshū are securely hers, many about love, dreams and the fading of beauty. Later legend made her a proud beauty who rejected suitors and ended as a wandering beggar, a story told in several Noh plays, including Sotoba Komachi.

## Key works

### The Colour of the Flowers (Hana no iro wa) (9th century)

- **Date:** 9th century
- **Type:** Waka
- **Written / first published:** Heian-kyō (Kyoto)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Colour%20of%20the%20Flowers%20%28Hana%20no%20iro%20wa%29%20Ono%20no%20Komachi)

Included in the Kokinshū's spring books and later in the Hyakunin Isshu, this tanka says that the colour of the flowers has faded while the poet gazed out at the long rains, idly passing through the world in vain. Its pivot words make the poem say two things at once: nagame means both long rains and pensive gazing, and furu both to fall and to grow old, so that the cherry blossoms' fading is also the poet's own loss of beauty and time. A model of the Kokinshū's technical brilliance and melancholy, it is the poem by which Komachi is best known, and it contributed to her legend as the beauty who grew old.
