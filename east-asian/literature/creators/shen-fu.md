# Shen Fu

*沈復*

**1763 – after 1810** · Chinese  
Born: Suzhou · Died:   
Movements & eras: [Qing fiction & drama](../movements/qing-fiction-drama.md)  

> Minor Qing clerk and painter whose memoir Six Records of a Floating Life is a classic of personal prose.

## Biography

Shen Fu spent his life as a secretary to officials, a merchant and a painter, often in debt and travelling. He married his cousin Chen Yun in 1780; the couple's happiness was strained by family disapproval, and after her death in 1803 he drifted further. Nothing is known of him after about 1810.

His memoir, of which four of six chapters survive, was discovered in a Suzhou bookstall and published in 1877. Its candid, tender account of married love, small pleasures, quarrels and grief was unlike anything in Chinese literature and became beloved in the twentieth century.

## Key works

### Six Records of a Floating Life (c. 1809; published 1877)

- **Date:** c. 1809; published 1877
- **Type:** Memoir
- **Written / first published:** Suzhou; first published Shanghai, 1877
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Six%20Records%20of%20a%20Floating%20Life%20Shen%20Fu)

Shen Fu planned six chapters but only four survive: on the joys of married life, the pleasures of leisure, the sorrows of hardship and his travels. The heart of the book is his wife Chen Yun, his cousin, whom he describes with unusual tenderness: she dresses as a man to attend a festival with him, puts tea leaves in lotus flowers overnight to scent them, and plans to find him a concubine. Disapproval from his parents, debts and illness drive them from home, and Yun dies in poverty. Written with candour and charm, outside the conventions of formal literature, the memoir was discovered in manuscript in the nineteenth century and became a favourite of modern Chinese readers; Lin Yutang translated it into English.
