# Kim Man-jung

*金萬重*

**1637 – 1692** · Korean  
Born: On a boat off Ganghwa (traditionally) · Died: Namhae Island  
Movements & eras: [Joseon sijo, gasa & early Hangul fiction](../movements/joseon-sijo-gasa.md)  

> Joseon official and novelist, author of The Cloud Dream of the Nine.

## Biography

Kim Man-jung was born after his father's death during the Manchu invasion, reportedly on a boat as his mother fled. He passed the examinations and held high office but was repeatedly exiled in factional disputes. He is said to have written The Cloud Dream of the Nine in exile to console his mother, and died in exile on Namhae.

Besides that novel, a Buddhist allegory of worldly glory, he wrote Record of Lady Sa's Journey South, a critique of the king's treatment of his queen. He argued that Korean literature should be written in Korean rather than Chinese, an unusual view among scholars of his time.

### The Cloud Dream of the Nine (Guunmong) (1687)

[![The Cloud Dream of the Nine (Guunmong)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/The_Cloud_Dream_of_the_Nine_in_museum.jpg/1280px-The_Cloud_Dream_of_the_Nine_in_museum.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Cloud_Dream_of_the_Nine_in_museum.jpg)

<sub>Old edition of Guunmong in a museum Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Cloud_Dream_of_the_Nine_in_museum.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1687
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Seoncheon, North Pyeongan (in exile)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Cloud%20Dream%20of%20the%20Nine%20%28Guunmong%29%20Kim%20Man-jung)

Seongjin, a young disciple of a Buddhist master on Mount Lianhua, is distracted by an encounter with eight fairy maidens and is punished by being reborn in the human world as Yang Soyu. In Tang China he becomes a brilliant scholar, general and chief minister, and meets in turn the eight women, reborn as princesses, courtesans and a swordswoman, who become his wives and concubines. At the height of his success and happiness, on a terrace looking at ancient tombs, he realises the vanity of it all and wakes up as Seongjin on the mountain; he and the eight women attain enlightenment. Said to have been written in a few nights in exile to console Kim's mother, the novel combines romance, Buddhist allegory and Confucian ideals, and it was one of the most read works of Joseon fiction. James Scarth Gale's English translation appeared in 1922.

### Record of Lady Sa's Journey South (Sa-ssi namjeonggi) (c. 1690)

- **Date:** c. 1690
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Namhae (in exile)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Record%20of%20Lady%20Sa%27s%20Journey%20South%20%28Sa-ssi%20namjeonggi%29%20Kim%20Man-jung)

Set in Ming China, the novel tells how Lady Sa, the virtuous wife of the official Yu Yeonsu, persuades him to take a concubine, Gyo, since she has no son. Gyo slanders her, has her son murdered and frames her, and Lady Sa is expelled and wanders south in hardship, until Gyo's crimes are discovered and Lady Sa returns. Contemporary readers understood it as a protest against King Sukjong's deposition of Queen Inhyeon in favour of the royal concubine Jang, a cause for which Kim was in exile. Written in Hangul, it is one of the first Korean novels of domestic life.
