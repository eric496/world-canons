## Biography

Born Takizawa Okikuni into a samurai family in Edo, Bakin gave up service, studied with the comic writer Santō Kyōden, and became one of the first Japanese authors to live from writing. His collaborations with illustrators, including Hokusai, were bestsellers. Blind in his last years, he dictated the end of Hakkenden to his daughter-in-law.

Bakin's yomihon, long illustrated novels influenced by Chinese fiction such as Water Margin, combine adventure and Confucian morality, rewarding good and punishing evil. Nansō Satomi Hakkenden, published 1814–42, follows eight heroes each bearing a Confucian virtue, and remains a source for modern manga and anime.

- **Type:** Yomihon (novel)
- **Written / first published:** Edo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Nans%C5%8D%20Satomi%20Hakkenden%20Kyokutei%20Bakin)

Bakin's huge novel, in 106 volumes published over twenty-eight years, is set in the fifteenth-century Warring States period. Princess Fuse, daughter of the lord Satomi, is promised to the dog Yatsufusa when it brings back an enemy's head; she lives with it in the mountains and dies, and the eight beads of her rosary, each marked with a virtue, among them benevolence, righteousness, loyalty and filial piety, scatter. Eight young men born with the beads and a peony birthmark, the 'dog warriors', find each other through many adventures and rally to the Satomi clan. Combining Water Margin, Buddhist karma and Confucian morality, the Hakkenden was the most popular Edo novel, the source of many ukiyo-e prints, kabuki plays and modern manga and anime.
