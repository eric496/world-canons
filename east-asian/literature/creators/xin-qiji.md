# Xin Qiji

*辛棄疾*

**1140 – 1207** · Chinese  
Born: Licheng (Jinan), Shandong · Died: Yanshan, Jiangxi  
Movements & eras: [Song ci and Song letters](../movements/song-ci.md)  

> Soldier and statesman of the Southern Song whose heroic ci lyrics lament the lost north.

## Biography

Born under Jurchen Jin rule in the north, Xin Qiji joined a rebel army at twenty-one and made his name by capturing a traitor who had murdered its leader and delivering him to the Song court at Lin'an. He served the Southern Song as an able provincial administrator and military organiser, but his calls for reconquest of the north were unwelcome to a court that preferred peace, and he spent some twenty years in enforced retirement in Jiangxi.

More than 600 of his ci survive, the largest number by any Song poet. They are bold and expansive, full of historical allusion and frustrated martial energy, but also include tender, humorous and rustic poems. With Su Shi he is considered the master of the 'heroic and unrestrained' (haofang) school of ci.

### Green Jade Table: Lantern Festival (c. 1170s)

- **Date:** c. 1170s
- **Type:** Ci (lyric)
- **Written / first published:** Lin'an (Hangzhou)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Green%20Jade%20Table%3A%20Lantern%20Festival%20Xin%20Qiji)

The lyric describes the Lantern Festival in the Southern Song capital: an east wind sets a thousand trees of fireworks blossoming at night and blows down stars like rain; jewelled carriages fill the roads, flutes sound, the jade-pot moon turns, and fish and dragon lanterns dance all night. Ladies in moth and willow ornaments pass with laughter and a trail of scent. The speaker searches the crowd a thousand times for one person, and suddenly turning his head, finds her where the lantern light is fading. The closing lines have been read as a love scene and as an image of the poet's own aloofness from the court; the scholar Wang Guowei later used them as a figure for the highest stage of learning.

### Recalling the Past at Beigu Pavilion, Jingkou (1205)

- **Date:** 1205
- **Type:** Ci (lyric)
- **Written / first published:** Zhenjiang, Jiangsu
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Recalling%20the%20Past%20at%20Beigu%20Pavilion%2C%20Jingkou%20Xin%20Qiji)

Xin Qiji wrote this lyric at sixty-five while prefect of Zhenjiang, as the court prepared an ill-judged campaign against the Jin. From the pavilion overlooking the Yangtze he remembers Sun Quan of the Three Kingdoms and Liu Yu, the fifth-century general who marched north from here, and warns of the rash Yuanjia campaign of 450 that ended in disaster. He recalls his own youth fighting in the north, forty-three years ago, and the temple to a barbarian conqueror where villagers now worship to drums. The last line asks who will inquire whether the old general Lian Po can still eat well, that is, whether the court still wants him. Dense with allusion yet passionate, it shows Xin's heroic style.
