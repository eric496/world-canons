> The poet of fields and wine who gave up office to farm, and the author of the prose fable Peach Blossom Spring.

## Biography

Tao Yuanming, also called Tao Qian, came from a family of declining gentry in what is now Jiangxi. He held a series of minor posts in a period of usurpation and civil war, and in 405, after only some eighty days as magistrate of Pengze, resigned rather than, as the story goes, bow to a visiting inspector for five pecks of rice. He spent the rest of his life farming near his home, often in poverty, writing poems and drinking with neighbours.

Tao's poems about his garden, chrysanthemums, wine and the satisfaction of ordinary country life were little valued in his own century, which preferred ornament. From the Tang onwards, and especially after Su Shi championed him, his plain style and his choice of integrity over career made him the model of the recluse poet. Peach Blossom Spring, his tale of a hidden valley untouched by history, gave East Asia its lasting image of utopia.

### Returning Home (405)

- **Date:** 405
- **Type:** Fu (rhapsody)
- **Written / first published:** Pengze and Chaisang, Eastern Jin
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Returning%20Home%20Tao%20Yuanming)

Tao Yuanming wrote this short rhapsody after resigning as magistrate of Pengze late in 405, with a preface explaining that he had taken the post out of poverty but could not bear to bend against his nature. The poem opens with the exclamation of its title, urging himself home since his fields and garden are growing wild, and describes the boat ride, his children and servants at the gate, the three paths overgrown with weeds but the pines and chrysanthemums still there, wine waiting in a jar. He resolves to break off social relations, climb the eastern hill, write poems by the clear stream and accept whatever fate brings. The piece became the classic statement of eremitic retirement, endlessly quoted, copied in calligraphy and painted.

### Drinking Wine (c. 417)

- **Date:** c. 417
- **Type:** Poem sequence
- **Written / first published:** Chaisang, Eastern Jin
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Drinking%20Wine%20Tao%20Yuanming)

In a preface Tao says that, living idly with little to please him and long autumn nights, he wrote these twenty poems whenever he had been drinking alone. They range over history, friendship, poverty and the folly of officials, but the best known is the fifth, in which he builds his hut among other people yet hears no noise of carriages, because a distant mind makes its place remote; picking chrysanthemums by the eastern hedge, he sees the southern mountain, and the birds returning together at dusk hold a meaning that he cannot put into words. This poem, with its quiet blend of daily life and insight, came to embody Tao's ideal and was much admired by Su Shi and later Zen-influenced readers.

### Peach Blossom Spring (c. 421)

- **Date:** c. 421
- **Type:** Prose fable with poem
- **Written / first published:** Xunyang, Eastern Jin / Liu Song China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Peach%20Blossom%20Spring%20Tao%20Yuanming)

Tao Yuanming's short prose record, accompanied by a poem, tells how a fisherman of Wuling, following a stream lined with blossoming peach trees, passes through a narrow cave into a fertile valley of farms, ponds and mulberry trees. Its people, descendants of refugees from the Qin wars, welcome him and ask about the outside world; they know nothing of the Han or the Wei and Jin dynasties. On leaving, he marks his route and reports to the governor, but the valley can never be found again. Written in plain, calm prose, the tale offers a Daoist and agrarian utopia outside the violence of politics, and 'Peach Blossom Spring' became a lasting East Asian name for an earthly paradise, painted and versified by countless artists from Wang Wei to the Korean painter An Gyeon.
