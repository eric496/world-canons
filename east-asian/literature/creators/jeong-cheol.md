# Jeong Cheol

*鄭澈*

**1536 – 1593** · Korean  
Born: Seoul · Died: Ganghwa Island  
Movements & eras: [Joseon sijo, gasa & early Hangul fiction](../movements/joseon-sijo-gasa.md)  

> Joseon statesman and poet, the supreme master of the gasa.

## Biography

Jeong Cheol, pen name Songgang, was a leading member of the Western faction at the court of King Seonjo and held high office, including chief state councillor. His career alternated between power and exile in the factional struggles, and he led a harsh purge of the opposing faction in 1589. He died on Ganghwa Island during the Japanese invasions.

His four gasa, including Gwandong byeolgok, a poetic journey through the Diamond Mountains, and the paired Samiingok and Sokmiingok, in which a woman longing for her lover expresses a minister's devotion to the king, are considered the summit of Korean vernacular poetry. He also wrote some eighty sijo.

### Gwandong byeolgok (Song of Gwandong) (1580)

- **Date:** 1580
- **Type:** Gasa
- **Written / first published:** Gangwon Province
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Gwandong%20byeolgok%20%28Song%20of%20Gwandong%29%20Jeong%20Cheol)

Written when Jeong Cheol was appointed governor of Gangwon Province, the gasa follows his official tour from the capital through the Geumgang (Diamond) Mountains, with their waterfalls and temples, to the eight famous scenic spots of the East Sea coast. In flowing four-beat lines mixing Korean and Chinese vocabulary, it describes the landscape with ecstatic imagery, recalls Daoist immortals and Chinese poets, and ends in a dream in which an immortal offers him wine and he wishes to share it with all the people of the land. It is considered the finest of Korean travel poems and a model for the gasa form.

### Hymn of Longing for the Beloved (Samiingok) (c. 1588)

- **Date:** c. 1588
- **Type:** Gasa
- **Written / first published:** Changpyeong, Jeolla Province
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Hymn%20of%20Longing%20for%20the%20Beloved%20%28Samiingok%29%20Jeong%20Cheol)

Written during Jeong Cheol's retirement in his home district after losing office, the poem is spoken by a woman separated from her beloved, whom she followed in a previous life. Through the four seasons she sews him clothes, sends him plum blossoms and moonlight, and worries about his cold, and finally wishes to become a butterfly to settle on his robe even if he does not recognise her. Read as an allegory of a loyal official's love for King Seonjo, the poem, with its sequel Sokmiingok, was praised by the novelist Kim Man-jung as one of the true works of Korean literature, comparable to the Li Sao.
