# Yi Kwang-su

*李光洙*

**1892 – 1950** · Korean  
Born: Jeongju, North Pyeongan · Died:   
Movements & eras: [Modern Korean literature](../movements/modern-korean.md)  

> Novelist whose Mujeong (The Heartless, 1917) is regarded as the first modern Korean novel.

## Biography

Orphaned young, Yi Kwang-su studied in Tokyo, where he drafted the 1919 Korean declaration of independence by students. He joined the provisional government in Shanghai, returned to Korea, and became the most famous writer of the colonial period. From the late 1930s he collaborated with the Japanese authorities, a fact that has clouded his reputation. He was taken north during the Korean War and died there.

Mujeong, serialised in a newspaper, broke with traditional fiction in its colloquial Hangul style and its themes of free love, education and enlightenment.

## Key works

### Mujeong (The Heartless) (1917)

- **Date:** 1917
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Serialised in Maeil sinbo, Seoul, 1917
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Mujeong%20%28The%20Heartless%29%20Yi%20Kwang-su)

Yi Hyeong-sik, an English teacher in Seoul, is torn between Yeong-chae, the daughter of his former benefactor, who has become a kisaeng to save her imprisoned father while keeping herself chaste for Hyeong-sik, and Seon-hyeong, the educated daughter of a wealthy Christian elder, to whom he becomes engaged. After Yeong-chae is raped and sets out to drown herself, she meets a modern young woman who persuades her to live and study. The characters meet on a train and resolve together to devote themselves to educating the nation, and all go abroad to study. Written in a colloquial Hangul prose free of classical conventions and full of the ideals of enlightenment and free love, it was a sensation and is considered the beginning of modern Korean fiction.
