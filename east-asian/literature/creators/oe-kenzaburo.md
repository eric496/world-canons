# Ōe Kenzaburō

*大江健三郎*

**1935 – 2023** · Japanese  
Born: Ōse, Ehime · Died:   
Movements & eras: [Postwar Japanese literature](../movements/postwar-japanese.md)  

> Novelist of moral engagement, winner of the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1994.

## Biography

Ōe grew up in a forested village on Shikoku and studied French literature at the University of Tokyo, winning the Akutagawa Prize in 1958 while still a student. The birth in 1963 of his son Hikari with a serious brain abnormality transformed his work. He was a prominent pacifist and anti-nuclear campaigner, wrote Hiroshima Notes, and declined Japan's Order of Culture in 1994.

His novels, from A Personal Matter (1964) and The Silent Cry (1967) onward, combine personal experience, myth of his native village and political themes in a dense style influenced by Sartre and Rabelais. The Nobel committee praised his creation of an imagined world where life and myth condense into a picture of the human predicament.

### A Personal Matter (1964)

- **Date:** 1964
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Tokyo: Shinchōsha, 1964
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Personal%20Matter%20%C5%8Ce%20Kenzabur%C5%8D)

Bird, a 27-year-old cram-school teacher who dreams of travelling to Africa, learns that his newborn son has a brain hernia and will probably be severely disabled. Unable to face the situation, he escapes into drink and an affair with a former girlfriend, Himiko, and together they plan to let the baby die by arranging with an unscrupulous doctor. At the last moment Bird decides to take responsibility and have his son operated on. Written after the birth of Ōe's own son Hikari, the novel combines existentialist themes and dark humour with an honest exploration of shame and responsibility, and it was a turning point in his work.

### The Silent Cry (1967)

- **Date:** 1967
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Tokyo: Kōdansha, 1967
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Silent%20Cry%20%C5%8Ce%20Kenzabur%C5%8D)

Its Japanese title, 'Football in the First Year of Man'en', refers to a peasant uprising of 1860 led by the narrator's great-grandfather's brother. Mitsusaburō, a withdrawn translator whose friend has committed suicide and whose child is disabled, returns with his alcoholic wife and his charismatic younger brother Takashi, back from the United States, to the family's forest village. Takashi organises the village youth into a football team and then a riot against a Korean supermarket owner, reliving the uprising, until a revelation of incest and his suicide. Interweaving myth, history and personal guilt, it is often considered Ōe's masterpiece.
