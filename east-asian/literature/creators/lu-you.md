## Biography

Lu You's family fled south after the fall of the north in 1127, when he was a baby. His career was hampered by his hawkish views; he served for a time on the frontier in Sichuan, an experience he idealised for the rest of his life, and spent long periods in retirement at his home in Shaoxing. His forced divorce from his first wife Tang Wan, and the poem he later wrote on the wall of the Shen Garden, became a famous love story.

Lu You left some 9,000 shi poems as well as ci and prose diaries, including an account of a journey up the Yangtze. His poems range from calls for recovery of the north, culminating in his deathbed poem to his son, to observant, gently humorous pieces on village life, old age and the seasons.

## Key works

### To My Son (1210)

- **Date:** 1210
- **Type:** Poem (quatrain)
- **Written / first published:** Shanyin (Shaoxing)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=To%20My%20Son%20Lu%20You)

In this quatrain, said to be the last poem Lu You wrote at eighty-five, he reflects that at death all things are empty, but his only grief is not to see the nine provinces united. When the imperial armies retake the central plains in the north, he asks his son not to forget to report it to him at the family sacrifice. The poem's directness and unfulfilled hope made it the most famous expression of Southern Song patriotism, and it is still taught in schools as a model of devotion to the nation.
