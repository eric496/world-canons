# Wu Jingzi

*吳敬梓*

**1701 – 1754** · Chinese  
Born: Quanjiao, Anhui · Died: Yangzhou  
Movements & eras: [Qing fiction & drama](../movements/qing-fiction-drama.md)  

> Qing novelist whose The Scholars is the great satire of the examination system and literati hypocrisy.

## Biography

Wu Jingzi came from a distinguished family of officials in Anhui. He squandered his inheritance in generosity and pleasure, moved to Nanjing in 1733, and declined an invitation to sit a special examination. He lived the rest of his life in increasing poverty among scholars and artists and died suddenly while visiting Yangzhou.

His novel The Scholars, completed around 1750, is a loosely linked series of episodes satirising those who chase degrees and fame, contrasted with a few true men of integrity. Its restrained, ironic realism made it, in Lu Xun's view, the first true satire in Chinese fiction.

## Key works

### The Scholars (c. 1750)

- **Date:** c. 1750
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Nanjing; first printed 1803
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Scholars%20Wu%20Jingzi)

Set nominally in the Ming dynasty but clearly reflecting the eighteenth century, The Scholars has no single plot; its 56 chapters pass from character to character like a scroll. Among its famous episodes, Fan Jin, an old candidate who has failed for decades, goes mad with joy on finally passing the provincial examination and is slapped back to sanity by his butcher father-in-law, who had always despised him; a stingy dying man cannot close his eyes until two lamp-wicks are reduced to one. Against these hypocrites the novel sets a few men of real integrity, including Du Shaoqing, a self-portrait of the author, and a group who rebuild a temple to an ancient sage. Its ironic understatement made it, for Lu Xun and Hu Shi, the first great Chinese satirical novel.
