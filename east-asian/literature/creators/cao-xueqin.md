# Cao Xueqin

*曹雪芹*

**c. 1715 – c. 1763** · Chinese  
Born: Nanjing · Died: Beijing  
Movements & eras: [Qing fiction & drama](../movements/qing-fiction-drama.md)  

> Author of Dream of the Red Chamber, generally regarded as the greatest Chinese novel.

## Biography

Cao Xueqin's family had for three generations held the lucrative post of imperial textile commissioner in Nanjing and hosted the Kangxi emperor on his southern tours. In 1728, under the Yongzheng emperor, the family was disgraced and its property confiscated, and they moved to Beijing. Cao spent his later years in poverty in the western hills outside the city, painting, drinking and writing, and died, according to friends' poems, shortly after the death of his son.

He worked for about ten years on The Story of the Stone, as the novel was first called, circulating it in manuscript with comments by a relative using the name Red Inkstone. Only eighty chapters were complete at his death; a 120-chapter version with an ending by others appeared in 1791. Its autobiographical depth, vast cast and poetic richness have generated an entire field of study, 'Redology'.

## Key works

### Dream of the Red Chamber (c. 1750s–1760s; printed 1791)

[![Dream of the Red Chamber](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/79/Hongloumeng2.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hongloumeng2.jpg)

<sub>Qing-dynasty illustration of the novel Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hongloumeng2.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1750s–1760s; printed 1791
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Beijing; first printed by Cheng Weiyuan and Gao E, 1791
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dream%20of%20the%20Red%20Chamber%20Cao%20Xueqin)

Also called The Story of the Stone, the novel begins with a magic stone left over when the goddess Nüwa repaired the sky, which is carried into the human world and born as Jia Baoyu, the sensitive heir of a great family in the capital, who prefers the company of girls to the study of the classics. He grows up in the Prospect Garden, built for a visit by his sister, an imperial consort, among a dozen remarkable young women, above all his frail, poetic cousin Lin Daiyu and the sensible Xue Baochai. Over 120 chapters and some 400 characters, the family's wealth and morals decay; Baoyu is tricked into marrying Baochai, Daiyu dies, and Baoyu leaves the world as a monk. Only the first 80 chapters are certainly by Cao Xueqin; the last 40 were published by Cheng Weiyuan and Gao E, perhaps using his drafts, a question long debated. Its psychological depth, poetry and detailed picture of Qing elite life make it the subject of 'Redology', a scholarly field in its own right.
