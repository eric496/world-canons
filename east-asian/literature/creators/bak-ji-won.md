# Bak Ji-won

*朴趾源*

**1737 – 1805** · Korean  
Born: Seoul · Died: Seoul  
Movements & eras: [Late Joseon pansori & Silhak prose](../movements/late-joseon-prose.md)  
Also in: [Philosophy](../../philosophy/creators/bak-ji-won.md)  

> Silhak scholar and satirist, author of The Jehol Diary and the Tale of the Yangban.

## Biography

Pak Ji-won, pen name Yeonam, came from a noble family but avoided the examinations and spent years in study with a circle of reform-minded scholars. In 1780 he accompanied a Korean embassy to the Qing court and to the emperor's summer retreat at Rehe (Jehol). He later served in local office.

His Jehol Diary praises Qing technology and argues for practical learning, and his tales, such as The Tale of the Yangban and Master Heo, satirise the idle nobility. His lively style, mixing colloquial and classical Chinese, was censured by King Jeongjo, who saw it as a threat to proper prose.

### The Tale of the Yangban (late 18th century)

- **Date:** late 18th century
- **Type:** Satirical tale
- **Written / first published:** Joseon Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Tale%20of%20the%20Yangban%20Bak%20Ji-won)

A poor but learned yangban in Jeongseon has borrowed so much government grain that he faces punishment. A rich commoner offers to pay the debt in exchange for his noble status, and the magistrate draws up a contract listing the obligations of a yangban: to rise before dawn, recite texts, walk slowly, never handle money or touch the abacus, never take off his hat in the heat. The commoner asks for something more profitable, and a second contract lists the privileges: to exploit the farmers, seize their oxen and take their wives. The commoner declares that the yangban are thieves and runs away. Written in classical Chinese, the short tale is a classic of Korean satire on the parasitism of the aristocracy.

### The Jehol Diary (Yeolha ilgi) (c. 1783)

[![The Jehol Diary (Yeolha ilgi)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a4/Pak_Chiw%C5%8Fn_Yeolha_ilgi.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pak_Chiw%C5%8Fn_Yeolha_ilgi.jpg)

<sub>Manuscript of the Jehol Diary Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pak_Chiw%C5%8Fn_Yeolha_ilgi.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1783
- **Type:** Travel diary
- **Written / first published:** Joseon Korea; journey to Rehe (Chengde), 1780
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Jehol%20Diary%20%28Yeolha%20ilgi%29%20Bak%20Ji-won)

In 1780 Pak Ji-won travelled with the Korean embassy sent to congratulate the Qianlong emperor on his seventieth birthday, a journey that took the envoys beyond Beijing to the summer palace at Rehe (Jehol). His diary in twenty-six books records the journey, conversations by written Chinese with Qing scholars, the Tibetan Panchen Lama, observations of carts, kilns, bricks and city planning, and arguments that Korea should learn from Qing technology rather than despise the Manchus as barbarians. It also contains two satirical tales, The Tiger's Rebuke and Master Heo. Its lively, colloquial Chinese style shocked conservatives, and King Jeongjo made it the target of his campaign to restore proper prose. It is the most celebrated of Korean travel writings and a key text of Silhak thought.
