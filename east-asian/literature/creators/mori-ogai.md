# Mori Ōgai

*森鷗外*

**1862 – 1922** · Japanese  
Born: Tsuwano, Iwami Province · Died: Tokyo  
Movements & eras: [Meiji to early Shōwa modern literature](../movements/meiji-modern.md)  

> Army surgeon, translator and novelist, a founding figure of modern Japanese literature.

## Biography

Born Mori Rintarō into a family of physicians to the lord of Tsuwano, Ōgai graduated in medicine from Tokyo and joined the army, which sent him to Germany from 1884 to 1888. He rose to Surgeon General of the Imperial Army while pursuing a parallel literary career, translating Goethe, Andersen and Ibsen and founding literary magazines.

His first story, The Dancing Girl (1890), about a Japanese student's love affair in Berlin, opened modern Japanese fiction of the self. After the death of Emperor Meiji and General Nogi's ritual suicide in 1912, he turned to historical fiction and biographies of Edo-period figures, written in a restrained, precise style that influenced many later writers.

### The Dancing Girl (1890)

- **Date:** 1890
- **Type:** Novella
- **Written / first published:** Kokumin no tomo magazine, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Dancing%20Girl%20Mori%20%C5%8Cgai)

Narrated on a ship returning to Japan by Ōta Toyotarō, a brilliant young official sent to study in Berlin, the novella tells how he met Elise, a poor young dancer, weeping outside a church, helped her pay for her father's funeral, and fell in love with her, losing his position as a result. When a friend offers him a way back into official favour through a minister, he accepts, and Elise, pregnant, goes mad on learning of his betrayal. Written in an elegant classical style, partly drawing on Ōgai's own experience, it introduced the confessional theme of the self torn between individual feeling and duty to the state.

### Sanshō the Bailiff (1915)

- **Date:** 1915
- **Type:** Short story
- **Written / first published:** Chūō kōron, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sansh%C5%8D%20the%20Bailiff%20Mori%20%C5%8Cgai)

Based on a medieval sekkyō-bushi tale, the story follows Zushiō and his sister Anju, children of an exiled governor, who are kidnapped with their mother while travelling, separated from her and sold to the cruel slave owner Sanshō in Tango. Anju sacrifices herself so that her brother can escape; Zushiō rises to become governor, abolishes slavery, and finally finds his mother, old and blind, on Sado Island. Ōgai's restrained historical style removes the violent revenge of the original and stresses compassion. Mizoguchi's film adaptation of 1954 is one of the masterpieces of world cinema.
