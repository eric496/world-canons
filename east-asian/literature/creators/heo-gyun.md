# Heo Gyun

*許筠*

**1569 – 1618** · Korean  
Born: Gangneung (traditionally) · Died: Seoul  
Movements & eras: [Joseon sijo, gasa & early Hangul fiction](../movements/joseon-sijo-gasa.md)  

> Unconventional Joseon official and writer, traditionally credited with The Tale of Hong Gildong.

## Biography

Heo Gyun came from a distinguished literary family; his sister was the poet Heo Nanseolheon. He passed the examinations and served as an envoy to Ming China, but his open interest in Buddhism and Catholicism and his disregard for Confucian decorum caused repeated dismissals. In 1618 he was accused of plotting rebellion and executed.

He was a prolific critic, poet and essayist, sympathetic to illegitimate sons and other outcasts barred from office. Since the seventeenth century he has been named as the author of Hong Gildong jeon, though the surviving versions are later and the attribution is debated by modern scholars.

## Key works

### The Tale of Hong Gildong (early 17th century (attributed))

[![The Tale of Hong Gildong](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ec/Honggildongjeon.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Honggildongjeon.jpg)

<sub>Woodblock-printed Hangul edition Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Honggildongjeon.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** early 17th century (attributed)
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Joseon Korea; surviving versions 19th century
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Tale%20of%20Hong%20Gildong%20Heo%20Gyun)

Hong Gildong is the son of a high minister and a servant concubine, and as such cannot call his father 'father' or hold office. After escaping an assassination plot by the family, he leaves home and becomes leader of a band of outlaws, the Hwalbindang, 'the band that helps the poor', which robs corrupt officials and temples. Using magic, he creates multiple straw doubles of himself to confound the authorities, is reconciled with the king, and sails to the island kingdom of Yul, which he conquers and rules justly. Traditionally attributed to Heo Gyun, though the surviving Hangul texts date from the nineteenth century and the attribution is debated, it is regarded as the first novel written in Korean, and its hero remains a popular figure in films and television.
