# Hwang Jini

*黃眞伊*

**c. 1506 – c. 1560** · Korean  
Born: Kaesong · Died:   
Movements & eras: [Joseon sijo, gasa & early Hangul fiction](../movements/joseon-sijo-gasa.md)  

> The most famous kisaeng of the Joseon dynasty, whose sijo are among the finest in Korean.

## Biography

Hwang Jini lived in Kaesong in the first half of the sixteenth century; her dates are uncertain and most of her biography comes from later anecdotes. She became a kisaeng, a female entertainer trained in poetry, music and dance, and was celebrated for her beauty, wit and learning, and her friendships with the Neo-Confucian philosopher Seo Gyeong-deok and other scholars.

Only a handful of sijo and Chinese poems survive under her name, but they show a mastery of the form, using native Korean words and natural images with startling directness. Her legend has been the subject of novels, films and television dramas in both North and South Korea.

## Key works

### The sijo of Hwang Jini (16th century)

- **Date:** 16th century
- **Type:** Sijo
- **Written / first published:** Kaesong
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20sijo%20of%20Hwang%20Jini%20Hwang%20Jini)

Only about six sijo are confidently attributed to Hwang Jini. In one, she tells a blue mountain stream not to boast of flowing fast, since once it reaches the sea it cannot return, and urges it to rest while the bright moon fills the empty mountain, a pun on the name of a royal prince, Byeokgyesu, and her own professional name, Myeongwol, 'bright moon'. In another, she wishes to cut the long midwinter night in half and fold it under the spring quilt, to unroll it on the night her lover returns. Others reflect on the constancy of mountains against the passing of water. Her use of native Korean words and witty imagery made her sijo the most admired by a woman in the Joseon period.
