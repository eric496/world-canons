# Mishima Yukio

*三島由紀夫*

**1925 – 1970** · Japanese  
Born: Tokyo · Died: Tokyo  
Movements & eras: [Postwar Japanese literature](../movements/postwar-japanese.md)  

> Novelist, playwright and nationalist, author of The Temple of the Golden Pavilion and The Sea of Fertility.

## Biography

Born Hiraoka Kimitake, Mishima was raised largely by his grandmother and published while still at school. His Confessions of a Mask (1949) made him famous. Enormously prolific, he wrote novels, modern Noh plays and essays, built up his body through bodybuilding and martial arts, and formed a private militia. On 25 November 1970, having finished his tetralogy The Sea of Fertility, he seized a general's office in Tokyo, called on soldiers to restore the emperor's power, and committed ritual suicide.

Mishima's ornate, intellectual prose explores beauty, death, the body and the gap between ideals and reality. The Temple of the Golden Pavilion (1956), based on the arson of Kinkaku-ji, is often considered his masterpiece. His spectacular death has continued to colour readings of his work.

## Key works

### The Temple of the Golden Pavilion (1956)

- **Date:** 1956
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Shinchō magazine, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Temple%20of%20the%20Golden%20Pavilion%20Mishima%20Yukio)

Based on the real arson of Kyoto's Kinkaku-ji by a young monk in 1950, the novel is narrated by Mizoguchi, the son of a poor country priest, who stammers and feels ugly. His father has taught him that the Golden Pavilion is the most beautiful thing in the world, and when he becomes an acolyte there, the temple comes to obsess him, standing between him and life, especially when he tries to make love to women. His friendships with the kind Tsurukawa and the cynical, club-footed Kashiwagi, and Zen koans such as Nansen killing the cat, shape his growing conviction that he must destroy beauty in order to live. Combining psychological study and philosophical meditation in ornate prose, it is widely considered Mishima's masterpiece.
