## Biography

Issa was the son of a farmer in the mountains of Shinano. After his mother's death and conflicts with his stepmother he was sent to Edo at fourteen, where he became a haikai poet and travelled widely as a teacher. Returning home after a long inheritance dispute, he married at fifty-one, but all his children and his first wife died young, and his house burned the year before his death.

Issa wrote some 20,000 haiku, many about frogs, snails, flies and sparrows, in a colloquial, playful and deeply compassionate voice shaped by his Pure Land Buddhist faith and personal grief. His prose-and-poetry journal The Year of My Life (Oraga haru) is a masterpiece of haibun.

## Key works

### The Year of My Life (Oraga haru) (1819; published 1852)

- **Date:** 1819; published 1852
- **Type:** Haibun (poetic journal)
- **Written / first published:** Kashiwabara, Shinano
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Year%20of%20My%20Life%20%28Oraga%20haru%29%20Kobayashi%20Issa)

Oraga haru, 'My Spring', covers the year 1819 in prose and some 250 haiku. It opens with a New Year that Issa greets 'so-so', and records village life, small animals, festivals and folk beliefs. At its centre is his daughter Sato, born the year before, whose playfulness he describes with delight, and her death from smallpox in midsummer, after which he writes the famous haiku saying that he knows this world is a dew-drop world, and yet, and yet. The journal ends with his acceptance of everything into the hands of Amida Buddha. Published long after his death, it is the finest example of Issa's compassionate, humorous and grieving voice.
