# Dazai Osamu

*太宰治*

**1909 – 1948** · Japanese  
Born: Kanagi, Aomori · Died: Tokyo  
Movements & eras: [Postwar Japanese literature](../movements/postwar-japanese.md)  

> Confessional novelist of postwar despair, author of The Setting Sun and No Longer Human.

## Biography

Born Tsushima Shūji, the son of a wealthy landowner and politician in northern Japan, Dazai studied French literature in Tokyo, flirted with communism and made several suicide attempts, one of which killed his companion. Addiction and scandal marked his life, but he produced a large body of work through the war. In 1948 he drowned himself with a lover in the Tamagawa Aqueduct.

His novel The Setting Sun (1947), about a declining aristocratic family, gave its name to the dispossessed postwar class, and No Longer Human, a fictionalised self-portrait of a man who feels unable to be human, is one of the best-selling Japanese novels. His humour and intimacy keep him popular with young readers.

### The Setting Sun (1947)

- **Date:** 1947
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Shinchō magazine, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Setting%20Sun%20Dazai%20Osamu)

Narrated by Kazuko, the daughter of a widowed aristocrat, the novel follows mother and daughter as they lose their Tokyo house after Japan's defeat and move to a small villa in Izu. Kazuko's mother, the last true aristocrat, declines and dies; her brother Naoji, a drug addict returned from the war, kills himself, leaving a desperate letter; and Kazuko, determined to survive, seeks out the dissolute novelist Uehara, has his child and resolves to raise it alone as a 'revolution'. The novel was a huge bestseller and gave the word shayōzoku, 'people of the setting sun', to the declining postwar upper class.

### No Longer Human (1948)

- **Date:** 1948
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Tenbō magazine, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=No%20Longer%20Human%20Dazai%20Osamu)

Framed by a narrator who has found three notebooks and photographs of their author, the novel presents the confessions of Ōba Yōzō, who from childhood feels alienated from other people and masks his fear by playing the fool. As a student in Tokyo he drifts into drinking, left-wing politics and affairs, survives a double suicide in which the woman dies, marries a trusting young woman whose rape by an acquaintance shatters him, becomes addicted to morphine and is committed to an asylum, declaring himself disqualified as a human being. Completed shortly before Dazai's own suicide in 1948 and closely based on his life, it is one of the best-selling novels in Japan and remains popular with young readers worldwide.
