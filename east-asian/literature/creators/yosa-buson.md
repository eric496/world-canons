## Biography

Buson left his home village near Osaka young, studied haikai in Edo, and spent years travelling in the north in Bashō's footsteps. He settled in Kyoto around 1751 and became a leading painter in the Chinese-inspired nanga style, collaborating with Ike no Taiga. Late in life he led a revival of haikai that looked back to Bashō.

Buson's poems are sensuous, pictorial and often romantic or narrative, reflecting his painter's eye and learning in Chinese poetry. He also illustrated haikai texts, including Bashō's Narrow Road. Masaoka Shiki's championing of him in the 1890s raised him almost to Bashō's level.

## Key works

### Spring Breeze on the Kema Embankment (1777)

- **Date:** 1777
- **Type:** Poem sequence (mixed form)
- **Written / first published:** Kyoto; published in Yahantei, 1777
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Spring%20Breeze%20on%20the%20Kema%20Embankment%20Yosa%20Buson)

In this sequence of eighteen pieces, Buson imagines a young woman who works as a servant in Osaka returning home for the spring holiday along the embankment of the Yodo River near his own native village of Kema. Haiku alternate with poems in Chinese and in a free Japanese verse form, describing her crossing the river, the dandelions, a tea house, and her memories of childhood and her mother. The work's mixture of forms and its nostalgia for a lost home were unprecedented, and modern critics have seen it as an anticipation of free verse.
