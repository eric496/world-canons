# Akutagawa Ryūnosuke

*芥川龍之介*

**1892 – 1927** · Japanese  
Born: Tokyo · Died: Tokyo  
Movements & eras: [Meiji to early Shōwa modern literature](../movements/meiji-modern.md)  

> Master of the modern Japanese short story, author of Rashōmon and In a Grove.

## Biography

Akutagawa was born in Tokyo; his mother became mentally ill soon after his birth and he was raised by her family. While studying English at Tokyo Imperial University he published Rashōmon (1915) and The Nose, which Natsume Sōseki praised. He became a celebrated writer, but suffered declining health, fear of inherited madness and anxiety, and took his own life at thirty-five.

Many of his stories retell tales from the Konjaku monogatarishū and other classics with modern psychological irony; later works turned to autobiography and despair. The Akutagawa Prize, Japan's most prestigious literary award for new writers, was founded in his memory in 1935. Kurosawa's film Rashomon (1950) combined two of his stories.

### Rashōmon (1915)

[![Rashōmon](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Rashomon-cover.png/1280px-Rashomon-cover.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rashomon-cover.png)

<sub>Cover of Akutagawa's story collection Rashōmon, 1917 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rashomon-cover.png).</sub>

- **Date:** 1915
- **Type:** Short story
- **Written / first published:** Teikoku bungaku magazine, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Rash%C5%8Dmon%20Akutagawa%20Ry%C5%ABnosuke)

Adapted from a tale in the Konjaku monogatarishū, the story is set in a decaying Kyoto in the twelfth century. A servant, recently dismissed, shelters from the rain under the great Rashōmon gate, wondering whether to starve or become a thief. Climbing to the upper floor, where unclaimed corpses are left, he finds an old woman pulling hair from a dead woman's head to make wigs; she justifies herself by saying the dead woman had cheated people to survive. Taking her logic, the servant strips the old woman of her clothes and vanishes into the night. Akutagawa's psychological retelling of an old tale made his name; Kurosawa used its setting for his film Rashomon (1950).

### In a Grove (1922)

- **Date:** 1922
- **Type:** Short story
- **Written / first published:** Shinchō magazine, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=In%20a%20Grove%20Akutagawa%20Ry%C5%ABnosuke)

The story consists of seven testimonies about the death of a samurai found in a bamboo grove: a woodcutter, a priest, a policeman and the old mother of the dead man's wife give their evidence, followed by the confession of the bandit Tajōmaru, who says he killed the samurai in a fair fight after raping his wife; the wife's confession, saying she killed her husband in shame; and the dead samurai's own account, through a medium, saying he killed himself. No resolution is offered. Based on a tale in the Konjaku monogatarishū, it became the main source of Kurosawa's film Rashomon, and the phrase 'in a grove' (yabu no naka) became a Japanese expression for an unknowable truth.
