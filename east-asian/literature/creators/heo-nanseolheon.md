# Heo Nanseolheon

*許蘭雪軒*

**1563 – 1589** · Korean  
Born: Gangneung · Died:   
Movements & eras: [Joseon sijo, gasa & early Hangul fiction](../movements/joseon-sijo-gasa.md)  

> Joseon woman poet whose Chinese poems became famous in Ming China.

## Biography

Heo Chohui, known by her pen name Nanseolheon, was the daughter of a scholarly family and the elder sister of Heo Gyun, and was taught poetry alongside her brothers. Her marriage was unhappy, and both her children died young; she died at twenty-six.

Before her death she asked that her poems be burned, but Heo Gyun preserved those he could and showed them to a Ming envoy, who published them in China; a collection later also appeared in Japan. Her poems include Daoist fantasies of immortal realms and laments on the lot of women.

## Key works

### Collected Works of Nanseolheon (1608)

- **Date:** 1608
- **Type:** Poetry collection
- **Written / first published:** Joseon Korea; poems also printed in Ming China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Collected%20Works%20of%20Nanseolheon%20Heo%20Nanseolheon)

After his sister's death, Heo Gyun gathered her surviving poems, about 210 in classical Chinese, and showed them to the Ming envoy Zhu Zhifan, who published them in China; a Korean edition appeared in 1608, and a Japanese edition in 1711. They include poems of wandering among Daoist immortals, which imagine a realm beyond the constraints of her life, as well as poems on the sufferings of poor women, the loneliness of wives, and the death of her children, whose graves she describes side by side. Her collection was the first by a Korean woman to be widely read abroad.
