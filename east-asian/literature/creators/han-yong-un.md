# Han Yong-un

*韓龍雲*

**1879 – 1944** · Korean  
Born: Hongseong, Chungcheong · Died: Seoul  
Movements & eras: [Modern Korean literature](../movements/modern-korean.md)  
Also in: [Philosophy](../../philosophy/creators/han-yong-un.md), [Religion](../../religion/creators/han-yong-un.md)  

> Buddhist monk, independence activist and poet, author of The Silence of Love.

## Biography

Han Yong-un, known as Manhae, became a Buddhist monk, sought to reform Korean Buddhism, and was one of the thirty-three signatories of the 1919 March First Declaration of Independence, for which he was imprisoned. He refused all cooperation with the colonial authorities until his death.

His collection The Silence of Love (1926) uses the voice of a lover addressing an absent 'you' (nim) who may be a beloved, the Buddha or the lost nation, in free verse that helped found modern Korean poetry.

## Key works

### The Silence of Love (Nim ui chimmuk) (1926)

[![The Silence of Love (Nim ui chimmuk)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a7/%EB%8B%98%EC%9D%98_%EC%B9%A8%EB%AC%B5.jpg/1280px-%EB%8B%98%EC%9D%98_%EC%B9%A8%EB%AC%B5.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%EB%8B%98%EC%9D%98_%EC%B9%A8%EB%AC%B5.jpg)

- **Date:** 1926
- **Type:** Poetry collection
- **Written / first published:** Seoul: Hoedong seogwan, 1926
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Silence%20of%20Love%20%28Nim%20ui%20chimmuk%29%20Han%20Yong-un)

Han Yong-un wrote the 88 poems of this collection at Baekdamsa temple in 1925. In the title poem, the speaker says that the beloved has gone, breaking through the green mountain colours, but that because parting is the start of meeting again, she will not let her love be lost in sorrow; though the beloved has gone, she has not sent him away. Han's preface says that 'nim', the beloved, is not only a lover but anything one longs for: the Buddha for Shakyamuni, spring for the willow, and, readers understood, Korea under Japanese rule. Its long free-verse lines, influenced by Tagore, opened new possibilities for modern Korean poetry.
