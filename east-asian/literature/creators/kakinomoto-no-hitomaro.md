## Biography

Hitomaro was a court poet in the reigns of Empress Jitō and Emperor Monmu, at the end of the seventh century. Almost everything known about him comes from his poems and their headnotes in the Man'yōshū: he composed laments for princes, poems for imperial excursions, and personal poems on parting from and mourning for his wives, and seems to have held a modest rank and served in the provinces, perhaps dying in Iwami.

Hitomaro brought the long chōka form to its height, with grand parallel structures, pillow words and a ceremonial dignity that also allowed deep personal grief, and he wrote many of the finest tanka. From the Heian period onward he was worshipped as a deity of poetry, and his imagined portrait was hung at poetry gatherings.

## Key works

### Poems at Iwami on Parting from His Wife (c. 700)

- **Date:** c. 700
- **Type:** Chōka with envoys
- **Written / first published:** Iwami Province
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Poems%20at%20Iwami%20on%20Parting%20from%20His%20Wife%20Kakinomoto%20no%20Hitomaro)

In these paired chōka, each followed by short envoy poems (hanka), Hitomaro describes the coast of Iwami on the Sea of Japan, where seaweed sways in the waves like his wife lying beside him, and his journey inland to the capital; as he climbs, he turns to look back again and again, but the village recedes and the mountains rise between them. He begs the mountains to bow down so that he may see her gate. The poems combine the formal devices of chōka, pillow words, parallelism and accumulated imagery, with a vivid personal emotion that made them models of the form. Whether they record Hitomaro's own experience or are a court performance in a persona is debated.
