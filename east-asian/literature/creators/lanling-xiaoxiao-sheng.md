# Lanling Xiaoxiao Sheng

*蘭陵笑笑生*

**fl. late 16th century** · Chinese  
Movements & eras: [Ming novels & chuanqi drama](../movements/ming-novel-drama.md)  

> 'The Scoffing Scholar of Lanling', pseudonymous author of the Ming novel Jin Ping Mei.

## Biography

The author of Jin Ping Mei hid behind a pen name meaning roughly 'the laughing, laughing scholar of Lanling'. Dozens of candidates have been proposed, from the writer Wang Shizhen to the dramatist Li Kaixian, but none has won general acceptance. Manuscript copies circulated among literati in the 1590s, and the first printed edition appeared in 1617.

The anonymity reflects the novel's notoriety. Its frank sexual content, detailed picture of a corrupt merchant household and satire of officialdom made it one of the most banned books in Chinese history, yet also one of the most admired by critics, who recognised it as the first Chinese novel written by a single author about domestic, everyday life.

## Key works

### Jin Ping Mei (c. 1590s; printed 1617)

[![Jin Ping Mei](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5a/Jin_ping_mei_chapt17.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jin_ping_mei_chapt17.jpg)

<sub>Woodblock illustration to chapter 17, Ming edition Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jin_ping_mei_chapt17.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1590s; printed 1617
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** China; first edition 1617
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jin%20Ping%20Mei%20Lanling%20Xiaoxiao%20Sheng)

Jin Ping Mei, whose title combines the names of three of its women (Golden Lotus, Vase and Plum Blossom), takes an episode from Water Margin, the adultery of Pan Jinlian and Ximen Qing, and develops it into a hundred-chapter novel. Ximen, a pharmacist turned official in a small town in Shandong, accumulates wealth, bribes officials and gathers wives and concubines, whose rivalries, schemes and pleasures fill the novel, until he dies of excess and his household disintegrates. Its graphic sexual scenes caused it to be banned repeatedly, but its realistic detail about food, clothing, money and speech, its satire and its complex structure were admired by critics such as Zhang Zhupo. Dream of the Red Chamber owes much to it.
