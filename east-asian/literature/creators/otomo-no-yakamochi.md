## Biography

Yakamochi was the son of the poet and statesman Ōtomo no Tabito and head of an ancient military clan whose power declined as the Fujiwara rose. He served as governor of Etchū Province (746–751), where he wrote many of his finest poems, and later in a succession of posts, becoming involved in court intrigues that twice led to his demotion. He died while serving as general on the northern frontier and was posthumously stripped of his rank because of a conspiracy, later restored.

About 470 of his poems, more than a tenth of the anthology, appear in the Man'yōshū, whose last four books read like his poetic diary. His work moves from public and clan poems to delicate, melancholy tanka on spring and solitude that look ahead to the Heian sensibility.

## Key works

### Man'yōshū (Collection of Ten Thousand Leaves) (c. 759)

- **Date:** c. 759
- **Type:** Poetry anthology
- **Written / first published:** Nara
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Man%27y%C5%8Dsh%C5%AB%20%28Collection%20of%20Ten%20Thousand%20Leaves%29%20%C5%8Ctomo%20no%20Yakamochi)

The Man'yōshū contains about 4,500 poems in twenty books, dating mostly from about 600 to 759, the date of its last poem, by Ōtomo no Yakamochi, who is generally thought to have given the collection its final shape. Most are tanka of 31 syllables, but there are also some 260 chōka, long poems, the greatest of them by Kakinomoto no Hitomaro, and a few other forms. Besides court poems for imperial journeys and laments for princes, the anthology includes love poems, songs of the eastern provinces in dialect, poems by frontier guards (sakimori) leaving their families, and Yamanoue no Okura's Dialogue on Poverty. They are written in man'yōgana, Chinese characters used phonetically, a script that became the origin of the kana syllabaries. Prized from the eighteenth century by nationalist scholars as the expression of an unspoiled Japanese spirit, it is still the source of names; the era name Reiwa (2019) was taken from its preface to a group of plum-blossom poems.
