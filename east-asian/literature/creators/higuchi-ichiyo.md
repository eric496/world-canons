# Higuchi Ichiyō

*樋口一葉*

**1872 – 1896** · Japanese  
Born: Tokyo · Died: Tokyo  
Movements & eras: [Meiji to early Shōwa modern literature](../movements/meiji-modern.md)  

> The first major woman writer of modern Japan, whose stories of Tokyo's poor were written in a last flowering of classical prose.

## Biography

Born Higuchi Natsu, Ichiyō studied classical poetry at a private academy. After her father's death in 1889 she became the support of her mother and sister, taking in sewing and briefly running a small shop near the Yoshiwara pleasure quarter. She began publishing fiction in 1892, and in the fourteen months before her death from tuberculosis at twenty-four she wrote the stories that made her famous.

Takekurabe (Child's Play), Nigorie (Troubled Waters) and other stories depict children, prostitutes and the urban poor with sympathy and irony, in an elegant pseudo-classical style. Her diaries are also valued, and her portrait has appeared on the 5,000-yen note since 2004.

## Key works

### Takekurabe (Child's Play) (1895–96)

- **Date:** 1895–96
- **Type:** Novella
- **Written / first published:** Bungakkai magazine, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Takekurabe%20%28Child%27s%20Play%29%20Higuchi%20Ichiy%C5%8D)

Set in the Ryūsenji district beside the Yoshiwara, where Ichiyō herself had run a shop, the novella follows a group of children through a year of festivals and gang rivalries. At its centre is Midori, a spirited girl whose elder sister is a celebrated courtesan and who is destined to follow her, and Nobuyuki, a shy, serious boy from a temple family who will become a priest. Their unspoken attraction is expressed in small incidents, such as a broken sandal thong in the rain; by the end Midori has suddenly changed, her hair done up as a young woman, and Nobuyuki leaves for the seminary, leaving a paper narcissus at her gate. Written in a flowing classical style, it was praised by Mori Ōgai and is Ichiyō's masterpiece.
