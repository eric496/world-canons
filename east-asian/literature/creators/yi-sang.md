# Yi Sang

*李箱*

**1910 – 1937** · Korean  
Born: Seoul · Died: Tokyo  
Movements & eras: [Modern Korean literature](../movements/modern-korean.md)  

> Avant-garde poet and fiction writer, the leading modernist of colonial Korea, author of Wings.

## Biography

Yi Sang, born Kim Hae-gyeong, trained as an architect and worked for the colonial government before tuberculosis forced him to resign. He ran a café in Seoul, joined the modernist Group of Nine, and went to Tokyo in 1936, where he was arrested as a suspicious Korean and died shortly after his release.

His Crow's-Eye View poems, with their numbers and diagrams, shocked readers when serialised in 1934, and his story Wings (1936) is a landmark of Korean psychological fiction. The Yi Sang Literary Award is named after him.

### Crow's Eye View (Ogamdo) (1934)

<sub>Ogamdo as printed in the Joseon jungang ilbo, 1934 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%EC%9D%B4%EC%83%81-%EC%98%A4%EA%B0%90%EB%8F%84.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1934
- **Type:** Poem sequence
- **Written / first published:** Joseon jungang ilbo newspaper, Seoul, 1934
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Crow%27s%20Eye%20View%20%28Ogamdo%29%20Yi%20Sang)

Yi Sang published fifteen poems of the Ogamdo series in the newspaper Joseon jungang ilbo in July and August 1934, until reader protests forced the paper to stop; he had planned thirty. The first describes thirteen children running down a road, each saying that they are afraid; others use mirror-reversed numbers, mathematical formulas and architectural diagrams, with no spaces between words. Influenced by Dada, Surrealism and his own architectural training, and pervaded by images of illness and fear, they were the most radical modernist poetry of colonial Korea and have been the subject of endless interpretation.

### Wings (1936)

- **Date:** 1936
- **Type:** Short story
- **Written / first published:** Jogwang magazine, Seoul, 1936
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Wings%20Yi%20Sang)

The narrator lives idly in a dark room of a boarding house in Seoul, sleeping and playing with his wife's cosmetics and mirror, while she receives clients in the adjoining room and gives him money he does not know how to use. Gradually he goes out at night, and after discovering that she has been giving him sleeping pills instead of aspirin, he wanders to the roof of the Mitsukoshi department store, where at noon he feels the itch of his vanished wings and wants to fly once more. With its paradoxical prologue and stream-of-consciousness narration, it is a landmark of Korean psychological fiction.
