## Biography

Born Satō Norikiyo into a warrior family, Saigyō served as a guard of the retired emperor Toba before suddenly renouncing the world at twenty-two, in 1140. He lived in hermitages at Mount Kōya and elsewhere and travelled widely, twice journeying to the far north, partly to raise funds for the rebuilding of Tōdai-ji.

His poems on cherry blossoms, the moon, travel and solitude combine Buddhist reflection with deep personal feeling. Ninety-four were included in the Shin Kokinshū, more than by any other poet, and his life as a travelling poet-monk became the model that Bashō consciously followed.

## Key works

### Mountain Home Collection (Sankashū) (late 12th century)

- **Date:** late 12th century
- **Type:** Poetry collection
- **Written / first published:** Mountain hermitages of Japan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Mountain%20Home%20Collection%20%28Sankash%C5%AB%29%20Saigy%C5%8D)

The Sankashū gathers about 1,550 of Saigyō's poems, arranged by the seasons, love and miscellaneous topics. Many record his life as a recluse and traveller: cherry blossoms at Mount Yoshino, the autumn dusk over a marsh where a snipe takes flight, sleeping on the road in the far north, and his wish to die beneath the blossoms under the full moon of spring, a wish famously fulfilled when he died at the time of the Buddha's death day. His style is plain and sincere compared with court poets, combining Buddhist detachment with a deep attachment to the beauty of the world. The collection made him the model of the poet-monk for later centuries, above all for Bashō.
