# Lady Hyegyeong

*惠慶宮 洪氏*

**1735 – 1816** · Korean  
Born: Seoul · Died: Seoul  
Movements & eras: [Late Joseon pansori & Silhak prose](../movements/late-joseon-prose.md)  

> Crown princess of Joseon whose memoirs recount the death of her husband, Crown Prince Sado.

## Biography

Born into the Hong family of officials, she was married at nine to Crown Prince Sado, son of King Yeongjo. Sado's mental illness and violence led Yeongjo in 1762 to order him confined in a rice chest, where he died after eight days. Lady Hyegyeong survived to see her son become King Jeongjo, and lived on into the reign of her grandson.

Her memoirs, written in Hangul in four parts between 1795 and 1805, defend her family and give a searing first-hand account of court life and the tragedy of Sado. They are a masterpiece of Korean prose and of court literature by women.

## Key works

### Memoirs of Lady Hyegyeong (Hanjungnok) (1795–1805)

- **Date:** 1795–1805
- **Type:** Memoir
- **Written / first published:** Changgyeong Palace, Seoul
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Memoirs%20of%20Lady%20Hyegyeong%20%28Hanjungnok%29%20Lady%20Hyegyeong)

Lady Hyegyeong wrote four memoirs in Hangul: the first in 1795, on her sixtieth birthday, at the request of her nephew, recounting her childhood and life at court; and three more in 1801–05, after the death of her son King Jeongjo, defending her family, the Hong, who had been accused of complicity in her husband's death, and finally narrating the events of 1762 themselves. She describes how Crown Prince Sado, neglected and terrorised by his father King Yeongjo, developed a mental illness, killing servants and terrorising the palace, and how Yeongjo, to save the dynasty, ordered him into a rice chest, where he died after eight days. Intimate, painful and politically shrewd, the memoirs are among the greatest works of Korean prose and the basis of many novels and films, such as The Throne (2015).
