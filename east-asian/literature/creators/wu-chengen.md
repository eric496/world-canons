# Wu Cheng'en

*吳承恩*

**c. 1500 – c. 1582** · Chinese  
Born: Huai'an, Jiangsu · Died: Huai'an  
Movements & eras: [Ming novels & chuanqi drama](../movements/ming-novel-drama.md)  

> Ming scholar and poet to whom Journey to the West has been attributed since the 1920s.

## Biography

Wu Cheng'en came from a merchant family in Huai'an, passed the lower examinations but repeatedly failed the higher ones, and held only a minor post late in life. He was known locally as a witty poet and a lover of strange tales. The 1592 first edition of Journey to the West is anonymous; in the 1920s Hu Shi and Lu Xun, following a local gazetteer, identified Wu as the author.

The attribution remains debated, since the gazetteer may refer to a different work, but most modern editions carry his name. If the novel is his, it reflects a satirical, comic imagination that turned a Buddhist pilgrimage legend into a fantasy of monsters, bureaucratic heavens and the irrepressible Monkey King.

## Key works

### Journey to the West (1592)

- **Date:** 1592
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Nanjing (first edition, Shidetang)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Journey%20to%20the%20West%20Wu%20Cheng%27en)

The novel is based on the real journey of the monk Xuanzang to India in 629–645 to collect Buddhist scriptures, transformed over centuries of popular storytelling into a fantasy. Its 100 chapters begin with the birth of Sun Wukong, a monkey born from a stone, who acquires magic powers and wreaks havoc in heaven until the Buddha imprisons him under a mountain. Released to serve as disciple to the monk Tripitaka, he travels west with the greedy Pigsy, the gloomy Sandy and a dragon-horse, overcoming eighty-one ordeals posed by demons who want to eat the monk's flesh. Humorous and satirical, it mocks the celestial bureaucracy while serving as an allegory of the mind's discipline in Buddhist, Daoist and Confucian terms. One of the Four Classic Novels, it is the source of endless operas, films, cartoons and manga, including Dragon Ball.
