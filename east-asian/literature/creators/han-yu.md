## Biography

Orphaned young, Han Yu passed the examinations at the fourth attempt and had a stormy official career. In 819 he submitted a memorial denouncing Emperor Xianzong's veneration of a relic of the Buddha's finger bone, and was nearly executed before being banished to Chaozhou in the far south, where he wrote a famous address ordering the local crocodiles to leave. Recalled, he ended as vice-minister of war and personnel.

Han Yu argued that prose should return to the vigorous, flexible style of the pre-Han classics rather than ornate parallel prose, and that writing should carry the Confucian Way. His essays, letters and epitaphs, together with those of Liu Zongyuan, made guwen the standard style of Chinese prose until the twentieth century, and his defence of Confucianism against Buddhism anticipated Song Neo-Confucianism. His poetry, deliberately strange and difficult, also had many imitators.

## Key works

### Discourse on Teachers (c. 802)

- **Date:** c. 802
- **Type:** Essay
- **Written / first published:** Chang'an
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Discourse%20on%20Teachers%20Han%20Yu)

Written when Han Yu was teaching at the Imperial Academy, the essay argues that in ancient times learners always had teachers, whose task is to transmit the Way, impart learning and resolve doubts. Anyone who knows the Way before oneself, whatever their age or rank, is a fit teacher. Han Yu attacks the literati of his day, who are ashamed to learn from others while sending their children to masters for rote learning, and notes that even Confucius learned from many people. Written for a young follower named Li Pan, it shows the direct, argumentative and balanced style of guwen and has been studied by schoolchildren for over a thousand years.
