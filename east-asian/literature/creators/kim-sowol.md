# Kim Sowol

*金素月*

**1902 – 1934** · Korean  
Born: Guseong, North Pyeongan · Died: Kwaksan, North Pyeongan  
Movements & eras: [Modern Korean literature](../movements/modern-korean.md)  

> The best-loved lyric poet of modern Korea, author of Azaleas.

## Biography

Born Kim Jeong-sik, Kim Sowol studied at the Osan School under the poet Kim Eok and briefly in Tokyo. He published his only collection, Azaleas, in 1925 and later ran a newspaper office in his home region, struggling with poverty and drink, and died at thirty-two.

His poems use the rhythms of Korean folk song and simple language to express han, a sorrow of separation and longing. Many have been set to music.

## Key works

### Azaleas (1925)

- **Date:** 1925
- **Type:** Poetry collection
- **Written / first published:** Seoul: Maemunsa, 1925
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Azaleas%20Kim%20Sowol)

The collection contains 127 poems written in the language and rhythms of Korean folk song. In the title poem, the speaker says that when her lover leaves, weary of her, she will let him go without a word, strewing armfuls of azaleas from Yaksan in Yeongbyeon on his path, and asks him to tread softly on them as he goes; she will not shed tears even if she dies. Its restraint and implied grief express han, the long-suffering sorrow often seen as central to Korean feeling. Other poems such as Mountain Flowers and Invocation have been set to music. A first edition of the book was registered as Korean cultural heritage in 2011.
