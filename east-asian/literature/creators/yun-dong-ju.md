# Yun Dong-ju

*尹東柱*

**1917 – 1945** · Korean  
Born: Longjing, Jiandao (Manchuria) · Died: Fukuoka Prison, Japan  
Movements & eras: [Modern Korean literature](../movements/modern-korean.md)  

> Poet of conscience of the colonial era, who died in a Japanese prison at twenty-seven.

## Biography

Yun Dong-ju grew up in a Korean Christian community in Manchuria and studied at Yonhi College (now Yonsei University) in Seoul. He went to Japan in 1942 to study English literature at Doshisha University. In 1943 he was arrested for alleged independence activities and died in Fukuoka Prison in February 1945, months before liberation.

His poems, published posthumously in 1948 as Sky, Wind, Stars and Poems, combine Christian faith, self-examination and quiet resistance in simple lyric language, and he is one of the most widely read poets in Korea.

## Key works

### Sky, Wind, Stars and Poems (1948)

- **Date:** 1948
- **Type:** Poetry collection
- **Written / first published:** Seoul: Jeongeumsa, 1948
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sky%2C%20Wind%2C%20Stars%20and%20Poems%20Yun%20Dong-ju)

Before leaving for Japan in 1941, Yun selected nineteen poems for a collection and made three manuscript copies, but could not publish it under colonial censorship. After his death in Fukuoka Prison in 1945, his friends published it in 1948 with other poems. In the 'Prologue' (Seosi), the poet wishes to have no shame before heaven until the day he dies, to love all things that are dying, and to walk the road given to him, as tonight the wind brushes the stars. Other poems, such as Self-Portrait, in which he looks at his reflection in a well, and Counting the Stars, combine Christian conscience, nostalgia and quiet resistance to the erasure of the Korean language. He is one of the most widely read poets in Korea and is also honoured in Japan.
