> The greatest Japanese playwright, who wrote for the puppet (jōruri) and kabuki theatres.

<sub>Portrait: Edo-period portrait of Chikamatsu Monzaemon · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chikamatsu_Monzaemon.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Born Sugimori Nobumori into a samurai family that lost its position, Chikamatsu served in the household of a Kyoto court noble and began writing for puppet theatre in the 1680s, in partnership with the chanter Takemoto Gidayū. He also wrote kabuki plays for the actor Sakata Tōjūrō, then from 1705 worked as staff writer for Gidayū's Takemoto-za in Osaka.

He wrote more than a hundred plays: historical dramas such as The Battles of Coxinga, a huge success in 1715, and domestic tragedies (sewamono) based on recent events, such as The Love Suicides at Sonezaki, in which townsmen torn between duty (giri) and feeling (ninjō) die for love. His plays remain staples of bunraku and kabuki.

### The Love Suicides at Sonezaki (1703)

- **Date:** 1703
- **Type:** Jōruri (puppet play)
- **Written / first published:** Takemoto-za, Osaka
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Love%20Suicides%20at%20Sonezaki%20Chikamatsu%20Monzaemon)

Written within a month of a real double suicide in Osaka, the play was Chikamatsu's first domestic tragedy for the puppet theatre. Tokubei, a soy-sauce shop clerk, loves the courtesan Ohatsu. He refuses an arranged marriage and must return the dowry, but lends the money to his friend Kuheiji, who denies the debt and publicly accuses him of forgery. Disgraced, Tokubei hides under Ohatsu's veranda in the brothel, and she signals her resolve to die with him by pressing his ankle against her throat. The lovers slip out at night and walk to the forest of Sonezaki, in a famous michiyuki journey of poetic chanting, where they die. The play's success saved the Takemoto theatre's finances and started a fashion for love-suicide plays so strong that the shogunate banned them in 1723.

### The Battles of Coxinga (1715)

[![The Battles of Coxinga](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/33/Komaz%C5%8D_Ichikawa_VII_as_Wat%C5%8D_Nai_in_Kokusenya_Kassen.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Komaz%C5%8D_Ichikawa_VII_as_Wat%C5%8D_Nai_in_Kokusenya_Kassen.jpg)

<sub>Ichikawa Komazō VII as Watōnai, kabuki print Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Komaz%C5%8D_Ichikawa_VII_as_Wat%C5%8D_Nai_in_Kokusenya_Kassen.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1715
- **Type:** Jōruri (puppet play)
- **Written / first published:** Takemoto-za, Osaka
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Battles%20of%20Coxinga%20Chikamatsu%20Monzaemon)

Loosely based on Zheng Chenggong (Koxinga), the son of a Chinese pirate and a Japanese mother who fought the Manchus, the play follows Watōnai, a fisherman in Hirado, who learns of the Ming emperor's overthrow and sails to China with his parents. In a famous scene he subdues a tiger in a bamboo forest with a charm from the Ise shrine; he wins over the general Kanki with the help of his half-sister, and his parents sacrifice themselves for the cause. Exotic settings, spectacle and patriotic pride made it a record success, running seventeen months, and it was quickly adapted for kabuki, where it remains in the repertory.

### The Love Suicides at Amijima (1720)

[![The Love Suicides at Amijima](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Acteurs_Sawamura_Sojuro_III_en_Iwai_Hanshiro_IV_als_Jihei_en_Koharu%2C_RP-P-1980-1.jpg/1280px-Acteurs_Sawamura_Sojuro_III_en_Iwai_Hanshiro_IV_als_Jihei_en_Koharu%2C_RP-P-1980-1.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Acteurs_Sawamura_Sojuro_III_en_Iwai_Hanshiro_IV_als_Jihei_en_Koharu%2C_RP-P-1980-1.jpg)

<sub>Actors as Jihei and Koharu, ukiyo-e print, Rijksmuseum Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Acteurs_Sawamura_Sojuro_III_en_Iwai_Hanshiro_IV_als_Jihei_en_Koharu%2C_RP-P-1980-1.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1720
- **Type:** Jōruri (puppet play)
- **Written / first published:** Takemoto-za, Osaka
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Love%20Suicides%20at%20Amijima%20Chikamatsu%20Monzaemon)

Jihei, a paper merchant in Osaka with a wife, Osan, and two children, is hopelessly in love with the courtesan Koharu, and the two have vowed to die together. Osan secretly writes to Koharu begging her to save his life, and Koharu pretends to reject him. When Jihei learns that a rival is about to buy her contract, Osan, fearing Koharu will kill herself, offers her own savings and clothes to ransom her; but Osan's father arrives and takes her home, forcing a divorce. Jihei and Koharu walk to the temple at Amijima, and so as not to wrong Osan, they die apart, she by his sword and he by hanging. Its portrait of a wife's generosity and the conflict of duty (giri) and love (ninjō) make it Chikamatsu's masterpiece; Shinoda Masahiro filmed it in 1969.
