## Biography

Sent to Tang China at twelve, Choe Chiwon passed the imperial examination at eighteen and served as an official, becoming famous for a proclamation against the rebel Huang Chao in 881. He returned to Silla in 885 but found his reform proposals blocked by the rigid bone-rank aristocracy of a declining kingdom, served in provincial posts, and withdrew into retirement, traditionally on Mount Gaya at Haeinsa temple.

His collection Gyewon pilgyeong, compiled from his writings in China, is the oldest surviving individual literary collection by a Korean. His poems in Chinese express the loneliness of the outsider, and later Koreans honoured him as the father of their classical literature; he was enshrined in the Confucian shrine in 1020.

## Key works

### Gyewon pilgyeong (Plowing the Cassia Grove with a Writing Brush) (886)

- **Date:** 886
- **Type:** Prose and poetry collection
- **Written / first published:** Silla; compiled for King Heongang
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Gyewon%20pilgyeong%20%28Plowing%20the%20Cassia%20Grove%20with%20a%20Writing%20Brush%29%20Choe%20Chiwon)

After returning to Silla in 885, Choe Chiwon presented to the king a collection of his writings from his years in China. The twenty-volume Gyewon pilgyeongjip contains official documents, memorials, letters and poems written in elegant parallel prose, most notably the proclamation against the rebel Huang Chao, which, according to legend, so frightened the rebel that he fell from his couch. Its title refers to the cassia, symbol of examination success, and to making a living by the brush. Preserved and reprinted in Joseon, it is the earliest Korean literary collection to survive complete and a testament to the shared culture of classical Chinese in East Asia.
