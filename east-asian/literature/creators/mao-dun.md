# Mao Dun

*茅盾*

**1896 – 1981** · Chinese  
Born: Tongxiang, Zhejiang · Died: Beijing  
Movements & eras: [May Fourth & modern Chinese literature](../movements/may-fourth-modern.md)  

> Leading realist novelist and critic of the May Fourth era, author of Midnight.

## Biography

Mao Dun, the pen name of Shen Yanbing, worked as an editor at the Commercial Press in Shanghai and in 1921 took over the influential Short Story Monthly, turning it into a platform for the new literature. He co-founded the Literary Research Association and was an early member of the Communist Party. After the failure of the 1927 revolution he began writing fiction. After 1949 he served as Minister of Culture until 1964.

Mao Dun aimed to depict Chinese society as a whole in the manner of European realism. His novel Midnight (1933) is a panorama of Shanghai's industrialists, speculators and workers, and his stories of silkworm farmers portray rural decline. The Mao Dun Literature Prize, one of China's highest, is named after him.

## Key works

### Midnight (1933)

- **Date:** 1933
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** Shanghai: Kaiming shudian, 1933
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Midnight%20Mao%20Dun)

The novel centres on Wu Sunfu, an ambitious silk-factory owner who dreams of building Chinese industry. Over a few months in 1930, as civil war rages, he battles the comprador financier Zhao Botao on the bond market, crushes a strike in his factory, and is finally ruined. Around him Mao Dun presents landlords fleeing peasant uprisings, decadent young intellectuals, revolutionaries and speculators. Intended as a Marxist analysis of why China could not develop an independent capitalism, it was the most ambitious realist novel of its time and remains a key work of left-wing literature.
