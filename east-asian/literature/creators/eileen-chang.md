# Eileen Chang

*張愛玲*

**1920 – 1995** · Chinese  
Born: Shanghai · Died: Los Angeles  
Movements & eras: [May Fourth & modern Chinese literature](../movements/may-fourth-modern.md)  

> Writer of wartime Shanghai and Hong Kong whose stories of love and survival made her one of the most celebrated modern Chinese authors.

## Biography

Eileen Chang (Zhang Ailing) was born into an aristocratic family, the great-granddaughter of the statesman Li Hongzhang, and grew up amid her parents' unhappy marriage. She studied at the University of Hong Kong until the Japanese invasion in 1941 and became a literary sensation in occupied Shanghai in 1943–45. Her brief marriage to Hu Lancheng, a collaborator, later counted against her. She left China in 1952 for Hong Kong and then the United States, where she lived increasingly reclusively.

Chang's stories, notably those in Romances (1944) such as Love in a Fallen City and The Golden Cangue, combine the textures of traditional fiction with a modern, sardonic sense of desire, money and the fragility of personal life in a time of war. Long excluded from the mainland canon, she has been hugely influential in Taiwan and Hong Kong, and widely read in China since the 1980s.

### Love in a Fallen City (1943)

- **Date:** 1943
- **Type:** Novella
- **Written / first published:** Shanghai, 1943
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Love%20in%20a%20Fallen%20City%20Eileen%20Chang)

Bai Liusu, a divorced woman humiliated by her declining Shanghai family, goes to Hong Kong with Fan Liuyuan, a wealthy, cynical overseas Chinese who wants her as a mistress, not a wife. Their elaborate courtship, a battle of wit and calculation, seems to end with her becoming a kept woman, until the Japanese attack on Hong Kong in December 1941. Amid the bombardment the two find a moment of genuine dependence, and they marry. The title plays on a classical phrase for a beauty who causes the fall of a city, inverting it: here, Chang suggests, a city fell so that one woman's marriage could be achieved. Its irony, rich imagery and understanding of women's economic constraints made it one of her most celebrated works, filmed by Ann Hui in 1984.

### The Golden Cangue (1943)

- **Date:** 1943
- **Type:** Novella
- **Written / first published:** Shanghai, 1943
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Golden%20Cangue%20Eileen%20Chang)

Cao Qiqiao, the daughter of a sesame-oil shopkeeper, is married off to the invalid second son of the wealthy Jiang family, where she is despised. After years of frustration, during which she is drawn to her rakish brother-in-law Jiping, she inherits her share of the family wealth, and the 'golden cangue', the heavy wooden collar of a prisoner made of gold, closes around her. She drives off Jiping when she suspects he wants her money, and over decades destroys the lives of her son and daughter, making her son an opium addict and wrecking her daughter's engagement. Chang's psychological study of avarice and repression was praised by the critic C. T. Hsia as the greatest novella in the history of Chinese literature.
