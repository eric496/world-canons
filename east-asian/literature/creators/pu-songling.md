## Biography

Pu Songling passed the first level of the civil-service examinations brilliantly at eighteen but then failed the provincial examination again and again until he was in his seventies. He spent most of his life as a private tutor in the household of a wealthy family in Shandong, writing in his spare time and collecting stories, legend says, by offering tea to passers-by in exchange for tales.

His Strange Tales, written over several decades in a terse, elegant classical style, use the supernatural to comment on injustice, corrupt officials, the examination system and the relationships of men and women; the fox spirits and ghost women are often more humane than the living. The book circulated in manuscript and was printed in 1766, and remains one of the most popular works of Chinese fiction, a source for countless films and operas.

## Key works

### Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio (c. 1679–1707; printed 1766)

[![Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8d/Nie_Xiaoqian_illustration.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nie_Xiaoqian_illustration.jpg)

<sub>Illustration of the tale Nie Xiaoqian from a Qing illustrated edition Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nie_Xiaoqian_illustration.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1679–1707; printed 1766
- **Type:** Story collection
- **Written / first published:** Zichuan, Shandong; first printed in Hangzhou, 1766
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Strange%20Tales%20from%20a%20Chinese%20Studio%20Pu%20Songling)

The Liaozhai zhiyi, literally 'Records of the Strange from the Studio of Leisure', contains some 490 pieces, from brief anecdotes to elaborate stories. In the most famous, a scholar falls in love with a beautiful woman who turns out to be a fox spirit or a ghost: in Nie Xiaoqian, a ghost girl enslaved by a demon is rescued by an upright scholar and returns to life; in The Painted Skin, a man takes home a beautiful girl who is really a demon wearing a painted human skin. Other tales mock examiners who cannot recognise talent, officials who torture the innocent, and the absurdities of daily life. Written in an allusive, compact classical prose that revived the Tang tale, the book circulated in manuscript for decades before its first printing. Its stories have been adapted into countless operas, films, such as A Chinese Ghost Story (1987), and television series.
