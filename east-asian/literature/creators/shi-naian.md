# Shi Nai'an

*施耐庵*

**c. 1296 – c. 1370** · Chinese  
Born: Jiangsu (traditionally) · Died:   
Movements & eras: [Ming novels & chuanqi drama](../movements/ming-novel-drama.md)  

> The traditional author of Water Margin, whose life is so obscure that his existence has been questioned.

## Biography

Early editions of Water Margin name Shi Nai'an as author, sometimes with Luo Guanzhong as editor. Later accounts, including a biography that appeared only in the twentieth century, describe him as a scholar from Jiangsu who served briefly as an official before retiring to write, but these are generally regarded as unreliable.

Whoever Shi Nai'an was, Water Margin grew from storytellers' and dramatists' tales about the bandit Song Jiang and his band, and its shaping into a novel of 100 or 120 chapters was the work of many hands up to the sixteenth century. The name has nonetheless become inseparable from one of the Four Classic Novels.

## Key works

### Water Margin (14th–16th century)

[![Water Margin](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e5/Shuihu_zhuan_-_juan_15-19_-_page_1.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shuihu_zhuan_-_juan_15-19_-_page_1.jpg)

<sub>Page from an early printed edition Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shuihu_zhuan_-_juan_15-19_-_page_1.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 14th–16th century
- **Type:** Novel
- **Written / first published:** China; earliest complete editions 16th century
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Water%20Margin%20Shi%20Nai%27an)

Water Margin grew from stories about the historical bandit Song Jiang, active around 1120 in the Northern Song. In its main versions of 100 or 120 chapters it tells how a series of heroes, each driven to outlawry by injustice, gather at the marshes of Mount Liang: the drinking tiger-slayer Wu Song, the hot-tempered monk Lu Zhishen, the drill instructor Lin Chong, the axe-wielding Li Kui and many others, until 108 chiefs are assembled under Song Jiang. They fight imperial armies, are eventually granted amnesty, and are destroyed fighting for the dynasty. Jin Shengtan's 70-chapter edition of 1641, which ends at the gathering of the heroes, was the most read version for three centuries. With its vivid colloquial language and episodes of violence and brotherhood, the novel is one of the Four Classics; it inspired Jin Ping Mei, Bakin's Hakkenden and countless ukiyo-e prints by Kuniyoshi.
