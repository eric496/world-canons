## Biography

Concerned that China lacked a complete code of monastic discipline (vinaya), Faxian set out from Chang'an in 399 with several companions, crossed the Taklamakan and the Pamirs, and spent years in northern India and Sri Lanka collecting texts. He returned by sea in 412, surviving storms, and landed on the Shandong coast. With the Indian monk Buddhabhadra he translated vinaya and other works in Jiankang.

His Record of Buddhist Kingdoms describes the monasteries, pilgrimage sites, kings and customs of India in the Gupta period and is a key source for historians of South Asia as well as of Buddhism. His journey inspired later pilgrims, Xuanzang above all.

## Key texts & works

### Record of Buddhist Kingdoms (Foguo ji) (c. 416)

[![Record of Buddhist Kingdoms (Foguo ji)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6f/Faxian_zhuan.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Faxian_zhuan.JPG)

<sub>Early edition of Faxian's record Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Faxian_zhuan.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 416
- **Type:** Pilgrimage record
- **Where written / held:** Jiankang (Nanjing)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Record%20of%20Buddhist%20Kingdoms%20%28Foguo%20ji%29%20Faxian)

In clear, unadorned prose, Faxian describes the crossing of the desert, guided only by the bones of the dead, the Buddhist kingdoms of Khotan and the Pamirs, the sites of the Buddha's life in the Ganges valley, the monasteries of Pāṭaliputra, two years in Sri Lanka, and a terrifying sea voyage home by way of Java. He notes the vegetarianism of the Gupta lands, festivals, relics and the rules of monasteries. Translated into French in 1836 and English in 1869 and 1886, the Foguo ji became an essential source for Indian history.
