## Biography

Sent by the London Missionary Society, Morrison arrived in Guangzhou in 1807, where foreigners were barely tolerated. He worked as translator for the East India Company, learned Chinese in secret, and with William Milne completed the Chinese Bible, published in 1823, as well as a six-volume dictionary.

He baptised only about ten Chinese, among them Liang Fa, whose tracts later inspired Hong Xiuquan. His translation was the basis of later Protestant Bibles in Chinese, and his dictionary and Anglo-Chinese College in Malacca laid foundations for Western sinology.

## Key texts & works

### Chinese Bible of Robert Morrison (Shentian shengshu) (1823)

- **Date:** 1823
- **Type:** Bible translation
- **Where written / held:** Malacca and Guangzhou
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Chinese%20Bible%20of%20Robert%20Morrison%20%28Shentian%20shengshu%29%20Robert%20Morrison)

Morrison translated the New Testament by 1813, drawing on a manuscript Catholic version found in the British Museum, and with William Milne the Old Testament, printing the complete Bible in 21 volumes at the Anglo-Chinese College in Malacca in 1823. Its style was stiff and later revised by Medhurst, Gützlaff and others, but it was the basis of the Protestant missionary Bible and of the tracts of Liang Fa, through which Hong Xiuquan learned of Christianity. The Taiping later printed a revised edition of a version descended from it.
