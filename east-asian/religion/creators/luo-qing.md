# Luo Qing

*羅清*

**1442 – 1527** · Chinese  
Born: Jimo, Shandong · Died: Miyun, near Beijing  
Traditions & eras: [Chinese popular and sectarian religion](../movements/popular-religion.md)  

> Ming-dynasty soldier turned lay preacher whose Five Books in Six Volumes founded the tradition of sectarian 'precious scrolls'.

## Biography

Luo Qing, also called Luo Menghong and venerated as Patriarch Luo (Luozu), served as a soldier on the Grand Canal transport near Beijing. After thirteen years of spiritual searching he reported an awakening in 1482 and began to preach to lay followers, including canal boatmen. His teachings, printed in 1509, were the first of the vernacular scriptures known as baojuan.

Luo taught that all beings originate from and can return to a primordial 'True Emptiness' or 'Native Place', drawing on Chan Buddhism; later sects reshaped this as the Eternal Venerable Mother (Wusheng laomu) who calls her lost children home. His followers formed the Luo teaching, among whose descendants are many later lay sects, some of them persecuted by the state as heterodox.

## Key texts & works

### Five Books in Six Volumes (Wubu liuce) (1509)

- **Date:** 1509
- **Type:** Sectarian scriptures (baojuan)
- **Where written / held:** Beijing region, Ming China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Five%20Books%20in%20Six%20Volumes%20%28Wubu%20liuce%29%20Luo%20Qing)

The five books, including Lamenting the World and Breaking Through Heterodoxy, recount Luo Qing's search for liberation and his realisation that the true self is identical with an original emptiness, the 'Native Place'. They mix Chan and Pure Land teaching with criticism of both monastic Buddhism and popular cults, in rhythmic prose and verse that ordinary people could chant. Reprinted many times despite periodic bans, they were adopted by later sects that developed the myth of the Eternal Mother calling her children home, and they set the form of the baojuan genre that flourished in the Ming and Qing.
