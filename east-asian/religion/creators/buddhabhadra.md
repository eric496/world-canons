# Buddhabhadra

*佛陀跋陀羅*

**359 – 429** · Indian  
Born: Kapilavastu, India (traditional) · Died: Jiankang (Nanjing)  
Traditions & eras: [Buddhism's arrival and the translators](../movements/buddhism-arrives.md), [Tiantai/Tendai and Huayan/Kegon](../movements/tiantai-huayan.md)  

> Indian meditation master and translator who produced the first Chinese version of the Flower Garland (Avataṃsaka) Sutra.

## Biography

Buddhabhadra was an Indian monk trained in meditation in Kashmir, who came to Chang'an around 408. After a dispute with Kumārajīva's circle he left for Mount Lu, where Huiyuan received him, and then moved to Jiankang, the southern capital. There he collaborated with Faxian on translations of monastic rules and the Mahāparinirvāṇa Sutra.

Between 418 and 421 he translated the Avataṃsaka Sutra in sixty fascicles from a manuscript brought from Khotan. This version became the basis of the Huayan school in China and of Hwaeom and Kegon in Korea and Japan, until it was supplemented by the eighty-fascicle translation of 699.

- **Date:** 418–421
- **Type:** Mahāyāna scripture (translation)
- **Where written / held:** Jiankang (Nanjing)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Flower%20Garland%20Sutra%20%28Huayan%20jing%29%20Buddhabhadra)

The Avataṃsaka Sūtra is a compilation of separately circulating texts assembled perhaps in Central Asia. It presents the Buddha's awakening as a visionary revelation of infinite world-systems mirrored in each other and in every particle, the ten stages of the bodhisattva path, and in its last book, the Gaṇḍavyūha, the pilgrimage of the youth Sudhana to fifty-three teachers. Buddhabhadra's 60-fascicle translation (418–421) was followed by Śikṣānanda's 80-fascicle version (699), on which Fazang lectured. It inspired the Huayan school, the Great Buddha of Tōdai-ji, and much East Asian art, and was copied and illuminated in Goryeo Korea.
