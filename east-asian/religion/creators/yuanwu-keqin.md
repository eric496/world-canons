## Biography

Yuanwu Keqin came from a Confucian family in Sichuan, became a monk and trained under Wuzu Fayan of the Linji lineage. He taught at several major monasteries, received honours from Emperor Huizong and the early Southern Song court, and returned to Chengdu at the end of his life.

Between about 1111 and 1125 he lectured on a hundred cases with verses by the earlier master Xuedou Chongxian; his disciples' notes became the Blue Cliff Record, the most celebrated work of kōan literature. His disciple Dahui Zonggao, who reportedly burned the woodblocks of the book, developed the method of contemplating a single key phrase (huatou).

## Key texts & works

### Blue Cliff Record (Biyan lu) (1125)

- **Date:** 1125
- **Type:** Kōan collection
- **Where written / held:** Lizhou and Jiashan, Hunan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Blue%20Cliff%20Record%20%28Biyan%20lu%29%20Yuanwu%20Keqin)

Each case gives an introduction, a classic exchange between Chan masters, such as Emperor Wu questioning Bodhidharma or Zhaozhou's 'Wash your bowl', with Xuedou Chongxian's verse, and Yuanwu's comments and interlinear notes, often ironic and allusive. Yuanwu's lectures at the Blue Cliff Hall at Jiashan were compiled by his students in 1125. Tradition says his disciple Dahui burned the blocks because students were memorising it instead of practising; it was reprinted in 1300. In Japan, as the Hekiganroku, it became central to Rinzai training.
