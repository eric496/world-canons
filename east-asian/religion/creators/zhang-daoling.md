## Biography

Accounts of Zhang Daoling (also Zhang Ling) were written down by his followers and later hagiographers, so details are uncertain. Tradition says he was a scholar from eastern China who moved to Sichuan to pursue immortality, and that in 142 Lord Lao appeared to him on Mount Heming and appointed him Celestial Master, charged with replacing the cults of blood-sacrificing demons with a covenant of orthodox unity. The movement is historically attested under his grandson Zhang Lu, who ran a theocratic state in the Hanzhong valley until he submitted to Cao Cao in 215.

The Celestial Masters organised believers into parishes led by libationers, collected a tax of five pecks of rice, healed illness through confession of sins written to the Three Officials, and forbade sacrifices to popular gods. Their communities spread across China after 215, and the hereditary line of Celestial Masters claiming descent from Zhang, later based on Mount Longhu in Jiangxi, continues today in Taiwan. Most Daoist ordination and liturgy traces itself to this foundation.

## Key texts & works

### Xiang'er Commentary on the Laozi (c. 190–220)

- **Date:** c. 190–220
- **Type:** Scriptural commentary
- **Where written / held:** Hanzhong; surviving in a Dunhuang manuscript (British Library, S. 6825)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Xiang%27er%20Commentary%20on%20the%20Laozi%20Zhang%20Daoling)

The Xiang'er reads Laozi's text not as philosophy of government but as the revealed teaching of the Dao, personified as the deity Taishang Laojun, and draws from it moral precepts and techniques for preserving vital essence. It warns against false practices, including some sexual and visualisation techniques, and links long life to keeping the Dao's commandments. Traditionally ascribed to Zhang Daoling, it is more often attributed by scholars to Zhang Lu in the Hanzhong theocracy. Lost for centuries, part of it was recovered among the Dunhuang manuscripts in a sixth-century copy, and it is a key source for the beliefs of the early Celestial Masters.
