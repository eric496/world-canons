# Priesthood of the Ise Shrines

*伊勢神宮神職*

**4th century – present** · Japanese  
Born: Ise, Mie · Died: Ise, Mie  
Traditions & eras: [Kami worship and Shinto](../movements/shinto.md)  

> The hereditary priestly families of Ise, the shrines of the sun goddess Amaterasu and the food goddess Toyouke, who created the first Shinto theology.

## Biography

The Inner Shrine (Naikū) of Amaterasu and Outer Shrine (Gekū) of Toyouke were served by hereditary lineages, the Arakida at the Inner Shrine and the Watarai at the Outer, under an imperial princess priestess until the fourteenth century. Since the late seventh century the shrine buildings have been rebuilt on adjacent sites, in principle every twenty years, in the Shikinen Sengū; the 62nd rebuilding took place in 2013.

In the thirteenth century Watarai priests produced the Five Books of Shinto, claiming great antiquity for teachings that elevated the Outer Shrine's deity and gave kami worship its own metaphysics in dialogue with Buddhism. This Ise or Watarai Shinto influenced Yoshida Shinto and later nativist thought. The shrine architecture is a subject for architecture rather than this section.

According to shrine records, the first periodic rebuilding of the Inner Shrine took place in 690 under Empress Jitō. Every twenty years, in principle, new main halls and some 65 other buildings are built of cypress on the alternate site beside the old, with all the treasures and costumes remade, and in the culminating ceremony (sengyo) the sacred mirror of Amaterasu is carried at night in procession to the new hall. The cycle was interrupted for over a century in the age of civil war and restored in 1585. The rite renews purity and transmits building and craft skills; the architecture of the shrines is a subject for the architecture section.

### Five Books of Shinto (Shintō gobusho) (13th century (claimed to be ancient))

[![Five Books of Shinto (Shintō gobusho)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/63/Geku20051116.JPG/1280px-Geku20051116.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Geku20051116.JPG)

<sub>The Outer Shrine (Gekū) at Ise, seat of the Watarai priests Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Geku20051116.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 13th century (claimed to be ancient)
- **Type:** Shinto theological texts
- **Where written / held:** Outer Shrine (Gekū), Ise
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Five%20Books%20of%20Shinto%20%28Shint%C5%8D%20gobusho%29%20Priesthood%20of%20the%20Ise%20Shrines)

The five texts claim to be records from the time of the shrine's founding but were written, scholars agree, by Watarai priests in the thirteenth century; Watarai Yukitada is often named. They elevate Toyouke, the deity of the Outer Shrine, to equality with Amaterasu by identifying her with the primordial kami Ame-no-minakanushi, and they teach that purity and honesty (shōjiki) are the essence of kami worship. Drawing on Buddhist esoteric and Chinese cosmological ideas while asserting the priority of the kami, they shaped Watarai Ieyuki's systematic Ise Shinto, Yoshida Kanetomo and Edo-period Confucian Shinto. Exposed as forgeries by Edo scholars, they remain important as the first Shinto theology.
