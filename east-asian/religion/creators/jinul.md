## Biography

Jinul became a monk at eight and passed the monastic examination in 1182, but, disgusted with a worldly Buddhist establishment, he and a few friends pledged to form a retreat society. After three awakenings, through reading the Platform Sutra, the Huayan commentaries of Li Tongxuan and the records of Dahui, he established the community in 1200 at a temple renamed Suseonsa, today's Songgwangsa.

His teaching of sudden awakening followed by gradual cultivation, and of the joint practice of concentration and wisdom, is treated in the philosophy section. As a founder he gave Korean Buddhism its distinctive synthesis and the Jogye Order, the largest in Korea today, regards him as its principal ancestor. Songgwangsa produced sixteen national preceptors after him.

## Key texts & works

### Compact of the Samādhi and Prajñā Society (Jeonghye gyeolsa mun) (1190)

- **Date:** 1190
- **Type:** Society compact and exhortation
- **Where written / held:** Geojosa, Mount Gong, Goryeo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Compact%20of%20the%20Sam%C4%81dhi%20and%20Praj%C3%B1%C4%81%20Society%20%28Jeonghye%20gyeolsa%20mun%29%20Jinul)

Jinul recalls the vow he and his fellow monks made in 1182 to leave the corrupt capital monasteries, and argues that in the age of the decline of the Dharma one should still practise concentration (samādhi) and wisdom (prajñā) together, since the mind is fundamentally the Buddha. He answers objections from Pure Land devotees and doctrinal scholars, insisting that his community welcomes practitioners of all schools. The society moved in 1200 to the temple Jinul renamed Suseonsa, today's Songgwangsa, and became the model of Korean Seon practice.
