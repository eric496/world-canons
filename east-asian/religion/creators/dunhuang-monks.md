# Monks of Dunhuang

*敦煌僧人*

**4th century – c. 1000** · Chinese  
Born: Dunhuang, Gansu · Died: Dunhuang, Gansu  
Traditions & eras: [Buddhism's arrival and the translators](../movements/buddhism-arrives.md)  

> The monastic communities of the Silk Road oasis of Dunhuang, whose sealed Library Cave preserved tens of thousands of manuscripts.

## Biography

Dunhuang, at the western end of the Gansu corridor, was a Buddhist centre from the fourth century, when tradition dates the first cave at Mogao to 366. Over a millennium its monks, nuns and lay patrons carved and painted some 500 cave shrines and kept monastery libraries in Chinese, Tibetan, Sanskrit, Uyghur and other languages. A side chamber, now numbered Cave 17, originally a memorial chapel for the monk Hongbian, was filled with manuscripts and paintings and walled up around 1000.

The cave was opened by the Daoist caretaker Wang Yuanlu in 1900, and its contents were dispersed by Aurel Stein, Paul Pelliot and others to London, Paris, St Petersburg, Kyoto and Beijing. They include the oldest dated printed book, early Chan and Pure Land texts, Daoist and Manichaean scriptures, and everyday documents, and they transformed the study of medieval Chinese religion.

- **Date:** sealed c. 1000; opened 1900
- **Type:** Manuscript cache
- **Where written / held:** Mogao Caves, Dunhuang; contents now in London, Paris, Beijing, St Petersburg, Kyoto and elsewhere
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Library%20Cave%20of%20Dunhuang%20%28Mogao%20Cave%2017%29%20Monks%20of%20Dunhuang)

Cave 17 opens off the corridor of Cave 16 and was first a memorial chapel to the monk Hongbian, whose portrait statue stood in it. Around 1000, for reasons still debated, perhaps to store sacred waste or out of fear of invasion, it was filled with documents and sealed. The Daoist Wang Yuanlu found it in 1900. Aurel Stein in 1907 and Paul Pelliot in 1908 bought thousands of items, and others went to Japan, Russia and Beijing. The cache contains Buddhist sutras in many languages, the earliest Chan texts and the Platform Sutra, Daoist and Manichaean scriptures, Christian texts of the Church of the East, popular ballads, contracts and letters, and the printed Diamond Sutra of 868. International digitisation projects have begun to reunite it.
