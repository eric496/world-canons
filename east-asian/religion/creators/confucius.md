## Biography

Kong Qiu, known as Kongzi or Master Kong, was a minor official and teacher in the state of Lu in the late Spring and Autumn period. He claimed to transmit rather than invent, devoting himself to the rites and music of the early Zhou kings, and taught a circle of disciples whose recollections of his sayings became the Analects. He is treated in the philosophy section as a thinker.

For the history of religion Confucius matters both as a teacher of ritual and as an object of cult. He taught reverence in ancestral offerings while famously declining to speculate about spirits and death. From the Han dynasty the state sacrificed to him, and by the Tang every county had a temple of Confucius where officials performed the spring and autumn rites; the ceremony (Seokjeon daeje) is still held at the Seonggyungwan in Seoul. Whether this cult made Confucianism a religion was debated by Jesuit missionaries and modern scholars alike.

## Key texts & works

### Classic of Filial Piety (Xiaojing) (c. 4th–3rd century BC)

[![Classic of Filial Piety (Xiaojing)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5c/Frontispiece_of_The_Classic_of_Filial_Piety_%28%E5%AD%9D%E7%B6%93%29_by_Niu_Shuyu.jpg/1280px-Frontispiece_of_The_Classic_of_Filial_Piety_%28%E5%AD%9D%E7%B6%93%29_by_Niu_Shuyu.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Frontispiece_of_The_Classic_of_Filial_Piety_%28%E5%AD%9D%E7%B6%93%29_by_Niu_Shuyu.jpg)

<sub>Frontispiece of an illustrated Classic of Filial Piety Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Frontispiece_of_The_Classic_of_Filial_Piety_%28%E5%AD%9D%E7%B6%93%29_by_Niu_Shuyu.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 4th–3rd century BC
- **Type:** Confucian classic
- **Where written / held:** China; traditionally a dialogue of Confucius with his disciple Zengzi
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Classic%20of%20Filial%20Piety%20%28Xiaojing%29%20Confucius)

The Xiaojing presents Confucius teaching his disciple Zengzi that filial piety (xiao) begins with caring for the body received from one's parents and ends with establishing one's name to honour them, and that it extends from the family to loyalty to the ruler and harmony with Heaven and Earth. Its attribution to Confucius or Zengzi is traditional; scholars date it to the late Warring States period. Short and easy to memorise, it was among the first texts children learned for two thousand years, was annotated by the Tang emperor Xuanzong, and was illustrated in albums. It linked everyday ethics to ancestor worship and was taught in Japan and Korea as well, and Wang Chongyang included it among Quanzhen readings.
