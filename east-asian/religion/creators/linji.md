## Biography

Linji studied the scriptures before training under Huangbo Xiyun, who, according to the famous story, struck him each time he asked about the essence of Buddhism until he awakened. From around 850 he taught at a small temple by the Hutuo River in Zhenzhou in Hebei, from which his name comes, under the patronage of a regional governor.

His Record presents a teacher who urged students to trust themselves as 'the true person of no rank' and to drop all reliance on buddhas, patriarchs and texts, using shouts and blows to cut through conceptual thinking. The Linji lineage became the dominant form of Chan in the Song and, as Rinzai, one of the two main Zen schools in Japan.

## Key texts & works

### Record of Linji (Linji lu) (compiled 9th century; standard edition 1120)

[![Record of Linji (Linji lu)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e8/RinzaiGigen.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:RinzaiGigen.jpg)

<sub>Linji Yixuan, Japanese ink portrait Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:RinzaiGigen.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** compiled 9th century; standard edition 1120
- **Type:** Chan recorded sayings
- **Where written / held:** Zhenzhou, Hebei; edited at Gushan, Fujian
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Record%20of%20Linji%20%28Linji%20lu%29%20Linji%20Yixuan)

Compiled by his disciple Sansheng Huiran and re-edited by Yuanjue Zongyan in 1120, the Record of Linji gathers his public sermons, encounters with students and colleagues, and an account of his training under Huangbo. Linji urges his hearers to stop seeking outside themselves and to recognise the 'true person of no rank' who is present in their seeing and hearing; to break attachment he speaks of slaying buddhas and patriarchs and uses shouts and blows. Scholars note that the text grew with the Linji school's later prestige. It is a basic text of Rinzai Zen and was one of the first Zen works translated into Western languages.
