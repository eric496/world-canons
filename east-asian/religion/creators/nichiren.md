# Nichiren

## Biography

Born to a fishing family on the Bōsō peninsula, Nichiren studied at Kiyosumi-dera and on Mount Hiei and became convinced that the Lotus Sutra was the Buddha's final truth. From 1253 he taught the chanting of its title, Namu Myōhō Renge Kyō, and denounced the Pure Land, Zen, Shingon and Ritsu schools as the cause of Japan's disasters. He was attacked, twice exiled, and in 1271 narrowly escaped execution at Tatsunokuchi.

Nichiren saw his sufferings as proof that he was the votary of the Lotus Sutra foretold in scripture, and his warning that Japan would be invaded seemed fulfilled by the Mongol attacks of 1274 and 1281. He inscribed calligraphic mandalas (gohonzon) as objects of worship. His followers divided into many lines, and in the twentieth century Nichiren Buddhism inspired lay movements such as Sōka Gakkai and Risshō Kōseikai.

### Establishing the Correct Teaching for the Peace of the Land (Risshō ankoku ron) (1260)

[![Establishing the Correct Teaching for the Peace of the Land (Risshō ankoku ron)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Risshou_Ankokuron.jpg/1280px-Risshou_Ankokuron.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Risshou_Ankokuron.jpg)

<sub>Manuscript of the Risshō ankoku ron Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Risshou_Ankokuron.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1260
- **Type:** Treatise in dialogue form
- **Where written / held:** Kamakura; presented to Hōjō Tokiyori
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Establishing%20the%20Correct%20Teaching%20for%20the%20Peace%20of%20the%20Land%20%28Rissh%C5%8D%20ankoku%20ron%29%20Nichiren)

Written after a series of earthquakes, famines and epidemics, the treatise is a dialogue between a host and a traveller. The host argues, from the Lotus, Golden Light and other sutras, that the protective gods have abandoned Japan because people follow the false teaching of Hōnen's Senchakushū, and predicts internal rebellion and foreign invasion if this continues. Nichiren presented it to the retired regent Hōjō Tokiyori in 1260; the result was an attack on his hut and exile to Izu. The Mongol invasions of 1274 and 1281 seemed to his followers to fulfil the prophecy. It remains the central statement of Nichiren's vision of religion and nation.

### Opening of the Eyes (Kaimokushō) (1272)

- **Date:** 1272
- **Type:** Doctrinal treatise
- **Where written / held:** Sado Island
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Opening%20of%20the%20Eyes%20%28Kaimokush%C5%8D%29%20Nichiren)

After the near-execution at Tatsunokuchi in 1271 and banishment to the island of Sado, Nichiren wrote this long work in a freezing hut. It surveys the teachings of Confucianism, Brahmanism and the Buddhist schools to show the supremacy of the Lotus Sutra, asks why, if he truly upholds it, he is not protected by the gods, and answers that his persecutions are exactly what the sutra predicts for its practitioners in the latter age. It ends with his vow to be 'the pillar, the eyes and the great ship of Japan'. With the Kanjin no honzonshō, it is one of his most important writings.

### Gohonzon mandala of Nichiren (1271–1282)

[![Gohonzon mandala of Nichiren](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Rinmetsudojihonzon.jpg/1280px-Rinmetsudojihonzon.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rinmetsudojihonzon.jpg)

<sub>A gohonzon mandala attributed to Nichiren Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rinmetsudojihonzon.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1271–1282
- **Type:** Calligraphic mandala
- **Where written / held:** Various Nichiren temples, such as Myōhon-ji and Taiseki-ji
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Gohonzon%20mandala%20of%20Nichiren%20Nichiren)

From 1271 Nichiren wrote on paper or silk a vertical inscription of the daimoku, Namu Myōhō Renge Kyō, flanked by the names of Śākyamuni and Prabhūtaratna, bodhisattvas, Indian and Chinese teachers, Buddhist protectors and the Japanese kami Amaterasu and Hachiman, depicting the assembly of the Lotus Sutra. Some 120 survive in his hand. Chanting the daimoku before a gohonzon is the central practice of Nichiren Buddhism. Which mandala is authoritative is a major point of difference between Nichiren schools, with Nichiren Shōshū venerating the Dai-Gohonzon of Taiseki-ji, whose authenticity other schools dispute.
