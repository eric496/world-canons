## Biography

Wang Zhe, known by his Daoist name Chongyang, came from a wealthy family in Shaanxi under Jurchen Jin rule and pursued an official career without success. In 1159 he reported meeting immortals, traditionally identified with Lü Dongbin and Zhongli Quan, who taught him; he lived for some years in a grave mound he called the Tomb of the Living Dead. In 1167 he went east to Shandong, where he gathered seven principal disciples, among them Qiu Chuji and the woman master Sun Bu'er.

Wang taught celibacy, renunciation of family and wealth, meditation and inner alchemy, together with study of the Daodejing, the Heart Sutra and the Classic of Filial Piety, presenting the three teachings as one. His order lived in monasteries under rules, like Buddhist monks, and after Qiu Chuji won the patronage of Chinggis Khan in 1222 it spread rapidly. The White Cloud Temple in Beijing remains its headquarters.

## Key texts & works

### Fifteen Articles for Establishing the Teaching (Chongyang lijiao shiwu lun) (12th century)

- **Date:** 12th century
- **Type:** Monastic guidelines
- **Where written / held:** Shandong, Jin China; preserved in the Daoist Canon
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Fifteen%20Articles%20for%20Establishing%20the%20Teaching%20%28Chongyang%20lijiao%20shiwu%20lun%29%20Wang%20Chongyang)

In fifteen brief sections Wang sets out the way of life of his followers: leaving home and living in a hut, travelling to seek teachers, studying texts but not to excess, gathering medicinal herbs, building simple dwellings, finding companions, sitting in meditation, harmonising the 'five energies' and 'nature and life', and ultimately leaving the mundane world while still in the body. Attributed to Wang himself, it became a charter of the Quanzhen order, whose monastic organisation made it a Daoist counterpart to Chan Buddhism.
