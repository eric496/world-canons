# Zhang Boduan

*張伯端*

**987 – 1082** · Chinese  
Born: Tiantai, Zhejiang · Died:   
Traditions & eras: [Inner alchemy, Quanzhen and the Daoist canon](../movements/later-daoism.md)  

> Northern Song author of the Awakening to Reality, the classic poem-cycle of Daoist inner alchemy.

## Biography

Little is certain about Zhang Boduan's life. He is said to have been a minor official who was exiled, travelled to Sichuan and there in 1069 received the secrets of inner alchemy from a perfected master. He wrote the Wuzhen pian in 1075 and is counted as the first patriarch of the Southern Lineage (Nanzong) of inner alchemy, a status assigned retrospectively by later disciples.

His verses describe the inner elixir as the union of opposites within the practitioner's body, using the symbolism of lead and mercury, trigrams and the furnace, and he argued that Daoism, Buddhism and Confucianism agree at their deepest level. Inner alchemy as he framed it became the dominant form of Daoist self-cultivation and was later absorbed into Quanzhen.

## Key texts & works

### Awakening to Reality (Wuzhen pian) (1075)

- **Date:** 1075
- **Type:** Poems on inner alchemy
- **Where written / held:** Northern Song China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Awakening%20to%20Reality%20%28Wuzhen%20pian%29%20Zhang%20Boduan)

The Wuzhen pian consists of 81 regulated verses and other poems, framed with a preface by Zhang Boduan. Using the alchemical language of lead and mercury, dragon and tiger, furnace and cauldron, and the trigrams of the Book of Changes, it describes a process in which the adept refines essence, breath and spirit within the body to form an immortal embryo, and ends with Chan-inspired verses on the emptiness of mind. It insists that external elixirs are useless. Its obscurity produced a large commentary literature, and it was adopted as a foundational text by both the Southern Lineage and Quanzhen.
