## Biography

Uisang studied in Tang China from 661 under the second Huayan patriarch Zhiyan, alongside Fazang, who later wrote him a warm letter. Returning in 671, he founded Buseoksa in 676 and other temples in the mountains, training a large body of disciples. His seal-diagram poem on the dharma realm is treated in the philosophy section.

Uisang's piety centred on the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, whom he is said to have encountered in a cave on the east coast at Naksan, and on Amitābha. A Japanese thirteenth-century scroll tells the legend of the Chinese woman Shanmiao, who loved him and became a dragon to protect his ship. His school became the main doctrinal tradition of Silla Buddhism.

## Key texts & works

### Vow at the White Lotus Site (Baekhwa doryang barwonmun) (7th century)

- **Date:** 7th century
- **Type:** Vow text
- **Where written / held:** Naksansa, east coast of Silla
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Vow%20at%20the%20White%20Lotus%20Site%20%28Baekhwa%20doryang%20barwonmun%29%20Uisang)

According to the Samguk yusa, Uisang prayed at a sea cave at Naksan until Avalokiteśvara appeared and instructed him to build a temple, which became Naksansa. This vow, attributed to him, describes the practitioner's wish to become one with the bodhisattva, to be born in the White Lotus land and to save beings as the bodhisattva does. It is an early example of the cult of Avalokiteśvara that made Naksansa and its hermitage Hongnyeonam one of Korea's principal pilgrimage sites.
