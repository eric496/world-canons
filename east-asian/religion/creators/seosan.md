# Seosan Daesa (Hyujeong)

*西山大師 休靜*

## Biography

Hyujeong, known from his mountain residence as Seosan, was orphaned young and became a monk after studying Confucian classics in Seoul. He passed the briefly restored monastic examination in 1552 and headed both the Seon and doctrinal orders. In 1592, when Toyotomi Hideyoshi's armies invaded Korea, the king appointed him commander of the monk militias, which with his disciple Samyeong fought in the recapture of Pyongyang.

His Mirror for Seon Students combined Seon practice, scriptural study and Pure Land recitation, and argued for the harmony of Buddhism, Confucianism and Daoism. Nearly all later Korean monks belong to his lineage. Portraits of him and his disciples are kept in many Korean temples.

## Key texts & works

### Mirror for Seon Students (Seonga gwigam) (1564; printed 1579)

- **Date:** 1564; printed 1579
- **Type:** Seon manual
- **Where written / held:** Joseon Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Mirror%20for%20Seon%20Students%20%28Seonga%20gwigam%29%20Seosan%20Daesa%20%28Hyujeong%29)

Seosan gathered essential passages from sutras and Chan records, each with his comment, to guide students in an era when Buddhism was restricted by the Confucian state. He teaches that the scriptures are the Buddha's words and Seon his mind, so that one should study the teachings and then set them aside to investigate a huatou, and he includes guidance on Pure Land recitation and monastic conduct. Written in Chinese and translated into Korean, it was reprinted in Japan, and remains a basic text of the Jogye Order.
