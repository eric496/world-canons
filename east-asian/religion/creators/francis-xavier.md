## Biography

Francis Xavier studied in Paris, where he became one of the first companions of Ignatius of Loyola and a founder of the Society of Jesus. Sent to Portuguese Asia, he worked in Goa, southern India, Malacca and the Moluccas from 1542. In Malacca he met a Japanese man, Anjirō, and in 1549 landed with him at Kagoshima.

In two years in Japan he preached in Kagoshima, Hirado, Yamaguchi and Bungo and visited Kyoto, concluding that missionaries must adapt to Japanese culture and win over the elite; his letters gave Europe its first detailed impression of Japan. He died of fever in 1552 on an island off the Chinese coast while waiting to enter China. He was canonised in 1622.

## Key texts & works

### Letters from Japan (1549–1552)

- **Date:** 1549–1552
- **Type:** Missionary letters
- **Where written / held:** Kagoshima, Yamaguchi and Cochin; printed in Europe from 1550s
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Letters%20from%20Japan%20Francis%20Xavier)

In his long letter from Kagoshima of November 1549 Xavier praised the Japanese as 'the best people yet discovered', curious, honourable and rational, described the Buddhist clergy and the questions he was asked about God, evil and the fate of unbaptised ancestors, and recommended sending learned missionaries who could debate. Later letters reported his journey to Kyoto, his change from poor clothing to formal dress to meet the lord of Yamaguchi, and his plan to go to China since the Japanese deferred to Chinese learning. Copied and printed in Europe, they launched the Jesuit mission, which by 1600 claimed some 300,000 Japanese Christians.
