## Biography

Tao Hongjing was a tutor to princes of the Southern Qi before resigning in 492 to retire to Mount Mao (Maoshan) near Nanjing. There he collected and authenticated the autograph manuscripts of the Shangqing revelations received by the medium Yang Xi in the 360s, and published them with commentary as the Declarations of the Perfected. Emperor Wu of Liang consulted him so often that he was nicknamed the 'prime minister in the mountains'.

Tao is regarded as a patriarch of the Shangqing school, whose meditations visualised gods within the body and journeys to the stars. He was also a major pharmacologist, whose annotated edition of the Shennong materia medica shaped Chinese medicine, an alchemist and a calligrapher, and he is said to have received Buddhist precepts late in life, an example of the fluid boundaries of the time.

## Key texts & works

### Declarations of the Perfected (Zhen'gao) (c. 499)

- **Date:** c. 499
- **Type:** Revealed texts with commentary
- **Where written / held:** Mount Mao, Jiangsu
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Declarations%20of%20the%20Perfected%20%28Zhen%27gao%29%20Tao%20Hongjing)

Between 364 and 370 Yang Xi, working for the Xu family of officials near Jiankang, received nightly visits from immortals, the 'Perfected' of the Shangqing heavens, who dictated poems, instructions and gossip about the spirit world, which Yang wrote down in fine calligraphy. A century later Tao Hongjing searched out the scattered manuscripts, distinguished them from forgeries by their handwriting, and published them with detailed commentary. The Zhen'gao is a uniquely intimate record of a religious revelation and of fourth-century southern society, and the source for Shangqing practices of visualisation, star-walking and the bureaucracy of the underworld.
