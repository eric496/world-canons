# Compilers of the Engishiki

*延喜式編者*

**905 – 927** · Japanese  
Born: Heian-kyō (Kyoto) · Died: Heian-kyō (Kyoto)  
Traditions & eras: [Kami worship and Shinto](../movements/shinto.md)  

> The Heian court officials, led by Fujiwara no Tokihira and Fujiwara no Tadahira, who codified rites and administration in the Engishiki.

## Biography

In 905 Emperor Daigo ordered a new code of procedures to supplement Japan's laws. The work was begun under the minister Fujiwara no Tokihira and completed under his brother Tadahira in 927, though it was put into force only in 967. Its fifty books cover the whole of government.

The first ten books, on the Office of Kami Affairs (Jingikan), list the 2,861 officially recognised shrines and regulate festivals, offerings and purifications, and preserve the oldest liturgical prayers (norito). Their work became the reference for the classification of 'shrines listed in the Engishiki' that still confers prestige today.

- **Date:** 927
- **Type:** Code of procedures with ritual prayers
- **Where written / held:** Heian-kyō (Kyoto); oldest manuscripts include the Kujō family copy (Tokyo National Museum)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Procedures%20of%20the%20Engi%20Era%20%28Engishiki%29%2C%20books%20on%20the%20kami%20Compilers%20of%20the%20Engishiki)

The Engishiki in fifty books regulated the whole of court administration. Books one to ten, on kami affairs, set out the festivals of the year, such as the Kinensai prayer for harvest, the Niiname harvest offering and the great Daijōsai at an accession, the offerings each shrine was to receive, the rites of the Ise priestess and of purification, and a register (jinmyōchō) of 3,132 deities in 2,861 shrines. Book eight records 27 norito, including the Great Purification (Ōharae) recited twice a year to remove the sins of the nation, which is still in use. The Engishiki is the fundamental source for early shrine religion and its listing gives shrines prestige to this day.
