# Sotaesan (Pak Chung-bin)

*少太山 朴重彬*

**1891 – 1943** · Korean  
Born: Yeonggwang, Jeolla · Died: Iksan, Korea  
Traditions & eras: [New religions](../movements/new-religions.md), [Religion and modernity](../movements/modern-religion.md)  

> Founder of Won Buddhism, who after his enlightenment in 1916 built a reformed, lay-centred Buddhism around the symbol of the circle.

## Biography

Pak Chung-bin was born to a farming family in Yeonggwang and spent years in ascetic searching before an enlightenment experience on 28 April 1916. He gathered disciples, led them in reclaiming tidal land, and in 1924 established the Society for the Study of the Buddhadharma at Iksan, which after his death was named Won Buddhism (Wonbulgyo) in 1947.

Sotaesan proposed making Buddhism practical for modern life: a single circular symbol (Il-won-sang) in place of images, simplified rites, equality of men and women, and the unity of spiritual practice with work, education and welfare. His Principal Book (Jeongjeon) appeared in 1943, just after his death. Won Buddhism is now one of Korea's major religions.

## Key texts & works

### Principal Book of Won Buddhism (Jeongjeon) (1943)

- **Date:** 1943
- **Type:** Scripture
- **Where written / held:** Iksan, Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Principal%20Book%20of%20Won%20Buddhism%20%28Jeongjeon%29%20Sotaesan%20%28Pak%20Chung-bin%29)

The Jeongjeon presents the Il-won-sang, the circle symbolising the Dharmakāya Buddha and the original nature of all beings, as the object of faith and model of practice; the 'four graces' of heaven and earth, parents, fellow beings and laws; the 'four essentials' including gender equality and education of others' children; and the three studies of spiritual cultivation, inquiry and choice in action. Published in 1943 under the title Bulgyo jeongjeon, it was revised as part of the Won Buddhist Scriptures (Wonbulgyo gyojeon) of 1962, together with the Daejonggyeong, the sayings of Sotaesan.
