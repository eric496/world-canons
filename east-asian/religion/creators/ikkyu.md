## Biography

Ikkyū was probably a son of Emperor Go-Komatsu by a lady of the court and was placed in a temple as a child. He trained under the severe master Kasō Sōdon and received his seal of awakening, then spent much of his life wandering, writing verse, frequenting brothels and taverns, and attacking the commercialised Zen of the great Kyoto monasteries. At eighty he was made abbot of Daitoku-ji and led its rebuilding after the Ōnin War.

His Chinese poems in the Crazy Cloud Anthology, including love poems to the blind singer Mori, are some of the most personal religious poetry in Japanese literature. He influenced Noh, tea and calligraphy, and became a folk hero, the clever boy monk of popular stories and children's cartoons.

## Key texts & works

### Crazy Cloud Anthology (Kyōunshū) (15th century)

- **Date:** 15th century
- **Type:** Collection of Chinese poems
- **Where written / held:** Kyoto and Sakai
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Crazy%20Cloud%20Anthology%20%28Ky%C5%8Dunsh%C5%AB%29%20Ikky%C5%AB%20S%C5%8Djun)

Ikkyū called himself Kyōun, 'Crazy Cloud'. The poems, collected by disciples, range from verses on kōans and Zen masters to attacks on his rival Yōsō of Daitoku-ji for selling enlightenment, celebrations of sake and the brothel, and late love poems to the blind singer Mori, whom he met in his seventies. They express the idea that awakening is found in, not beyond, the passions, and the defiance of convention associated with Zen 'madness'. The collection survives in several manuscripts, with a sequel, and has been widely translated.
