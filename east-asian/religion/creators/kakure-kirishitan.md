# Kakure Kirishitan communities

*隠れキリシタン*

**1614 – present** · Japanese  
Born: Nagasaki and Hirado region · Died: Nagasaki region  
Traditions & eras: [Christianity in East Asia](../movements/christianity-east-asia.md)  

> The Hidden Christians of Japan, who kept a secret, increasingly Japanese form of Christianity through two and a half centuries of prohibition.

## Biography

After the Tokugawa shogunate banned Christianity in 1614 and crushed the Shimabara rebellion of 1637–38, villages around Nagasaki, the Gotō Islands and Ikitsuki continued to practise in secret. They outwardly registered at Buddhist temples, were made to tread on images of Christ and Mary (fumi-e), and passed down prayers (orasho) in garbled Latin and Portuguese, baptism, a liturgical calendar and hidden images.

In 1865 some revealed themselves to a French priest at the new Ōura Church in Nagasaki; after persecution ended in 1873 many rejoined the Catholic Church, but others kept their ancestral form, now called Kakure Kirishitan, which survives in a few dwindling communities. Their sites were inscribed by UNESCO in 2018.

### Maria Kannon images (17th–19th century)

[![Maria Kannon images](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c8/Maria_Kannon_Edo.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Maria_Kannon_Edo.jpg)

<sub>Maria Kannon figure, Edo period Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Maria_Kannon_Edo.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 17th–19th century
- **Type:** Devotional statues
- **Where written / held:** Nagasaki region; examples in the Tokyo National Museum and local collections
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Maria%20Kannon%20images%20Kakure%20Kirishitan%20communities)

Under the ban on Christianity, believers around Nagasaki prayed before white porcelain figures of the child-giving Kannon, many made in Dehua in China, and other Buddhist images, which they understood as Maria with the infant Jesus, sometimes marking them with small crosses. Officials confiscated many in the persecutions of the 1850s and 1860s, and some passed to the Nagasaki magistrate's office and later to museums. The images show both the pressure of the Tokugawa inquisition and the creative fusion by which a forbidden faith survived.

### The Beginning of Heaven and Earth (Tenchi hajimari no koto) (19th century)

- **Date:** 19th century
- **Type:** Hidden Christian scripture
- **Where written / held:** Sotome and Gotō Islands, Nagasaki region
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Beginning%20of%20Heaven%20and%20Earth%20%28Tenchi%20hajimari%20no%20koto%29%20Kakure%20Kirishitan%20communities)

Transmitted orally and in manuscripts copied secretly, the text begins with the creation of the world by Deusu, tells of Adam and Eve and the flood, the birth of Christ to Maria, the massacre of the innocents, the crucifixion and the end of the world, in a form shaped by two centuries without priests: Maria is a princess of Luzon, the flood comes when the eyes of a temple lion turn red, and the dead are judged in Buddhist terms. Known from manuscripts copied in the nineteenth century and studied by twentieth-century folklorists, it is the main evidence for how the Kakure Kirishitan reinterpreted Christianity in isolation.
