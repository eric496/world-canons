# Eisai

> Tendai monk who twice travelled to Song China and brought back the Rinzai lineage of Zen, and tea.

## Biography

Eisai (also read Yōsai) trained on Mount Hiei and went to China briefly in 1168 and again from 1187 to 1191, when he received the Linji transmission from Xu'an Huaichang on Mount Tiantai. Back in Japan he founded Shōfuku-ji in Hakata and, with the support of the Kamakura shogunate, Jufuku-ji in Kamakura and Kennin-ji in Kyoto in 1202.

He presented Zen as a revival of strict monastic discipline that would protect the state, and combined it with Tendai and esoteric rites; he was not founding a separate sect so much as reforming within the old order. His treatise on tea promoted drinking powdered green tea for health, and he is remembered as a founder of Japanese tea culture.

### Treatise on Promoting Zen for the Protection of the Country (Kōzen gokokuron) (1198)

- **Date:** 1198
- **Type:** Treatise
- **Where written / held:** Kyoto or Kamakura
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Treatise%20on%20Promoting%20Zen%20for%20the%20Protection%20of%20the%20Country%20%28K%C5%8Dzen%20gokokuron%29%20Eisai)

Faced with a court ban on the 'Daruma school' in 1194, Eisai wrote this treatise in ten sections to show that the Zen he had received in China was orthodox, that Saichō himself had transmitted Chan along with Tendai, and that Zen practice depends on keeping the precepts. He argued that if monks observe discipline and meditate the nation will be secure, aligning Zen with the 'state-protecting' role of established Buddhism. The text helped secure shogunal patronage and the foundation of Kennin-ji, and marks the start of Rinzai Zen as an institution in Japan.

### Drinking Tea for Health (Kissa yōjōki) (1211; revised 1214)

[![Drinking Tea for Health (Kissa yōjōki)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/42/%E5%96%AB%E8%8C%B6%E9%A4%8A%E7%94%9F%E8%A8%98-Kissa_Y%C5%8Dj%C5%8Dki%2C_Vol.1%2C_p.4.png/1280px-%E5%96%AB%E8%8C%B6%E9%A4%8A%E7%94%9F%E8%A8%98-Kissa_Y%C5%8Dj%C5%8Dki%2C_Vol.1%2C_p.4.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%E5%96%AB%E8%8C%B6%E9%A4%8A%E7%94%9F%E8%A8%98-Kissa_Y%C5%8Dj%C5%8Dki%2C_Vol.1%2C_p.4.png)

<sub>Page from an early edition of the Kissa yōjōki Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%E5%96%AB%E8%8C%B6%E9%A4%8A%E7%94%9F%E8%A8%98-Kissa_Y%C5%8Dj%C5%8Dki%2C_Vol.1%2C_p.4.png).</sub>

- **Date:** 1211; revised 1214
- **Type:** Treatise on tea and health
- **Where written / held:** Kamakura; presented to the shōgun Minamoto no Sanetomo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Drinking%20Tea%20for%20Health%20%28Kissa%20y%C5%8Dj%C5%8Dki%29%20Eisai)

Eisai explains, with references to Chinese medicine and five-phases theory, that tea strengthens the heart, the organ most in need in Japan, and describes how tea was grown and prepared in Song China. According to the Azuma kagami, he presented the revised version in 1214 to the shōgun Minamoto no Sanetomo, who was suffering after a night of drinking. The text connected the monastic tea culture of Chan with Japanese elite life and is honoured as a founding document of the Japanese way of tea.
