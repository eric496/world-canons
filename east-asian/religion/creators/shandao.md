## Biography

Shandao became a monk young and, after meeting the Pure Land teacher Daochuo, devoted himself to devotion to Amitābha. He spent his career in Chang'an, where he is said to have copied the Amitābha Sutra many thousands of times, painted images of the Pure Land, and helped supervise the colossal Vairocana at Longmen commissioned by the court.

His commentary on the Contemplation Sutra argued that the sutra was addressed to ordinary deluded people and that the 'right practice' was calling Amitābha's name with sincere faith. Hōnen in twelfth-century Japan wrote that reading this passage changed his life, so that Shandao came to be revered in Japan as a patriarch of the Jōdo and Jōdo Shinshū schools.

## Key texts & works

### Commentary on the Contemplation Sutra (Guan wuliangshou jing shu) (7th century)

- **Date:** 7th century
- **Type:** Scriptural commentary
- **Where written / held:** Chang'an
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Commentary%20on%20the%20Contemplation%20Sutra%20%28Guan%20wuliangshou%20jing%20shu%29%20Shandao)

The Contemplation Sutra describes sixteen visualisations of Amitābha and his land, taught by the Buddha to Queen Vaidehī. Earlier commentators read it as a manual for advanced meditators; Shandao, in four fascicles, insisted that it was taught for ordinary beings in an evil age, that the nine grades of rebirth it describes all refer to such people, and that calling the name is the act that accords with Amitābha's vow. His parable of the two rivers and the white path became a favourite subject of painting. Hōnen founded the Jōdo school on its authority in 1175.
