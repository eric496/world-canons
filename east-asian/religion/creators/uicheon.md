## Biography

Uicheon was the fourth son of King Munjong and became a monk at eleven. In 1085 he travelled secretly to Song China, met over fifty eminent monks and studied Huayan and Tiantai, returning in 1086 with some 3,000 fascicles. He set up a directorate at Heungwang-sa to collect and print East Asian Buddhist commentaries.

Hoping to reconcile doctrine and meditation, he founded the Cheontae school at Gukcheong-sa in 1097, drawing many Seon monks to it. His catalogue of sectarian writings and the 'supplementary canon' he printed preserved works lost in China. He died at 46 and received the title National Preceptor Daegak.

## Key texts & works

### New Catalogue of the Teachings of All Schools (Sinpyeon jejong gyojang chongnok) (1090)

- **Date:** 1090
- **Type:** Bibliographic catalogue
- **Where written / held:** Heungwangsa, Gaegyeong (Kaesong), Goryeo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=New%20Catalogue%20of%20the%20Teachings%20of%20All%20Schools%20%28Sinpyeon%20jejong%20gyojang%20chongnok%29%20Uicheon)

The Buddhist canon included sutras, monastic rules and Indian treatises but not most Chinese, Korean and Khitan commentaries. Uicheon, having gathered such works in Song China, Liao and Japan, catalogued about 1,010 titles in 4,857 fascicles by scripture and had many of them carved as a 'supplementary canon' (gyojang) at Heungwangsa. Most of the blocks were lost in the Mongol invasions, but some prints survive in Japan, and the catalogue preserves the titles of works otherwise unknown. It is a landmark of East Asian Buddhist bibliography.
