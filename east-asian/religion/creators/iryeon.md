# Iryeon

*一然*

**1206 – 1289** · Korean  
Born: Gyeongsan, Goryeo · Died: Ingaksa, Goryeo  
Traditions & eras: [Buddhism in Silla and Goryeo](../movements/korean-buddhism.md)  
Also in: [History](../../history/creators/iryeon.md)  

> Goryeo Seon monk who compiled the Samguk yusa, the principal source for Korean myth, Buddhist legend and the Dangun story.

## Biography

Iryeon became a monk in his youth, passed the Seon examination in 1227 and lived through the decades of Mongol invasion. He served as royal preceptor under King Chungnyeol and in old age retired to Ingaksa in Gyeongsang Province to care for his mother.

There he completed the Memorabilia of the Three Kingdoms, which preserves foundation myths, including that of Dangun, temple origin stories, miracle tales, accounts of eminent monks and fourteen hyangga songs. In a period of foreign domination it gave Koreans a record of their antiquity complementary to the official Confucian history of Kim Busik.

## Key texts & works

### Memorabilia of the Three Kingdoms (Samguk yusa) (c. 1281–1289)

- **Date:** c. 1281–1289
- **Type:** Collection of history, myth and Buddhist legend
- **Where written / held:** Ingaksa, Goryeo; oldest complete edition 1512
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Memorabilia%20of%20the%20Three%20Kingdoms%20%28Samguk%20yusa%29%20Iryeon)

In five books, Iryeon gathered material ignored by the Confucian History of the Three Kingdoms: the founding myth of Gojoseon by Dangun, son of the Heavenly Prince and a bear-woman, the birth legends of Silla, Goguryeo and Gaya kings, and above all Buddhist history: the arrival of Buddhism in each kingdom, the martyrdom of Ichadon, temple origins, images and relics, eminent monks such as Wonhyo and Uisang, and miracles of devotion. His sources included lost histories, inscriptions and oral tales. It preserves fourteen of the 25 surviving hyangga, and its Dangun story became central to modern Korean national identity.
