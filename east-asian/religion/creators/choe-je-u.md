# Choe Je-u

*崔濟愚*

**1824 – 1864** · Korean  
Born: Gyeongju, Joseon · Died: Daegu, Joseon  
Traditions & eras: [New religions](../movements/new-religions.md)  
Also in: [Philosophy](../../philosophy/creators/choe-je-u.md)  

> Founder of Donghak (Eastern Learning), the Korean religion that became Cheondogyo, executed by the Joseon state.

## Biography

Choe Je-u, also known as Suun, was the son of a provincial scholar by a remarried widow, which barred him from office. After years of wandering, on 5 April 1860 he reported an encounter with the Lord of Heaven (Hanullim), who gave him a talisman and a sacred incantation. He preached in the Gyeongju region to large numbers of peasants.

His teaching, called Eastern Learning in contrast to Western (Catholic) learning, is treated in the philosophy section. The government saw it as Catholicism in disguise and executed him in Daegu in 1864. His successor Choe Si-hyeong compiled his scriptures; the movement fuelled the Donghak Peasant Revolution of 1894 and was renamed Cheondogyo (Religion of the Heavenly Way) in 1905 by Son Byong-hi, who led the March First Movement of 1919.

### Great Scripture of Eastern Learning (Donggyeong daejeon) (1860–1863; printed 1880)

[![Great Scripture of Eastern Learning (Donggyeong daejeon)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Tong_Kyung_Dae_Jeon_%281%29.jpg/1280px-Tong_Kyung_Dae_Jeon_%281%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tong_Kyung_Dae_Jeon_%281%29.jpg)

<sub>Early edition of the Donggyeong daejeon Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tong_Kyung_Dae_Jeon_%281%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1860–1863; printed 1880
- **Type:** Scripture
- **Where written / held:** Written in Gyeongju; printed by Choe Si-hyeong in Inje, Gangwon Province
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Great%20Scripture%20of%20Eastern%20Learning%20%28Donggyeong%20daejeon%29%20Choe%20Je-u)

The scripture gathers Choe Je-u's essays, including the Podeongmun, his account of the revelation of 1860 and the worship of the Lord of Heaven, the Nonhangmun, defining Eastern Learning against Western learning, and the 21-character incantation. After his execution in 1864 his successor Choe Si-hyeong, hunted by the authorities, reconstructed the texts, partly from memory, and printed them secretly in 1880. As the scripture of Donghak, it inspired the peasant uprising of 1894; it remains the central scripture of Cheondogyo.

### Hymns from Yongdam (Yongdam yusa) (1860–1863; printed 1881)

[![Hymns from Yongdam (Yongdam yusa)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cd/Yong-Dam-Yu-Sa_%281%29.jpg/1280px-Yong-Dam-Yu-Sa_%281%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yong-Dam-Yu-Sa_%281%29.jpg)

<sub>Early edition of the Yongdam yusa Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Yong-Dam-Yu-Sa_%281%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1860–1863; printed 1881
- **Type:** Religious songs in Korean
- **Where written / held:** Written at Yongdam, Gyeongju; printed in Danyang, Chungcheong Province
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Hymns%20from%20Yongdam%20%28Yongdam%20yusa%29%20Choe%20Je-u)

Alongside his classical Chinese writings, Choe Je-u wrote nine songs in the gasa form in Hangul, intended for peasants and women who could not read Chinese. They describe his search and revelation, praise the Lord of Heaven, exhort followers to sincerity and respect, and announce a coming 'opening' of a new age. Printed by Choe Si-hyeong in 1881, they were sung in Donghak communities and are among the first religious scriptures composed in Korean.
