# Baegun Gyeonghan

*白雲景閑*

**1298 – 1374** · Korean  
Born: Gobu, Jeolla · Died: Chwiamsa, Yeoju, Goryeo  
Traditions & eras: [Buddhism in Silla and Goryeo](../movements/korean-buddhism.md), [Chan, Seon and Zen](../movements/chan-zen-seon.md)  

> Goryeo Seon master whose anthology of patriarchs' teachings, the Jikji, is the oldest extant book printed with movable metal type.

## Biography

Baegun went to Yuan China in 1351 and received teachings from the Linji master Shiwu Qinggong and from the Indian monk Zhikong. Back in Korea he served as abbot of several temples and taught a direct, 'no-mind' Seon.

Late in life he compiled an anthology of sayings of buddhas and patriarchs on recognising the mind. In 1377, three years after his death, his disciples printed it at Heungdeoksa in Cheongju with movable metal type, some 78 years before Gutenberg's Bible. Only the second volume survives, in Paris.

## Key texts & works

### Jikji (Baegun hwasang chorok buljo jikji simche yojeol) (1377)

- **Date:** 1377
- **Type:** Seon anthology printed with movable metal type
- **Where written / held:** Printed at Heungdeoksa, Cheongju; volume 2 in the Bibliothèque nationale de France, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jikji%20%28Baegun%20hwasang%20chorok%20buljo%20jikji%20simche%20yojeol%29%20Baegun%20Gyeonghan)

Baegun selected passages from Chan transmission records on recognising the true mind, beginning with the seven buddhas of the past and continuing through Indian and Chinese patriarchs. His disciples Seokchan and Daldam printed it in two volumes in the seventh month of 1377 at Heungdeoksa, a temple outside Cheongju, as the colophon states. Only the second volume survives, lacking its first leaf; it was acquired by the French diplomat Victor Collin de Plancy in Seoul and passed through the collector Henri Vever to the Bibliothèque nationale in 1950. Its rediscovery in 1972 established Korean priority in metal-type printing, and it is listed in UNESCO's Memory of the World. Korea has long sought its return.
