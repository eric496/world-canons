# Yamato-e painters

*大和絵の絵師*

**9th century – 14th century** · Japanese  
Born: Kyoto · Died: Kyoto  
Traditions & eras: [Kami worship and Shinto](../movements/shinto.md)  
Also in: [Art](../../art/creators/yamato-e-painters.md)  

> The largely anonymous court and temple painters of narrative handscrolls in the native Japanese style; the same collective appears in the art section.

## Biography

Yamato-e, 'Japanese painting', developed from the Heian period as a style distinct from Chinese models, with bright mineral colours and Japanese subjects. Its practitioners worked for the court, aristocrats and temples and are mostly anonymous.

Among their works are many illustrated legends of the origins of shrines and temples (engi emaki), which served both devotion and fundraising and show kami and buddhas acting together in Japanese history.

## Key texts & works

### Illustrated Legends of Kitano Tenjin (Kitano Tenjin engi emaki) (c. 1219)

[![Illustrated Legends of Kitano Tenjin (Kitano Tenjin engi emaki)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/0b/Kitano_Tenjin_Engi_Emaki_-_Jokyo_-_Michizane_in_exil.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kitano_Tenjin_Engi_Emaki_-_Jokyo_-_Michizane_in_exil.jpg)

<sub>Michizane in exile, from the Jōkyū version of the Kitano Tenjin engi emaki Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kitano_Tenjin_Engi_Emaki_-_Jokyo_-_Michizane_in_exil.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1219
- **Type:** Illustrated handscroll
- **Where written / held:** Kitano Tenmangū, Kyoto (Jōkyū version)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Illustrated%20Legends%20of%20Kitano%20Tenjin%20%28Kitano%20Tenjin%20engi%20emaki%29%20Yamato-e%20painters)

Sugawara no Michizane (845–903), a scholar and minister exiled to Dazaifu through the intrigues of the Fujiwara, died in disgrace; when lightning, plague and deaths struck the court, they were attributed to his angry spirit, and he was enshrined at Kitano in Kyoto in 947 as Tenjin, later a god of learning. The Jōkyū version of the scrolls, dated around 1219 and painted in yamato-e, narrates his life, exile and the vengeance of his spirit, and then the visions of the monk Nichizō, who travels through the six realms and the hells. It shows how kami cults, angry-spirit (goryō) beliefs and Buddhist cosmology fused in medieval Japan, and served as the shrine's origin legend.
