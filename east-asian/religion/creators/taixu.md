# Taixu

*太虛*

**1890 – 1947** · Chinese  
Born: Haining, Zhejiang · Died: Shanghai  
Traditions & eras: [Religion and modernity](../movements/modern-religion.md)  

> Chinese monk and reformer whose 'Buddhism for human life' became the basis of modern Humanistic Buddhism.

## Biography

Taixu became a monk at sixteen and was drawn into revolutionary politics and new learning in the last years of the Qing. From 1913 he called for a revolution in Buddhist doctrine, institutions and property, founded seminaries and the journal Haichao yin, travelled to Europe and America in 1928–29, and organised Buddhist associations under the Republic.

He argued that Buddhism should serve this world rather than funerals and the afterlife, and should engage with science and social reform. His student Yinshun developed these ideas as 'Humanistic Buddhism' (renjian fojiao), which inspired the large Taiwanese organisations of Foguangshan, Tzu Chi and Dharma Drum Mountain.

## Key texts & works

### Humanistic Buddhism and the journal Haichao yin (1920 onward)

- **Date:** 1920 onward
- **Type:** Reform journal and lectures
- **Where written / held:** Hangzhou and Shanghai
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Humanistic%20Buddhism%20and%20the%20journal%20Haichao%20yin%20Taixu)

Haichao yin, founded by Taixu and his disciples in 1920, became the longest-running Chinese Buddhist periodical, carrying debates on monastic education, property, Buddhism and science, and Buddhist responses to Christianity and Marxism. In its pages and in lectures such as those on 'Buddhism of human life' (rensheng fojiao), Taixu argued that Buddhism should centre on human beings and social service rather than rites for the dead. His approach, developed by Yinshun, became Humanistic Buddhism, the dominant ideal of modern Chinese Buddhism in Taiwan and beyond.
