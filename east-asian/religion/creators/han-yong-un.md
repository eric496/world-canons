# Han Yong-un

*韓龍雲*

**1879 – 1944** · Korean  
Born: Hongseong, Joseon · Died: Seoul  
Traditions & eras: [Religion and modernity](../movements/modern-religion.md)  
Also in: [Philosophy](../../philosophy/creators/han-yong-un.md), [Literature](../../literature/creators/han-yong-un.md)  

> Buddhist monk, reformer, poet and independence leader who campaigned to modernise Korean Buddhism under Japanese rule.

## Biography

Han Yong-un, known as Manhae, became a monk at Baekdamsa in 1905, and travelled to Japan in 1908 to observe modern Buddhism. In 1919 he was one of the 33 signatories of the March First Declaration of Independence and was imprisoned for three years. His thought and his reform treatise are treated in the philosophy section, and his poetry in the literature section.

As a religious reformer he argued for Buddhism in the cities rather than the mountains, education of monks, simplified ritual and even clerical marriage, and resisted the colonial government's control of temples. He compiled a one-volume anthology of scripture for modern readers and edited the journal Yusim. He died in Seoul in 1944, a year before liberation.

## Key texts & works

### Great Buddhist Scripture (Bulgyo daejeon) (1914)

- **Date:** 1914
- **Type:** Anthology of scriptures
- **Where written / held:** Beomeosa, Busan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Great%20Buddhist%20Scripture%20%28Bulgyo%20daejeon%29%20Han%20Yong-un)

While studying the Tripitaka Koreana at Tongdosa, Han Yong-un selected passages from over 400 scriptures and treatises and arranged them in nine chapters on such topics as faith, the self, society, the nation and practice, so that lay people could grasp Buddhism without the full canon. Published in 1914, it embodied his reformist conviction, set out in his treatise on Korean Buddhist reform, that Buddhism should be accessible, socially engaged and modern. It was one of the first works of its kind in East Asia.
