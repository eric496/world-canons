# Genshin

**942 – 1017** · Japanese  
Born: Yamato Province · Died: Yokawa, Mount Hiei  
Traditions & eras: [Pure Land](../movements/pure-land.md)  

> Tendai scholar-monk whose Essentials of Rebirth, with its descriptions of the hells and the Pure Land, spread Pure Land piety in Japan.

<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Genshin, Japanese hanging scroll · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Genshin-gazo.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Genshin entered Mount Hiei as a child and studied under the abbot Ryōgen. Declining court honours, he withdrew to the Yokawa precinct, where he led a fraternity of monks who pledged to support one another in nenbutsu practice and to attend each other's deathbeds.

His Ōjōyōshū of 985 collected scriptural passages describing the realms of rebirth, especially the hells, and the glories of Amida's Pure Land, and gave detailed guidance on contemplating Amida and on deathbed rites. It shaped Heian aristocratic religion, the iconography of hell scrolls and welcoming-descent (raigō) paintings, and was sent to China. Hōnen began from his work.

## Key texts & works

### Essentials of Rebirth in the Pure Land (Ōjōyōshū) (985)

- **Date:** 985
- **Type:** Pure Land anthology and manual
- **Where written / held:** Yokawa, Mount Hiei
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Essentials%20of%20Rebirth%20in%20the%20Pure%20Land%20%28%C5%8Cj%C5%8Dy%C5%8Dsh%C5%AB%29%20Genshin)

In ten chapters drawn from some 160 scriptures and treatises, Genshin first describes with terrible vividness the eight great hells and the other realms of rebirth, so that the reader will 'loathe this defiled world', then the joys of the Pure Land, and finally methods of practice, above all contemplating Amida's form and calling his name, and the conduct of the deathbed, where the dying hold a cord tied to an image of Amida. It became the guide for Heian aristocrats such as Fujiwara no Michinaga, inspired hell scrolls and paintings of Amida's descent, and was sent to Song China, where it was admired. Hōnen's turn to exclusive nenbutsu began from it.
