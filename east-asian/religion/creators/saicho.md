## Biography

Saichō was ordained at Tōdai-ji in 785 but almost at once withdrew to Mount Hiei above the site of the future capital Kyoto to meditate and study Tiantai texts. With the backing of Emperor Kanmu he sailed to China in 804, on the same embassy as Kūkai, and studied Tiantai, Chan, esoteric rites and precepts at Mount Tiantai. On his return in 805 the court recognised Tendai as a school with its own annual ordinands.

He spent his last years fighting the Nara establishment for the right to ordain monks on Mount Hiei with Mahāyāna bodhisattva precepts alone, and the court granted it a week after his death. His regulations required a twelve-year mountain training. Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei became the mother temple of Japanese Buddhism: Hōnen, Shinran, Eisai, Dōgen and Nichiren all trained there. He received the posthumous title Dengyō Daishi.

## Key texts & works

### Regulations for Students of the Mountain School (Sange gakushō shiki) (818–819)

[![Regulations for Students of the Mountain School (Sange gakushō shiki)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/79/Tendai_hokkesh%C5%AB_nenbun_engi.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tendai_hokkesh%C5%AB_nenbun_engi.jpg)

<sub>Tendai hokkeshū nenbun engi, Saichō's petitions in his own hand (Enryaku-ji) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tendai_hokkesh%C5%AB_nenbun_engi.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 818–819
- **Type:** Monastic regulations and petitions
- **Where written / held:** Mount Hiei; Saichō's autograph in the Tendai hokkeshū nenbun engi (Enryaku-ji)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Regulations%20for%20Students%20of%20the%20Mountain%20School%20%28Sange%20gakush%C5%8D%20shiki%29%20Saich%C5%8D)

The three documents, in six, eight and four articles, were submitted to Emperor Saga in 818 and 819. Saichō asked that Tendai's annual ordinands receive only the bodhisattva precepts of the Brahmā's Net Sutra on Mount Hiei, rather than the full monastic precepts at the Nara-controlled platform of Tōdai-ji, and that they then remain on the mountain for twelve years of study and practice. He called those who could speak and act on the Way 'treasures of the nation'. The Nara clergy objected; Saichō answered in his Kenkairon, and permission came only after his death in 822. The independent Hiei ordination made Enryaku-ji the main training centre of Japanese Buddhism.
