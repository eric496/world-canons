> Sixth Patriarch of Chan, the illiterate woodcutter of tradition, whose teaching of sudden awakening defined Chinese Chan.

<sub>Portrait: Liang Kai, The Sixth Patriarch Cutting Bamboo, Southern Song (Tokyo National Museum) · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Sixth_Patriarch_Cutting_the_Bamboo.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

According to the Platform Sutra, Huineng was a poor, illiterate seller of firewood in the far south who heard the Diamond Sutra recited, went to study under the Fifth Patriarch Hongren, and won the robe of succession with a verse showing that the mind is originally pure. He then taught at Caoxi near Shaoguan, where his mummified body is still venerated at Nanhua Temple.

Historians note that his fame derives largely from his disciple Shenhui, who from 732 campaigned against a rival northern lineage, and that the Platform Sutra grew over time. Nonetheless almost every later Chan, Seon and Zen lineage traces itself through him, and he stands for the ideal of awakening as available to anyone, whatever their learning.

## Key texts & works

### Platform Sutra of the Sixth Patriarch (Liuzu tanjing) (8th century (Dunhuang text c. 780); expanded 1291)

[![Platform Sutra of the Sixth Patriarch (Liuzu tanjing)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Double_page_from_the_Korean_print_of_%22The_Sixth_Patriarch%27s_Dharma_Jewel_Platform_Sutra%22.jpg/1280px-Double_page_from_the_Korean_print_of_%22The_Sixth_Patriarch%27s_Dharma_Jewel_Platform_Sutra%22.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Double_page_from_the_Korean_print_of_%22The_Sixth_Patriarch%27s_Dharma_Jewel_Platform_Sutra%22.jpg)

<sub>Korean woodblock print of the Platform Sutra Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Double_page_from_the_Korean_print_of_%22The_Sixth_Patriarch%27s_Dharma_Jewel_Platform_Sutra%22.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 8th century (Dunhuang text c. 780); expanded 1291
- **Type:** Chan scripture
- **Where written / held:** Shaozhou, Guangdong; Dunhuang manuscript in the British Library (S. 5475)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Platform%20Sutra%20of%20the%20Sixth%20Patriarch%20%28Liuzu%20tanjing%29%20Huineng)

The text presents Huineng's sermon from the platform at the Dafan temple in Shaozhou, his life story with the verse contest that won him the patriarchate, and dialogues with disciples, teaching that the Buddha-nature is inherently present and awakening is sudden. The earliest version, found at Dunhuang, is about half the length of the 1291 Yuan edition by Zongbao that became standard. Scholars see the Platform Sutra as a product of the Southern school's campaign for Huineng's legacy, gradually expanded by his heirs. As a religious text it was recited, used for transmission rituals, and studied by Jinul and many others.
