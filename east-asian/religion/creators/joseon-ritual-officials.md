# Joseon ritual officials

*조선 예관*

**1392 – 1910** · Korean  
Born: Hanseong (Seoul) · Died: Hanseong (Seoul)  
Traditions & eras: [Confucian ritual and the state cult](../movements/confucian-ritual.md)  

> The scholar-officials of the Joseon Ministry of Rites who codified and performed the Confucian state rites of Korea.

## Biography

The Joseon dynasty (1392–1910) took Neo-Confucianism as its state ideology and set out to govern by ritual. Officials of the Ministry of Rites (Yejo) and the court scholars of the Hall of Worthies researched Chinese precedents and wrote manuals for every ceremony of state, culminating in the Five Rites of the State completed in 1474. They also supervised the royal ancestral shrine, Jongmyo, and the altars of earth and grain.

Their work made Korea one of the most thoroughly Confucian societies in history, with ancestor rites performed in elite households according to Zhu Xi's Family Rituals and royal rites performed with court music and dance. The Jongmyo rite and its music, maintained by the descendants of the royal family, are still performed each May and are recognised by UNESCO.

### Jongmyo Royal Ancestral Rite (Jongmyo jerye) (1395 – present)

[![Jongmyo Royal Ancestral Rite (Jongmyo jerye)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/be/Korean_Royal_Ancestral_Ritual_Music-Jongmyo_Jeryeak-01.jpg/1280px-Korean_Royal_Ancestral_Ritual_Music-Jongmyo_Jeryeak-01.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Korean_Royal_Ancestral_Ritual_Music-Jongmyo_Jeryeak-01.jpg)

<sub>Musicians performing the Jongmyo jeryeak at Jongmyo, Seoul Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Korean_Royal_Ancestral_Ritual_Music-Jongmyo_Jeryeak-01.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1395 – present
- **Type:** Ritual and court music
- **Where written / held:** Jongmyo shrine, Seoul
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jongmyo%20Royal%20Ancestral%20Rite%20%28Jongmyo%20jerye%29%20Joseon%20ritual%20officials)

Jongmyo was built in 1394–95 when the dynasty moved its capital to Seoul, to house the spirit tablets of deceased kings and queens. The rite consists of the welcoming of the spirits, three offerings of wine and food, and their sending off, conducted by the royal descendants in ceremonial robes, accompanied by the Jongmyo jeryeak orchestra and the line dance ilmu. Its present music derives from compositions attributed to King Sejong in the fifteenth century. Suspended for periods during colonial rule and war, the rite is now performed annually by the Jeonju Yi Royal Family Association. The shrine is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the rite and music are listed as intangible heritage.

### Five Rites of the State (Gukjo orye ui) (1474)

- **Date:** 1474
- **Type:** Ritual code
- **Where written / held:** Hanseong (Seoul), Joseon
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Five%20Rites%20of%20the%20State%20%28Gukjo%20orye%20ui%29%20Joseon%20ritual%20officials)

Begun under King Sejong and completed by Sin Suk-ju, Jeong Cheok and others in 1474 under King Seongjong, the Gukjo orye ui prescribed, with illustrations of vessels, regalia and arrangements, every rite of the Korean state: sacrifices at the altars of earth and grain and the ancestral shrine, the royal wedding and coronation, the reception of Chinese envoys, military reviews and archery, and royal funerals and mourning. It adapted Chinese classical and Ming models to a Korean kingdom and remained the standard reference, with later supplements, until the end of the dynasty. Together with the household rites of Zhu Xi, it made Confucian ritual the organising framework of Joseon life.
