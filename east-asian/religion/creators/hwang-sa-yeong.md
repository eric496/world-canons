# Hwang Sa-yeong

*黃嗣永*

**1775 – 1801** · Korean  
Born: Hanseong (Seoul) · Died: Hanseong (Seoul)  
Traditions & eras: [Christianity in East Asia](../movements/christianity-east-asia.md)  

> Korean Catholic scholar whose secret silk letter of 1801 recorded the persecution of the early Korean church.

## Biography

Hwang Sa-yeong passed the civil examination at sixteen and married into the family of the scholar Jeong Yak-yong, through whom he encountered Catholicism, which Korean scholars had brought from Beijing in 1784 without any missionary. Korea's first Catholics were largely aristocratic Southerner (Namin) scholars studying 'Western learning'.

In the Sinyu persecution of 1801, when the Chinese priest Zhou Wenmo and some three hundred believers were killed, Hwang hid in a pottery cave and wrote a long letter on silk to the bishop of Beijing describing events and asking for Western military pressure on Korea. It was intercepted, and he was executed. The letter, now in the Vatican, is a key source and remains controversial in Korea.

## Key texts & works

### Silk Letter (Hwang Sa-yeong baekseo) (1801)

- **Date:** 1801
- **Type:** Secret letter
- **Where written / held:** Written in Jecheon, Joseon; now in the Vatican archives
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Silk%20Letter%20%28Hwang%20Sa-yeong%20baekseo%29%20Hwang%20Sa-yeong)

Hiding during the Sinyu persecution of 1801, Hwang Sa-yeong wrote in minute characters on a silk cloth about 62 by 38 centimetres, intended to be carried to Bishop Alexandre de Gouveia in Beijing. He described the martyrdom of the priest Zhou Wenmo and many believers, the history of the Korean church founded by lay scholars, and proposed that the Pope ask the Qing emperor to protect Korean Christians, or even that Western ships and troops be sent to force toleration. Seized before it left Korea, it led to his execution and intensified persecution. Recovered from the Joseon archives in 1894 and given to the Pope in 1925, it is a key source for early Korean Catholicism and remains controversial for its appeal to foreign force.
