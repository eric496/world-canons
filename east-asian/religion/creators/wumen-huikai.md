# Wumen Huikai

*無門慧開*

**1183 – 1260** · Chinese  
Born: Hangzhou · Died: Hangzhou  
Traditions & eras: [Chan, Seon and Zen](../movements/chan-zen-seon.md)  

> Southern Song Chan master who compiled the Gateless Gate, the most widely used kōan collection.

## Biography

Wumen Huikai, a monk of the Linji lineage, is said to have worked on the kōan 'Mu' for six years before awakening at the sound of a drum. He taught at a number of monasteries around Hangzhou and was honoured by the Southern Song court, which asked him to pray for rain.

In 1228 he published the Gateless Gate (Wumenguan), forty-eight cases with his own brief comments and verses. Its Japanese student Shinchi Kakushin brought it to Japan in 1254, where as the Mumonkan it became a central text of Rinzai training.

## Key texts & works

### Gateless Gate (Wumenguan) (1228)

[![Gateless Gate (Wumenguan)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2d/M%C5%ABmon-kan_01.jpg/1280px-M%C5%ABmon-kan_01.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C5%ABmon-kan_01.jpg)

<sub>Page of a Japanese edition of the Mumonkan Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:M%C5%ABmon-kan_01.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1228
- **Type:** Kōan collection
- **Where written / held:** Hangzhou
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Gateless%20Gate%20%28Wumenguan%29%20Wumen%20Huikai)

Wumen Huikai compiled the collection for his students in 1228, choosing short cases such as Zhaozhou's dog, Gutei's finger, Nanquan killing the cat and the Buddha holding up a flower, and adding a pithy comment and a four-line verse to each. His comment on the first case urges students to concentrate on 'Mu' with their whole body until doubt and understanding break open, a method linked to Dahui's 'phrase-contemplation'. Brought to Japan in 1254 by Shinchi Kakushin, the Mumonkan became a standard text of Rinzai Zen and, through twentieth-century translations, of Western Zen.
