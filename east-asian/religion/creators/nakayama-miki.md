# Nakayama Miki

*中山みき*

**1798 – 1887** · Japanese  
Born: Sanmaiden, Yamato Province · Died: Shōyashiki, Yamato Province  
Traditions & eras: [New religions](../movements/new-religions.md)  

> Farm wife who in 1838 declared herself the 'Shrine of God' and founded Tenrikyō, revered by followers as Oyasama.

## Biography

Miki married into the Nakayama, a farming family in Yamato, and raised six children. In 1838, during a healing rite for her son, she entered a trance and announced that God the Parent (Tenri-Ō-no-Mikoto) had taken her as his shrine. She gave away the family's wealth to the poor, began offering safe-childbirth blessings and healing, and from 1866 composed songs and dances.

She taught that humanity was created to live a 'joyous life' and that suffering comes from 'dust' on the mind. Arrested repeatedly by the authorities in her final years, she died in 1887; Tenrikyō teaches that she remains present at the Jiba in Tenri, the place of human origin. Recognised as a sect of Shinto in 1908 to gain legality, Tenrikyō later redefined itself as an independent religion.

### Songs for the Service (Mikagura-uta) (1866–1875)

- **Date:** 1866–1875
- **Type:** Liturgical songs
- **Where written / held:** Shōyashiki, Yamato Province
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Songs%20for%20the%20Service%20%28Mikagura-uta%29%20Nakayama%20Miki)

Beginning in 1866, Miki taught her followers songs composed in simple Japanese, numbering in all five sections, with hand movements and music played on traditional instruments. The central Kagura Service around the Kanrodai at the Jiba, performed by ten masked dancers, re-enacts the creation of humanity, while the Dance with Hand Movements is performed in all Tenrikyō churches. The songs speak of sweeping away the dust of the mind and building the joyous life. The authorities repeatedly arrested Miki and her followers for performing the service.

### Ofudesaki (Tip of the Writing Brush) (1869–1882)

- **Date:** 1869–1882
- **Type:** Revealed scripture in verse
- **Where written / held:** Shōyashiki, Yamato Province (Tenri, Nara)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ofudesaki%20%28Tip%20of%20the%20Writing%20Brush%29%20Nakayama%20Miki)

In seventeen sections of waka-form verse written over thirteen years, Miki, writing as the brush of God the Parent, explains God's intention to save humanity and bring about the 'Joyous Life', the significance of the Jiba as the place of human creation, the Kanrodai pillar to be built there, the sweeping away of the mind's 'dust', and the coming salvation dance. Initially read only within the community, it was suppressed by the authorities in the period of State Shinto and officially published by Tenrikyō only in 1928. With the Mikagura-uta and Osashizu it forms the three scriptures of the religion.
