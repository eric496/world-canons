# Rennyo

*蓮如*

**1415 – 1499** · Japanese  
Born: Kyoto · Died: Yamashina, Kyoto  
Traditions & eras: [Pure Land](../movements/pure-land.md)  

> Eighth head of the Honganji, whose pastoral letters turned Jōdo Shinshū into a mass movement.

## Biography

Rennyo, a descendant of Shinran, became head of the then small Honganji in 1457. Driven from Kyoto by Enryaku-ji monks, he settled at Yoshizaki in Echizen in 1471, where he organised followers into congregations (kō) that met to chant and discuss his letters. He later built the Yamashina Honganji near Kyoto.

His letters (ofumi) explained Shinran's teaching in plain language for village readers and were read aloud at gatherings. Under him the Honganji grew into one of the most powerful institutions in Japan; armed leagues of followers (ikkō ikki) took control of Kaga Province in 1488, though Rennyo urged obedience to authorities. He married five times and had 27 children.

## Key texts & works

### Letters (Ofumi) (1461–1498)

- **Date:** 1461–1498
- **Type:** Pastoral letters
- **Where written / held:** Yoshizaki, Echizen, and Yamashina, Kyoto
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Letters%20%28Ofumi%29%20Rennyo)

Rennyo wrote hundreds of short letters to followers and congregations in plain Japanese mixed script, explaining that one is saved at the moment of entrusting oneself to Amida, that the nenbutsu is an expression of gratitude, and that believers should respect gods, buddhas and secular authorities while holding faith in their hearts. His grandson Ennyo selected 80 into five fascicles, the Gojō ofumi (called Gobunshō in the Ōtani branch), which are read at Shin Buddhist services. The 'Letter on White Ashes', on the impermanence of life, is recited at funerals.
