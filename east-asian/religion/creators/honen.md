# Hōnen

*法然*

**1133 – 1212** · Japanese  
Born: Mimasaka Province · Died: Kyoto  
Traditions & eras: [Kamakura Buddhism](../movements/kamakura-buddhism.md), [Pure Land](../movements/pure-land.md)  

> Founder of the Jōdo (Pure Land) school, who taught that reciting Amida's name alone suffices for rebirth.

<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Hōnen, Kamakura period · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Takanobu-no-miei.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Hōnen, ordained as Genkū, was a son of a provincial warrior killed in a feud. He studied on Mount Hiei for decades and in 1175, reading Shandao's commentary, concluded that in the age of the decline of the Dharma ordinary people could be saved only by relying on Amida's vow through the spoken nenbutsu. He left the mountain to teach in Kyoto, attracting courtiers, warriors, women and the poor.

His teaching that the nenbutsu was the one practice chosen by Amida drew fierce opposition from Kōfuku-ji and Enryaku-ji. In 1207 the court banned exclusive nenbutsu, executed some disciples and exiled Hōnen and others, including Shinran. He was pardoned in 1211 and died in Kyoto the following year. Chion-in in Kyoto is the head temple of his Jōdo-shū.

### Passages on the Nenbutsu Selected in the Primal Vow (Senchaku hongan nenbutsu shū) (1198)

- **Date:** 1198
- **Type:** Doctrinal treatise
- **Where written / held:** Kyoto, written for the regent Kujō Kanezane
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Passages%20on%20the%20Nenbutsu%20Selected%20in%20the%20Primal%20Vow%20%28Senchaku%20hongan%20nenbutsu%20sh%C5%AB%29%20H%C5%8Dnen)

Written at the request of the former regent Kujō Kanezane, the Senchakushū in sixteen chapters quotes scriptures and Shandao's commentary, adding Hōnen's own explanations. It divides Buddhism into the difficult Path of Sages and the easy Pure Land path, and argues that Amida, in his eighteenth vow, selected the nenbutsu above all other practices because it is easy for everyone, so that other practices should be set aside. Hōnen allowed only close disciples to copy it during his life. After its publication it was attacked by the monk Myōe for denying the aspiration to enlightenment, and in 1227 Enryaku-ji monks burned its printing blocks. It is the basic text of Jōdo-shū.

### One-Sheet Document (Ichimai kishōmon) (1212)

- **Date:** 1212
- **Type:** Testament
- **Where written / held:** Kyoto; autograph at Konkaikōmyō-ji
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=One-Sheet%20Document%20%28Ichimai%20kish%C5%8Dmon%29%20H%C5%8Dnen)

Two days before his death in 1212, at a disciple's request, Hōnen wrote a short statement summarising his teaching: the nenbutsu he taught was not the contemplative practice of Chinese and Japanese scholars nor a fruit of learning, but simply saying 'Namu Amida Butsu' in the belief that one will be reborn; even those who have studied all the Buddha's teaching should become like the ignorant and simply recite. It is recited daily in Jōdo temples and is regarded as the essence of his teaching.
