# Kou Qianzhi

*寇謙之*

**365 – 448** · Chinese  
Born: Shanggu (Hebei) · Died: Pingcheng (Datong)  
Traditions & eras: [Celestial Masters and early religious Daoism](../movements/early-daoism.md)  

> Daoist reformer whose revelations led the Northern Wei emperor to make Daoism the state religion for a time.

## Biography

Kou Qianzhi belonged to a family of Celestial Master adherents and spent years in the mountains, eventually on Mount Song. In 415 and 423 he reported revelations from Lord Lao and from a divine descendant, Li Puwen, commissioning him to reform the Celestial Master church: to abolish its rice tax, sexual rites and hereditary offices and to restore proper ceremonies and precepts. In 424 he went to the Northern Wei capital, Pingcheng, with the support of the chief minister Cui Hao.

Emperor Taiwu accepted registers from him in 442 and was proclaimed a 'Perfect Lord of Great Peace', so that for a short period Daoism was the state cult of a dynasty, and the persecution of Buddhism in 446 occurred in this setting, though historians emphasise Cui Hao's role more than Kou's. The arrangement did not outlast them, but Kou's precept text survives in the Daoist canon as evidence of the movement's reform.

## Key texts & works

### Scripture of Precepts of Lord Lao (Laojun yinsong jiejing) (c. 415)

- **Date:** c. 415
- **Type:** Daoist precepts
- **Where written / held:** Mount Song, Henan; preserved in the Daoist Canon
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Scripture%20of%20Precepts%20of%20Lord%20Lao%20%28Laojun%20yinsong%20jiejing%29%20Kou%20Qianzhi)

Kou Qianzhi presented this text as part of a revelation from Lord Lao received on Mount Song in 415, to be chanted to a melody 'in the clouds'. Its precepts denounce the corruption of Celestial Master priests, ban the hereditary transmission of office, the rice levy and sexual rites, and set out rules for ordination, assemblies and penance, mirroring the precept literature of contemporary Buddhism. It is the main surviving witness of Kou's movement, which briefly made Daoism the official religion of the Northern Wei.
