# Tan Dun

*譚盾*

**1957** · Chinese  
Born: Simao, Changsha, Hunan · Died:   
Genres & eras: [Postwar & avant-garde](../movements/postwar-avant-garde.md)  

> Chinese composer of operas, concertos and film scores who blends Chinese traditions with the Western avant-garde.

## Biography

Tan Dun grew up in rural Hunan, was sent to work on a farm during the Cultural Revolution, and played in a Peking opera troupe before entering the reopened Central Conservatory in Beijing in 1978. In 1986 he moved to New York to study at Columbia University.

His music mixes Chinese instruments, ritual and folk sounds with orchestral and electronic resources, and sometimes uses water, paper and stones as instruments. His works include the opera Marco Polo (1996), Symphony 1997 for the Hong Kong handover, and the score for Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon (2000).

## Key works & forms

### Symphony 1997: Heaven Earth Mankind (1997)

- **Date:** 1997
- **Type:** Symphony with bianzhong, cello and children's choir
- **Where performed / held:** Hong Kong (premiere 1 July 1997)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Symphony%201997%3A%20Heaven%20Earth%20Mankind%20Tan%20Dun)

Written for the ceremonies marking the return of Hong Kong to China in 1997, the symphony combines a symphony orchestra, a children's choir, solo cello played by Yo-Yo Ma, and a replica of the bianzhong of Marquis Yi of Zeng, the bells of the fifth century BC. Its three parts, Heaven, Earth and Mankind, quote Cantonese opera and folk song and pay tribute to Chinese history and the new era. The piece shows how the ancient bell set became a national symbol, and how Tan Dun bridges ancient sound and modern orchestra. It is still in copyright.
