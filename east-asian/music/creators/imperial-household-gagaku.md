# Gagaku musicians of the Imperial court

*宮内庁式部職楽部*

**c. 701 – present** · Japanese  
Born: Nara, Kyoto, Tokyo · Died:   
Genres & eras: [Japanese gagaku & bugaku](../movements/gagaku.md)  

> The hereditary court musicians of Japan, from the Nara period Gagakuryō to today's Music Department of the Imperial Household Agency.

## Biography

Japan's Taihō code of 701 set up the Gagakuryō, a bureau of court music staffed by Japanese, Chinese and Korean musicians. In the Heian period the many imported styles were reorganised into music of the Left (Chinese and Indian origin) and of the Right (Korean origin), and hereditary families of musicians in Kyoto, Nara and Osaka (Tennōji) guarded the repertory. In 1870 they were united in Tokyo in what is now the Music Department (Gakubu) of the Imperial Household Agency.

The musicians play wind, string and percussion instruments, dance bugaku, sing Shinto and court songs, and since the Meiji period have also played Western music for state occasions. Their repertory of some ninety instrumental pieces and dances is transmitted largely by oral memorisation of solmisation syllables (shōga) before the instrument is touched.

### Ranryō-ō (Tang origin; Japanese performance from 8th century)

[![Ranryō-ō](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/Bugaku_mask_Ry%C5%8D%C5%8D%2C_Masumida_jinja.jpg/1280px-Bugaku_mask_Ry%C5%8D%C5%8D%2C_Masumida_jinja.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bugaku_mask_Ry%C5%8D%C5%8D%2C_Masumida_jinja.jpg)

<sub>Bugaku mask for Ryōō (Ranryō-ō), Masumida Shrine Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bugaku_mask_Ry%C5%8D%C5%8D%2C_Masumida_jinja.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** Tang origin; Japanese performance from 8th century
- **Type:** Bugaku dance
- **Where performed / held:** Imperial court and great shrines and temples
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ranry%C5%8D-%C5%8D%20Gagaku%20musicians%20of%20the%20Imperial%20court)

Ranryō-ō, the 'King of Lanling', is based on the story of Gao Changgong, a prince of the Northern Qi in sixth-century China, so handsome that he wore a terrifying mask to frighten his enemies. A dance about him existed at the Tang court and came to Japan, traditionally in the Nara period. In bugaku a single dancer in a gilded dragon-topped mask with a movable jaw, orange robes and a baton performs vigorous yet stately movements to music of the Left, the Chinese-derived repertory. Old masks survive at Tōdai-ji, Itsukushima and elsewhere. It is performed at court, at Shitennō-ji's Shōryō-e and at Kasuga Wakamiya, and remains one of the best loved pieces of the repertory.

### Etenraku (Heian period (Tang origin traditional))

[![Etenraku](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e5/Koto_to_sh%C5%8D_LCCN2008660815.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Koto_to_sh%C5%8D_LCCN2008660815.jpg)

<sub>Koto and shō, two instruments of the gagaku ensemble, woodblock print, Library of Congress Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Koto_to_sh%C5%8D_LCCN2008660815.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** Heian period (Tang origin traditional)
- **Type:** Gagaku instrumental piece
- **Where performed / held:** Imperial court, Kyoto; today the Imperial Household Agency, Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Etenraku%20Gagaku%20musicians%20of%20the%20Imperial%20court)

Etenraku, 'music of the heavens', belongs to the tōgaku repertory of Chinese origin, though its exact source is uncertain and some accounts credit Japanese courtiers with its composition. It is played by the full gagaku ensemble: the hichiriki oboe and ryūteki flute carry the melody, the shō mouth organ sustains clusters of tones, the biwa and koto mark the beats with broken chords and patterns, and the kakko, taiko and shōko drums frame each phrase. The pace is very slow and the texture shimmering, the result of centuries of gradual slowing of what was probably once lively banquet music. It exists in versions in three modes, and in the Edo period its melody was sung as the popular song 'Etenraku imayō' and adapted in koto music. In the twentieth century Hidemaro Konoe arranged it for Western orchestra, and it is often the first gagaku that listeners hear.

### Seigaiha (Heian period)

- **Date:** Heian period
- **Type:** Bugaku dance
- **Where performed / held:** Imperial court, Kyoto
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Seigaiha%20Gagaku%20musicians%20of%20the%20Imperial%20court)

Seigaiha, 'blue ocean waves', is a dance of the Left for two dancers in costumes embroidered with waves and plovers, its music traditionally said to be of Indian origin. In the seventh chapter of The Tale of Genji, Hikaru Genji and his friend Tō no Chūjō dance it before the emperor beneath autumn leaves, and Genji's beauty moves the audience to tears. The overlapping wave pattern on the costumes gave its name to a design used across Japanese art. The dance is rarely performed today because of its demanding staging, but it remains a symbol of Heian courtly elegance.
