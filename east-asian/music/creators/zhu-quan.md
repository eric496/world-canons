# Zhu Quan

*朱權*

**1378 – 1448** · Chinese  
Born: Nanjing · Died: Nanchang  
Genres & eras: [The guqin tradition](../movements/guqin-tradition.md), [Yuan zaju](../movements/yuan-zaju.md)  

> Ming prince, playwright and scholar who compiled the Shenqi mipu, the earliest surviving printed handbook of qin music.

## Biography

Zhu Quan was the seventeenth son of the Hongwu emperor, founder of the Ming, and was enfeoffed as Prince of Ning on the northern frontier. Drawn into his brother's usurpation of the throne in 1402, he was afterwards moved to Nanchang and kept away from politics, devoting himself to Daoism, scholarship and the arts.

He wrote zaju plays and an important guide to northern drama, the Taihe zhengyin pu, and in 1425 he published the Shenqi mipu (Handbook of Mysterious and Marvellous Secrets), gathering qin pieces including old ones handed down from the Song. It is the foundation of the modern qin repertory.

## Key works & forms

### Shenqi mipu (1425)

[![Shenqi mipu](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e1/Shenqi_Mipu_vol_3_pg_1.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shenqi_Mipu_vol_3_pg_1.jpg)

<sub>A page of qin tablature from the Shenqi mipu Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shenqi_Mipu_vol_3_pg_1.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1425
- **Type:** Qin handbook
- **Where performed / held:** Printed in Nanchang (1425)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Shenqi%20mipu%20Zhu%20Quan)

Prince Zhu Quan compiled the Handbook of Mysterious and Marvellous Secrets over twelve years and printed it in 1425. Its first volume, the 'Pieces from Remote Antiquity', preserves music he regarded as handed down from the Song and earlier, including Guangling san, Flowing Water and Plum Blossom, while later volumes contain more recent pieces. Each piece has a short preface about its origins and is written in the reduced tablature (jianzi pu) of qin notation, which combines parts of characters to show string, finger and stud. As the oldest large collection, it is the main source for reconstructions of early qin music and is the starting point for almost every history of the instrument.
