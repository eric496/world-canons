# Shin Jae-hyo

*신재효 (申在孝)*

**1812 – 1884** · Korean  
Born: Gochang, Jeolla Province · Died: Gochang  
Genres & eras: [Pansori & sanjo](../movements/pansori-sanjo.md)  

> Local official and patron who wrote down, edited and theorised pansori, and trained the first women singers.

## Biography

Shin Jae-hyo was a wealthy hereditary clerk in Gochang. He became the leading patron and teacher of pansori in the mid nineteenth century, housing and supporting singers in his home and, unusually, training female singers such as Jin Chae-seon, who performed before the regent Heungseon Daewongun in 1867.

He wrote down and revised the texts of six pansori stories, refining their language for educated listeners, and wrote short songs of his own. In Gwangdaega (Song of the Singer) he named the four qualities a singer needs: presence, words, voice and acting. His editions shaped the modern repertory, though some critics feel he removed earthy humour from the oral tradition.

## Key works & forms

### Gwangdaega (Song of the Singer) (c. 1870s)

- **Date:** c. 1870s
- **Type:** Short song (danga) and poetics
- **Where performed / held:** Gochang
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Gwangdaega%20%28Song%20of%20the%20Singer%29%20Shin%20Jae-hyo)

In Gwangdaega Shin Jae-hyo set out in verse what a pansori singer needs: first, presence (inmul); second, the text (sasol); third, a well-trained voice (deugeum); and fourth, acting and gesture (neoreumsae). He compared the master singers of his day to great Chinese poets. The song is the first piece of pansori criticism and, with his edited texts of six pansori, shows how the genre was becoming a recognised art for educated audiences in the late nineteenth century.
