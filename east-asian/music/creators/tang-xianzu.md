## Biography

Tang Xianzu passed the highest examination in 1583 and served as an official until he retired in 1598 after clashes with superiors. At home in Linchuan he wrote the four 'Linchuan dreams', plays in which dreams reveal the truth of feeling.

His plays were written in the southern chuanqi form and were adapted for kunqu singing, which Tang himself did not entirely favour. The Peony Pavilion became the core of the kunqu repertory and remains its most performed work; his literary achievement is treated in the literature section.

## Key works & forms

### The Peony Pavilion (in kunqu performance) (1598)

[![The Peony Pavilion (in kunqu performance)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/40/Mudan_Ting_Huanhun_Ji.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mudan_Ting_Huanhun_Ji.jpg)

<sub>Page from an early edition of The Peony Pavilion (Mudan ting huanhun ji) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mudan_Ting_Huanhun_Ji.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1598
- **Type:** Kunqu opera
- **Where performed / held:** Linchuan; staged across Jiangnan from c. 1600
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Peony%20Pavilion%20%28in%20kunqu%20performance%29%20Tang%20Xianzu)

Tang Xianzu's fifty-five-scene chuanqi tells of Du Liniang, who falls in love with a young man in a dream, pines away and dies, and is brought back to life by her love. Within a few decades it was being sung to kunqu melodies, and editors adjusted its text to fit the rules of the music, which Tang resented. On the stage it lives mainly in excerpts (zhezixi), above all 'Wandering in the Garden, Interrupted Dream' (Youyuan jingmeng), in which the dan heroine, with fan and long sleeves, discovers the spring garden and her own longing. Mei Lanfang and later kunqu stars made these scenes famous, and modern productions have staged the whole play over several nights, including Bai Xianyong's 'Young Lovers' version of 2004 and Peter Sellars's Western adaptation of 1998. The play as literature is treated in the literature section.
