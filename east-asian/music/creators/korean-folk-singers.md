# Korean folk singers

*민요 가수*

**traditional – present** · Korean  
Genres & eras: [Pansori & sanjo](../movements/pansori-sanjo.md)  

> The anonymous singers who created and passed on Korea's folk songs (minyo), including the many regional forms of Arirang.

## Biography

Korean folk songs were the music of farmers, fishermen, women at work and travelling entertainers, sung in regional styles from the northwest to Jeolla and the eastern mountains. They were transmitted orally and varied by every singer and village.

In the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries professional singers, gramophone records and the radio spread certain songs nationally, above all Arirang, which became a symbol of Korean identity at home and in the diaspora.

## Key works & forms

### Arirang (traditional; standard version 1926)

- **Date:** traditional; standard version 1926
- **Type:** Folk song
- **Where performed / held:** Throughout Korea
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Arirang%20Korean%20folk%20singers)

Arirang is not a single song but a family of folk songs sharing the refrain 'Arirang, arirang, arariyo' and the image of crossing the Arirang pass. Regional forms such as Jeongseon Arirang, Jindo Arirang and Miryang Arirang have very different melodies and texts. The standard 'Bonjo Arirang' became known nationwide after it was used in Na Un-gyu's silent film Arirang (1926), a story of resistance under Japanese rule. The song became a symbol of longing, separation and national identity, sung by Koreans in the North and the South and abroad, and the combined Korean team marched to it at the Olympics. It was inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Heritage list by South Korea in 2012 and by North Korea in 2014.
