# Hong Sheng

*洪昇*

**1645 – 1704** · Chinese  
Born: Qiantang (Hangzhou) · Died: Wuzhen (Zhejiang)  
Genres & eras: [Ming chuanqi & kunqu](../movements/kunqu.md)  

> Qing playwright of The Palace of Eternal Youth, one of the last great kunqu masterpieces.

## Biography

Hong Sheng came from a scholarly Hangzhou family and spent long years in Beijing as a student of the Imperial Academy without gaining office. He worked for over a decade on his play about Emperor Xuanzong and Yang Guifei, completing it in 1688. A private performance in 1689 during a period of state mourning led to his expulsion from the Academy and the punishment of many guests. He drowned in 1704 after falling from a boat while drunk.

Together with Kong Shangren's The Peach Blossom Fan, his play marks the high point of kunqu in the early Qing; the pairing is known as 'Southern Hong and Northern Kong'. Its arias are noted for their musical craft and remain staples of the kunqu stage.

## Key works & forms

### The Palace of Eternal Youth (Changsheng dian) (1688)

- **Date:** 1688
- **Type:** Kunqu opera
- **Where performed / held:** Beijing
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Palace%20of%20Eternal%20Youth%20%28Changsheng%20dian%29%20Hong%20Sheng)

Hong Sheng retold the love of Emperor Xuanzong and Yang Guifei, the An Lushan rebellion and her forced death at Mawei, and the lovers' final reunion in the palace of the moon, drawing on Bai Juyi's 'Song of Everlasting Regret' and Bai Pu's Yuan play Rain on the Paulownia Tree. Completed in 1688 after more than a decade of revision, it was admired for the musical correctness of its arias, which were set with the help of the musician Xu Lin. A performance in 1689 during the mourning period for the empress dowager led to Hong Sheng's ruin, but the play remained popular, and scenes such as 'The Dance of the Rainbow Skirt' and 'Bidding Farewell to the Grave' are still standards of kunqu.
