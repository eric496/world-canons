## Biography

Danjūrō II took over the family name in 1704 after his father's murder, still a youth. He grew into the most celebrated actor of his day in Edo, said to earn the then-unheard-of salary of a thousand ryō a year. Late in life he took the name Ichikawa Ebizō.

He softened his father's bombast with elegance, creating the swaggering yet graceful hero of Sukeroku in 1713, and established many of the forms of the family repertory. Prints by the Torii school record his roles, and the family's later actors looked back to him as a model.

## Key works & forms

### Sukeroku (1713)

[![Sukeroku](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4f/Sukeroku%2C_from_the_series_The_Eighteen_Great_Kabuki_Plays.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sukeroku%2C_from_the_series_The_Eighteen_Great_Kabuki_Plays.jpg)

<sub>Sukeroku, from the print series The Eighteen Great Kabuki Plays Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sukeroku%2C_from_the_series_The_Eighteen_Great_Kabuki_Plays.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1713
- **Type:** Kabuki play
- **Where performed / held:** Yamamura-za, Edo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sukeroku%20Ichikawa%20Danj%C5%ABr%C5%8D%20II)

First staged in 1713 and refined over the following decades, Sukeroku presents Hanakawado Sukeroku, the picture of Edo style in a black kimono, purple headband and yellow socks, who picks quarrels in the Yoshiwara in order to make men draw their swords. He is really the warrior Soga no Gorō, searching for a sword stolen from his family, and his rival the arrogant samurai Ikyū has it. His lover is the great courtesan Agemaki. The play is famous for its spectacular entrance along the hanamichi, accompanied by the singing of a special style of jōruri called katōbushi, and for its portrait of Edo city culture. It is one of the Kabuki Jūhachiban.
