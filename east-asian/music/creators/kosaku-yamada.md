# Kōsaku Yamada

*山田耕筰*

**1886 – 1965** · Japanese  
Born: Tokyo · Died: Tokyo  
Genres & eras: [Western-style composers](../movements/modern-composers.md)  

> Composer and conductor who founded Japan's first professional orchestra and wrote the songs Akatombo and Karatachi no hana.

## Biography

Yamada Kōsaku studied at the Tokyo Music School and from 1910 to 1913 in Berlin, where he wrote the first Japanese symphony. Back in Japan he conducted, founded the forerunner of the NHK Symphony Orchestra in 1925 with Hidemaro Konoe, and composed operas, orchestral works and some 1,600 songs and choral pieces. He worked with the poet Kitahara Hakushū on many songs. During the war he led wartime musical organisations, which drew criticism after 1945.

His songs seek to fit melody to the natural accents of Japanese speech, and they made him the most popular Japanese composer of his generation. Akatombo (Red Dragonfly) is among the best-loved Japanese songs.

## Key works & forms

### Akatombo (1927)

- **Date:** 1927
- **Type:** Song
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Akatombo%20K%C5%8Dsaku%20Yamada)

Miki Rofū's poem remembers seeing red dragonflies at sunset as a child, carried on the back of a young nursemaid who later married and left. Yamada's 1927 setting follows the pitch accents of the Japanese words closely in a gentle pentatonic melody. Recorded and sung in schools, it became one of the most familiar songs in Japan, often played at dusk by public loudspeakers. The work is still in copyright.
