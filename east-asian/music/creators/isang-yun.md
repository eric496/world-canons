# Isang Yun

*윤이상 (尹伊桑)*

**1917 – 1995** · Korean-German  
Born: Tongyeong, Gyeongsang Province · Died: Berlin  
Genres & eras: [Postwar & avant-garde](../movements/postwar-avant-garde.md)  

> Korean-born composer in Germany who forged a modernist style from Korean court music; abducted by South Korean agents in 1967.

## Biography

Isang Yun studied music in Osaka and Tokyo, was imprisoned by the Japanese for resistance activity during the war, and after 1945 taught in Tongyeong and Busan. In 1956 he went to study in Paris and Berlin and settled in West Germany. In 1967 South Korean intelligence agents abducted him from Berlin and he was sentenced to life imprisonment in Seoul on charges of spying for North Korea; international protests by musicians led to his release in 1969. He took German citizenship and taught at the Berlin Hochschule für Musik until 1985. He never returned to the South.

Yun's technique of the 'main tone' (Hauptton), in which a single sustained pitch is ornamented, bent and coloured, was inspired by Korean court music and Daoist ideas. His output includes operas, five symphonies, concertos and chamber music. Réak (1966) for orchestra was his international breakthrough.

## Key works & forms

### Réak (1966)

- **Date:** 1966
- **Type:** Orchestral work
- **Where performed / held:** Donaueschingen Festival
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=R%C3%A9ak%20Isang%20Yun)

Réak takes its title from the Korean word for ritual music (yeak), and was written for the Donaueschingen Festival of 1966. Its sustained tones, swelling and decaying with glissandi and vibrato, and its punctuating clapper strokes evoke the opening of Korean court and ritual music, while the language is entirely that of the European avant-garde. It established Yun's international reputation shortly before his abduction from West Berlin in 1967. It is still in copyright.
