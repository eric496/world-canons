# Michio Miyagi

*宮城道雄*

**1894 – 1956** · Japanese  
Born: Kobe · Died: Kariya, Aichi  
Genres & eras: [Western-style composers](../movements/modern-composers.md), [Koto & shamisen music](../movements/koto-shamisen.md)  

> Blind koto virtuoso and composer who modernised Japanese traditional music, author of Haru no Umi.

## Biography

Miyagi lost his sight in childhood and was apprenticed to a koto teacher of the Ikuta school in Kobe. As a teenager he lived in Korea, where his family had moved, and there wrote his first piece. He moved to Tokyo in 1917 and with the shakuhachi player Yoshida Seifū and others led the 'new Japanese music' (shin nihon ongaku) movement. He taught at the Tokyo Music School and invented new instruments, including the seventeen-string bass koto. He died in 1956 after falling from a night train.

Miyagi wrote over four hundred works that combine traditional koto technique with Western forms and harmony, and he was also a fine essayist. Haru no Umi became famous internationally after he recorded it with the French violinist Renée Chemet in 1932.

## Key works & forms

### Haru no Umi (1929)

- **Date:** 1929
- **Type:** Koto and shakuhachi duet
- **Where performed / held:** Tokyo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Haru%20no%20Umi%20Michio%20Miyagi)

Miyagi wrote Haru no Umi for the Imperial poetry reading of 1930, whose theme was 'The Sea near Rocks', inspired by memories of the Inland Sea at Tomonoura. In three parts, it pairs the koto with the shakuhachi in a lively, transparent dialogue suggesting waves, birds and the songs of fishermen, using Western-influenced form and harmony. After Miyagi recorded it with the French violinist Renée Chemet in 1932, playing violin in place of shakuhachi, it became internationally known. It is played at Japanese New Year and is one of the most recorded works for traditional instruments. The work is still in copyright.
