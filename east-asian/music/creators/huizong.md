## Biography

Zhao Ji reigned from 1100 to 1126. Devoted to the arts and to Daoism, he ran an academy of painting, collected antiquities and developed the 'slender gold' calligraphy. His government was weakened by factionalism and costly projects, and in 1127 the Jurchen Jin captured the capital Kaifeng and carried him north, where he died in captivity.

In music Huizong set up the Dashengfu, a Bureau of Great Brilliance, to create new ritual music based on ancient models. Bells were cast in 1105, modelled on an ancient bronze bell found in Hubei, with pitches set by the length of the emperor's own fingers according to an adviser's theory. Captured by the Jin, many of the Dasheng bells were carried off, and some later reached the Korean court along with the ritual music Huizong had sent to Goryeo in 1116.

## Key works & forms

### Dasheng bells (Dasheng bianzhong) (1105)

- **Date:** 1105
- **Type:** Bronze bell set
- **Where performed / held:** Surviving bells in the Palace Museum, Beijing, Liaoning Provincial Museum and elsewhere
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dasheng%20bells%20%28Dasheng%20bianzhong%29%20Emperor%20Huizong%20of%20Song)

In 1104 Huizong set up a bureau to create new ritual music, the Dasheng yue (Music of Great Brilliance), on the basis of ancient models. Its centrepiece was a new series of bronze bells cast in 1105, modelled on six ancient bells said to have been found at Yingtian, and inscribed with their pitch names. The project expressed the Song belief that correct music would bring cosmic harmony and a prosperous state. When the Jurchen took Kaifeng in 1127 the bells were carried north and later reused by Jin and Yuan courts; dozens survive today. In 1116 Huizong had sent a complete set of ritual instruments and music to the Goryeo king of Korea, the origin of Korean aak.
