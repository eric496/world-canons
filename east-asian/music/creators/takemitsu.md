# Tōru Takemitsu

*武満徹*

**1930 – 1996** · Japanese  
Born: Tokyo · Died: Tokyo  
Genres & eras: [Postwar & avant-garde](../movements/postwar-avant-garde.md)  

> The most internationally celebrated Japanese composer, known for his sensuous orchestral music, film scores and November Steps.

## Biography

Takemitsu was largely self-taught. After war service he discovered Western music through American forces radio and in 1951 co-founded the experimental arts group Jikken Kōbō. His Requiem for Strings (1957) was praised by Igor Stravinsky on a visit to Japan. He wrote more than ninety film scores, including for Kurosawa's Ran and Teshigahara's Woman in the Dunes.

Influenced by Debussy, Messiaen and John Cage, his music values timbre, silence and the individual sound, which he related to Japanese aesthetics. After resisting Japanese tradition in his youth he wrote November Steps (1967) for biwa, shakuhachi and orchestra, and later works for gagaku. He is remembered for luminous orchestral works such as A Flock Descends into the Pentagonal Garden and his many pieces with 'water' and 'garden' imagery.

## Key works & forms

### November Steps (1967)

- **Date:** 1967
- **Type:** Concerto for biwa, shakuhachi and orchestra
- **Where performed / held:** Lincoln Center, New York (New York Philharmonic, Seiji Ozawa)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=November%20Steps%20T%C5%8Dru%20Takemitsu)

Commissioned for the 125th anniversary of the New York Philharmonic and premiered in November 1967 under Seiji Ozawa, November Steps features Tsuruta Kinshi on satsuma biwa and Yokoyama Katsuya on shakuhachi. Takemitsu did not try to blend the two worlds: the orchestra, divided into two mirrored groups, frames long passages in which the traditional instruments play alone, with their noises, breath and silence. The title refers to the eleven 'steps' or sections, playing on the Japanese term dan. The work made Takemitsu internationally famous and helped launch a wider interest in Japanese instruments among composers. It is still in copyright.
