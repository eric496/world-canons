# Ahn Eak-tai

*안익태 (安益泰)*

**1906 – 1965** · Korean  
Born: Pyongyang · Died: Mallorca, Spain  
Genres & eras: [Western-style composers](../movements/modern-composers.md)  

> Composer and conductor of the Korea Fantasy, whose melody became the South Korean national anthem.

## Biography

Ahn Eak-tai studied cello in Tokyo and then in the United States, and from 1936 lived in Europe, studying with Richard Strauss and conducting orchestras in Germany, Italy and Spain. He settled in Mallorca, founding its symphony orchestra, and returned to Korea for festivals after independence.

His Korea Fantasy, completed in 1938, ends with a choral setting of the Aegukga text that became South Korea's national anthem. Revelations since the 2000s that he conducted for Nazi and Japanese wartime cultural events have made him a controversial figure in Korea.

## Key works & forms

### Korea Fantasy (1938)

- **Date:** 1938
- **Type:** Symphonic fantasy with chorus
- **Where performed / held:** Dublin (premiere, Radio Éireann Orchestra, 1938)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Korea%20Fantasy%20Ahn%20Eak-tai)

The Korea Fantasy is a symphonic poem in several sections depicting Korean history from its foundation through the suffering of the Japanese occupation to liberation, closing with a choral setting of the Aegukga (Patriotic Song). Ahn had written that melody in 1935 to replace the Scottish tune 'Auld Lang Syne' to which the text had been sung. It was adopted by the provisional government in exile and later by the Republic of Korea. The work was revised several times, adding a section on the Korean War. Controversy over Ahn's wartime career has led to debates about the anthem. It is still in copyright.
