# Mei Lanfang

*梅蘭芳*

**1894 – 1961** · Chinese  
Born: Beijing · Died: Beijing  
Genres & eras: [Peking opera](../movements/peking-opera.md)  

> The most famous Peking opera performer of the twentieth century, a male player of female roles who brought the art to Japan, the United States and the Soviet Union.

## Biography

Mei Lanfang was born into a Beijing family of actors and trained from childhood in the dan, female roles. By the 1910s he was a star, and with the writer Qi Rushan he created new plays and dances and revived older ones, enriching the qingyi role with acting and movement. He toured Japan in 1919, the United States in 1930 and the Soviet Union in 1935, where his performances impressed Stanislavski, Eisenstein and Brecht. During the Japanese occupation he grew a moustache and refused to perform.

His elegant style, the Mei school, set the standard for dan performers, and works such as Farewell My Concubine and The Drunken Concubine remain associated with him. After 1949 he became head of the China Peking Opera Academy.

### The Drunken Concubine (Guifei zuijiu) (Qing; Mei Lanfang version 1914 onward)

- **Date:** Qing; Mei Lanfang version 1914 onward
- **Type:** Peking opera
- **Where performed / held:** Beijing
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Drunken%20Concubine%20%28Guifei%20zuijiu%29%20Mei%20Lanfang)

The short opera shows Yang Guifei, who has prepared a banquet for Emperor Xuanzong only to learn that he has gone to another consort. Hurt, she drinks alone, sinking into intoxication in a famous series of dance movements, including backbends to drink from a cup on a tray. The play existed in the nineteenth century, and Mei Lanfang learned it from the actor Lu Sanbao, then refined it over decades, removing vulgar elements and stressing the heroine's loneliness. It became the signature work of the Mei school.

### Farewell My Concubine (Bawang bie ji) (1922)

- **Date:** 1922
- **Type:** Peking opera
- **Where performed / held:** Beijing
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Farewell%20My%20Concubine%20%28Bawang%20bie%20ji%29%20Mei%20Lanfang)

Surrounded by the Han army at Gaixia and hearing the songs of Chu from the enemy camp, the defeated King of Chu, Xiang Yu, drinks with his beloved consort Yu Ji. She dances for him with two swords, then kills herself so that he can fight free. Mei Lanfang created the play in 1922 with the writer Qi Rushan and the actor Yang Xiaolou as Xiang Yu, based on older plays about the same events. Yu Ji's sword dance was one of his most famous creations. It became one of the most popular Peking operas and gave its name to Chen Kaige's film of 1993. It is still in copyright.
