# Hwang Byungki

*황병기 (黃秉冀)*

**1936 – 2018** · Korean  
Born: Seoul · Died: Seoul  
Genres & eras: [Postwar & avant-garde](../movements/postwar-avant-garde.md)  

> Gayageum virtuoso and the leading composer of new music for Korean traditional instruments.

## Biography

Hwang Byungki began studying gayageum as a schoolboy during the Korean War in Busan, while training in law at Seoul National University. He taught at Seoul National University and Ewha Womans University, toured internationally as a soloist and was the first Korean musician to record gayageum music abroad in quantity.

Starting with Forest (1963) he wrote new pieces for the gayageum in a lyrical, often meditative style that drew on Silla and court traditions as well as modern techniques, including the avant-garde The Labyrinth (1975). Chimhyangmu (1974) is his best-known work. He did more than anyone to create a contemporary repertory for the instrument.

## Key works & forms

### Chimhyangmu (1974)

- **Date:** 1974
- **Type:** Gayageum solo with janggu
- **Where performed / held:** Seoul
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Chimhyangmu%20Hwang%20Byungki)

Chimhyangmu was inspired by Buddhist art of the Silla period and the imagined dances of the heavenly beings depicted on it. Hwang used a special tuning and the full resources of the gayageum, with the janggu drum, to create a piece that moves from meditative opening to rhythmic dance. It became one of the most frequently performed works for gayageum, a model for later Korean composers writing for traditional instruments. It is still in copyright.
