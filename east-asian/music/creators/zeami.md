# Zeami Motokiyo

*世阿弥*

**c. 1363 – c. 1443** · Japanese  
Born:  · Died: Sado or Yamato  
Genres & eras: [Noh & kyōgen](../movements/noh-kyogen.md)  
Also in: [Literature](../../literature/creators/zeami.md)  

> Actor, playwright and theorist who gave Noh its classic form and its aesthetic of yūgen.

## Biography

Zeami was the son of Kan'ami and from boyhood a favourite of the shogun Ashikaga Yoshimitsu. He succeeded to the leadership of the troupe in 1384 and spent his career refining Noh for an audience of warriors and courtiers. Under later shoguns he lost favour; his son and heir Motomasa died young, and in 1434 the shogun Yoshinori exiled him to the island of Sado. His last years are obscure.

Zeami wrote or revised many of the finest plays, including Takasago, Izutsu and Atsumori, and created the 'dream' (mugen) Noh in which a ghost relives the past. His secret treatises, beginning with Fūshikaden, discuss acting, music and the 'flower' of performance. Plays and treatises are treated more fully in the literature section.

- **Date:** early 15th century
- **Type:** Noh play
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Takasago%20Zeami%20Motokiyo)

A priest travelling from Kyushu to the capital stops at Takasago Bay, where an old couple sweep beneath a pine. They explain that this pine and the one at Sumiyoshi across the bay are a married pair, though far apart, and that pines stand for the eternity of Japanese poetry. Revealed as the spirits of the pines, they invite him to Sumiyoshi, where the god of Sumiyoshi appears in the second half and dances a vigorous dance under the moon. Takasago is the model of the first category of Noh, plays of gods, which opened a full programme, and it shows Zeami's art of weaving classical poetry into the text. A passage from it, 'Takasago ya', was long chanted at weddings, and the image of the old couple is a common New Year's decoration.
