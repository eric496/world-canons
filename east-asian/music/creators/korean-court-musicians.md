# Korean court musicians

*궁중 악사*

**c. 1116 – present** · Korean  
Genres & eras: [Korean aak & hyangak](../movements/korean-court-music.md)  

> The musicians of the Goryeo and Joseon court music offices, and of today's National Gugak Center, who transmitted Korea's court repertory.

## Biography

The court music of Korea was performed from Goryeo times by musicians of a court music office, in Joseon called the Jangagwon, divided into players of ritual aak and of native hyangak and Tang-derived dangak. They were of low social status but highly trained, and court treatises such as the Akhak gwebeom (1493) recorded their instruments, costumes and dances.

After the end of the Korean Empire the institution survived as the Yi Royal Household Music Department under Japanese rule, and in 1951 it became the National Gugak Center, which today preserves and performs the Jongmyo music, Sujecheon and the other court pieces.

### Sujecheon (origin c. 7th century (traditional); present form Joseon)

- **Date:** origin c. 7th century (traditional); present form Joseon
- **Type:** Court instrumental piece
- **Where performed / held:** Korean court; National Gugak Center, Seoul
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sujecheon%20Korean%20court%20musicians)

Sujecheon is believed to derive from a song of the Baekje kingdom, Jeongeupsa, about a woman who climbs a hill to await her husband's return, though this tradition is uncertain. Transmitted as instrumental court music, it was played for royal banquets and processions, and it is often described as the oldest music in the Korean repertory still performed. It is played by a wind-dominated ensemble led by the piri oboe, with daegeum flute, haegeum fiddle, ajaeng and drums, in a free, extremely slow rhythm in which each phrase swells and fades. Its grandeur has made it a symbol of the Korean court tradition, and it is often played at state occasions.

### Akhak gwebeom (1493)

[![Akhak gwebeom](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/Akhak_Gwebeom_1.jpg/1280px-Akhak_Gwebeom_1.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Akhak_Gwebeom_1.jpg)

<sub>A copy of the Akhak gwebeom Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Akhak_Gwebeom_1.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1493
- **Type:** Music treatise
- **Where performed / held:** Compiled at the Joseon court by Seong Hyeon and others
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Akhak%20gwebeom%20Korean%20court%20musicians)

Commissioned by King Seongjong and completed in 1493 by Seong Hyeon and other officials, the Akhak gwebeom describes the theory, instruments, performances, dances, costumes and stage properties of the court's ritual aak, Chinese-derived dangak and native hyangak. Its illustrations of every instrument with measurements made it the reference for reconstructing instruments after the Japanese invasions of the 1590s destroyed many, and it is still used by makers today. It also records the words of Goryeo songs that would otherwise be lost. It is one of the most important sources for the history of Korean music.
