# Biwa storytellers of the Heike

*琵琶法師*

**c. 1200 – c. 1600** · Japanese  
Genres & eras: [Biwa narrative & Buddhist chant](../movements/biwa-narrative.md)  
Also in: [Literature](../../literature/creators/heike-storytellers.md)  

> The blind lute-playing monks (biwa hōshi) who composed and transmitted The Tale of the Heike by chanting it to the biwa.

## Biography

From the thirteenth century blind performers in monk's dress travelled and performed for courtiers and warriors, chanting episodes of the Genpei war to the biwa lute. The Tale of the Heike grew through their performances, and in 1371 the master Kakuichi dictated an authoritative text. They were organised into a guild, the Tōdōza, which later included blind koto and shamisen musicians.

Their musical style, heikyoku, alternates speech-like recitation and melodic passages with short lute interludes. It shaped the vocal music of Noh and later narrative song, and a few traditions of heikyoku have survived into modern times through careful transmission.

## Key works & forms

### Heikyoku: chanting The Tale of the Heike (c. 1200; Kakuichi text 1371)

- **Date:** c. 1200; Kakuichi text 1371
- **Type:** Narrative recitation with biwa
- **Where performed / held:** Kyoto and the provinces
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Heikyoku%3A%20chanting%20The%20Tale%20of%20the%20Heike%20Biwa%20storytellers%20of%20the%20Heike)

The Tale of the Heike was meant to be heard. Blind biwa hōshi chanted its episodes, the rise and fall of the Taira, the deaths of young warriors, the drowning of the child emperor, in a style that alternates almost spoken lines with melodic formulas and short lute interludes, each formula with its own name and expressive purpose. The text that the master Kakuichi dictated in 1371 became the standard. The art was organised in the blind guild, the Tōdōza, and in the Edo period was taught to samurai as a refined pastime. Its formulas and vocal style shaped Noh chant and the later jōruri of the puppet theatre. Today only a handful of performers carry on traditional heikyoku, though many modern biwa players draw on it.
