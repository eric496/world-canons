# Tan Xinpei

*譚鑫培*

**1847 – 1917** · Chinese  
Born: Jiangxia, Hubei · Died: Beijing  
Genres & eras: [Peking opera](../movements/peking-opera.md)  

> The leading Peking opera singer of the late Qing, master of the laosheng role of older men and star of China's first film.

## Biography

Tan Xinpei was the son of an opera actor and trained in Beijing as a military role specialist before switching to the laosheng type of mature male characters. He studied with Cheng Changgeng and Yu Sansheng and in 1890 became an actor for the imperial palace, where the Empress Dowager Cixi was an ardent patron.

His smooth, melancholy singing created the Tan school, the most influential style of laosheng singing, and nearly every later laosheng singer descends from it. In 1905 he was filmed performing scenes from Dingjunshan, the first Chinese film, and his gramophone recordings preserve his voice.

## Key works & forms

### Dingjunshan (1905 (film))

- **Date:** 1905 (film)
- **Type:** Peking opera / first Chinese film
- **Where performed / held:** Fengtai Photo Studio, Beijing
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dingjunshan%20Tan%20Xinpei)

Dingjunshan (Mount Dingjun) is a Peking opera about the aged general Huang Zhong, who defeats the Wei commander Xiahou Yuan in 219 and proves that age has not weakened him, a showpiece for laosheng singers. In 1905 Ren Qingtai of the Fengtai photo studio in Beijing filmed Tan Xinpei performing excerpts of the fighting scenes, the first film made in China. The short silent film was lost, possibly in a fire, but stills survive, and the event is celebrated as the birth of Chinese cinema.
