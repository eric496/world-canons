# Guqin masters (traditional)

*古琴家*

**c. 500 BC – present** · Chinese  
Genres & eras: [The guqin tradition](../movements/guqin-tradition.md)  

> The long, largely anonymous line of qin players who composed, transmitted and reinterpreted the instrument's classic repertory.

## Biography

Most of the qin repertory has no reliable author. Pieces are attached to legendary figures such as Boya, Confucius or Cai Yong, but they reach us through handbooks compiled from the fifteenth century onward by players who edited, expanded and sometimes recomposed them. Each handbook gives its own version, so a single title can survive in dozens of forms.

Qin players were usually amateurs in the old sense, scholars and monks who played for themselves or a few friends. The tradition was carried into modern times by teachers such as Guan Pinghu, whose reconstructions of old tablatures are widely played today, and the art of the qin was inscribed by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2003 (proclaimed) and 2008 (listed).

### High Mountains and Flowing Water (Gaoshan liushui) (legend c. 5th century BC; score 1425)

[![High Mountains and Flowing Water (Gaoshan liushui)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/32/ZhongNiShi.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ZhongNiShi.jpg)

<sub>A guqin of the Zhongni shape Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ZhongNiShi.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** legend c. 5th century BC; score 1425
- **Type:** Guqin piece
- **Where performed / held:** Earliest score in the Shenqi mipu (1425)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=High%20Mountains%20and%20Flowing%20Water%20%28Gaoshan%20liushui%29%20Guqin%20masters%20%28traditional%29)

The Liezi tells of the qin player Boya, whose friend Zhong Ziqi understood exactly what he was thinking as he played, saying 'how lofty, like Mount Tai' or 'how vast, like rivers'. When Zhong died, Boya broke his strings and never played again, and zhiyin, 'one who knows the sound', became the Chinese word for a true friend. In the Shenqi mipu of 1425 the music appears as a pair of pieces, High Mountains and Flowing Water, and later handbooks expanded them. The version of Flowing Water from the nineteenth-century Sichuan school, with its famous passage of rolling and sliding imitating rushing water, was played by Guan Pinghu in a recording chosen for the Voyager Golden Record in 1977. A guzheng piece of the same name is a different work.

### Plum Blossom Three Variations (Meihua sannong) (attributed 4th century; score 1425)

- **Date:** attributed 4th century; score 1425
- **Type:** Guqin piece
- **Where performed / held:** Earliest score in the Shenqi mipu (1425)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Plum%20Blossom%20Three%20Variations%20%28Meihua%20sannong%29%20Guqin%20masters%20%28traditional%29)

The piece is traditionally derived from a flute melody played by the Eastern Jin general Huan Yi, but the first score is in the Shenqi mipu of 1425. Its name comes from a refrain repeated three times, each time at a different position on the instrument, in the bell-like harmonics of the qin, evoking plum blossoms that defy the cold. The plum, flowering at the end of winter, symbolised integrity in adversity, and the piece became one of the most popular in the repertory, also arranged for the xiao flute and other instruments.
