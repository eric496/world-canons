## Biography

Danjūrō I made his debut in Edo around 1673 playing the strongman Sakata Kintoki in red and black make-up, wielding an axe, a performance often counted as the birth of aragoto, the 'rough style'. He became the leading actor of Edo and wrote plays under the name Mimasuya Hyōgo. In 1704 he was stabbed to death on stage at the Ichimura-za by a fellow actor.

His superhuman heroes, painted faces (kumadori) and stylised poses (mie) created the characteristic look of Edo kabuki, and his descendants have carried his name for over three centuries. Shibaraku and other plays in his style were later included in the Kabuki Jūhachiban, the family's eighteen great plays.

## Key works & forms

### Shibaraku (1697)

[![Shibaraku](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b6/Kuniyoshi_Utagawa%2C_Japan%2C_The_actor.jpg/1280px-Kuniyoshi_Utagawa%2C_Japan%2C_The_actor.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kuniyoshi_Utagawa%2C_Japan%2C_The_actor.jpg)

<sub>Utagawa Kuniyoshi, woodblock print of an actor in Shibaraku Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kuniyoshi_Utagawa%2C_Japan%2C_The_actor.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1697
- **Type:** Kabuki play
- **Where performed / held:** Nakamura-za, Edo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Shibaraku%20Ichikawa%20Danj%C5%ABr%C5%8D%20I)

Shibaraku, 'Just a moment!', began as a scene that Danjūrō I inserted into the annual opening (kaomise) plays of Edo theatres from 1697. Just as a villainous lord is about to execute innocent people, a voice cries out from the back of the theatre and the hero enters along the hanamichi walkway in vast persimmon-coloured robes with the family crest, red-and-white kumadori make-up, a huge sword and a fan-like headdress, to rescue them with superhuman strength. The scene, repeated with variations for over a century, became one of the Kabuki Jūhachiban, the eighteen great plays of the Ichikawa family, and its costume and poses are among the most familiar images of kabuki.
