# Yoshizawa Kengyō II

*二代 吉沢検校*

**1800 – 1872** · Japanese  
Born: Owari Province (Nagoya) · Died:   
Genres & eras: [Koto & shamisen music](../movements/koto-shamisen.md)  

> Blind koto master of Nagoya who created the kokin-gumi songs on classical poems, including Chidori no Kyoku.

## Biography

Yoshizawa was a blind musician of the Nagoya region, trained in koto, shamisen, kokyū and heike biwa, who rose to the rank of kengyō. Tired of the elaborate jiuta of his day, he turned for inspiration to the older music of the court and the ideals of the classical age.

He set waka poems from the Kokinshū and other anthologies as the kokin-gumi, using a new koto tuning (kokin-jōshi) influenced by gagaku. Chidori no Kyoku is the best known. His style, with its restraint and archaism, inspired later Meiji koto composers.

## Key works & forms

### Chidori no Kyoku (c. 1855)

- **Date:** c. 1855
- **Type:** Koto song
- **Where performed / held:** Nagoya
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Chidori%20no%20Kyoku%20Yoshizawa%20Kengy%C5%8D%20II)

Chidori no Kyoku sets two poems about plovers, small shore birds, one from the Kokinshū and one from the Kin'yōshū, in Yoshizawa's archaic kokin-gumi style, framed by a long instrumental interlude whose rippling figures suggest waves and the cries of birds over the beach at Awaji. Yoshizawa wrote it for koto with the kokyū fiddle, though it is now often played with shakuhachi. Its clear, restrained manner, consciously reaching back to court music, made it one of the most popular koto works and a model for Meiji composers.
