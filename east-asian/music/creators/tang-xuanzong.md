## Biography

Li Longji took the throne in 712 after a palace coup and ruled for over four decades, the longest reign of the Tang. The first half of his reign, the Kaiyuan era, is remembered as a golden age of prosperity and culture, home to Li Bai, Du Fu and Wang Wei. In later years he left government to ministers and grew devoted to his consort Yang Guifei; the An Lushan rebellion of 755 drove him from the capital, his guards forced the death of Yang Guifei, and he abdicated in 756.

Xuanzong played the jie drum and composed, and he reorganised court music, setting up the Pear Garden (Liyuan) where he personally trained hundreds of musicians and dancers. Later Chinese actors called themselves 'disciples of the Pear Garden' and honoured him as patron of their profession. The Rainbow Skirt and Feathered Garment dance, said to have been arranged by him, and the tragic love story of the emperor and Yang Guifei became lasting themes of poetry and theatre.

### Pear Garden (Liyuan) (714)

- **Date:** 714
- **Type:** Court music academy
- **Where performed / held:** Chang'an
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pear%20Garden%20%28Liyuan%29%20Emperor%20Xuanzong%20of%20Tang)

In 714 Xuanzong selected some three hundred musicians and several hundred palace women to study the new 'law music' (faqu) in the Pear Garden of the palace, where he personally corrected their mistakes. The academy formed part of a larger reorganisation of court music that also created teaching offices (jiaofang) for popular music. The Pear Garden was devastated by the An Lushan rebellion, but its name passed to the whole profession of theatre: actors became 'disciples of the Pear Garden' (liyuan dizi), and Xuanzong was worshipped by them as a patron deity. Its fame illustrates the high prestige of music at the Tang court, at a time when Chang'an drew performers from across Asia.

### Dance of the Rainbow Skirt and Feathered Garment (Nishang yuyi qu) (c. 718–720)

- **Date:** c. 718–720
- **Type:** Court dance suite
- **Where performed / held:** Tang palace, Chang'an
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dance%20of%20the%20Rainbow%20Skirt%20and%20Feathered%20Garment%20%28Nishang%20yuyi%20qu%29%20Emperor%20Xuanzong%20of%20Tang)

According to legend Emperor Xuanzong composed this music after dreaming of a visit to the palace of the moon, but historical sources suggest he adapted an Indian-derived Brahman melody presented by a frontier governor. It was a large suite (daqu) in many sections, beginning with free-rhythm preludes and building to a fast dance, performed by richly costumed dancers, among them Xuanzong's consort Yang Guifei. After the An Lushan rebellion it came to symbolise the lost splendour of the Kaiyuan era, and Bai Juyi's 'Song of Everlasting Regret' made it an emblem of the doomed love story. The music was lost, though the Southern Tang queen Zhou Ehuang and the Song poet Jiang Kui each claimed to have recovered parts of it. It lives on in poetry, painting and kunqu, notably in The Palace of Eternal Youth.
