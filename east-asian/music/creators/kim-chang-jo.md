# Kim Chang-jo

*김창조 (金昌祖)*

**1856 – 1919** · Korean  
Born: Yeongam, Jeolla Province · Died:   
Genres & eras: [Pansori & sanjo](../movements/pansori-sanjo.md)  

> Gayageum player traditionally credited with creating sanjo, the virtuoso solo form of Korean instrumental music.

## Biography

Kim Chang-jo came from a family of musicians in South Jeolla and was a master of the gayageum and of the improvisatory shaman music sinawi. In the late nineteenth century he combined its melodies with the rhythmic cycles of pansori into a long solo work for gayageum.

This was gayageum sanjo, a suite of movements that grows from the slow jinyangjo to fast hwimori, with the player accompanied by a janggu drum. Other masters soon created sanjo for the geomungo, daegeum and other instruments. Kim taught pupils including his granddaughter Kim Juk-pa and Han Seong-gi, whose schools are still played. Some scholars stress that others developed similar music at the same time.

## Key works & forms

### Gayageum sanjo (c. 1890)

- **Date:** c. 1890
- **Type:** Instrumental solo
- **Where performed / held:** Jeolla Province
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Gayageum%20sanjo%20Kim%20Chang-jo)

Sanjo, 'scattered melodies', is a suite of movements for a solo instrument and the janggu drum, each in a different rhythmic cycle (jangdan): the slow jinyangjo, then jungmori, jungjungmori, jajinmori and the fast hwimori and danmori, so that the music accelerates over thirty minutes to an hour. It draws on the modes and melodies of pansori and shamanic sinawi and gives the performer room for personal expression and improvisation. Kim Chang-jo is credited with creating the first gayageum sanjo in the late nineteenth century, and his pupils' versions, such as those of the Kim Juk-pa and Seong Geum-yeon schools, are still taught. Sanjo for geomungo, daegeum, haegeum, ajaeng and piri followed, and it became the central genre of solo instrumental music in Korea.
