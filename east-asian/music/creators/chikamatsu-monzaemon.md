## Biography

Born into a samurai family, Chikamatsu entered the service of a Kyoto noble and began writing for the puppet theatre in the 1680s. He wrote kabuki plays for the actor Sakata Tōjūrō in the 1690s, then from 1705 worked as staff playwright of Takemoto Gidayū's theatre in Osaka.

His history plays and domestic tragedies of love suicide shaped both bunraku and kabuki, and he held that art lies in the thin margin between truth and fiction. His major plays are discussed in the literature section; here the focus is his partnership with Gidayū.

## Key works & forms

### Shusse Kagekiyo (1685)

- **Date:** 1685
- **Type:** Jōruri puppet play
- **Where performed / held:** Takemoto-za, Osaka
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Shusse%20Kagekiyo%20Chikamatsu%20Monzaemon)

Kagekiyo Victorious tells of the Taira warrior Kagekiyo, who plans to assassinate the victorious Minamoto Yoritomo, is betrayed by his jealous mistress, escapes and is captured, and finally blinds himself so that he will never again see his enemy. Written by Chikamatsu for Gidayū's new theatre in 1685, it gave the hero a more human psychology and introduced elements of Noh drama, and later critics regarded it as dividing old jōruri from new. It began the partnership between playwright and chanter that dominated the Osaka puppet stage for two decades.
