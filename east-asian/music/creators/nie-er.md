# Nie Er

*聶耳*

**1912 – 1935** · Chinese  
Born: Kunming, Yunnan · Died: Fujisawa, Japan  
Genres & eras: [Western-style composers](../movements/modern-composers.md)  

> Young composer of the left-wing film songs of 1930s Shanghai, including the March of the Volunteers, now China's national anthem.

## Biography

Nie Er grew up in Kunming, joined a song-and-dance troupe in Shanghai in 1931 and joined the Chinese Communist Party in 1933. He wrote songs for left-wing films and plays that dealt with workers, poverty and resistance to Japan. In 1935 he left for Japan to avoid arrest, intending to go on to Europe, but drowned while swimming at Fujisawa aged twenty-three.

In just two years of work he wrote about forty songs in a vigorous style suited to mass singing, among them the 'Graduation Song' and the March of the Volunteers, written for the film Children of Troubled Times (1935).

## Key works & forms

### March of the Volunteers (1935)

- **Date:** 1935
- **Type:** Song (national anthem)
- **Where performed / held:** Shanghai; theme of the film Children of Troubled Times
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=March%20of%20the%20Volunteers%20Nie%20Er)

Tian Han wrote the words and Nie Er the music for the film Children of Troubled Times, made in Shanghai in 1935 after Japan's seizure of Manchuria. Its call for people who refuse to be slaves to build a new Great Wall with their flesh and blood spread rapidly during the war, and in 1949 it was chosen as the provisional national anthem of the People's Republic; it was confirmed in the constitution in 1982 after a period in the Cultural Revolution when Tian Han was persecuted and the words were not sung. It is still in copyright in some countries through its lyricist.
