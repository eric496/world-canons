# Chinese folk & solo musicians

*民間樂師*

**traditional – present** · Chinese  
Genres & eras: [Chinese folk & solo instrumental music](../movements/chinese-instrumental.md)  

> The anonymous and lineage musicians of China's regional traditions, whose pieces were handed down orally and printed in collections from the nineteenth century.

## Biography

Much Chinese instrumental and folk music has no known composer. Pipa players passed down large programmatic pieces within lineages before they were printed, as in Hua Qiuping's collection of 1818. Folk songs circulated in many regional versions and were taken up by opera, street musicians and, from the 1920s, the gramophone.

In the twentieth century reformers in Shanghai and later state conservatories arranged these pieces for modern Chinese orchestras and solo concert performance, fixing versions that are now taught in conservatories.

### Jasmine Flower (Mo li hua) (traditional; published 1804)

- **Date:** traditional; published 1804
- **Type:** Folk song
- **Where performed / held:** Jiangsu folk song; published in John Barrow, Travels in China (London, 1804)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jasmine%20Flower%20%28Mo%20li%20hua%29%20Chinese%20folk%20%26%20solo%20musicians)

Mo li hua, praising the jasmine flower, exists in many regional versions, of which the Jiangsu version is the best known. It was heard by members of Lord Macartney's embassy to the Qianlong emperor in 1793, and a transcription appeared in John Barrow's Travels in China in 1804, one of the first Chinese melodies published in Europe. Puccini used it as a recurring theme in Turandot (1926). In China it has been sung at state ceremonies and was performed at the Hong Kong handover and the Beijing Olympics.

### Ambush from Ten Sides (Shimian maifu) (traditional; first printed 1818)

[![Ambush from Ten Sides (Shimian maifu)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4c/Pipa_MET_DP216710.jpg/1280px-Pipa_MET_DP216710.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pipa_MET_DP216710.jpg)

<sub>A Chinese pipa, Metropolitan Museum of Art Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pipa_MET_DP216710.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** traditional; first printed 1818
- **Type:** Pipa solo
- **Where performed / held:** Printed in Hua Qiuping's Pipa pu (Wuxi, 1818)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ambush%20from%20Ten%20Sides%20%28Shimian%20maifu%29%20Chinese%20folk%20%26%20solo%20musicians)

Ambush from Ten Sides depicts the final battle between Liu Bang, founder of the Han, and Xiang Yu, King of Chu, at Gaixia in 202 BC, in which Xiang Yu's army was surrounded and destroyed. In a series of titled sections, the pipa represents the camps, the sounding of the drums and horns, the battle, the noise of horses and swords and Xiang Yu's death at the Wu River, using strummed chords, string-pulling, slaps and buzzes. The piece belongs to the 'martial' (wu) style of pipa music and was first printed in the collection of Hua Qiuping in 1818, though its legend claims older roots. Its companion is the 'civil' piece The Tyrant Removes His Armour. It is one of the most famous showpieces of Chinese instrumental music.

### Spring River Flower Moon Night (Chunjiang huayue ye) (traditional; arranged 1925)

- **Date:** traditional; arranged 1925
- **Type:** Ensemble piece
- **Where performed / held:** Shanghai (Datong Music Society)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Spring%20River%20Flower%20Moon%20Night%20%28Chunjiang%20huayue%20ye%29%20Chinese%20folk%20%26%20solo%20musicians)

The pipa piece Xiyang xiao gu (Flute and Drum at Sunset) was printed in the nineteenth century and evokes a river at evening. In 1925 Liu Yaozhang and Zheng Jinwen of the Datong Music Society in Shanghai arranged it for an ensemble of Chinese instruments and named it after Zhang Ruoxu's famous Tang poem 'A Moonlit Night on the Spring River'. In ten sections, it became a classic of the new Chinese orchestra and is one of the best known pieces of Chinese instrumental music.
