# 19th-Century Philosophy (1801 – 1900)

Boundary notes: Bentham (b. 1748) and Hegel (b. 1770) are starting points; Peirce (d. 1914), James (d. 1910), Nietzsche (d. 1900, unproductive after 1889) close it — their formative work is 19th-c. though influence is 20th-c.

## Era overview
Philosophy after Kant — and after Hegel. Kant limited what reason could know; everyone here completes, rebels against, or replaces that settlement.
Drivers: (1) Rise and collapse of Hegel's system; after 1831 school split Right vs Young/Left Hegelians (Feuerbach, Stirner, young Marx); century = extracting one piece (history, alienation, recognition, dialectic), dropping the system. (2) History becomes a philosophical category (Hegel, Marx; Nietzsche's "genealogy"). (3) Industrialization/capitalism/revolution: 1848, factory labor, Smith/Ricardo → Mill and Marx as theorists of industrial-commercial society. (4) Prestige of natural science: Darwin Origin of Species (1859), thermodynamics, experimental psychology → become scientific (Mill, Peirce, James) or defend space science can't occupy (Kierkegaard, Schopenhauer, Nietzsche). (5) Split defining next century: British-American empiricist/logical line (Mill→Peirce/James→Russell/analytic) vs German-European existence/history/critique line (Kierkegaard, Marx, Nietzsche→existentialism, Marxism, critical theory, post-structuralism).
Recurring question: If inherited religion, timeless reason and traditional morality can't be simply assumed, what grounds knowledge and life — history, utility, will, material production, individual choice, or practical consequences?

## Learning order within era
1. Mill first (+Bentham box): On Liberty, Utilitarianism ch. 2 — clear, no prerequisites, liberal-empiricist pole.
2. Hegel with guide: secondary, then Phenomenology Preface + Lordship/Bondage. Needed to understand Marx/Kierkegaard/Feuerbach starting positions; unaided Hegel first = commonest dropout.
3. Feuerbach extract → Marx: Manifesto + 1859 Preface + 1844 alienation.
4. Schopenhauer: essays then WWR selections — after Hegel (deliberate anti-Hegel), before Nietzsche (begins as disciple).
5. Kierkegaard: Fear and Trembling — after Hegel (target), alongside Schopenhauer as other anti-systematic inward turn.
6. Nietzsche: Genealogy First Essay, then Gay Science selections — argues with almost everyone above.
7. Peirce → James last (pedagogically, not importance): Peirce's two papers, then James Pragmatism Lectures 2, 6. Pragmatism as response to era's idealism/empiricism standoff.
Stirner optional sidebar between 3 and 4.

## Dating nuances to preserve
Schopenhauer WWR: 1818 (completed/published Dec) vs 1819 (title page). Mill Utilitarianism: 1861 serial / 1863 book. Hegel Science of Logic: 1812–16 (some SEP editions 1812–18). Stirner Ego: 1844 pub / 1845 title page. Marx 1844 Manuscripts and German Ideology unpublished in lifetime, first published 20th c. (1932 for Manuscripts).
