# Ancient Philosophy (c. 600 BCE – c. 270 CE)

Scope: c. 624 BCE (Thales) to 270 CE (Plotinus). Dates before Aristotle are approximate. Presocratics survive only in fragments and testimonia (second-hand reports).

## Era overview
Three conversations in sequence:
1. Presocratics (6th–5th c. BCE): "What is everything made of, and how does change happen?" Search for a natural first principle (archē) instead of myth. Heraclitus (change) vs Parmenides (change logically impossible); atomism answers Parmenides while saving change.
2. Classical Athenian turn (5th–4th c. BCE): "How should we live, and what do our words mean?" Socrates redirects philosophy to virtue/knowledge/the good life; Plato builds metaphysical and political systems; Aristotle systematizes logic, biology, ethics, politics, metaphysics, grounding knowledge in this world.
3. Hellenistic/Roman schools (c. 323 BCE–3rd c. CE): "How can I be untroubled in a world I don't control?" Competing ways of life — Epicurean, Stoic, Skeptic, Cynic — key goal ataraxia (untroubledness). Logic/physics serve ethics. Closes with Plotinus fusing Plato into religious metaphysics (Neoplatonism), shaping medieval thought.
Threads: being vs becoming; reason vs senses; one vs many; nature (physis) vs convention (nomos); virtue and happiness (eudaimonia); fate vs responsibility.

## Learning order within era (beginner)
1. Socrates via Plato: Apology, Crito, Euthyphro.
2. Presocratic problem in miniature: overview, then Heraclitus vs Parmenides (fragments + commentary), then Democritus. Thales/Pythagoras: short secondary only.
3. Plato's system: Phaedo (sel.) → Republic I–II, VI–VII → Symposium.
4. Aristotle's answer: Nicomachean Ethics I–II (+X), guided four-causes/Metaphysics overview.
5. Cynics as bridge: Diogenes Laertius VI.
6. Hellenistic therapy schools in dialogue: Epicurus → Stoics → Skeptics (who attack both).
7. Plotinus last (I.6, then V.1): presupposes Plato and Aristotle.
Habit: one glossary page for Greek terms — archē, logos, eudaimonia, ataraxia, epochē, physis/nomos, Form, telos.

## Oversimplifications checklist
- Thales: archē is originating principle, not literal H2O chemistry.
- Pythagoras: proved no theorem securely; famous in life as religious teacher on the soul (SEP).
- Heraclitus: flux is Logos-governed; river quote is paraphrase.
- Parmenides: produced an argument successors had to answer, not just denial of obvious.
- Democritus: atoms uncuttable/argued, not discovered/observed modern atoms.
- Socrates ≠ Plato's dialogues wholesale (early vs late differ; Xenophon/Aristophanes differ).
- Plato's Forms: explanatory principles, self-criticized in Parmenides.
- Aristotle's mean: not "moderate everything"; eudaimonia = lifetime flourishing activity.
- Epicurean ≠ indulgence.
- Stoic ≠ emotionless suppression.
- Skeptics did not claim nothing can be known.
- Cynic ≠ modern cynical; jar was a pithos.
- Plotinus ≠ vague mysticism/world-hating; emanation = dependence, matter = privation not evil creation.
