# Early Modern Philosophy (c. 1600 – 1800)

## Era overview
Medieval Aristotelian-Scholastic synthesis breaks down under Scientific Revolution (Copernicus, Galileo, Newton). Dominating question: What can we actually know, and how — reason, senses, or combination?
- Rationalism (Descartes→Spinoza→Leibniz): some substantive knowledge available to reason alone, independent of experience; innate ideas; knowledge modelled on mathematics/deduction; reality has intelligible necessary structure; high metaphysical ambition (complete systems).
- Empiricism (Locke→Berkeley→Hume): all ideas ultimately from experience (sensation + reflection); no innate ideas; method modelled on experimental science, modest. Trajectory: Locke constructive → Berkeley dissolves "matter" using Locke's principles → Hume dissolves causation, self, inductive certainty.
- Newton's reception (du Châtelet): Newtonian physics rebuilt for continental readers — the *Institutions de Physique* argues over foundations (hypotheses, forces, vis viva) rather than merely reporting results, and her *Principia* translation and commentary long remained the standard French Newton.
- Political thread (Machiavelli→Hobbes→Locke→Rousseau, then Smith and Wollstonecraft): the thread opens with Machiavelli, the era's political-realist forerunner, who judges politics by its "effectual truth" rather than by moral or theological sanction. Divine right then loses credibility; authority is rebuilt from individuals: state of nature → social contract → legitimate government; different human nature → radically different state. Smith extends the thread from contract to sympathy and commercial society; Wollstonecraft turns the rights argument against the exclusion of women.
- Kant's synthesis: Hume woke him from "dogmatic slumber". Rationalists right that mind contributes necessary structure; empiricists right knowledge begins with and is limited to experience. "Thoughts without content are empty, intuitions without concepts are blind." Mind actively structures experience; we know things only as they appear under those structures.
Arc: Bacon proclaims a rebuilt method of inquiry → Descartes deletes everything doubtful, rebuilds from thinking self → Spinoza/Leibniz total systems → Locke restarts from experience → Berkeley/Hume push experience-first until metaphysics/causality wobble → Kant rebuilds by mapping what mind contributes and where knowledge stops.

## Learning order within era
1. Bacon Novum Organum, aphorisms (selections) — the era's opening call for a new method, read before Descartes answers it.
2. Hobbes Leviathan 13–17 (optional, cheap) — contract device + materialist provocation.
3. Descartes Meditations I–III — non-negotiable main-line start.
4. Locke Essay II.1, II.8, II.27 + Second Treatise selections — clearer second step pedagogically (Spinoza/Leibniz chronologically between, saved for later).
5. Spinoza Ethics Part I guided + Appendix.
6. Leibniz Monadology — "Spinoza's one substance pluralised into infinitely many".
7. Du Châtelet Institutions (selections) — Newton rebuilt in French; argues with Leibnizian metaphysics as much as with Newton, so read after Leibniz.
8. Berkeley Three Dialogues 1–2 — lands harder after Locke (critique from inside Locke's premises).
9. Hume Enquiry — empiricist climax; §§4–7 slowly.
10. Adam Smith Theory of Moral Sentiments (selections) — Hume's moral sentiment developed into a system of sympathy and the impartial spectator.
11. Rousseau Social Contract I–II (capstone political, anywhere after Locke) + Pascal selections (palate cleanser after Spinoza or before Kant).
12. Kant Groundwork I–II, then Prolegomena — synthesis read last by design, illegible without Hume + rationalists.
13. Wollstonecraft Vindication of the Rights of Woman (selections) — the rights argument answered back; read after Rousseau and Kant.
14. Capstone optional/advanced: Critique of Pure Reason Aesthetic+Analytic guided; Ethics Parts II–V.
Study tip (repo): for each figure learn (a) the one problem they're obsessed with, (b) their one signature move, (c) strongest objection from NEXT figure. Era is a conversation — sequence of replies is more durable than isolated systems.

## Oversimplifications (general)
- "Rationalists used reason, empiricists used experience" false — dispute is: does substantive knowledge of reality come from reason independently of experience, and are any ideas innate? Locke does a priori reasoning; Descartes does empirical science.
- "Rationalism = Continent, Empiricism = Britain" is later textbook map, not law; constant cross-Channel correspondence; Leibniz's reply to Locke unpublished until 1765.
- Plus per-figure cautions in each entry's own "Misreadings to avoid" section (Descartes cogito not syllogism, insisted on mind-body union in lived experience; Spinoza pantheism/atheism contested; Leibniz optimism technical; Locke tabula rasa later label; Berkeley; Hume; Kant; Rousseau; Pascal).

## Flagged uncertainties
Locke Essay 1689 vs 1690 convention. Leibniz composition vs publication gaps decades. Hume Treatise 1739–40 (Books I–II 1739, III 1740). Spinoza Ethics, Hume Dialogues, Pascal Pensées all posthumous — mark in repo (explains reception).
