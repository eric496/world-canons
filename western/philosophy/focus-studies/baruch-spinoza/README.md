# Focus Study: Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677)

## The figure in brief

Baruch Spinoza — Bento in the Portuguese of his birth community, Benedictus in the Latin of his books — lived forty-four years: educated for the rabbinate in Amsterdam's Portuguese-Jewish schools, expelled from that community at twenty-three under one of the most ferocious writs of excommunication on record, and thereafter a lens grinder in rented rooms, declining a university chair, publishing one book under his own name, and leaving his masterpiece in a drawer to be brought out by friends after his death. The philosophy matches the life in extremity. The *Ethics*, demonstrated in geometrical order, argues from a handful of definitions to a single substance — God, or Nature, *Deus sive Natura* — of which everything that exists is a mode, following from the divine nature with the same necessity with which the properties of a triangle follow from its essence. Mind and body are one thing under two attributes; nothing could have been otherwise; final causes are human fictions; freedom is not exemption from necessity but understanding it, and its fruit is the intellectual love of God. The *Theological-Political Treatise* (1670), published anonymously and promptly banned, applied the same intellect to Scripture and the state, founding modern biblical criticism and making an early, radical case for freedom of thought. In his lifetime "Spinozist" was an accusation; within a century and a half Hegel could declare that either Spinozism or no philosophy.

## Why he rewards weeks of study

Three reasons, beyond the library's portrait. First, *totality*: the *Ethics* is the most complete single system in early modern philosophy — metaphysics, theory of knowledge, psychology of the emotions, ethics, and the philosophy of religion derived in one chain from one set of definitions — so it is the best training ground there is for watching a philosophy work as a machine with no loose parts, and for learning to locate the exact premise where you stop being able to follow. Second, *position*: Spinoza is the hinge between Descartes and the Enlightenment. He takes Descartes's own definition of substance more seriously than Descartes did and collapses three kinds of substance into one; nearly everything after him — the German recovery, the pantheism controversy, modern naturalism, dual-aspect theories of mind, contemporary affect theory — passes through his workshop. Third, *the revival is live*: the last forty years of scholarship (Curley's editions, the Anglophone renaissance in readings of the affects and the political writings) have made Spinoza arguably the most actively reinterpreted figure of his century, so a deep study meets real, unsettled questions — pantheist or atheist, the coherence of the unknown attributes, the eternity of the mind in Part V — rather than a closed monument.

## The study plan at a glance

The library's [learning path](../../learning-path.md) meets Spinoza in **Stage 3** (Early Modern to Kant) as a *core* figure: *Ethics* Part I read whole with a guide, Part II in selection, Parts IV–V in selection, and the *Theological-Political Treatise* as a summary pass. This dossier deepens that core treatment into a full one. [07-study-plan.md](07-study-plan.md) lays out a **7-week plan of roughly 40 hours of scheduled study (about 47 hours all-in once the daily card queue is counted)**: the *Ethics* read whole (building on the library's proposition-by-proposition guide to Part I rather than duplicating it), the *Theological-Political Treatise* read properly — its scriptural method and its political chapters in full, not as a summary — the early works that show the system forming, the letters that show it thinking aloud, and selections from the *Political Treatise*. It runs at the learning path's weekly rhythm: primary reading first, reconstruction before commentary, the daily FSRS queue fed by the dossier's cards. It plugs into Stage 3 at the point the path reaches Spinoza; the hours sit on top of the Stage 3 budget, as focus-study hours always do, and the plan document shows the arithmetic week by week.
