# Study Plan — Spinoza in Seven Weeks: The Ethics Whole, the TTP Properly

## What this plan is, and where it plugs in

The [learning path](../../learning-path.md) meets Spinoza in Stage 3 as a core figure (●), in a single compressed block: *Ethics* Part I whole and guided, Part II in selections, Parts IV–V in selections, and the *Theological-Political Treatise* (TTP) as a summary pass. That treatment is enough to know what Leibniz, Hume, and Kant are answering. This focus study replaces and deepens it: **the *Ethics* read whole, all five parts, and the TTP read properly** — the chapters where modern biblical criticism and the defense of free thought are actually founded, not summarized. Run it in place of the learning path's Stage 3 Spinoza block. Two placement rules are not negotiable: **Descartes comes first** (the learning path's order — Spinoza's system is unintelligible except as an answer to Descartes, and Week 6 makes the contrast explicit), and the Kant block follows naturally afterward, since Kant inherits a philosophy already transformed by the Spinoza who is about to become, posthumously, the *Pantheismusstreit*'s central figure.

**Total cost, stated honestly: ≈40 hours of scheduled study across seven weeks (~5.5–6 hrs/week of reading, dossier work, and media), plus the daily [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net) queue (~10 minutes, ~6 days a week ≈ 7 hrs across the seven weeks) — ≈47 hours all-in.** That is roughly five times the learning path's selections budget for this figure. What it buys: the complete *Ethics* at working depth, the TTP's load-bearing chapters in full, the two great letter-exchanges, and a dossier's worth of argument reconstructions — the difference between knowing *about* Spinozism and being able to run it.

**Reading texts.** The plan's default for the *Ethics* is **Edwin Curley's Penguin translation (1996)** — the learning path's ruling, and the same Curley text as the Princeton *Collected Works*, so citations transfer. Samuel Shirley's Hackett translation is the sanctioned alternative (his one-volume Hackett *Ethics, Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect, and Selected Letters* conveniently covers Weeks 1, 4, and 5's letter assignments in a single purchase). For the TTP, use the **Cambridge edition (ed. Jonathan Israel; trans. Michael Silverthorne and Jonathan Israel, 2007)** or Curley's translation in *Collected Works* Vol. II. Citations throughout the plan are by part, proposition, and scholium (E1P14, E2P40S2, and so on), which are stable across every serious translation. The one absolute rule, inherited from the learning path: **do not read the *Ethics* unguided.** The geometric order is beginner-hostile by design; every week below pairs the text with its guide.

**The order, and why.** Week 1 orients — the life, the system map, and Spinoza's own unfinished methodological treatise — before any geometry. Weeks 2–6 then take the *Ethics* in Spinoza's order, one part per week, because the work is genuinely cumulative: Part III's affects presuppose Part II's mind, Part IV's bondage presupposes Part III's conatus, and Part V's freedom is only the earlier machinery turned therapeutic. The two letter-assignments (Blyenbergh on evil in Week 4, the conscious stone in Week 5) are placed where the correspondence argues with the part just read. The TTP waits for Week 7 deliberately: read after the *Ethics*, its separation of theology from philosophy is visible as the metaphysics applied to Scripture and the state; read before, it looks like mere polemic. Week 7 closes with the *Political Treatise* selections and the capstone.

## Week 1 — Orientation: the man, the map, the method (~5 hrs)

**Primary.** The *Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect* (TdIE), §§1–30 in Curley's numbering: the autobiographical opening — the search for a good that is "true" and shareable, the interim rules of life adopted while the search continues — and the four modes of perception, Spinoza's first sketch of the kinds of knowledge the *Ethics* will formalize. It is unfinished and it is short; it is also the only place where Spinoza tells you, in the first person, what the whole project is *for*. Then the **IEP's Spinoza articles, before anything else systematic** — the learning path's explicit rule for this figure (IEP first, SEP after): the main "Benedict de Spinoza" article for the life-and-system survey, with the IEP's "Spinoza on Free Will and Freedom" held for Week 3 if time runs short.

**Alongside.** [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md) (the *cherem*, the lens grinder's independence, the withheld *Ethics*), [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md) (where each text you will read sits in the corpus and its publication fate), and [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) in full — the architecture and the figure glossary. Read 03 now, before the geometry: knowing that substance, attribute, and mode are load-bearing terms with exact jobs is what makes Week 2 survivable.

**Media.** *Philosophize This!* episode #033 ("Spinoza Pt. 1 — From Baruch to Benedicto!", Stephen West, 2014, ~26 min) as a *preview* before the dossier files: the life, the excommunication, the Burgh correspondence, told briskly. Popularizer's compression — orientation only, never evidence; check its drama against 01. Optional context: Peter Adamson's [*History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/all-episodes), episode 502, "United States: the Dutch Republic" (October 2026, ~22 min) — the political and religious scene of the Republic Spinoza wrote in. Note the honest gap, recorded here so nobody goes looking: **Adamson's Spinoza episodes do not exist yet.** The series is still working through the Dutch context (Grotius and Dutch science come first); in his published 2026 preview Adamson expects to reach Spinoza around early 2027. When those episodes land they will become this week's natural spine; until then, the relay above is the verified state of the field.

**Checkpoints (book closed; short answers given, full treatments in the dossier files named).**
1. *What is the TdIE's opening problem, and what are the interim rules of life for?* Experience has taught him that the ordinary goods — riches, honor, sensual pleasure — are uncertain and absorbing; he seeks a good whose discovery would yield continuous, supreme joy. The rules of life are provisional maxims (speak to the multitude's understanding, take only necessary pleasures, acquire only necessary money) that keep ordinary life running while the intellect is reformed. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), on the kinds of knowledge.
2. *State the four modes of perception in the TdIE and name the one the whole method aims at.* Knowledge by hearsay; by random, unexamined experience; by inference from something else (adequate as to the conclusion, not the thing's essence); and by perception through a thing's essence alone. The method aims to move the mind from the first two toward the fourth — the seed of the *Ethics*' three kinds of knowledge. (Same file.)
3. *Why did the cherem matter philosophically, not just biographically?* It severed him from every institution that could discipline his conclusions, and he never joined another: the system's independence — no church, no university, a declined Heidelberg chair — is the life-form of its method. [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md).
4. *In one sentence each: substance, attribute, mode.* Substance: what is in itself and conceived through itself. Attribute: what the intellect perceives as constituting substance's essence. Mode: what is in another, and conceived through that other. If any of the three wobbles, Week 2 will hurt — fix it now. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), glossary.

**Reconstructions.** None yet — the first blank-page reconstruction comes with Week 2's monism argument. This week's retrieval work is the checkpoint set plus re-deriving the substance/attribute/mode triad from memory at week's end.

**Cards.** Import the first 5 cards of [cards.tsv](cards.tsv) (substance/attribute/mode, *Deus sive Natura*, *natura naturans/naturata*, *causa sui*, adequate idea) when the week's reading is done — never before.

## Week 2 — God: Ethics Part I, with the guide open beside you (~6 hrs)

**Primary.** *Ethics* Part I, whole: the eight definitions and seven axioms, propositions 1–36, and the Appendix. This is the metaphysical engine room — substance monism (E1P5, P11, P14), God as immanent cause (E1P18), the *naturans/naturata* distinction (E1P29S), universal necessity (E1P29, P33), and the Appendix's demolition of teleology. **Session companion: the repo's own [study guide to Part I](../../study/spinoza-ethics-part1.md), used as designed** — read each block of propositions with the guide's section-by-section walkthrough in Session B, work from its argument reconstructions (monism; God/Nature identity and necessity; the Appendix argument), and treat its fifteen checkpoint questions, with their printed answers, as this week's extended retrieval set. This plan does not duplicate the guide; it schedules it.

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) (the metaphysics sections) and [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) — "Substance monism," "Necessary existence and universal necessity," "The teleology critique."

**Media.** *Philosophize This!* episode #034 ("Spinoza Pt. 2 — Force of Nature," 2014, ~29 min) as a *preview* before the first Part I session: substance, God/Nature, and the free-will consequence, in West's compressed form — then watch the text out-argue the compression. BBC Radio 4, *In Our Time*: "Spinoza" (first broadcast 3 May 2007; Melvyn Bragg with Jonathan Rée, Sarah Hutton, and John Cottingham) as *review* after the Appendix: three scholars, strong on the life, the radical reputation, and the pantheism/atheism question — which is [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md)'s home ground, and which you should hear staged before you read it staged.

**Checkpoints (the plan's five; the guide's fifteen are the full set — do both, guide first in Session C).**
1. *Reconstruct E1P5: why can't two substances share an attribute?* If two substances shared an attribute they would have to be distinguished by their modes; but substance is prior to its modes (E1P1), so considered in themselves they could not be distinguished at all — and what cannot be distinguished is not two. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Substance monism"; the guide's checkpoint 3.
2. *Which premise would Leibniz reject, and what would he owe instead?* The no-shared-attribute premise (or the definition of substance it rides on); he owes an alternative account of what being "in itself" means that lets substances be many — his monads are exactly that attempt. (Same section; the library entry's study question 1.)
3. *Why is E1P33 more radical than ordinary determinism?* Determinism says every event has a determining cause; E1P33 says things could not have been produced by God "in any other manner or in any other order" — no alternative arrangement of the whole was ever possible. Contingency itself, not just uncaused events, is denied. Guide checkpoint 8.
4. *What psychological mechanism produces belief in final causes, per the Appendix?* Men are conscious of their appetites and ignorant of their causes; finding many things in nature useful to them, they infer nature was made for their use, and project a purposive God on the same model. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "The teleology critique."
5. *Is Spinoza a pantheist or an atheist? Answer in the dossier's form.* The question is a live scholarly dispute, not a fact: his God lacks intellect, will, and purpose as persons have them (the atheist reading's evidence), yet substance — not the sum of things — is God, and the system's climax is the intellectual love of God (the pantheist reading's evidence). One-word answers are the misreading. [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).

**Reconstructions.** The monism argument and the teleology argument, both written blank-page by week's end; they enter the +2d/+1wk/+3wk rhythm (cadence table below). The guide's reconstruction (a) is your comparison text — compare *after* writing, per the learning path's rule.

**Cards.** +5 (substance monism, necessary existence and universal necessity, the teleology critique, immanent cause, eternity vs. duration).

## Week 3 — Mind: Ethics Part II (~5.5 hrs)

**Primary.** *Ethics* Part II, whole: the mind as the idea of the body (E2P13), parallelism (E2P7 and its scholium — "the order and connection of ideas is the same as the order and connection of things"), imagination and error (E2P17S, P29S, P35), the three kinds of knowledge (E2P40S2), and the identity of will and intellect (E2P48–49). This is the part where Spinoza answers Descartes without naming him on every page: no interaction, no pineal gland, no free faculty of assent — because there are not two things to interact.

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) (parallelism, adequate ideas, the kinds of knowledge) and [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) — "Parallelism (E2P7)," "No free will," "Against Descartes' pineal interaction" (read the reconstruction now; its primary-text moment, the E5 Preface, comes in Week 6).

**Media.** [*The Partially Examined Life*](https://www.partiallyexaminedlife.com), episode 24, "Spinoza on God and Metaphysics" (full episode ~1 hr 36 min, on *Ethics* Books 1–2) as *review*. Honest access note: the full episode is a premium episode; a free 32-minute preview is posted. The preview plus this week's reading is the assigned minimum; the full episode is worth the small outlay if the discussion format suits you — four readers arguing slowly through the text is the learning path's discussion substitute (§2.6) in its natural habitat.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *State parallelism exactly, and what it refuses.* The order and connection of ideas is the same as the order and connection of things, because a mode of Thought and a mode of Extension are one and the same thing expressed in two ways (E2P7S). It refuses causal traffic across the attributes: the body does not move the mind, the mind does not move the body — there is no gap for traffic to cross. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Parallelism."
2. *What is the human mind, on E2P13 — and what follows for the union of mind and body?* The mind is the idea of the actually existing body. Union is not a coupling of two things but the identity of one mode under two attributes; the mind knows the body only through the ideas of the body's affections (E2P19), which is why self-knowledge is so indirect. (Same file.)
3. *Distinguish the three kinds of knowledge with an example of each.* Imagination (first kind): fragmentary ideas from how external bodies affect ours — the sun looking small. Reason (second): common notions and adequate ideas of properties — knowing the sun's nature through astronomy's shared structures. Intuitive knowledge (third): grasping things through their essence in God, in a single view — seeing *why* this theorem, this act, must be so. (Same file; [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).)
4. *Why does E2P49 ("will and intellect are one and the same") matter beyond psychology?* It abolishes the faculty Descartes needed for free assent and error: volitions are just ideas affirming or denying, so there is no neutral intellect for a free will to overrule — the machinery of Part IV's bondage and Part V's therapy is being installed. (Same file, "No free will.")
5. *Press the standing objection to parallelism, in one paragraph.* If the two series never interact, the perfect correspondence can look like a coincidence relabeled — Leibniz's pre-established harmony with God as the harmonizer removed. Spinoza's answer is identity, not coordination: not two clocks synchronized, but one thing read twice. Judge whether identity explains the correlation or assumes it. [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).

**Reconstructions.** Parallelism reconstructed by week's end; monism gets its +1-week touch and teleology its first revisit in Session C.

**Cards.** +4 (parallelism, the three kinds of knowledge, action and passion, no free will — the stone).

## Week 4 — Striving: Ethics Part III and the problem of evil (~5.5 hrs)

**Primary.** *Ethics* Part III: the Preface (human actions treated "as though I were concerned with lines, planes, and solids"), conatus (E3P6–8), the affect machinery (E3P11 and scholium on joy, sadness, and desire as the three primary affects; the definitions of the affects at the part's end, sampled for love, hate, hope, fear, envy, pity), and affect imitation (E3P27). Alongside, the **Blyenbergh correspondence (Letters 18–24 and 27, 1664–65)**: the grain merchant Willem van Blyenbergh presses the system's hardest pastoral question — if all follows necessarily from God, what are sin and evil, and how was Adam's act his fault? Read Spinoza's replies (Letters 19, 21, 23, 27) closest: evil as privation relative to our model of a thing, not a positive thing God produced.

**Alongside.** [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) — "Conatus," and the affects material in "Bondage and the remedies of the affects" (its Part IV half lands next week).

**Media.** None assigned — deliberately. Part III is the *Ethics*' longest psychological haul and this week's letters double the primary load; the media rule cuts both ways, and nothing verified earns a slot against it. If a session runs short, the sanctioned filler is Clare Carlisle's eight-part *Guardian* walk-through of Spinoza (2011) — clear, proposition-level, free (linked from the bibliography) — read after the primary text.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *Reconstruct the conatus argument (E3P6–7) and state its most dangerous objection.* Each thing, insofar as it is in itself, strives to persevere in its being; that striving is its actual essence. The objection: the doctrine looks unfalsifiable — suicide and self-sacrifice get reassigned to overpowering external causes, so no behavior could count against it. Spinoza's reply and the standing assessment are in [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Conatus" — and the library entry's study question 3 stages the same fight.
2. *Define joy, sadness, and desire as Spinoza uses them — technically, not colloquially.* Joy: the passion by which the mind passes to a greater perfection (power of acting). Sadness: the passage to a lesser. Desire: appetite conscious of itself — the conatus as experienced. Every other affect is a species or compound of these three with an idea of a cause. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).
3. *Run the entry's test case: anger at a colleague, analyzed Spinozistically.* Anger is a passive affect: sadness (diminished power) joined to the idea of an external cause imagined as free. Forming adequate ideas of its actual causes — the colleague's history, one's own fatigue, the situation's structure — does not excuse anyone; it converts a passion undergone into an action understood, which is what freedom means here: the outcome is still necessitated, but now partly through one's own nature as adequate cause. (Same method as [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Bondage and the remedies of the affects.")
4. *What is Spinoza's answer to Blyenbergh on evil?* "Evil" names a privation relative to a model we set up (a blind man lacks sight relative to the human model; sight is not a thing God withheld). God causes everything that has essence and reality; evil, as such, has neither — so the question "why did God make evil?" misfires. Note where Blyenbergh remains unsatisfied: responsibility and punishment still need grounding, which is Part IV's and the TTP's work. [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md).
5. *What is affect imitation, and why will the politics need it?* If we imagine a being like us affected by an affect, we are thereby affected by a like affect (E3P27) — the mechanism of pity, envy, ambition, and crowd emotion. It is the transmission belt between Part III's psychology and Week 7's theory of the multitude. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).

**Reconstructions.** Conatus reconstructed by week's end; parallelism gets its +1-week touch; monism its +3-week touch.

**Cards.** +4 (conatus, the affect triad, affect imitation, hope and fear).

## Week 5 — Bondage: Ethics Part IV and the conscious stone (~6 hrs)

**Primary.** *Ethics* Part IV: the Preface (good and evil as modes of thinking — "good" names what we certainly know to be useful to us, not a property things wear), the core propositions (E4P18S, the dictates of reason; P24–28, virtue as acting from one's own nature understood; P35–37, human agreement in nature and the good of community), the **free man** sequence (E4P67–73 — "a free man thinks of nothing less than of death"), and the Appendix's thirty-two chapters as a rapid summary pass. Alongside, **Letter 58 (to Schuller, October 1674)** — three pages that contain the thrown stone: conscious of its motion, ignorant of its causes, believing it flies freely; "men are mistaken in thinking themselves free" because they are in the stone's position.

**Alongside.** [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) — "Good and evil as modes of thinking," "Bondage and the remedies of the affects," and [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) for the debate over whether an ethics of exhortation can survive necessitarianism (his answer: exhortation is itself a cause — weigh it).

**Media.** *The Partially Examined Life*, episode 25, "Spinoza on Human Nature" (on *Ethics* Books II–V), as *review* of the whole psychological arc and a preview of Week 6. Same access note as episode 24: premium, with a free preview posted; the preview is the assigned minimum.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *What does the Part IV Preface actually claim about good and evil — and what does it not claim?* Good and evil indicate nothing positive in things considered in themselves; they are modes of thinking, formed by comparison against a model of human nature we set before ourselves. It does **not** claim good is arbitrary: what aids our conatus and power of acting is objectively better *for us*, given what we are. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Good and evil as modes of thinking."
2. *State the dictates of reason (E4P18S) in plain terms.* Reason demands nothing contrary to nature: that each person love himself, seek his own advantage — what is really useful — and strive to preserve his being; from this follow sociability, honesty, and the shared life as rational goods, not concessions. (Same file; [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).)
3. *Rebuild the stone argument and locate its exact target.* A stone that could think would believe its flight free, being conscious of the motion and ignorant of the throw. The target is not deliberation or choice among motives — those are real — but the inference from *felt* freedom to *uncaused* freedom. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "No free will."
4. *Why does the free man think of death least of all things (E4P67)?* His concern is life understood, not its negation: fear of death is an inadequate idea — death is an external limit, not a content of his striving — so wisdom is "a meditation on life." Connect this forward: Part V will claim something in the mind is eternal; it is not a reprieve from this proposition. (Same file, "Bondage and the remedies of the affects.")
5. *Press the exhortation paradox at full strength, then give his answer.* If all is necessary, why write an *Ethics* urging anyone to change? His answer: the urging is itself a cause operating on readers' conatus; necessity does not make causes idle, it makes them the only levers there are. Whether that fully saves moral address — or quietly changes its meaning — is a live debate. [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).

**Reconstructions.** Good/evil and the stone both reconstructed; conatus gets its +1-week touch; parallelism its +3-week touch.

**Cards.** +5 (good and evil as modes of thinking, bondage and the remedies of the affects, virtue = power, the dictates of reason, the free man).

## Week 6 — Freedom: Ethics Part V, and Descartes in the dock (~5.5 hrs)

**Primary.** *Ethics* Part V, whole — it is the shortest part and the most contested. Read it in two passes: first the therapeutic machinery (E5P1–20: the remedies of the affects summarized at P20S; the mind's power in ordering its ideas; love toward God built from the third kind of knowledge), then the eternity claims (E5P21–42: "the human mind cannot be absolutely destroyed with the body, but something of it remains which is eternal," P23; blessedness "is not the reward of virtue, but virtue itself," P42). **Read with the interpretation dispute open from the first page**: Spinoza denies personal immortality and memory's survival outright (E5P21, P34S) while asserting the mind's eternity — what that eternity *is* (a timeless truth about the mind's essence in God? an experienced timelessness in intuitive knowledge? a doctrine scholars from Donagan to Nadler still divide over) is [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md)'s set piece, and this plan takes no side. Then the **Descartes contrast**, in Spinoza's own texts: the Preface to Part V (his explicit attack: Descartes' pineal gland as the soul's seat of interaction, judged an hypothesis "more occult than any occult quality," and the will's supposed absolute dominion over the affects) plus the Preface to his *Principles of Descartes' Philosophy* — Lodewijk Meyer's warning, written with Spinoza's knowledge, that the expositor does not endorse the philosophy he geometrically displays.

**Alongside.** [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) — "Intellectual love of God and the eternity of the mind" (dispute flagged in the reconstruction itself), "Against Descartes' pineal interaction"; [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) on Part V's readings and the pantheism/atheism afterlife.

**Media.** None assigned. Part V rewards slowness more than any week in the plan, and its media (the PEL episode already used) would be a repeat. If a session runs long, stop early rather than skim P38–42 — the plan's hours assume two sittings for this part, not one.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *List the five remedies of the affects (E5P20S) in your own words.* Knowledge of the affect itself; separating the affect from the thought of an external cause; the passage of time's effect on imagined objects; multiplying the causes that sustain good affects; and ordering one's thoughts by understanding necessity — the capstone remedy being the referral of all things to God. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Bondage and the remedies of the affects."
2. *What exactly is the intellectual love of God, and why can it not ask for love back?* Joy accompanied by the idea of God as its cause, arising from the third kind of knowledge; it is God's love of himself insofar as he is expressed through the human mind (E5P36). Demanding reciprocity imagines God as a person affected by us — the Appendix's projection again. (Same file, "Intellectual love of God and the eternity of the mind.")
3. *State what Part V denies and what it asserts about the mind after death — in two sentences that do not blur them.* Denied: memory, imagination, and personal duration — the mind endures only while the body endures (E5P21, P34). Asserted: the idea expressing the body's essence under a species of eternity is eternal, and in that sense something of the mind does not perish (E5P23). Any answer that turns the second sentence into survival has failed the checkpoint. (Same file; dispute per [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).)
4. *Why is the pineal gland, for Spinoza, worse than an unsolved problem?* Because it poses as a solution: an unobserved organ, moved by an unextended soul, through quantities of motion nobody measured — interaction relabeled, not explained, violating the very physics Descartes founded. Parallelism is offered as the honest alternative: no transaction, because no separation. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Against Descartes' pineal interaction."
5. *Compare the two philosophers' therapies in one paragraph.* Descartes: master the passions by a will trained to withhold assent and redirect the body's machinery — freedom as dominion. Spinoza: no dominion is available; an affect is displaced only by a stronger affect, ideally the joy of understanding its causes — freedom as comprehension. The learning path's Descartes weeks are the control group. (Same file.)

**Reconstructions.** The Part V core (remedies → intellectual love → eternity, stating the dispute rather than resolving it) reconstructed; good/evil gets its +1-week touch; conatus its +3-week touch.

**Cards.** +5 (intellectual love of God and the eternity of the mind, *amor Dei intellectualis*, blessedness, the pineal-interaction critique, *sub specie aeternitatis*).

## Week 7 — Scripture and the state: the TTP read properly (~6.5 hrs + essay)

**Primary.** The *Theological-Political Treatise*, in the Cambridge (Israel) edition or Curley's *Collected Works* II translation. **In full:** the Preface (fear as the engine of superstition — "men are most prone to every kind of superstition" when afraid); ch. 6, "Of Miracles" (nothing happens contrary to nature's fixed order; a "miracle" is an event whose cause we cannot explain by another known natural thing); ch. 14 (faith's seven dogmas reduced to obedience — justice and charity — and the separation thesis: faith requires "not so much true as pious dogmas"); ch. 15 (theology and philosophy governed separately — neither handmaid to the other); ch. 16 (the state founded: in the state of nature, **right is coextensive with power**; the contract as a transfer of nothing anyone can actually alienate); ch. 20 (the conclusion the book was written to reach: "the true aim of government is liberty," and freedom to philosophize cannot be granted or safely suppressed). **Summary pass:** chs. 1–2, on prophecy — prophets as men of vivid imagination and moral seriousness, not superior intellect; revelation adapted to its audience's opinions — and ch. 7's hermeneutic rule in outline (Scripture from Scripture, by its history, as nature is studied from nature). Then the ***Political Treatise*** selections: ch. 1 (men taken as they are, not as philosophers wish them to be), ch. 2 §§1–8 (natural right defined by power, restated without the *Ethics*' scaffolding), and ch. 11 — the unfinished final chapter, where the argument turns to democracy as the most natural regime, "closest to the freedom nature grants to each," and breaks off mid-course at his death.

**Alongside.** [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md) (Maimonides behind him on prophecy and interpretation; Hobbes beside him on the state; the Locke and Enlightenment lines running forward) and [08-bibliography.md](08-bibliography.md) — skim it before the capstone and choose your next book while your map of your own gaps is fresh (Nadler's *A Book Forged in Hell* is this week's natural companion if the TTP catches you).

**Media.** *The Partially Examined Life*, episodes 165–166, "Spinoza on Politics and Religion" (2017), as *review*: ep. 165 on the TTP's first movement (prophecy, miracles, interpretation), ep. 166 on chs. 12–20 and the *Political Treatise* (~51 min). These are regular episodes, not premium — the full discussions are the assigned listening, and they model two readers testing the separation thesis against hard cases, which is the capstone's home ground.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *State the TTP's separation thesis and the two errors it refuses.* Scripture teaches obedience — justice and charity — not philosophy; philosophy aims at truth, faith at pious conduct. The errors: making reason the handmaid of theology (philosophy's conclusions pre-censored by doctrine) and making theology rationalist (demanding Scripture teach metaphysics, Maimonides' error as Spinoza reads him). [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "The TTP's separation of theology and philosophy."
2. *Why can't a miracle, in Spinoza's sense, ever occur — and why is the argument stronger than "science says no"?* God's decrees and nature's order are one and the same; an event against that order would be God against himself. The strength: it is deduced from the metaphysics of Part I, not induced from experience — which is also its exposure: it stands or falls with E1. (Same file; [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).)
3. *Reconstruct "right = power" and say what it forbids the contract story to claim.* In the state of nature each thing's right extends exactly as far as its power; the social contract therefore cannot transfer a right anyone never had, and sovereignty lasts exactly as long as the multitude's power sustains it. It forbids the claim that obligation floats free of the power relations that enforce it. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Right = power."
4. *Why is democracy, for the unfinished Political Treatise, the most natural regime?* Because it transfers right to a multitude that retains it collectively rather than surrendering it to one or few — the arrangement under which each person "continues to live, as in the state of nature, by the right of nature" most nearly, and obedience is owed to a body of which one is a member. The work is unfinished; state that as part of the answer. (Same file.)
5. *Give ch. 20's argument for freedom to philosophize as statecraft, not just principle.* Suppressing judgment breeds flattery, resentment, and sedition; a state that lets men think what they like and say what they think removes the grievance that actually destabilizes states — Amsterdam's prosperity is his standing exhibit. Liberty is the aim because it is also the mechanism. [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md).

**Reconstructions.** The TTP separation argument and right = power, both reconstructed; the Part V core gets its +1-week touch. After this week, every argument on the cadence table has had its three spaced sessions inside the dossier or is explicitly handed to Stage 3's Session C rotation.

**Cards.** +5 (the TTP separation and freedom to philosophize, right = power and democracy, superstition, the miracle critique, prophecy and imagination) — deck complete at 33. The deck stays in the daily [FSRS](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html) queue permanently; Spinoza cards will surface during the Kant and German-idealism weeks exactly when the *Pantheismusstreit* needs them.

## Capstone essay (800–1,200 words)

Write in Week 7, after the TTP checkpoints, book closed for the first draft. **Prompt 1 is the one built to serve toward the Stage 3 gate essay**: it stages the era's central quarrel (Descartes against his greatest reader, on the mind's nature and freedom) and can be expanded with the Hume or Kant material the gate requires. The others stand as dossier essays in their own right.

1. **One thing, read twice.** Descartes split the human being in two and paid for it with the pineal gland; Spinoza's parallelism refuses the split. Reconstruct both positions from the primary texts, state the strongest objection to parallelism (correspondence without interaction: explanation or relabeling?), and judge whether Spinoza solves the mind–body problem or dissolves it — and whether dissolution, here, is a success. (Gate-eligible.)
2. **Freedom without free will.** Spinoza denies uncaused choice absolutely, then writes an *Ethics* whose last two parts are a therapy. Reconstruct the necessity, walk the remedies, and answer the exhortation paradox: is "freedom as understanding necessity" a genuine freedom, a redefinition that earns its keep, or a consolation prize? Argue one verdict against the best statement of another.
3. **What remains when the body dies.** Part V denies personal immortality and asserts the mind's eternity in adjacent propositions. Set out the two leading readings of E5P23 and its sequels, say which the text of P21 and P34S supports, and assess the cost: what is left of blessedness if nothing recognizably personal survives?
4. **The Treatise's wager.** The TTP separates theology from philosophy and concludes that the state's true aim is liberty. Reconstruct the argument from prophecy and miracles through ch. 16's right = power to ch. 20, and test it: does the separation protect philosophy, protect religion, or protect the state — and are those the same protection?
5. **The geometer's definitions.** Bennett's standing challenge: the *Ethics*' conclusions are only as good as definitions a critic may simply refuse. Take the monism argument: reconstruct it, identify the premise a Leibniz must reject, and judge whether Spinoza's defense — that his definitions capture real essences, so refusal owes an alternative — survives the objection or restates it.

## Mastery checklist — you are done when you can…

- □ Reconstruct substance monism from the E1 definitions, name the premise Leibniz must reject, and say what alternative account of substance he owes.
- □ State E1P29 and E1P33 in your own words and explain why Spinoza's necessity is stronger than determinism — no alternative world was ever possible.
- □ Explain parallelism (E2P7) and what it refuses, and deploy the pineal-gland critique as Spinoza's argument, not a joke at Descartes' expense.
- □ Define conatus, derive joy, sadness, and desire from it, and generate one complex affect (hope, envy, pity) on demand.
- □ Take a live case of anger or anxiety and run it from passion to action: inadequate idea, causes made adequate, freedom increased while the outcome stays necessitated.
- □ State why good and evil are not properties of things (E4 Preface), and what replaces them: virtue as power, and reason's dictates.
- □ Describe the free man (E4P67–73) and explain why he thinks of death least of all — without smuggling in immortality.
- □ Say precisely what is eternal in Part V and what is not, and name the two main scholarly readings of the eternity doctrine as a dispute.
- □ Summarize the TTP's separation of theology and philosophy and reproduce ch. 20's conclusion from its premises.
- □ Explain right = power, why democracy is the most natural regime in the *Political Treatise*, and why that work's unfinished state matters to the claim.
- □ Recommend, for a new reader, the right first text and edition — and talk them out of opening the *Ethics* unguided.
