# Study Plan — Ludwig Wittgenstein in Seven Weeks

## What this plan is, and where it plugs in

The [learning path](../../learning-path.md) meets Wittgenstein twice in **Stage 5A (Analytic)**, both times as a core figure (●): early Wittgenstein at row 4 — *Tractatus* §§1–4 and 6–7 in selection — and late Wittgenstein at row 9 — *Philosophical Investigations* §§1–38, 89–133, and 243–311. Between the rows stand the Vienna Circle via Ayer (row 5) and Quine (row 8), because the path's rule is absolute: **the *Investigations* is not read before the *Tractatus* and the positivists it argues against** — without them you are reading one side of a phone call. This dossier keeps that order, and expands the path's two slots into seven weeks: the *Tractatus* whole (it is only about 75 pages), the 1929 turn in outline, the *Investigations* at roughly double the path's depth — family resemblance in full, the rule-following sections the path omits, aspect-seeing — and *On Certainty* as the exit the path's budget cannot fund.

Run it as one block beginning when Stage 5A reaches row 4, with the stage's other figures in their own weeks around it — but read Ayer's *Language, Truth and Logic* selections, at minimum, before Week 3. Or split it on the path's strict order: Weeks 1–2 expand row 4, pause for rows 5–8, Weeks 3–7 expand row 9. Either way the hard dependencies hold: *Tractatus* before *Investigations*, positivists before the method remarks, and Stage 3's Kant behind you — Week 7's capstone has a prompt that spends him.

**Total cost, stated honestly: ≈38 hours of scheduled study across seven weeks (~5–6 hrs/week), plus the daily [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net) [FSRS](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html) queue (~10 min/day ≈ 7 hrs across the plan) — ≈45 hours all-in.**

**Reading texts.** Follow the path's rulings. *Tractatus*: **Pears & McGuinness (Routledge)**; Ogden/Ramsey (1922) is the historical translation, worth a glance, not the working text. *Investigations*: **Anscombe, rev. Hacker & Schulte, 4th ed. (Wiley-Blackwell, 2009)**, bilingual — cite Part I by section (§§1–693), Part II (*Philosophy of Psychology — A Fragment*) by chapter; aspect-seeing is PPF §xi. *On Certainty*: Anscombe & von Wright edition (Blackwell), cited by remark number. Quotations from the *Investigations* are in copyright: paraphrase first, quote short, check wording against the 4th edition.

**The guide is your companion, by design.** The library's [study guide to the *Investigations*](../../study/wittgenstein-investigations.md) — walkthroughs, four reconstructions, sixteen checkpoints with answers, the misreadings — is not duplicated here. Weeks 3–6 assign its blocks and checkpoint sets by number; the checkpoints printed below are the dossier's own additions, aimed at what the guide leaves to [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) and [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md).

## Week 1 — The world as facts: *Tractatus* 1–3 (~5.5 hrs)

**Primary.** Propositions 1–3 with their decimal sub-remarks, slowly. The world as the totality of *facts*, not things (1); facts analyzing into states of affairs, combinations of simple objects (2); the thought as logical picture of the facts (3). Then the document that says what the book is *for*: the 1919 letter to publisher Ludwig von Ficker — the book's point is ethical; it consists of the part written and all the part *not* written, and the unwritten part is the important one. Read it now, not at the end; [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md) gives its sources.

**Alongside.** [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md), [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md), and [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md)'s early-system sections.

**Media.** BBC Radio 4, *In Our Time*: "Wittgenstein" (first broadcast 4 December 2003; Bragg with Ray Monk, Barry Smith, and Marie McGinn; ~42 min) as *preview* — the whole two-philosophy arc in one sitting, so the early book is read knowing a second is coming.

**Checkpoints (book closed).**
1. *Why "facts, not things"?* Truth and falsity live at the level of states of affairs: "the cat is on the mat" is true or false of a fact, not of the cat. A picturing language requires the world as totality of facts (1, 1.1). [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).
2. *The embarrassment about simple objects?* Determinacy of sense demands them; Wittgenstein never produced a settled example. A demand of the theory, exhibited by nothing — a pressure point for 1929. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "The picture theory: a proposition means what it does by picturing a possible fact."
3. *What does the von Ficker letter forbid?* Concluding the book's point is its logic. It assigns the point to ethics and the unwritten part — the Vienna Circle's positivist reading (Week 2) largely does not survive the author's stated intent. [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).
4. *State bipolarity.* A genuine factual proposition can be true and can be false: it pictures a *possible* state of affairs. Bipolarity separates sense from tautology, which says nothing. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).

**Reconstructions.** The picture theory, first blank-page reconstruction (200–400 words); it enters the +2d/+1wk/+3wk rhythm below.

**Cards.** First 6 of [cards.tsv](cards.tsv) (picture theory, saying/showing, logical form, simples, tautology, the mystical) — after the week's reading, never before.

## Week 2 — The ladder and the silence: *Tractatus* 4–7, and the turn (~5.5 hrs)

**Primary.** Propositions 4–5 (the proposition as truth-function of elementary propositions; tautology and contradiction as the limiting cases that *say* nothing), the 5.6s (the limits of *my* language mean the limits of *my* world; the subject as a limit of the world, the eye not in its visual field), and 6–7: ethics as transcendental (6.421), the mystical, the ladder (6.54), proposition 7's silence. Then the 1929 turn **in outline only**, via the guide's closing section, "From the *Tractatus* to the *Investigations*: what is overcome" — Ramsey's color-exclusion problem ("red all over" and "green all over" exclude each other, though elementary propositions were to be independent) and the return to Cambridge. Detail belongs to Weeks 3–6; this week, know *that* the system cracked, and where.

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) (saying/showing; the self) and [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) ("Saying and showing: the ladder, and what to do with it"; "The limits of language and the self: solipsism at the boundary of the world"), including the dispute those arguments carry: *traditional* readings (truths shown, not said) against *resolute* readings (Diamond, Conant: plain nonsense, nothing salvaged). The plan does not settle it; the capstone makes you try.

**Media.** *Philosophize This!* #097 (Stephen West; both books, the *Tractatus* at its center) as *review*. Bounded seminar option: *The Partially Examined Life*, episode 7, "Wittgenstein's *Tractatus*: What Is There and Can We Talk About It?" (2009) — first half only (episode 8 continues the discussion). Sanctioned alternative: BBC Radio 3, *Free Thinking*, "Wittgenstein's *Tractatus* at 100" (2021), with Ray Monk among the guests.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *"What can be shown, cannot be said" (4.1212) — shown by what?* By meaningful propositions themselves: logical form is mirrored in them and cannot be stated from outside, since any statement already uses it. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md).
2. *Why are tautologies not counterexamples to the picture theory?* They picture nothing and so say nothing — but they are *senseless* (*sinnlos*), not *nonsensical* (*unsinnig*): they show the logical structure of language and world. Keep the distinction; the ladder debate turns on it. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).
3. *The ladder's self-application problem, and the two camps?* The book's own propositions are neither pictures nor tautologies. Traditional: they elucidate truths that show themselves. Resolute: they are plain nonsense whose work ends when the reader stops salvaging doctrine. [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).
4. *Color-exclusion as an internal objection?* The system *requires* elementary propositions to be independent; color-contents exclude each other as a matter of what they say. A fact the system cannot house, inside its own paradigm case. [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Ramsey."

**Reconstructions.** Saying/showing and the ladder; the picture theory gets its +1-week touch.

**Cards.** +3 (limits and the self; resolute vs. traditional; grammatical vs. empirical).

## Week 3 — The Augustinian picture: PI §§1–64, with the guide (~5.5 hrs)

**Primary.** The Preface (grave mistakes recognized; Ramsey and Sraffa credited; the album form), then §§1–64: Augustine's picture (§1), the builders (§2), the toolbox (§11), language on holiday (§38), the §43 slogan *with its hedge*, ostensive definition's stage-setting (§§28–30). The path's row-9 opening, at dossier depth.

**The guide.** Its §§1–38 walkthrough (ostensive definition included) read *after* your own first pass; reconstruction (a) written book-closed before you open the guide's version; its **checkpoints 1–5** as this week's extended set, book-closed, Session C.

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) (language-game, form of life, criteria, as introduced).

**Media.** None assigned — the week's support budget is the guide, and this book punishes skimming more than any other in the plan.

**Checkpoints (the dossier's own).**
1. *Why Augustine, not Frege or the Tractatus, as the opening exhibit?* The picture governs as a pre-philosophical temptation, not one school's doctrine; Augustine states it innocently, and the *Tractatus* is among its governed. (Guide, §§1–38 block.)
2. *What do the builders show, and not show?* A complete language working as calls within an activity — so naming cannot be language's essence. Not that ordinary language is that simple, or naming unimportant within it. (Guide, checkpoint 2.)
3. *§43's hedge — its force?* "For a large class of cases — though not for all." Dropping it turns a redirection of attention into a new essence of meaning: the exact move the book refuses. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).

**Reconstructions.** The Augustinian-picture argument (guide (a)); saying/showing gets its +3-week touch — notice, doing it, how completely its terms have changed hands.

**Cards.** +6 (Augustinian picture, meaning as use, language-game, form of life, ostensive definition, language on holiday).

## Week 4 — Games, method, therapy: PI §§65–133 (~5.5 hrs)

**Primary.** §§65–77: the confrontation staged at §65 ("you never say what language *is*"), answered at §66 — look and see; games surveyed, no common property; family resemblances; the thread of overlapping fibers (§67); blurred edges (§§69–71). Then §§89–133, the method remarks, read as the operating manual: §109 (arranging what we have always known), §116 (words back from metaphysical to everyday use), §122 (perspicuous representation), §124 (philosophy "leaves everything as it is"), §133 (methods, like different therapies). The path assigns §§89–133; the dossier restores §§65–88 as their foundation.

**The guide.** Its §§65–77 and §§109–133 walkthroughs; reconstruction (b) written before checking; **checkpoints 6, 7, and 16** as the extended set.

**Media.** *Philosophize This!* #231, "The Late Work of Wittgenstein — Language Games" (28 June 2025, ~26 min) as *review* — test its family-resemblance exposition against §67's thread.

**Checkpoints (the dossier's own).**
1. *Why is the thread (§67) an argument, not decoration?* It supplies the model the essentialist says is impossible: strength — a concept's unity and usability — without one continuous fiber. If coherent for thread, "no essence, no unity" fails for concepts; the burden shifts to looking. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Family resemblance: a concept without an essence still holds together."
2. *§124 governs what, and not what?* Language: philosophy does not legislate grammar. Not everything else: the extension into political quietism (Gellner's charge) adds a premise the text lacks; the tendency is a debate, not a quotation. (Guide, checkpoint 16.)
3. *What is a perspicuous representation for, if not explanation?* The surveyable arrangement in which a confusion's *source* becomes visible — understanding as seeing connections; the therapy's working instrument. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).

**Reconstructions.** Family resemblance; the Augustinian picture gets its +1-week touch.

**Cards.** +5 (family resemblance, blurred edges, perspicuous representation, therapy, §124).

## Week 5 — Rules: PI §§138–242, and Kripke, bounded (~6 hrs)

**Primary.** §§138–184 (understanding: no inner process or image fixes a meaning's application), then the rule-following core whole: the pupil continuing 1004, 1008 beyond 1000 (§185); the paradox at §201 *with Wittgenstein's response in the same section* — a way of grasping a rule that is *not an interpretation*; §202 (obeying a rule is a practice; thinking one is obeying is not obeying); bedrock (§217); agreement in judgments, not opinions (§§241–242). The path omits this block; the dossier treats it as the late book's center of gravity.

**The bounded provocation.** Kripke, *Wittgenstein on Rules and Private Language* (1982): the core of chapter 2 only — the skeptical paradox and "quus," a function agreeing with plus on every case so far but diverging on the next (~25 pages) — plus a skim of the "skeptical solution." Read it as [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) instructs: the strongest rival in the room, labeled *interpretation*. Kripke presented the argument as Wittgenstein's *as it struck him*; Baker and Hacker, prominently, hold that §201's paradox is to be *dissolved* — its premise rejected — not solved by a community theory. "Kripkenstein" is the standard label for the possibly distinct philosopher of that book.

**The guide.** Its §§185–242 walkthrough (Kripke warning included) and reconstruction (c), written before checking; **checkpoints 8–11** as the extended set.

**Media.** *The Partially Examined Life*, episode 55, "Wittgenstein on Language" (2012; PI §§1–133 and 191–360) — its rule-following stretch as *review*. The early episodes now sit in PEL's premium tier; the bounded use survives on the free preview plus your text work if you do not subscribe.

**Checkpoints (the dossier's own).**
1. *Why is an interpretation "only another sign"?* It is itself a formula, picture, or table — something that must in turn be applied, and can be variously taken. Stacking interpretations defers the question forever; grasp must somewhere be unmediated, shown in going on. (§201; guide, checkpoint 9.)
2. *What does quus add to §185's pupil?* Generality and menace: the pupil is one corrigible anomaly; quus argues *no fact* — past use, dispositions, inner states — constitutes my having meant one function rather than a deviant one, for anyone. Whether that escalation is Wittgenstein's is the dispute; keep the cases labeled separately. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "The rule-following paradox: no interpretation reaches the ground."
3. *Bedrock (§217): defeat or design?* Design: justification is practice-internal, and demanding a justification of the practice itself, from outside all practices, repeats §201's regress. "This is simply what I do" is where the concept lives. (Guide, checkpoint 11.)
4. *Community or practice?* §202 and §§241–242 ground rule-following in practice and agreement in judgments. The community view: correctness requires a community of checkers. Individualist replies: the appeal is to *technique*, not head-count — a solitary agent trained into a practice keeps seeming-right distinct from being-right. The text underdetermines the strong claim; flag, don't settle. [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).

**Reconstructions.** Rule-following — the plan's heaviest single reconstruction; family resemblance gets its +1-week touch.

**Cards.** +5 (rule-following, beetle — the deck places it here, since §202 draws the privacy conclusion in advance — bedrock, agreement in judgments, Kripkenstein/quus).

## Week 6 — The inner, and seeing-as: PI §§243–315 + PPF selections (~5.5 hrs)

**Primary.** §§243–315 whole: the question posed at §243 (a language in principle unintelligible to anyone but its speaker); the diary (§258 — whatever seems right is right, "and that only means that here we can't talk about 'right'"); the newspaper (§265 — memory cannot independently check memory); the positive account (§244 — sensation-words grown from natural expression); the beetle (§293). Then the excursion: PPF §xi selections — the duck-rabbit, the dawning of an aspect against continuous seeing, aspect-blindness.

**The guide.** Its §§243–315 walkthrough and reconstruction (d), written before checking; its Part II aspect-seeing block; **checkpoints 12–14** as the extended set.

**Alongside.** [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) ("The private language argument: no criterion, no language"; "The beetle in the box: the private object cancels out"; "Aspect-seeing: the duck-rabbit, and seeing what does not change"), with scope discipline throughout: the target is a *picture* of sensation-language, not the existence of pains, solipsism, or other minds, in either direction.

**Media.** *The Partially Examined Life*, episode 56, "Wittgenstein, Continued" (2012) as *review* — family resemblance revisited and the private language argument argued over; the hosts' behaviorist leanings are themselves a checkpoint (test them against §244 and the guide's misreading 2).

**Checkpoints (the dossier's own).**
1. *Does the castaway refute §258?* No — he concedes it: his diary works by borrowing criteria from a public practice; §243's stipulation strips exactly that borrowing away. (Guide, reconstruction (d), reply.)
2. *What does the beetle cancel?* The private object *as the word's meaning*: contents could change, differ, or be absent while use proceeds unchanged. Not pain — a theory of its naming. (§293; guide, checkpoint 13.)
3. *Why is "interpretation" wrong for the duck-rabbit flip?* When the aspect dawns you do not see neutral lines and infer "rabbit" — you *see* a rabbit; yet the drawing is unchanged, and you can be told to try. Neither plain seeing nor detached judgment. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Aspect-seeing: the duck-rabbit, and seeing what does not change."

**Reconstructions.** The private language argument; rule-following gets its +1-week touch — write it with §258 in hand and notice what the two arguments share.

**Cards.** +5 (private language, criteria vs. symptoms, aspect-seeing, aspect-blindness, the duck-rabbit).

## Week 7 — Hinges, consolidation, capstone (~5.5 hrs + essay)

**Primary.** *On Certainty* (written 1949–51; published 1969), selections: §§1–65 close (Moore's "I know" propositions and why claiming to know them misplaces them), §§94–99 (the world-picture; the river-bed at §97), §§341–343 (the *hinges* on which questioning turns), the closing remarks in skim. Then consolidation: [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md) and [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) in full, and the guide's checkpoint 15 (two things abandoned from the *Tractatus*, one that survives) taken cold as the exit examination.

**Media.** *The Partially Examined Life*, episode 309, "Wittgenstein On Certainty" (Part One, 2023; Part Two is the sanctioned extra) as *review* — a close reading of this week's text, answering its Moore episodes (307–308), which you may sample if Moore's side feels thin.

**Checkpoints — the comparative set (the capstone's raw material).**
1. *A hinge is not a well-confirmed belief — why?* Beliefs are known, doubted, checked: they move. Hinges stand fast *for* us so checking can happen (§§341–343) — the scaffolding of thought, not its traffic. The river-bed (§97) shifts, but slowly, and not at the point under test. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Hinge certainty: doubt presupposes what it cannot doubt."
2. *Bedrock to hinges, one sentence each.* §217: justification runs out at practice — this is what I do. *On Certainty*: doubt runs out at what stands fast — this is what I cannot coherently question while questioning anything else. The second is the first, turned from rules to knowledge. [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md).
3. *One Wittgenstein or two?* One lifelong struggle against bewitchment by language (4.112 already: philosophy as activity, not doctrine), conducted by two incompatible theories of meaning; the Preface's wish to publish old and new together presupposes both contrast and continuity. State your emphasis; the capstone may demand it. [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).

**Reconstructions.** Final touches: re-run the picture theory (Week 1) and the private language argument (Week 6) cold in Session C. Anything that fails moves into the next stage's Session C rotation, not into extra drilling.

**Cards.** +4 (hinges, world-picture, doubt presupposes certainty, the *Nachlass*/two-Wittgensteins) — deck complete at 34, and permanent in the FSRS queue.

## Capstone essay (800–1,200 words)

Write in Week 7, after the comparative checkpoints, book closed for the first draft. Stage 5 has no gate essay of the earlier stages' kind; the path closes instead with its own **capstone** (§9.3: ~2,000 words, re-answering one of Stage 0's five problems with at least six authors across at least three eras). This essay stands alongside it, and prompts 1 and 3 are built to feed it — prompt 1 supplies the *knowledge* or *mind* problem's limits thread, prompt 3 its Wittgenstein. Write the dossier essay first; the path capstone can then quote its own author.

1. **Two ways to draw a limit (cross-dossier).** Kant drew the limit of knowledge from inside reason: the conditions of possible experience mark what thought can reach; the thing in itself can be thought but not known. Wittgenstein, twice, drew the limit of *sense*: in the *Tractatus*, from inside language, the beyond honored in silence; in the *Investigations*, by dissolving the idea that the limit was a line at all. Using the [Kant dossier](../immanuel-kant/README.md) and this one, argue which drawing survives its author's own self-application problem — Kant's reason judging reason, Wittgenstein's propositions indicting themselves — and what the survivor implies for philosophy's remaining work.
2. **The ladder on trial.** State the resolute reading at full strength (plain nonsense; no ineffable truths; therapy complete only when nothing is salvaged) and the traditional reply (truths about logical form and the mystical, shown not said). Judge on two exhibits only — the von Ficker letter and 6.54 — then say what your verdict does to the continuity question.
3. **Kripkenstein.** Reconstruct the skeptical paradox from quus and the skeptical solution; then give the Baker–Hacker dissolution its best run — the premise that grasp must be an interpretation or a meaning-constituting fact is the confusion §201 names. Decide: distortion of Wittgenstein, development from him, or both at once — and name the exact premise where the two part company.
4. **Therapy: liberation or abdication?** Russell's contempt and Gellner's *Words and Things* press one point from two directions: a philosophy that only dissolves problems has resigned from answering any. Defend or convict §124's conception through one worked case from this plan — the diary (§258) or the pupil (§185) — showing what the therapeutic treatment *does* there, and whether anything a theory would call an answer has been lost.
5. **Hinges against the skeptic.** *On Certainty* refuses both Moore ("I know here is a hand" answers the skeptic) and the skeptic (universal doubt is coherent). Reconstruct the refusal, then test it against one live doubt — a simulation hypothesis, a brain in a vat, a five-minute-old world. Does the hinge framework *answer* the doubt, *disqualify* it, or merely decline it — and is declining, here, enough?

## Mastery checklist — you are done when you can…

- □ Rebuild the picture theory from facts, objects, and bipolarity, and say what a proposition shares with the fact it pictures — and why that sharing can be shown but not said.
- □ Apply the ladder to the *Tractatus*'s own sentences, state the resolute and traditional verdicts, and say which exhibit — the letter, 6.54 — cuts which way.
- □ State §43 exactly, hedge included, and explain what the hedge prevents.
- □ Run the builders (§2) and ostensive definition (§§28–30) as one case: naming presupposes a practice; it cannot found one.
- □ Explain family resemblance from §66's survey and §67's thread, and defend a blurred-edge concept with a fresh example of your own.
- □ Reconstruct §185's pupil and §201's paradox *with its immediate response*, and mark precisely where Kripke's version departs into Kripkenstein.
- □ Reconstruct the diary (§258) and the newspaper (§265), and say what the beetle (§293) cancels and what it leaves standing.
- □ Distinguish an aspect's dawning from continuous seeing, and say why seeing-as is neither plain seeing nor interpretation.
- □ State a hinge, distinguish it from a well-confirmed belief, and connect *On Certainty*'s bedrock to §217's spade.
- □ Give the reception facts cold: the Vienna Circle's partly mistaken embrace; the *Nachlass* problem (some twenty thousand pages, editorially arranged) and what it forbids claiming; the poker anecdote told *as* a contested anecdote.
- □ Place him in conversation: what he takes from Frege and Russell and refuses in both; what Austin, Ryle, and Anscombe take from him; and which book you would hand a new reader first — against his own instruction, if that is your verdict, with reasons.
