# Study Plan — Friedrich Nietzsche in Eight Weeks

## What this plan is, and where it plugs in

The [learning path](../../learning-path.md) meets Nietzsche in **Stage 4**, week 11, as a core figure (●): *Twilight of the Idols* selections, then *On the Genealogy of Morals* whole, then *Beyond Good and Evil* selections, then *The Gay Science* selections (§§125, 341, 373), with *Zarathustra* last and optional as a literary pass. This focus study keeps the path's order and its rules — and expands the block from one heavy week into eight, restoring the two books the path's budget had to skip (the early *Birth of Tragedy* and the free-spirit turn) and the 1888 finale the path could only point at. Run it when Stage 4 reaches him: **Kant before him, always** (the path is blunt: do not start Nietzsche at all before Kant and Schopenhauer — "without them the aphorisms decay into slogans"), Feuerbach's religion-as-critique as the on-ramp, and Mill and Marx as his Stage 4 neighbors, whose answers to the gate question he is the third voice against.

One predecessor is designed, not incidental: the [Schopenhauer dossier](../arthur-schopenhauer/README.md). Nietzsche is the reply that dossier kept preparing — he takes Schopenhauer's diagnosis (existence as striving and suffering) and refuses its prescription (denial of the will) at exactly one premise. If you ran the Schopenhauer focus study, its cards are still in your [FSRS](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html) queue and Week 8's capstone is built to cash them in; if you did not, the path's selections-level Schopenhauer is enough, but expect to lean harder on [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) where the inheritance is mapped. When you finish, return to Stage 4 as normal. The Stage 4 gate essay ("Is morality a discovery, an invention, or a symptom? — Mill, Marx and Nietzsche give three different answers; defend one against the other two") is unaffected, and the capstone below has one prompt built to serve toward it.

**Total cost, stated honestly: ≈50 hours of scheduled study across eight weeks (~6 hrs/week of reading, dossier work, and media), plus the daily [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net) queue (~10 minutes, ~6 days a week ≈ 8 hrs across the eight weeks) — ≈58 hours all-in.** That buys the whole arc a one-week treatment cannot: the early system, the free-spirit laboratory, the *Genealogy* whole with its guide, *Beyond Good and Evil* and *The Gay Science* at depth rather than in extract, a deliberately bounded *Zarathustra* pass, and the 1888 books read as his self-verdict.

**Reading texts.** Follow the learning path's rulings exactly. Default: **Walter Kaufmann's Vintage translations** — the *Genealogy* in Kaufmann & Hollingdale, *Beyond Good and Evil* in Kaufmann, *The Gay Science* in Kaufmann. The *Genealogy* has two other sanctioned editions, either fully acceptable: Diethe, edited by Ansell-Pearson (Cambridge Texts), and Clark & Swensen (Hackett). *Beyond Good and Evil* may instead be read in Judith Norman's Cambridge translation. **Not Thomas Common** (dated and unreliable, however cheap the reprint). **Not *The Will to Power* as a book**: it is a posthumous construction from the notebooks, assembled by his sister Elisabeth's circle and demoted by the Colli–Montinari critical edition (KSA), which prints the *Nachlass* chronologically. The citation rule for the whole plan, from [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md): cite only what Nietzsche published; where a week or a card rests on notebook material, it is flagged as *Nachlass* and used as evidence about his workshop, never as his considered view. Section numbers (§) are stable across all sanctioned editions, and everything below cites by book, part, and section.

**The order, and why.** The path's order is itself an argument, and the plan keeps it. *Twilight* (written last, read first) states the late positions in their most compressed form, so the works that argue them are read with the conclusions in view and the premises exposed. The *Birth of Tragedy* and the free-spirit books then rewind to show how those positions were earned — the Schopenhauerian youth, the break, the laboratory of the drives. The *Genealogy* sits at the center (Week 4) because it is the hinge: his most systematic book, the one the path already centers, and the text the dossier's [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) is mostly built from. *Beyond Good and Evil* and *The Gay Science* follow at depth — the typology of morals and the affirmative doctrines (death of God, *amor fati*, recurrence) in the forms the *Genealogy* presupposes. *Zarathustra* comes where the path puts it: late, as literature, after the arguments it dramatizes are already in hand. The 1888 books close the primary reading because *Ecce Homo* is Nietzsche reading Nietzsche — it only works as a finale. Week 8 steps outside the corpus: reception, the two modern Nietzsches, and the capstone.

## Week 1 — Orientation and the aphorism method (~6 hrs)

**Primary.** The Preface to *On the Genealogy of Morals*, whole and slowly — this is Nietzsche's own instruction manual for reading him, placed first on purpose. His demand: an aphorism "has not been 'deciphered' when it has simply been read"; it requires exegesis, rumination — his word is *Wiederkäuen*, chewing the cud. Then *Twilight of the Idols* selections, in this order: "The Problem of Socrates"; "'Reason' in Philosophy"; "Morality as Anti-Nature"; "The Four Great Errors"; "The 'Improvers' of Mankind"; "What the Germans Lack"; selections from "Skirmishes of an Untimely Man"; "What I Owe to the Ancients." *Twilight* is 1888 Nietzsche at maximum compression — the late positions (decadence, the errors of moral causality, the case against Socrates) stated as verdicts whose arguments the coming weeks will supply.

**Alongside.** [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md) (the life, with its legends flagged — keep its hedges in mind; Week 7 tests them), [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md) (the corpus map and the *Nachlass* ruling), and [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) in full (the architecture and glossary — the week it pays for itself, above all the treatments of perspectivism, decadence, and the aphorism as a form).

**Media.** *Philosophize This!* episode #090 ("Nietzsche Pt. 1 — God is Dead And So Is Captain Morgan," Stephen West, 2016) as a *preview* before *Twilight* — the first of his four-part Nietzsche run (#090–#093), an orientation to the death of God and the project it launches. Popularizer's compression: orientation only, never evidence.

**Checkpoints (book closed; short answers given, full treatments in the dossier files named).**
1. *What does the Genealogy's Preface demand of a reader of aphorisms, in Nietzsche's own terms?* That an aphorism properly stamped and molded has not been deciphered when it has merely been read: it needs an exegesis, a rumination — slow reading as a discipline, the opposite of slogan-collecting. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), on method.
2. *In "The Problem of Socrates," why is Socrates a symptom rather than a cause?* Because decadence — instincts grown anarchic, life turning against itself — was already advanced in Athens; Socrates' dialectics is the weapon a declining culture reached for, and "rationality at any price" is itself a sickness posing as the cure. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "Decadence"; [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Socrates."
3. *Name the four great errors.* Confusion of cause and effect; false causality; imaginary causes; and the error of free will — the last invented so that men could be made responsible, guilty, punishable. (*Twilight*, "The Four Great Errors.")
4. *Why does the plan read Twilight — written in 1888 — in Week 1, before the books of the 1870s–80s?* Because it is the path's order and a sound one: *Twilight* is the late system's table of contents, verdicts in compressed form; the earlier weeks then recover the arguments at full length. [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md).
5. *State perspectivism in one sentence, and why it is not relativism.* Knowledge is always from a perspective structured by drives and affects, but more perspectives, honestly set against each other, give a *more* complete concept of the thing — GM III §12's "more eyes" — a discipline of objectivity, not a license for "anything goes." [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Perspectivism and objectivity."

**Reconstructions.** Write the first blank-page reconstruction (200–400 words) of the perspectivism argument by week's end; it enters the +2d/+1wk/+3wk rhythm (see the cadence table below).

**Cards.** Import the first 5 cards of [cards.tsv](cards.tsv) (death of God/nihilism, perspectivism, and the method concepts) when the week's reading is done — never before.

## Week 2 — The early system: *The Birth of Tragedy* (~6 hrs)

**Primary.** *The Birth of Tragedy* (1872) §§1–10 (the Apollonian and Dionysian drives and their fusion in tragedy), §§15–18 (the death of tragedy at the hands of Socratic optimism), §§24–25 (the metaphysical consolation), with §§11–14 and 19–23 read as summary; then the 1886 preface, "An Attempt at a Self-Criticism," whole — the old Nietzsche grading the young one. Read this book with the Schopenhauer dossier still warm: its metaphysics — the primal unity behind individuation, existence justified only as an aesthetic phenomenon — is Schopenhauer's system transposed into art, and its music doctrine is the young Nietzsche's version of the Book 3 anomaly you met there (music as the immediate language of the underlying unity).

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) (Apollo/Dionysus in the glossary, and the early system's place in the architecture) and [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md) (the Wagner household at Tribschen, Ritschl, and what the book cost him professionally).

**Media.** *The Great Philosophers* (BBC, 1987), episode 11: Bryan Magee in conversation with **J. P. Stern** on Nietzsche (~45 min) — watch as *review*, after the Self-Criticism. Stern is the learning path's own named companion for this figure (his Fontana *Friedrich Nietzsche* is the "Magee/Stern" of the path's companion rule), and the dialogue form is the path's recommended discussion substitute (§2.6): two people arguing slowly, with Magee interrupting to clarify. Select episodes circulate on YouTube; the conversation also survives in the series' book.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *Define the Apollonian and the Dionysian as drives, not as gods.* Apollo: form, image, dream, the principium individuationis made beautiful. Dionysus: intoxication, the dissolution of the individual, the primal unity of suffering and joy beneath individuation. Tragedy at its peak is their fusion, not Apollo's victory. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), glossary.
2. *How does Socratism kill tragedy?* By insisting that existence must be intelligible to be justified — knowledge as virtue, the optimistic dialectic of Euripides and Socrates — which expels the Dionysian truth tragedy existed to bear. [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md), "Socrates"; [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), "Apollonian and Dionysian."
3. *What does the 1886 Self-Criticism retract, and what does it keep?* It retracts the book's romanticism, its Wagnerian hopes, its Schopenhauerian formulas — "it should have sung, not spoken." It keeps the question: the problem of science and art, existence affirmed in full knowledge of its terror, against every moral interpretation. (Same file as your notes; [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) on the period readings.)
4. *State the difference between this book's music and Schopenhauer's, in one sentence each.* Schopenhauer: music copies the Will directly, bypassing the Ideas — an anomaly his system asserts more than explains. Young Nietzsche: Dionysian music is the dissolution of individuation itself, the primal unity heard — the metaphysics is Schopenhauer's, but the valuation has already flipped from consolation to affirmation. [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md).
5. *Why is "existence is justified only as an aesthetic phenomenon" not yet the mature position?* Because it still justifies existence *to* someone, on metaphysical grounds supplied by the primal unity — a consolation the later Nietzsche withdraws, keeping the affirmation and dropping the metaphysics. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).

**Reconstructions.** The Apollonian/Dionysian argument reconstructed by week's end; perspectivism gets its +1-week touch in Session C.

**Cards.** +4 (Apollonian, Dionysian, and the early-system concepts).

## Week 3 — The free-spirit turn (~5.5 hrs)

**Primary.** *Human, All Too Human* (1878): selections from Part One, "Of First and Last Things" (including §1, the "chemistry of concepts and feelings," and §9), and from the chapter "On the History of Moral Feelings"; selections from *The Wanderer and His Shadow* (1880). Then *Daybreak* (1881): the 1886 Preface (slow reading, again, as method) and a selection of the moral-psychology books — the aphorisms on the drives, on pity and the morality of custom — in your edition's arrangement. Read 1878 biographically as well as philosophically: the dedication to Voltaire, on the centenary of his death, was a public declaration of war on Bayreuth, and Wagner read it as one. This is the laboratory period — historical philosophy, suspicion turned on morality's origins, the drive-psychology in embryo — where the *Genealogy*'s method is invented a decade early.

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) (the free spirit's stages, and genealogy as a method before it had the name) and [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md) (Paul Rée, the break with Wagner, and what the friendship with Lou Salomé will later cost — the file's forward look).

**Media.** *Philosophize This!* episode #091 ("Nietzsche Pt. 2 — The Will to Power," 2016) as *review* after *Daybreak*. One caution, and it is the dossier's standing one: where the episode leans on "the book" *The Will to Power*, discount accordingly — the doctrine's published basis is thinner and more hypothetical than the notebook construction suggests, and [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md)'s card on it flags the status dispute. Check the episode's claims against the published texts you have now read.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *What is "historical philosophy," as HAH §1 announces it?* The refusal of eternal facts and metaphysical origins: concepts and feelings have a chemistry — they developed, from errors and mixtures — and the philosopher's task is the small, unapparent truths of that development, not the primal ground. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).
2. *Why was the Voltaire dedication an act of war?* Voltaire stands for Enlightenment, France, clarity, and the war on priestcraft — everything Wagner's Bayreuth, with its German nationalism, anti-Semitism, and Schopenhauerian-Christian *Parsifal* piety, was becoming. The dedication announced the free spirit's allegiance in public. [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md).
3. *What changes between the Birth of Tragedy's psychology and this period's?* The metaphysical unity drops away; what remains is a naturalistic psychology of drives, habits, and inherited errors — the self as a polity of drives rather than a soul. The *Genealogy*'s machinery is assembled here. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md).
4. *What is a "free spirit" at this stage, and how does the figure develop later?* Here: the emancipated thinker who has unlearned reverence and tests inherited values against history and physiology — still an Enlightenment figure. By *Beyond Good and Evil* the free spirit has become the herald of the "philosophers of the future," a creator of values, not only a critic of them (Week 5 tests the difference). [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), glossary.

**Reconstructions.** The genealogical method (as it appears in embryo in HAH and the *Genealogy*'s Preface) reconstructed; Week 1's perspectivism gets its +3-week touch.

**Cards.** +4 (genealogy, free spirit, herd, and the psychology concepts).

## Week 4 — The center: *On the Genealogy of Morals*, whole (~7 hrs)

**Primary.** The *Genealogy* (1887) whole: Preface (quick re-read — you read it in Week 1; it reads differently now), then Essay I ("'Good and Evil,' 'Good and Bad'"), Essay II ("'Guilt,' 'Bad Conscience,' and Related Matters"), and Essay III ("What Is the Meaning of Ascetic Ideals?") in order and unabridged. This is the plan's heaviest week and its load-bearing one — the learning path's center of the Nietzsche block, treated here at the depth the path's budget cannot.

**The guide is your companion, by design.** Work with [the study guide to the *Genealogy*](../../study/nietzsche-genealogy.md) in Session B, exactly as the guide itself prescribes: read its essay-by-essay section *before* each essay (the skim-for-structure pass), write your own reconstruction of each essay's argument book-closed *before* reading the guide's four reconstructions (a)–(d) as the check, and take the guide's checkpoint set — sixteen questions, with its printed answers — as this week's **extended checkpoint set**, all of it book-closed, in Session C. The dossier does not duplicate the guide's reconstructions or checkpoints; the five checkpoints below are the dossier's own additions, aimed at what the guide leaves to [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md).

**Media.** Two assignments, both as review after Essay III. BBC Radio 4, *In Our Time*: "Nietzsche's Genealogy of Morality" (first broadcast 12 January 2017; Melvyn Bragg with Stephen Mulhall, Fiona Hughes, and Keith Ansell-Pearson, ~45 min) — a scholarly panel on exactly this book, and Ansell-Pearson edited one of your sanctioned editions. And the bounded Sadler assignment: Gregory B. Sadler's *Genealogy of Morals* **Core Concepts** videos (YouTube, "Gregory B. Sadler — That Philosophy Guy") — the three on the First Essay's machinery only: the original valuation, good vs. bad as against good vs. evil; the slave revolt and the transvaluation of values; and his video on the meanings and purposes of punishment for Essay II. Three short lectures, watched per-essay as review; his full class-session series on the book exists but is *not* assigned — it is a course, not a companion.

**Checkpoints (the dossier's own; the guide's sixteen are the extended set).**
1. *What exactly is "creative" about the slave revolt?* Ressentiment itself becomes creative and gives birth to values: unable to act, the priestly type invents "evil," and with it a whole imaginary revenge and a new table of goods in which its own impotence is renamed virtue. GM I §§7–10; [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "The slave revolt and the priestly inversion."
2. *Trace bad conscience in three steps.* (i) Enclosure within society blocks the outward discharge of the instincts of cruelty and domination; (ii) those instincts turn inward — man tears at himself, and the soul acquires depth; (iii) the priest and the Christian God maximize the debt into guilt that can never be discharged. GM II §§16–22; [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Bad conscience."
3. *What does the ascetic ideal do for suffering humanity — and what does it cost?* It gives suffering a *meaning* — man would rather will nothingness than not will — and so preserves the suffering animal from suicidal nihilism; the cost is that the meaning is life turned against life, a will willing its own diminishment. GM III §§13, 28; [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "The ascetic ideal."
4. *What is the sovereign individual, and why does he appear in Essay II rather than Essay I?* The ripest fruit of the morality of custom: the human being bred to be *permitted to promise*, master of a free will in the only sense Nietzsche honors — earned self-mastery, not metaphysical liberty. He is the counter-ideal the genealogy of conscience makes visible. GM II §§1–3; [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "The sovereign individual and promising."
5. *Press the genetic-fallacy objection in one sentence, and give the defender's reply in one sentence.* Objection: a value's disreputable origin does not settle its present worth. Reply: genealogy's work is to destroy morality's aura of purity and self-evidence and force the question it cannot ask itself — what is the value of these values? — after which evaluation proceeds on grounds the genealogy has cleared. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "The slave revolt and the priestly inversion"; the guide's "How to use" section makes the same distinction.

**Reconstructions.** Three this week, the plan's core: the slave revolt (GM I), bad conscience (GM II), the ascetic ideal (GM III) — each written before the guide's corresponding reconstruction is opened.

**Cards.** +6 (master/slave typology, ressentiment, slave revolt, bad conscience, sovereign individual, ascetic ideal).

## Week 5 — *Beyond Good and Evil*: the philosophy of the future (~6.5 hrs)

**Primary.** *Beyond Good and Evil* (1886) at depth: Part One, "On the Prejudices of Philosophers" (§§1–23, whole — §17 on the superstition of the "I" and §19's analysis of willing are the load-bearing sections), Part Five, "Natural History of Morals" (§§186–203, whole), and Part Nine, "What Is Noble?" (§§257–296, whole — §260 restates the master/slave typology as doctrine, §259 gives will to power its published life-formula); selections from Part Four's epigrams as relief and evidence of range. Three sections get dossier-level depth treatment: §36 (will to power, stated as a hypothesis — "supposing that…"), §46 (faith and the slave revolt in religion), and §211 (the philosopher as commander and legislator of values).

**Alongside.** [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) (the "no doer behind the deed," will to power, and typology arguments) — and note the companion rule from the learning path: a Nietzsche companion is read **after** the first pass at the *Genealogy*, never before. That condition is now met, which is why Week 7's sanctioned filler is Leiter.

**Media.** *The Partially Examined Life*, episode 11: "Nietzsche's Immoralism: What Is Ethics, Anyway?" (2009) — a long, close discussion (~1 hr 50 min) of the *Genealogy*'s first two essays together with *Beyond Good and Evil* chapters 1, 5, and 9: precisely last week's book and this week's parts. Assign the episode's first half after Part One, the balance after Part Nine; treat it as the week's seminar — hosts who disagree with each other and with him, which is the correct register.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *Reconstruct "no doer behind the deed."* Grammar seduces us into doubling the deed: lightning *and* its flash, strength *and* its expression, a neutral subject free to be strong or not. There is no being behind the doing; the doer is a fiction added to the deed — and slave morality needs that fiction, so the strong can be *blamed* for being strong. BGE §17; GM I §13. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "No doer behind the deed."
2. *What is the exact logical form of BGE §36?* A supposition, honestly labeled: *supposing* all organic functions could be traced to will to power, one would have gained the right to define all efficient force as will to power — an experiment in interpretation, offered as such. Any card or summary that states it as a demonstrated metaphysics has overstated the published text. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Will to power."
3. *What does §186 demand that previous moral philosophy never supplied?* A typology of morals — a comparative natural history of the many moralities that have ruled — as the precondition of the real problem: the value of morality itself. Description before verdict; the *Genealogy* you read last week is the execution. (BGE Part Five.)
4. *Distinguish the two moral types of §260 from the herd morality of Part Five.* Master morality: good/bad, self-glorification, values created from abundance. Slave morality: good/evil, values created from ressentiment against an external enemy. Herd morality is slave morality become the modern European norm — utility, pity, and security as the goods of the flock. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), glossary.

**Reconstructions.** "No doer behind the deed" and the will-to-power hypothesis reconstructed; the Week 4 trio gets its +1-week touches in Session C.

**Cards.** +4 (no doer behind the deed, will to power, pathos of distance, revaluation).

## Week 6 — Affirmation: *The Gay Science* and *Zarathustra* (~6.5 hrs)

**Primary.** *The Gay Science* (1882; expanded 1887) core, at minimum: §108 (the Buddha's shadow — God's shadow still to be vanquished), §124 (on the infinite sea), §125 (the madman), §276 (*amor fati* as a New Year's resolution), §290 ("one thing is needful" — giving style to one's character), §341 ("the greatest weight" — recurrence), §343 (the meaning of our cheerfulness — the news arriving at last, in Book V), §373 ("science" and its faith), §382 (the great health). Then the literary pass, *Thus Spoke Zarathustra*, selections only, in the path's spirit: the Prologue (§§1–5), "On the Three Metamorphoses," "On the Despisers of the Body," "On the Gift-Giving Virtue" (selections) — and "On Little Old and Young Women," read whole and in context: the notorious whip sentence is spoken there, by the old woman to Zarathustra, inside a discourse on women that [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) treats in full — the feminist debate over mask, irony, and plain prejudice. Read it as an object of that criticism, not as an aphorism to collect, and do not let the dossier sanitize it for you.

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) (recurrence, *amor fati*, Übermensch and last man in the glossary) and [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) ("Eternal recurrence — test or cosmology," where the dispute is stated with its evidence on both sides; the notebook arguments for a literal cosmology are *Nachlass* and are flagged as such wherever they appear).

**Media.** *Philosophize This!* episode #092 ("Nietzsche Pt. 3 — Thus Spoke Zarathustra," 2016) as *preview* before the Zarathustra selections — the one case where a preview earns its place, since the book's genre (parody gospel) is itself a barrier. And episode #159 ("The Creation of Meaning — Nietzsche — Amor Fati," 2021) as *review* after §276 and §341; its companion #158, on the ascetic ideal, is the sanctioned extra if Week 4's Essay III still feels unsettled.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *What differs between §125 (1882) and §343 (1887)?* The event is the same — God is dead — but §125's madman speaks too early to a laughing marketplace, while §343 registers the news *arriving* for "we" free spirits: the horizon wiped away is also an open sea, and the first response Nietzsche records is gratitude and cheerfulness, not despair. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "The death of God and nihilism."
2. *State the recurrence test's exact demand.* Not belief in a cosmology but a response: this life, in every detail, eternally recurring — could you will it again, suffering included, without editing? The demon's question is a selective test of affirmation, a formula for *amor fati*. GS §341; [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Eternal recurrence."
3. *Give the three metamorphoses and what each carries.* Camel: reverent bearing of inherited weights ("thou shalt"). Lion: the sacred No, freedom *from* — but no creation yet. Child: innocence and forgetting, a new beginning, the sacred Yes — creation of values. (Zarathustra, Part One.)
4. *What is the last man, and why is he the Übermensch's true opposite?* Not the slave of ressentiment but the comfortable end-product of herd morality: riskless, medicated contentment that "invented happiness" and blinks — the future in which the question of values no longer arises at all. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), glossary.
5. *Why does the plan keep Zarathustra in selections, against the dossier's usual instinct for whole books?* Because the path's judgment is right: it is a literary work whose doctrines are all stated better, as arguments, in the books already read; the pass reads it as the performance of those doctrines, after them. [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md).

**Reconstructions.** The death-of-God/nihilism argument and the recurrence test reconstructed; BGE's pair gets its +1-week touch.

**Cards.** +5 (eternal recurrence, *amor fati*, Übermensch, last man, the three metamorphoses).

## Week 7 — 1888: the self-verdict (~6 hrs)

**Primary.** *The Antichrist* (written 1888) selections: §§1–7 (the good, the bad, the Hyperboreans), §§24–27 (the priestly history), §39 (the one Christian — his strange, respectful portrait of Jesus against the Church built on him), §§46–47, §§54–55, and §62 (the concluding "law against Christianity"). Then *Ecce Homo* (written 1888, published 1908) selections: "Why I Am So Wise," the *Birth of Tragedy* and *Genealogy* chapters of "Why I Write Such Good Books," and "Why I Am a Destiny" §§1 and 8. Read *Ecce Homo* as the dossier's final primary text and its most dangerous source: a self-portrait by a man weeks from collapse, brilliant and unreliable in the same sentence — use it as evidence of how he wished to be read, checked against everything else, which is exactly the discipline [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) prescribes.

**Alongside.** [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md), re-read for the Turin collapse as a **fact-check exercise**: what is documented (the letters, the Turin lodging, Burckhardt's alarm, Overbeck's journey), what is tradition (the horse — beloved, and poorly documented), and what the sister's archive later made of both. And [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md)'s misconception sections, read this week while the 1888 rhetoric is loudest in your ears.

**Media.** None assigned — deliberately. This is a short, dense week of late texts, and the media rule cuts both ways. The sanctioned filler, if a session runs short: selections from Brian Leiter's *Nietzsche on Morality* (see [08-bibliography.md](08-bibliography.md)) — permitted now, and only now, because the learning path's companion rule (after the first pass at the *Genealogy*, never before) is satisfied and Week 8 will need you to have a naturalist reading in hand.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *What is The Antichrist's target — Jesus, or Christianity?* The Church and the priestly type: §39's Jesus is the one figure treated without contempt ("there was only one Christian, and he died on the cross"), while Paul and the institution are the ressentiment machine built over him. The distinction disciplines any summary that calls the book simply anti-Christian. (*The Antichrist* §§24–47.)
2. *How should Ecce Homo be cited, if at all?* As self-fashioning, not testimony: its accounts of the books are the author's own revaluation of his career, invaluable for his intended emphases and worthless unchecked for fact — the chronology in [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md) outranks it wherever they differ.
3. *State what is actually documented about January 1889.* Collapse in Turin in the first days of January 1889; lucid intervals and the "madness letters" (to Burckhardt, Overbeck, and others); Overbeck brought him to Basel; he never recovered, and died in Weimar in 1900. The beaten-horse embrace is a later, weakly sourced story — say so whenever it is repeated to you. [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md).
4. *Apply "decadence" to its author: what does the dossier do with the 1888 books' stridency?* It reads them as works of the final productive year whose arguments continue the earlier books (the *Antichrist*'s psychology is GM III's), while noting — as [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) does — that the grandiosity is data about the coming collapse, not a reading key that dissolves the arguments. Neither worship nor pathography.

**Reconstructions.** The Schopenhauer inversion reconstructed this week — deliberately late, so it is built from both dossiers' material at once (Schopenhauer's pessimism argument from that dossier's Week 4; Nietzsche's refusal located in GS §370 and the *Genealogy*'s Preface). Its cadence continues into Week 8 and beyond.

**Cards.** +3 (decadence, self-overcoming, pessimism of strength).

## Week 8 — The figure whole: conversations, reception, capstone (~6.5 hrs + essay)

**Primary.** No new Nietzsche. Read [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md) in full (Schopenhauer behind him as educator and opponent; Emerson and the French moralists beside him; Heidegger, Sartre, and Foucault answering ahead — each linked into the library's reply chains) and [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) in full (Elisabeth and the archive; the Nazi appropriation and its collapse; Kaufmann's rehabilitation and the gentrification charge against it; the French recovery; the current state of the fights). Skim [08-bibliography.md](08-bibliography.md) and choose your next book before the capstone, while your judgment of your own gaps is fresh.

**The two modern Nietzsches, staged.** With 06's map in hand, set the week's two interpreters against each other in your notes: **Brian Leiter's naturalist** (a philosopher of drives, types, and causal psychology, continuous with the sciences of his day — the reading your Week 7 filler sampled) against the **French line** descending from Deleuze's *Nietzsche and Philosophy* (1962) (a philosopher of difference, affirmation, and forces, ancestor of post-structuralism) — with Nehamas's literary Nietzsche (*Life as Literature*) as the third position that refuses both reductions. The capstone's third prompt requires this staging; the mastery checklist requires you to have a verdict.

**Checkpoints — the comparative set (these double as the capstone's raw material).**
1. *State Kaufmann's rehabilitation and the gentrification objection to it.* Kaufmann (translator and author of the 1950 *Nietzsche: Philosopher, Psychologist, Antichrist*) recovered a humane, anti-anti-Semitic, philosophically serious Nietzsche from the Nazi image. The objection, pressed by later scholars: the recovery softened the genuinely anti-egalitarian, anti-democratic harshness that is on the published page and should be confronted, not edited away. [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).
2. *Give the editorial facts about The Will to Power, cold.* Assembled posthumously from the notebooks by Elisabeth Förster-Nietzsche's circle (1901; expanded 1906), presented as his masterwork; never published or planned in that form by Nietzsche; demoted by the Colli–Montinari edition to an artifact of the sister's project. Hence the citation rule: published works only. [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md).
3. *Locate the single premise where Nietzsche refuses Schopenhauer.* Not the diagnosis — willing as suffering, existence as lack and striving, is accepted almost whole. The refusal is at the valuation: that suffering's structural place indicts existence and licenses denial. Nietzsche inverts the prescription into affirmation — the same pendulum, willed. [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md); the Schopenhauer dossier's [04-arguments](../arthur-schopenhauer/04-arguments.md), "Striving, suffering, and pessimism."

**Reconstructions.** Final touches: every core argument should by now have had its three spaced successful reconstructions (+2d/+1wk/+3wk). In Session C, re-run the slave revolt (Week 4) and the recurrence test (Week 6) cold; anything that fails goes into the next stage's Session C rotation, not into extra drilling this week.

**Cards.** +3 (the Schopenhauer inversion, sublimation, the great health) — deck complete at 34. The deck stays in the daily FSRS queue permanently; Nietzsche cards will surface in Stage 5 exactly when Foucault and the existentialists need them.

## Capstone essay (800–1,200 words)

Write in Week 8, after the comparative checkpoints, book closed for the first draft. Prompt 1 can serve toward the **Stage 4 gate essay** ("Is morality a discovery, an invention, or a symptom?") if you write it so that Nietzsche's answer is defended against the gate's other two voices, Mill and Marx — and it deliberately doubles the Schopenhauer dossier's gate-eligible prompt 3, so the two dossiers' capstones can be planned as a pair. Otherwise the capstone stands alongside the gate, and the dossier's honest hours already assume one essay, not two.

1. **The diagnosis shared, the cure refused (gate-eligible).** Schopenhauer and Nietzsche agree that existence is striving and suffering. Reconstruct the agreement premise by premise from both dossiers, isolate the single premise where they divide, and defend one valuation against the other — then say, in closing, what your verdict implies for the gate question: is the morality built on each valuation a discovery, an invention, or a symptom?
2. **Genealogy on trial.** Press the genetic-fallacy objection against the First Essay at full strength: origins do not settle worth. Then give the best defense — that genealogy destroys morality's aura and forces the question of the value of values — and judge whether the defense saves the argument or quietly changes its subject.
3. **Two Nietzsches.** Leiter's naturalist and the French line's philosopher of affirmation (with Nehamas's literary Nietzsche as referee or third party) cannot both be the whole story. Using BGE §36 and GM III §12 read together, argue which reading survives both texts — and what the loser still sees that the winner misses.
4. **Science as the ascetic ideal's latest form.** Reconstruct GM III's closing argument — that modern scholarship's unconditional will to truth is the ideal's own kernel, inherited from the morality it opposes — and test it against one scholarly practice of your choosing (the academy, peer review, this dossier's own reconstruction discipline). Does the test case confirm, limit, or embarrass the claim?
5. **Test, cosmology, or therapy?** The eternal recurrence has been read as a cosmological doctrine (with notebook sketches — *Nachlass*, flagged), an existential test (GS §341), and a therapeutic discipline of affirmation (*amor fati*, §276). Argue for one reading against the best statement of another, and say what practically hangs on the choice for a reader who intends to *use* the thought.

## Mastery checklist — you are done when you can…

- □ State what "God is dead" diagnoses (a cultural collapse, effected by Christian truthfulness itself) and why nihilism is Nietzsche's prognosis to be overcome, never his position.
- □ Rebuild GM I from good/bad through ressentiment to good/evil, saying exactly what is *creative* in the slave revolt — and press the genetic-fallacy objection and its best reply without notes.
- □ Rebuild GM II's bad conscience in its three steps, name the steps' weakest joint, and explain the sovereign individual as the essay's counter-ideal.
- □ Explain the ascetic ideal's service — it gives suffering a meaning; "man would rather will nothingness than not will" — and its cost, including its final form in science's will to truth.
- □ Distinguish Nietzsche's master/slave typology from Hegel's lordship-and-bondage dialectic instantly, and catch the confusion in the wild.
- □ State perspectivism from GM III §12 ("more eyes") and defend it against the charge of relativism in two sentences.
- □ Present will to power's three candidate statuses (psychological hypothesis, metaphysics, interpretive heuristic), give §36's suppositional form, and explain the *Nachlass* problem that keeps the question open.
- □ Run the recurrence test in its §341 form and say which reading — test, cosmology, or therapy — you argued for in the capstone, and why.
- □ Trace one doctrine across the periods (music from *The Birth of Tragedy* to the late works, or the free spirit from 1878 to the philosophers of the future) without flattening the differences.
- □ Give the facts about Elisabeth, the archive, and *The Will to Power* cold, and apply the citation rule — published works only, notebook material flagged — to any Nietzsche claim put to you.
- □ Place him in conversation: what he takes from Schopenhauer and the premise he refuses; what Foucault takes from him; and which modern Nietzsche — Leiter's, the French line's, or Nehamas's — you would hand a new reader first, with your reasons.
