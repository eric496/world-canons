# Study Plan — Arthur Schopenhauer in Six Weeks

## What this plan is, and where it plugs in

The [learning path](../../learning-path.md) meets Schopenhauer in Stage 4, week 3, at selections level (marker ◐): enough *World as Will and Representation* (hereafter WWR) to hear what Nietzsche is answering, with the full system left at map level. This focus study replaces that single week with six. Run it when Stage 4 reaches him — Hegel (Stage 4, week 2) behind you, Kierkegaard and Mill still ahead — or as a standalone detour afterward; either way, do not start it before Kant, since every week presupposes the *Critique of Pure Reason*'s appearance/thing-in-itself distinction. When you finish, return to Stage 4, week 4 (Kierkegaard) as normal. The Stage 4 gate essay is unaffected, but the capstone below is the natural place to draft it.

**Total cost, stated honestly: ≈30 hours of scheduled study across six weeks (~5 hrs/week of reading, dossier work, and media), plus the daily [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net) queue (~10 minutes, ~6 days a week ≈ 5–6 hrs across the six weeks) — ≈35 hours all-in.** That is roughly six times the learning path's selections budget for this figure, and it buys the whole system: both volumes of WWR in their load-bearing parts, the dissertation, and the ethics essays.

**Reading texts.** This dossier's ruling (see [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md)) is the Cambridge edition (Judith Norman, Alistair Welchman, and Christopher Janaway) if you are buying: it is the current scholarly text with the better apparatus. E. F. J. Payne's translation (the two Dover volumes) is equally serviceable if you already own it or find it cheaper — complete, vigorous, and consistent. Pick one and stay in it: the checkpoint answers and [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) cite by book and section (§), which are stable across both translations, so nothing in the plan depends on the choice. Week 1's on-ramp is R. J. Hollingdale's Penguin selection, *Essays and Aphorisms* (from *Parerga and Paralipomena*). Full edition guidance is in [08-bibliography.md](08-bibliography.md).

**The order, and why.** Week 1 comes in through the *Parerga* essays — Schopenhauer's own late, accessible voice — and then reads the *Fourfold Root*'s account of the principle of sufficient reason, the one piece of machinery every later week uses. Only then does WWR begin, one book per week in Schopenhauer's own order (representation → Will → art → ethics), because the system is genuinely cumulative: Book 4's denial of the will is unintelligible without Book 2's metaphysics, and Book 3's aesthetics is the ethics in rehearsal. Volume II and the prize essays come in Week 5, once the system they supplement is standing. Week 6 steps outside the system — reception, the scholarly fights, and the capstone.

## Week 1 — The voice first, then the tool (~4.5 hrs)

**Primary.** *Essays and Aphorisms* (Hollingdale): "On the Suffering of the World," "On the Vanity of Existence," and "Aphorisms on the Wisdom of Life" selections — the pessimism and the practical counsel in his most readable prose. Then *On the Fourfold Root of the Principle of Sufficient Reason*: the introduction and the chapters setting out the four classes of objects and the four forms of the principle — of *becoming* (causality, for material objects), of *knowing* (grounds of judgment), of *being* (space and time), and of *acting* (motivation). Read for the taxonomy and for his central complaint: earlier philosophers, Kant included, conflated these forms — above all, they confused causes with logical grounds.

**Alongside.** [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md) (the life that produced this voice, and the neglect that sharpened it) and [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md) (where each text you will read sits in the corpus).

**Media.** *Philosophize This!* episode #098 ("Schopenhauer pt. 1 — Metaphysics and Love," Stephen West) as a *preview* before the *Fourfold Root* chapters — it previews the Kant correction and the will to life you will meet in Week 2. Popularizer's compression: orientation only, never evidence.

**Checkpoints (book closed; short answers given, full treatments in the dossier files named).**
1. *State the principle of sufficient reason and its four forms, with one example of each.* Nothing is without a reason why it is so rather than otherwise; the reason takes four forms — cause (becoming), logical ground (knowing), mathematical ground in space/time (being), motive (acting). [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "The fourfold root."
2. *What error does Schopenhauer accuse earlier philosophy of, in one sentence?* Running the four forms together — especially treating every ground as a cause and every cause as a logical ground. (Same section.)
3. *Why does the plan read the essays before the system?* Because the *Parerga* voice states the conclusions (suffering as structure, not accident) in plain prose, so the system's machinery is read as an answer to a felt problem rather than as scholasticism. [02-works-and-texts.md](02-works-and-texts.md).
4. *Is his pessimism an inference or a temperament, on this week's evidence?* An inference he insists follows from what willing is; Week 4 tests whether the inference holds. [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md) gives the biographical temptation to read it the other way — note the difference.

**Reconstructions.** Write the first blank-page reconstruction (200–400 words) of the fourfold-root argument by week's end; it enters the +2d/+1wk/+3wk rhythm (see the cadence table below).

**Cards.** Import the first 6 cards of [cards.tsv](cards.tsv) (the fourfold root, the principle of sufficient reason, *Vorstellung*, and the representation argument) when the week's reading is done — never before.

## Week 2 — Representation, then Will (~5.5 hrs)

**Primary.** WWR Vol. I, Book 1 (§§1–16): the world as representation — objects exist only for a subject, under the forms of space, time, and causality; his simplifications of Kant (one category, causality, does the intellect's work; the thing-in-itself is not one object among others). Then Book 2 (§§17–29): the body known in two ways — as representation among representations, and immediately, from within, as *will*; the generalization to all nature as grades of the Will's objectification; the Platonic Ideas as those grades.

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) in full (the architecture and glossary — this is the week it pays for itself), and [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) for the representation, Kant-correction, body, and individuation arguments.

**Media.** BBC Radio 4, *In Our Time*: "Schopenhauer" (29 October 2009, ~43 min; Melvyn Bragg with A. C. Grayling, Beatrice Han-Pile, and Christopher Janaway) — as *review*, after Book 2. A scholarly panel, and Janaway is one of the dossier's main secondary authors: hearing him compress the system is a good check on your own compression.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *What exactly does Schopenhauer keep from Kant, and what does he throw out?* Keeps: transcendental idealism — the experienced world is representation structured a priori. Throws out: the twelve categories (causality alone remains), the thing-in-itself as a second world of objects, and Kant's refusal to say anything about it. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "The world as representation."
2. *State the body's double knowledge, and the inference it is asked to carry.* My body is given both as representation and immediately as willing; Schopenhauer infers that willing is what every phenomenon is from inside. State the inference's weak joint in one sentence: the step from one privileged case to all nature is an analogy, not a deduction. (Same file, "The body's double knowledge.")
3. *What work does the principium individuationis do?* Space and time individuate: they make the one Will appear as a plurality of competing individuals, and so make egoism and conflict metaphysically superficial but experientially inescapable. (Same file, "Individuation.")
4. *Why "Will" with a capital W is not willpower.* Willpower is one individuated, motive-driven appearance; the Will is the single, aimless striving that appears as all of them. If your answer mentions "deciding to try harder," start over. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), glossary.

**Reconstructions.** Books 1–2's master argument (representation → body → Will) reconstructed by week's end; Week 1's fourfold-root reconstruction gets its +1-week touch in Session C.

**Cards.** +7 (body/Will, individuation, grades of objectification, Platonic Ideas, will-to-live).

## Week 3 — Art: the temporary exit (~5 hrs)

**Primary.** WWR Vol. I, Book 3 (§§30–52): aesthetic contemplation as will-less knowing; the pure subject of knowing; the hierarchy of the arts as a tour of the Ideas' grades; the sublime; and §§51–52 on music — not a copy of appearances or even of the Ideas, but a direct copy of the Will itself.

**Alongside.** [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) (the aesthetics sections and the terms *pure subject of knowing*, *genius*, *the sublime*) and [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) ("Aesthetic release and music").

**Media.** [*The Great Philosophers*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Philosophers) (BBC, 1987), episode 10: Bryan Magee in conversation with Frederick Copleston on Schopenhauer (~45 min) — watch *after* Book 3. The Magee format is the learning path's own recommended discussion substitute (§2.6): two people arguing slowly, with Magee interrupting to clarify. Note, for accuracy, that the Schopenhauer conversation belongs to the 1987 series, not to *Men of Ideas* (1978), whose guests discussed contemporary philosophy. Select episodes circulate on YouTube; the dialogue also survives, revised, in the book *The Great Philosophers*.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *What changes, exactly, in aesthetic contemplation?* The knower stops relating the object to his own will (where, when, why — the questions of the principle of sufficient reason) and rests in perception of the Idea; subject and object of ordinary striving both fall away, briefly. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Aesthetic release and music."
2. *Why is music ranked above every other art — and why is that ranking an embarrassment for the system?* Because music copies the Will directly, not its objectifications. The embarrassment: the system says the Ideas mediate all other art, so music's directness is an anomaly Schopenhauer asserts more than he explains. State both halves. (Same section.)
3. *Distinguish the beautiful from the sublime in his usage.* Both are will-less contemplation; the sublime adds a felt hostility of the object to the will that must be consciously surmounted. [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), glossary.
4. *Is aesthetic release a solution to suffering, on his own terms?* No — temporary, individual, and it leaves the Will untouched; it is evidence that will-less knowing is possible, which Book 4 turns into a permanent possibility. (Same file.)

**Reconstructions.** The aesthetics argument reconstructed; Books 1–2 argument gets its +1-week touch; fourfold root its +3-week touch — this is the week the rhythm starts stacking, and Session C is where it lives.

**Cards.** +5 (pure subject of knowing, genius, the sublime, aesthetic release, music).

## Week 4 — Ethics: compassion, and the denial of the will (~5.5 hrs)

**Primary.** WWR Vol. I, Book 4 (§§53–71): willing as suffering — desire as lack, satisfaction as merely negative and brief, boredom as the other pole of the pendulum; the affirmation of the will and its conflicts; eternal justice; compassion as the breakthrough of individuation; and the denial of the will-to-live in asceticism — with §§68–71 on why suicide is not denial but a paradoxical affirmation.

**Alongside.** [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) (the pessimism, worst-of-all-worlds, compassion, eternal-justice, and denial arguments) and [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) (the misconceptions in full form — read this week, while your guard is down).

**Media.** *Philosophize This!* episode #099 ("Schopenhauer pt. 2 — Ethics," ~23 min) as *review* after Book 4 — a brisk compression of exactly this week's material; check its sage/ascetic summary against §§68–71 and note where the compression flattens the suicide argument.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *Reconstruct the pessimism argument in four premises.* Willing is the essence; willing springs from lack; lack is suffering; satisfaction is negative and transient, breeding new willing or boredom — so suffering is structural, not accidental. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Striving, suffering, and pessimism."
2. *What does "worst of all possible worlds" mean as an argument rather than a slogan?* A world much worse could not sustain itself at all; this one is arranged as badly as is compatible with continued existence — offered as a rebuttal of Leibnizian optimism on its own terms. (Same file.)
3. *Why is compassion, and not duty, the basis of morality — and what metaphysics does the explanation smuggle in?* Because in compassion the barrier between self and other is phenomenally lifted; the explanation — that individuation is appearance and the sufferer is, in himself, the same Will — is the metaphysics of Weeks 2 doing ethical work. If the metaphysics goes, the foundation is under pressure; [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) stages that fight (Shapshay vs. the metaphysical reading). (Same file, "Compassion as the basis of morality.")
4. *Why is suicide affirmation rather than denial?* The suicide wills life and rejects only its sufferings; he destroys one appearance of the Will while endorsing willing itself. Denial, by contrast, is the will turning against willing as such. (Same file, "Denial of the will — and why suicide is affirmation.")
5. *What is eternal justice, and how does it differ from retribution after death?* The tormentor and the tormented are one Will at odds with itself; justice is already executed in the structure of the world, not postponed to a judgment. (Same file.)

**Reconstructions.** The pessimism argument and the compassion argument both reconstructed this week (they are the dossier's center of gravity); earlier arguments continue on cadence.

**Cards.** +5 (pessimism, Mitleid, denial of the will-to-live, eternal justice, worst of all possible worlds).

## Week 5 — The supplements: death, sex, freedom, character (~5.5 hrs)

**Primary.** WWR Vol. II, four chapters: ch. 17, "On Man's Need for Metaphysics" (why religion and philosophy exist at all); ch. 41, "On Death and Its Relation to the Indestructibility of Our Inner Nature" (death destroys the individual, not the Will — the system's cold comfort); ch. 44, "The Metaphysics of Sexual Love" (the species' will working through the individual's illusion of personal happiness — a famous, unsettling chapter, and a plain content note belongs here: it advances its argument in the vocabulary of 1844, and the surrounding corpus, especially the *Parerga* essay "On Women," contains claims about women that are hostile and dated; read them as objects of criticism, as [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) does, not as the dossier's teaching); ch. 46, "On the Vanity and Suffering of Life" (the pessimism restated at full strength). Plus the prize essays (*The Two Fundamental Problems of Ethics*, Payne translation): *On the Freedom of the Will* — its conclusion on character and necessity — and *On the Basis of Morality*, the compassion chapters, where the Week 4 doctrine gets its independent, Kant-critical statement.

**Alongside.** [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md) ("Freedom and character," "Primacy of will over intellect") and [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md) (the Kant and Fichte arguments this week's essays are fighting).

**Media.** None assigned — deliberately. This is the plan's densest reading week, and the media rule cuts both ways: nothing earns a slot against Vol. II. If a session runs short, the Magee book's critical chapters (see [08-bibliography.md](08-bibliography.md)) are the sanctioned filler, read after the primary text.

**Checkpoints.**
1. *State operari sequitur esse and the freedom it leaves standing.* Action follows being: a person's deeds follow necessarily from his inborn, unchangeable character under motives; the only freedom is transcendental — the intelligible character is outside time and so "free" precisely where nothing can be altered by it. [04-arguments.md](04-arguments.md), "Freedom and character."
2. *Distinguish intelligible, empirical, and acquired character.* Intelligible: the will's timeless act, what one *is*. Empirical: that character unfolded in time, known only through one's deeds. Acquired: the self-knowledge, gained through experience, of which empirical character one has — the nearest thing to self-improvement the system allows. (Same file; [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md), glossary.)
3. *Why is intellect the will's tool — and what is the best evidence he offers from ordinary life?* Intellect evolved/arose as an instrument of willing and normally serves it (rationalization, self-deception, the tail wagged by the dog); his standing exhibits are the intellect's helplessness against desire and its ignorance of the will's decisions until after the fact. (Same file, "Primacy of will over intellect.")
4. *What does ch. 41 claim survives death, and why is it not personal immortality?* The Will — one's inner nature as thing-in-itself — is untouched by the death of its appearance; but memory, intellect, and individuality belong to the appearance, so nothing recognizably *you* persists. (Same file; [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md) on the Upanishadic and Buddhist parallels, and their limits.)

**Reconstructions.** Freedom-and-character reconstructed; pessimism and compassion get their +1-week touches.

**Cards.** +4 (intelligible character, empirical character, *operari sequitur esse*/freedom, primacy of intellect-as-tool).

## Week 6 — The figure whole: conversations, reception, capstone (~5 hrs + essay)

**Primary.** No new Schopenhauer. Read [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md) in full (Kant behind him; Plato and the Upanishads beside him; Wagner, Tolstoy, Freud, and above all Nietzsche ahead of him — the reply the whole dossier has been preparing) and [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) in full (the delayed fame, the schools that claimed him, the live disputes: the thing-in-itself objection, the compassion foundation, the two standpoints on salvation). Skim [08-bibliography.md](08-bibliography.md) and choose your next book before the capstone, while your judgment of your own gaps is fresh.

**Checkpoints — the comparative set (these double as the capstone's raw material).**
1. *State the thing-in-itself objection at full strength, and the best reply.* His Kantian premises forbid knowledge of the thing-in-itself, yet the Will is asserted as exactly that. Reply: the body's double knowledge is not representation-knowledge at all, so the prohibition is not breached — it is circumvented at one point. Say whether you find the reply sufficient, in one sentence, and why. [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).
2. *Nietzsche read this system and said no. Locate the exact premise he refuses.* Not the suffering — the valuation: that suffering's structural place indicts existence and licenses denial. Nietzsche accepts the diagnosis of striving and inverts the prescription into affirmation. [05-conversations.md](05-conversations.md).
3. *Name the two places where the system pulls against itself.* (Candidates: music's direct copy vs. the Ideas' mediation, Week 3; denial by an individuated saint vs. individuation as illusion, Week 4; the intellect knowing the will that supposedly precedes it, Week 5.) Defend your pair as the deepest. [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md).

**Reconstructions.** Final touches: every core argument should by now have had its three spaced successful reconstructions (+2d/+1wk/+3wk). In Session C, re-run the master argument (Week 2) and the pessimism argument (Week 4) cold; anything that fails goes back into the next stage's Session C rotation, not into extra drilling this week.

**Cards.** +2 (acquired character, the quietive) — deck complete at 29. The deck stays in the daily [FSRS](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html) queue permanently; Schopenhauer cards will surface during Stage 5 exactly when Nietzsche needs them.

## Capstone essay (800–1,200 words)

Write in Week 6, after the comparative checkpoints, book closed for the first draft. This essay can double as the Stage 4 gate essay if your chosen prompt does the gate's work (Mill, Marx, and Nietzsche in argument) — prompt 3 is built for that. Otherwise it stands alongside the gate, and the dossier's honest hours already assume one essay, not two.

1. **The keyhole and the leap.** Schopenhauer claims the body gives knowledge of the thing-in-itself that Kant's system forbids. Reconstruct the claim, state the contradiction objection at full strength, and judge whether the reply saves the system or relocates the problem.
2. **Compassion's foundation.** Does the ethics of *Mitleid* need the metaphysics of the one Will? Argue for the metaphysical reading or for a reconstruction on independent (phenomenological or naturalistic) grounds — and say what is lost on your chosen side.
3. **The diagnosis shared, the cure refused.** Schopenhauer and Nietzsche agree that existence is striving and suffering. Reconstruct the agreement premise by premise, isolate the single premise where they divide, and defend one valuation against the other. (Gate-eligible.)
4. **Music as anomaly.** Book 3 ranks music as a direct copy of the Will, bypassing the Ideas that mediate every other art. Is this the system's deepest insight into art or a crack in its architecture? Argue one side against the best statement of the other.
5. **A pessimism audit.** Take the pessimism argument of Book 4 and test it against one life Schopenhauer did not consider — a life of absorbed craft, love, or contemplation of your choosing. Does the argument absorb the case (as boredom, as temporary release, as self-deception), or does the case expose a premise?

## Mastery checklist — you are done when you can…

- □ State the principle of sufficient reason's four forms and catch a conflation of cause and ground in the wild.
- □ Reconstruct the master argument — representation, the body's double knowledge, the Will as thing-in-itself — and name its weakest joint without being prompted.
- □ Explain individuation: how space and time make one Will appear as many competing wills, and why that single doctrine powers both the ethics and the pessimism.
- □ Rebuild the pessimism argument from *willing is lack* to *suffering is structural*, including the pendulum of pain and boredom and the "worst of all possible worlds" rebuttal of Leibniz.
- □ Say why compassion, not duty or consequence, is morality's basis — and what metaphysical claim the explanation depends on.
- □ Distinguish the three exits — aesthetic release, compassion, denial of the will — by how complete and how permanent each is, and explain why suicide belongs to none of them.
- □ Explain music's anomalous rank in Book 3 and state the tension it creates.
- □ Give the three characters (intelligible, empirical, acquired) and *operari sequitur esse*, and say what freedom survives them.
- □ State eternal justice and how it differs from post-mortem retribution.
- □ Place him in conversation: what he takes from Kant and Plato, what he takes from the Upanishads, and the exact premise Nietzsche refuses.
- □ Name the two deepest internal tensions of the system and the current scholarly positions on each, as disputes — without needing to resolve them.
- □ Recommend, for a new reader, the right first text and edition — and talk them out of starting with Vol. II or the "On Women" essay.
