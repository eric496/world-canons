# Bibliography — Kant: What to Read, in What Order, and Why

Tiered like the study plan: the texts first, then the companions in the order to read them, then the scholarly anchors for when a particular fight catches you, then reference and media. Every media item below was verified to exist as described; one famous absence is recorded where it occurs.

## 1. Primary texts and editions

All consistent with the learning path's Kant row; the dossier adds the texts the path defers. Akademie pagination (volume: page) is printed in every edition named and is how the shorter works are cited throughout the dossier.

**Paul Guyer and Allen W. Wood (trans.), *Critique of Pure Reason* (Cambridge Texts in the History of Philosophy, 1998).** The dossier's *Critique* and the path's ruling: modern, terminologically consistent, with A/B pagination in the margins throughout — the feature daily use actually depends on. **Do not substitute Norman Kemp Smith (1929) or J. M. D. Meiklejohn (1855)**, and do not read an unlabeled Gutenberg text: the terminology misleads beginners and the cheap reprints often drop the A/B numbers altogether.

**Mary Gregor (trans.), *Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals*, revised by Jens Timmermann, introduction by Christine Korsgaard (Cambridge Texts, 2012).** The plan's *Groundwork*: the standard classroom text, with Korsgaard's introduction doubling as your first piece of scholarly Kant (see tier 3). Allen Wood's Yale translation (2002) is the sanctioned alternative; J. W. Ellington's Hackett version is older but serviceable if it is what you own.

**Gary Hatfield (ed. and trans.), *Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics* (Cambridge Texts, rev. ed., 2004).** Week 1's text: Kant's own short restatement of the first *Critique*, with an introduction that maps the 1781 reception disaster it was written to repair.

**Mary Gregor (trans.), *Critique of Practical Reason*, in *Practical Philosophy* (Cambridge Edition of the Works of Immanuel Kant, 1996).** The plan's second *Critique*; *Practical Philosophy* also carries the *Groundwork* in Gregor's unrevised text and "What Is Enlightenment?", making it the one-volume practical Kant. Werner Pluhar's Hackett translation (2002) is the standalone alternative.

**Allen W. Wood and George di Giovanni (trans.), *Religion and Rational Theology* (Cambridge Edition, 1996).** Contains the *Religion within the Boundaries of Mere Reason* — Week 6's Book One (radical evil) in the standard modern translation, with the essays that show Kant's moral religion in its working clothes.

**Paul Guyer and Eric Matthews (trans.), *Critique of Judgment* (Cambridge Texts, 2000).** Week 7's selections (the Analytic of the Beautiful, the sublime). Supersedes Bernard and Meredith for a first read: consistent with the Guyer and Wood *Critique*'s terminology, which matters when the third *Critique* spends the first one's capital.

**H. B. Nisbet (trans.), in Hans Reiss (ed.), *Kant's Political Writings* (Cambridge Texts, 2nd ed., 1991).** The home of *Perpetual Peace* (1795) and "What Is Enlightenment?" in one volume — Weeks 1 and 7 from a single purchase.

## 2. First secondary — read in this order

**Roger Scruton, *Kant: A Very Short Introduction* (Oxford University Press, 2001).** The learning path's assigned alongside for the Kant block, and Week 1's orientation: ninety-odd pages that state the system's shape — including its difficulties — before the *Prolegomena*. Scruton is a guide with a verdict forming; read him as the path directs, *before* the primary texts, and let the texts argue back.

**Paul Guyer, *Kant* (Routledge Philosophers, 2nd ed., 2014).** The first full companion, and the plan's default Session B voice across all eight weeks: one volume covering the theoretical philosophy, the ethics, and the aesthetics with a specialist's judgment and a teacher's order. The learning path already names Guyer as the stall-breaker if the *Prolegomena* sticks; the dossier promotes him to standing companion. Where Guyer and the SEP differ on a reading, you have found a live dispute — [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) is the dossier's map of them.

**Sebastian Gardner, *Routledge Philosophy Guidebook to Kant and the Critique of Pure Reason* (Routledge, 1999).** The session-level companion for Weeks 2–4: chapter-by-chapter on the first *Critique*, particularly patient on the Deduction and the Schematism — the two stretches where the plan budgets outline grasp and Gardner quietly deepens it. Use alongside, never instead of, the repo's own [guide](../../study/kant-critique.md): the guide assigns the selections and the checkpoints; Gardner explains them at length.

**Allen W. Wood, *Kant* (Blackwell Great Minds, 2005).** Short, opinionated, and centered where the *Groundwork* and second *Critique* weeks need help: the moral philosophy and its religious completion. Wood reads Kant's ethics as a doctrine of reason's self-legislation with the *Religion* as its honest appendix — read him in Weeks 5–6, after your own reconstructions, as the first scholarly voice to argue with.

## 3. The scholarly anchors — for when a fight catches you

**Henry E. Allison, *Kant's Transcendental Idealism: An Interpretation and Defense* (Yale University Press, 1983; rev. and enlarged ed., 2004).** The standard-bearer of the two-aspect (methodological) reading: phenomena and things in themselves as one world under two standpoints, not two worlds. Allison's Kant is the one the dossier's [03-system-and-concepts.md](03-system-and-concepts.md) presupposes where it must choose; read him after the plan, when the Aesthetic's conclusion has started to itch, and watch how much of the *Critique* his reading rearranges.

**P. F. Strawson, *The Bounds of Sense* (Methuen, 1966).** The great salvage operation — flagged here, as the dossier flags it everywhere: Strawson discards transcendental idealism itself (the "imaginary subject of transcendental psychology") and reconstructs from the wreck an austere, still-influential argument about objectivity and self-consciousness. It is a reconstruction with its own agenda, written by a philosopher building his own position out of Kant's materials; capstone prompt 3 puts it on trial. Read it as an opponent of genius, never as a report of what Kant thought.

**Christine Korsgaard, *Creating the Kingdom of Ends* (Cambridge University Press, 1996), with her introduction to the Cambridge *Groundwork*.** The leading contemporary Kantian treatment of the moral philosophy: the categorical imperative's formulations as one principle, autonomy as self-constitution, and the *Groundwork* read as a work a modern philosopher can still *use*. The introduction you already own (tier 1) is the digest; the book is the full argument, and Week 5's natural sequel.

**Paul Guyer (ed.), *The Cambridge Companion to Kant's Critique of Pure Reason* (Cambridge University Press, 2010), and *The Cambridge Companion to Kant* (1992).** The survey volumes: essays by the field's senior figures on every part of the *Critique* (2010) and on the whole system (1992), respectively. The place to go when a dossier topic needs a second scholarly voice before you commit to a capstone verdict.

**Manfred Kuehn, *Kant: A Biography* (Cambridge University Press, 2001).** The standard modern biography — the source the SEP's life section itself draws on — and the corrective to the clock-setting caricature: a sociable, publishing, polemicizing Kant inside a real eighteenth-century city. Read alongside [01-life-and-times.md](01-life-and-times.md) if the life catches you.

## 4. Reference

**[SEP, "Immanuel Kant"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant/) — Michael Rohlf.** The standard scholarly survey of the whole system, and the plan's Week 8 capstone reading (skim §1 in Week 1 per the learning path's ritual; read whole after). Its spin-off entries serve the weeks named: ["Kant's Moral Philosophy"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-moral/) (Robert Johnson and Adam Cureton; Weeks 5–6), ["Kant's Aesthetics and Teleology"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-aesthetics/) (Hannah Ginsborg; Week 7), ["Kant's Transcendental Idealism"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-transcendental-idealism/) (Nicholas Stang; Weeks 2–4), ["Kant's Critique of Metaphysics"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-metaphysics/) (Michelle Grier; Week 4), ["Kant's Philosophy of Religion"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-religion/) (Lawrence Pasternack and Courtney Fugate; Week 6), and ["Kant's Social and Political Philosophy"](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-social/) (Frederick Rauscher; Week 7).

**[IEP, "Immanuel Kant"](https://iep.utm.edu/) — Tim Jankowiak.** The article the plan assigns *before* the primary reading (the learning path's IEP-first rule for this figure: IEP's Kant coverage is the plain-language map beginners most need). Its specialist companions fill out the system: ["Immanuel Kant: Metaphysics"](https://iep.utm.edu/) for the first *Critique*, ["Kant: Philosophy of Mind"](https://iep.utm.edu/kantmind/) for apperception and the Paralogisms, and the IEP's Kant-and-religion article for Week 6.

**[PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/), category "Immanuel Kant"; and KantPapers.** The research indexes: PhilPapers for chasing the Deduction and two-aspect debates past the Companions; KantPapers.org, the Kant-specific bibliography built on PhilPapers' database, for exhaustive coverage of a single section (the Second Analogy literature alone runs to hundreds of items — filter by proposition, not by "Kant").

**Journals: *Kant-Studien* and *Kantian Review*.** Named for orientation, not assignment: the field's two house journals, whose review sections are the fastest honest answer to "what do Kant scholars currently argue about?" — the question [06-reception-and-debates.md](06-reception-and-debates.md) answers at dossier length.

## 5. Media (all verified; assigned in the plan as marked)

- **Stephen West, [*Philosophize This!*](https://www.philosophizethis.org/podcast), the Kant run, episodes #056–063 (April–July 2015, ~26–35 min each)** — #056 "Kant pt. 1 — An Introduction to the Introduction" and #057 "Kant pt. 2 — The Actual Introduction" (Week 1, preview); #058 "Kant pt. 3 — Deontology vs Consequentialism" and #059 "Kant pt. 4 — Categorical Trolley Cars" (Week 5); #060 "Kant pt. 5 — The Sublime" and #061 "Kant pt. 6 — What is Enlightenment?" (Week 7); #062 "Kant pt. 7 — Suicide" (Week 6); #063 "Kant pt. 8 — Limits of Knowledge" (Week 4). The learning path's rigor caveat applies with special force here: West is the Stage 3 audio spine where Adamson has not arrived, but he simplifies Kant more than most figures — preview and review only, always against your own reconstruction.
- **Dan Robinson, *Kant's Critique of Pure Reason*, Oxford Podcasts (eight lectures given at Oxford in 2011, ~43 min each)** — the plan's scholarly lecture spine for Weeks 2–4: 1, "Just what is Kant's 'project'?"; 2, "The broader philosophical context"; 3 (space, time, and the Aesthetic); 4, "How are a priori synthetic judgements possible?"; 5, "Idealisms and their refutations"; 6, "Concepts, judgement and the Transcendental Deduction of the Categories"; 7, "The 'Self' and the Synthetic Unity of Apperception"; 8, "The discipline of reason: The paralogisms and Antinomies of Pure Reason." Demanding, text-assuming, and the best free second exposition of the Deduction available. Audio via Oxford Podcasts.
- **BBC Radio 4, *In Our Time*, ["Kant's Categorical Imperative"](https://www.bbc.co.uk/programmes/b0952zl3) (first broadcast 21 September 2017; ~49 min)** — Melvyn Bragg with Alison Hills (Oxford), David Oderberg (Reading), and John Callanan (King's College London): the formulations worked hard, with the panel's own disagreements left in. BBC programme b0952zl3. Week 5 review.
- **Peter Millican, [*General Philosophy*](https://podcasts.ox.ac.uk/series/general-philosophy) (Oxford Podcasts)** — the Oxford first-year course; for Kant, honestly bounded: part **2.7, "Overview: Kant and Modern Science"** (~17 min), the survey's close, presenting Kant as Hume's answerer, with part **3.1, "Hume's Argument Concerning Induction"** as the problem it answers (Week 1). Millican gives Kant an overview, not a lecture block — the Robinson series above is the dossier's lecture spine, and this entry records the difference rather than inflating it.
- **Gregory B. Sadler, *Sadler's Lectures* podcast playlists: *Groundwork for the Metaphysics of Morals* (16 episodes) and *Prolegomena to Any Future Metaphysics* (41 episodes)** — short core-concept walk-throughs, indexed at [Sadler's Substack](https://gregorybsadler.substack.com/p/the-sadlers-lectures-podcast). The plan assigns the *Groundwork* set as its one Sadler set (Weeks 5–6, walk-time re-consolidation, per the learning path's rule); the *Prolegomena* set is listed for completeness and is the sanctioned rescue if Week 1's text stalls you.
- **Peter Adamson, [*History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*](https://historyofphilosophy.net/podcast/all-episodes) — the absence, recorded.** The series' Western run has reached only the later seventeenth century (ep. 502, on Bayle and the Dutch Republic, is the current frontier); it has **not yet reached Locke, Hume, or Kant**, and at its pace of ~25 episodes a year through dense centuries it will not for years. When the Kant episodes exist, Weeks 1–4 should adopt them as spine; until then this dossier assigns only what exists, and the *Philosophize This!* relay carries the audio load with its caveat attached.
