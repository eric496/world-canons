## Editions and study design

`learning-path.md` (rewritten 2026-10-31) is built on a dedicated research pass rather than on the survey sources above. Its key sources:

- **Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham (2013), "Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques," *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 14(1): 4–58** — the basis of the plan's retention system: practice testing and distributed practice rated high utility; elaborative interrogation, self-explanation and interleaved practice moderate; summarizing, highlighting and rereading low. Hence argument cards, blank-page reconstructions, and book-closed checkpoints instead of re-reading. **Superseded in part by the round 6 revision (below):** card scheduling moved from the fixed +2 days/+1 week/+3 weeks marks to Anki's FSRS scheduler, and the marks were reassigned to reconstructions and checkpoints under the successive-relearning account (Rawson & Dunlosky). (Abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26173288/)
- **MIT OCW 24.01, *Classics of Western Philosophy*, and 24.200, *Ancient Philosophy*** — the model for core-text selection and within-author order (e.g. Plato's *Five Dialogues* in Grube/Hackett before selective *Republic*; Descartes in Cress/Hackett; Hume's *Enquiry* before the *Treatise*; Kant's *Groundwork* before *Critique* selections). https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/24-01-classics-of-western-philosophy-spring-2016/pages/readings/

- **Peter Adamson, *History of Philosophy Without Any Gaps*** — the model for the map-level tier: chronological, no famous-name jumping, episode-per-figure alongside support, including the Islamic/Jewish bridge figures the compressed medieval stage covers at summary level.

### Method updates since Dunlosky (2013)

- **Donoghue & Hattie (2021)** meta-analysis of the same ten techniques (242 studies) again ranks distributed practice and practice testing on top — Dunlosky's ranking has held; no structural change resulted. (Read via secondary summary in this pass.)
- **Successive relearning (Rawson & Dunlosky)** — the active ingredient is *successful retrieval of the same material in multiple separate spaced sessions* (~3 sessions), not any particular expanding-gap schedule; equal intervals perform comparably (Latimier, Peyre & Ramus 2021, via secondary summary), and extra same-session drilling washes out (the "relearning override," Vaughn, Dunlosky & Rawson 2016). This is why the path's +2d/+1wk/+3wk marks now govern reconstructions/checkpoints while FSRS schedules cards. Boundary condition: for procedural material the advantage is small (pooled d = 0.28; Rawson, Dunlosky & Janes 2020) — the path claims strength for arguments and concepts, not for everything. https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/teaching-current-directions-studying-psychopathology ; https://www.mendeley.com/catalogue/d0a3d400-9a2c-38a9-bb0e-698e5269d647/ ; https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2020-28663-001
- **Brunmair & Richter (2019), *Psychological Bulletin*** — interleaving meta-analysis (59 studies, 238 effects): overall g = 0.42 but material-dependent (visual categories 0.67; math 0.34; expository texts nonsignificant; word learning reversed, −0.39). Hence §1.1's narrowed claim: first-pass reading stays blocked; the spiral return is spaced retrieval + comparison of similar positions. https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2019-57442-001
- **Retrieval practice for concepts** — Karpicke & Blunt (2011): retrieval practice beat elaborative concept-mapping even on conceptual questions; Butler (2010): repeated testing improves transfer. Practitioner synthesis (Matuschak) adds the constraint the path adopts: conceptual prompts must be well-formed and multi-angle, and cards stay subordinate to blank-page reconstruction. https://notes.andymatuschak.org/z9Vi7YVx7NzxU2wawNgsJbk
- **Writing to learn** — Graham, Kiuhara & MacKay (2020), *Review of Educational Research*: writing about content improves learning of it, g ≈ 0.30 (mostly school populations — supportive, not decisive, for an adult self-studier). Hence gate essays kept at one per stage, not expanded. https://www.clearinghouse.edu.tum.de/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/CHU-KR-29_ENG_Graham-2020.pdf
