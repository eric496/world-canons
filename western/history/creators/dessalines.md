## Biography

Jean-Jacques Dessalines was born in 1758 in the French colony of Saint-Domingue, the richest slave colony in the Caribbean, and was himself enslaved. He joined the great slave uprising that began in 1791 and became one of Toussaint Louverture's leading generals. After Toussaint's capture by the French in 1802, Dessalines led the war against Napoleon's expedition, which aimed to restore slavery, and defeated it at Vertières in November 1803. On 1 January 1804 he proclaimed Haiti's independence. He made himself emperor later that year and was assassinated in 1806.

Haiti became the first independent nation in Latin America and the first state founded by formerly enslaved people. In 1804 Dessalines ordered the killing of most of the remaining French inhabitants, a massacre that has shaped his contested reputation. In Haiti he is honoured as a founding father, and the national anthem, La Dessalinienne, is named after him.

- **Type:** Declaration
- **Written / held:** Proclaimed at Gonaïves, 1 January 1804; only known original printed copy in The National Archives, Kew
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Haitian%20Declaration%20of%20Independence%20Jean-Jacques%20Dessalines)

On 1 January 1804, at Gonaïves, Jean-Jacques Dessalines proclaimed the independence of the former French colony of Saint-Domingue under the Taíno name Haiti. The declaration, written by his secretary Louis Boisrond-Tonnerre, addresses the people of Haiti and urges them to renounce France forever and to defend their liberty to the death, recalling the cruelty of French rule and of Napoleon's attempt to restore slavery. It followed thirteen years of revolution that began with the slave uprising of 1791. Haiti became the second independent republic in the Americas and the first in Latin America, but France and other powers were slow to recognise it, and in 1825 France imposed a crippling indemnity. In 2010 the historian Julia Gaffield found a printed copy of the declaration in the British National Archives, the only known original printing.
