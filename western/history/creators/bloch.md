# Marc Bloch

**1886 – 1944** · French  
Born: Lyon, France · Died: Saint-Didier-de-Formans, near Lyon  
Traditions & eras: [Annales school](../movements/annales-school.md)  

> A medievalist and co-founder of the Annales journal, Bloch transformed the study of medieval society and was executed by the Gestapo as a Resistance fighter.

## Biography

Marc Bloch was born in Lyon in 1886 into a family of Alsatian Jewish origin; his father was a professor of Roman history. He studied at the École normale supérieure and in Germany, fought in the First World War, and taught at the University of Strasbourg, where in 1929 he and Lucien Febvre founded the journal Annales d'histoire économique et sociale. He moved to the Sorbonne in 1936. After serving again in 1939–40 he wrote a searching analysis of France's defeat, Strange Defeat. Barred from teaching by antisemitic laws, he joined the Resistance in Lyon, was arrested and tortured, and was shot by the Gestapo in June 1944.

Bloch's works, including The Royal Touch, French Rural History and Feudal Society, used comparison across regions and disciplines such as geography, linguistics and anthropology to understand whole societies and their beliefs. His unfinished reflections on method, published as The Historian's Craft, remain one of the most widely read books on what history is for. He is among the most admired historians of the twentieth century.

### Feudal Society (1939–1940)

- **Date:** 1939–1940
- **Type:** Social history (2 volumes)
- **Written / held:** Written in Paris; published by Albin Michel in the series L'Évolution de l'humanité
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Feudal%20Society%20Marc%20Bloch)

La Société féodale, published in two volumes in 1939 and 1940, sought to understand feudalism not merely as a set of legal relations between lords and vassals but as a whole social structure. Bloch examined the impact of the Viking, Magyar and Muslim invasions, the conditions of life and mental climate of the age, including attitudes to time, memory and nature, ties of kinship and dependence, and the formation of classes and governments. He compared developments across Europe and beyond, including Japan. Its breadth and humanity made it one of the most influential works of medieval history of the twentieth century.

### The Historian's Craft (1949 (written 1941–43))

- **Date:** 1949 (written 1941–43)
- **Type:** Essay on historical method
- **Written / held:** Written in occupied France; published posthumously by Lucien Febvre, Paris, 1949
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Historian%27s%20Craft%20Marc%20Bloch)

Bloch wrote Apologie pour l'histoire, ou Métier d'historien between 1941 and 1943, while living under persecution and joining the Resistance, without access to his library. It opens with a child asking his father what history is for. Bloch defines history as the science of men in time, discusses observation, criticism of evidence and analysis, warns against the 'idol of origins', and insists that historians must understand the past through the present and the present through the past. The manuscript was unfinished when he was arrested and shot in 1944, and Lucien Febvre published it in 1949. It remains one of the most widely read introductions to the historian's work.
