# Eric Hobsbawm

**1917 – 2012** · British  
Born: Alexandria, Egypt · Died: London  
Traditions & eras: [20th-century history & human-rights documents](../movements/twentieth-century-history.md)  

> A Marxist historian of exceptional range, Hobsbawm wrote a four-volume history of the modern world from 1789 to 1991.

## Biography

Eric Hobsbawm was born in Alexandria in 1917 to a British father and an Austrian mother and grew up in Vienna and Berlin, where he joined the Communist movement as a schoolboy before his family moved to London in 1933. He studied at King's College, Cambridge, served in the British army during the Second World War, and taught for most of his career at Birkbeck College, London. He remained a member of the Communist Party of Great Britain until it dissolved in 1991, a loyalty for which he was often criticised. He died in London in 2012.

Hobsbawm's studies of labour history, social banditry and peasant rebellion helped create British social history. His series The Age of Revolution, The Age of Capital, The Age of Empire and The Age of Extremes offered a synthesis of modern history centred on the 'dual revolution' of French politics and British industry. With Terence Ranger he edited The Invention of Tradition (1983), which showed how many supposedly ancient traditions were recent creations. Admirers praised his range and lucidity; critics faulted his treatment of Soviet communism.

### The Age of Revolution: Europe 1789–1848 (1962)

- **Date:** 1962
- **Type:** History
- **Written / held:** Published in London by Weidenfeld & Nicolson
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Age%20of%20Revolution%3A%20Europe%201789%E2%80%931848%20Eric%20Hobsbawm)

The Age of Revolution argued that the modern world was made by a dual revolution: the French Revolution of 1789, which transformed politics, and the British Industrial Revolution, which transformed the economy. Hobsbawm traced their effects on land, labour, ideology, religion, science and the arts across Europe and beyond, up to the revolutions of 1848. Written from a Marxist perspective but with wide learning and lucid prose, it became a standard text and was followed by The Age of Capital (1975), The Age of Empire (1987) and The Age of Extremes (1994). The series is among the most widely read works of modern historical synthesis.

### The Age of Extremes: The Short Twentieth Century, 1914–1991 (1994)

- **Date:** 1994
- **Type:** History
- **Written / held:** Published in London by Michael Joseph
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Age%20of%20Extremes%3A%20The%20Short%20Twentieth%20Century%2C%201914%E2%80%931991%20Eric%20Hobsbawm)

The Age of Extremes divides the period from 1914 to 1991 into an 'age of catastrophe' of world wars, depression and the rise of fascism, a 'golden age' of economic growth and social transformation after 1945, and a 'landslide' of crisis and the collapse of the Soviet bloc. Hobsbawm, who had lived through much of it, wrote with personal involvement and global range. It was a bestseller and widely translated. Reviewers praised its breadth and insight while some criticised its treatment of communism as too indulgent of Soviet crimes, a charge Hobsbawm partly acknowledged.
