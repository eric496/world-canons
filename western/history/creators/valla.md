## Biography

Lorenzo Valla was born in Rome around 1407 and educated in humanist circles. Quarrelsome and brilliant, he taught rhetoric at Pavia, which he had to leave after attacking a famous jurist, and then served as secretary to King Alfonso of Aragon and Naples, an enemy of the papacy. His critical writings brought him before the Inquisition in Naples, but the king protected him. Late in life he was reconciled with Rome and worked as a papal secretary under Nicholas V. He died in Rome in 1457.

Valla's treatise on the Donation of Constantine (1440) showed by historical and linguistic analysis that the document could not date from the fourth century. His Elegances of the Latin Language set new standards for classical Latin, and his annotations on the New Testament inspired Erasmus. His method, judging a text by the language and circumstances of its supposed time, became a foundation of historical source criticism.

## Key histories & documents

### On the Donation of Constantine (1440)

- **Date:** 1440
- **Type:** Critical treatise
- **Written / held:** Written in Naples; first printed 1506, influential edition by Ulrich von Hutten 1518–19
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20the%20Donation%20of%20Constantine%20Lorenzo%20Valla)

The Donation of Constantine purported to be a decree by which the emperor Constantine, grateful for his cure from leprosy, handed authority over Rome and the western empire to Pope Sylvester I. It had been used to support papal claims since the eleventh century, although its authenticity had been doubted before. Writing in 1440 for his patron Alfonso of Aragon, then at odds with the pope, Valla showed that its Latin contained words and forms unknown in the fourth century, that it contradicted known history, and that no contemporary source mentioned it. The forgery is now generally dated to the eighth century. The treatise circulated in manuscript and was printed by Protestant reformers in the sixteenth century. It is a landmark in the development of historical criticism of sources.
