# Procopius of Caesarea

**c. 500 – c. 565** · Byzantine (Greek-speaking)  
Born: Caesarea Maritima, Palestine · Died: probably Constantinople  
Traditions & eras: [Late antique & church history](../movements/church-history.md)  

> Secretary to the general Belisarius, Procopius wrote the official history of Justinian's wars, and secretly a scandalous attack on the emperor and empress.

## Biography

Procopius was born around 500 in Caesarea in Palestine and trained in law. In 527 he became legal adviser and secretary to Belisarius, Justinian's leading general, and accompanied him on campaigns against Persia, against the Vandals in North Africa and against the Ostrogoths in Italy. He was in Constantinople during the Nika riots of 532 and the great plague of 542, which he described. His later life is obscure; he probably died in the 560s.

Procopius wrote three very different works: the Wars, a classical narrative of Justinian's campaigns; the Buildings, a panegyric of the emperor's construction projects, including Hagia Sophia; and the Secret History, a venomous account of Justinian, Theodora and Belisarius that was not published in his lifetime. Reconciling the three has been a puzzle for scholars. He is the principal source for the age of Justinian and the last major historian of antiquity in the classical manner.

### Secret History (c. 550)

- **Date:** c. 550
- **Type:** Invective history
- **Written / held:** Written in Constantinople; unpublished in antiquity, rediscovered in the Vatican Library and first printed in 1623
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Secret%20History%20Procopius%20of%20Caesarea)

The Secret History, whose Greek title Anekdota means 'unpublished things', was written around 550 as a hidden companion to the Wars. In it Procopius claims to reveal what he could not write openly: that Justinian was a demon in human form who ruined the empire with taxes and wars, that the empress Theodora had been a notorious actress and prostitute, and that Belisarius was dominated by his wife Antonina. The work was known in Byzantium but rediscovered in the West only in the seventeenth century, when its first editor printed it from a Vatican manuscript in 1623. Historians have long debated how to reconcile its venom with the official praise of the Buildings. It remains a fascinating and much-quoted source, used with caution.

### History of the Wars (c. 545–553)

<sub>Manuscript of Procopius' Wars, Biblioteca Marciana, Venice, Gr. 398 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Procopius%2C_De_bellis%2C_Venice%2C_Gr._398.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 545–553
- **Type:** Prose history (8 books)
- **Written / held:** Written in Constantinople
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=History%20of%20the%20Wars%20Procopius%20of%20Caesarea)

The Wars recount the campaigns of the emperor Justinian, mostly led by Belisarius, in three parts: against the Sasanian Persians in the east, against the Vandal kingdom in North Africa, and against the Ostrogoths in Italy, followed by an eighth book bringing events up to 553. Procopius, writing in classicising Greek modelled on Herodotus and Thucydides, was present at many of the events as Belisarius' secretary. The work includes a famous description of the Nika riots of 532, in which Theodora is said to have persuaded Justinian not to flee, and the first clear account of the bubonic plague that struck Constantinople in 542. It is the principal narrative source for the age of Justinian.
