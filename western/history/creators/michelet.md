## Biography

Jules Michelet was born in Paris in 1798, the son of a struggling printer, and grew up poor. A brilliant student, he taught history and philosophy, translated Vico, and in 1831 became head of the historical section of the National Archives, which gave him unrivalled access to documents. He was appointed to the Collège de France in 1838, where his lectures against the Jesuits and in favour of the people made him a hero of the republican left. He lost his posts after refusing to swear allegiance to Napoleon III in 1851 and continued writing in semi-retirement until his death in Hyères in 1874.

Michelet's History of France and History of the French Revolution aimed at the 'resurrection of the whole life of the past'. He wrote with passionate empathy and a visionary, poetic style, making the people, rather than kings, the hero of French history, and giving a central place to figures such as Joan of Arc. He popularised the term Renaissance for the cultural rebirth of the sixteenth century. Later historians criticised his partisanship and imagination, but the Annales historians saw him as an ancestor.

## Key histories & documents

### History of France (1833–1867)

[![History of France](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/89/Histoire_de_France_Michelet_Hachette.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Histoire_de_France_Michelet_Hachette.png)

<sub>Cover of a Hachette edition of Michelet's Histoire de France Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Histoire_de_France_Michelet_Hachette.png).</sub>

- **Date:** 1833–1867
- **Type:** National history (17 volumes)
- **Written / held:** Written in Paris; published by Hachette and others
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=History%20of%20France%20Jules%20Michelet)

Michelet published the first six volumes of his Histoire de France, on the Middle Ages, between 1833 and 1844, using his privileged access to the National Archives. He then interrupted it to write his History of the French Revolution (1847–53), and returned to complete the story from the Renaissance to 1789 in eleven more volumes, finished in 1867. His famous 'Tableau de la France' describes the regions of the country as a living landscape, and his portrait of Joan of Arc as a saint of the people became a national icon. The volumes on the sixteenth century popularised the word Renaissance for the age of rediscovery of the world and of man. Later volumes are more anticlerical and polemical. Michelet's ambition was to resurrect the whole life of the past.
