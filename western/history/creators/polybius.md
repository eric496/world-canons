## Biography

Polybius was born around 200 BC in Megalopolis, a leading city of the Achaean League, and was the son of one of its statesmen. After Rome's victory over Macedon in 168 BC he was among a thousand Achaean notables deported to Italy. In Rome he became the friend and mentor of the young Scipio Aemilianus, and he was present at the destruction of Carthage in 146 BC. He later helped settle the affairs of Greece after its conquest by Rome. He is said to have died after a fall from a horse at the age of 82.

His Histories, in forty books of which five survive complete, cover the years from about 264 to 146 BC. Polybius insisted that historians must have political and military experience, visit the places they describe and examine documents, and he attacked earlier writers who wrote for entertainment. His analysis of the Roman constitution as a stable mixture of monarchy, aristocracy and democracy, and his theory of a cycle of constitutions, deeply influenced Machiavelli, Montesquieu and the framers of the United States Constitution.

## Key histories & documents

### Histories (c. 150–120 BC)

[![Histories](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/13/Polybius_1548.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Polybius_1548.jpg)

<sub>Polybius' Histories, 1548 edition Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Polybius_1548.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 150–120 BC
- **Type:** Universal history (40 books, partly preserved)
- **Written / held:** Written in Rome and Greece; books 1–5 survive complete in medieval manuscripts
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Histories%20Polybius)

Polybius asked a question he thought no one could ignore: how and under what kind of government the Romans had brought nearly the whole inhabited world under their rule in less than fifty-three years. To answer it he wrote a history linking events in Italy, Greece, Africa and Asia, which he saw as having become a single interconnected story. The surviving books cover the Punic Wars and Hannibal's invasion of Italy in detail, and book 6 contains his famous analysis of the Roman constitution as a balance of consuls, Senate and people. He set out strict principles for historians, insisting on personal inquiry, knowledge of geography and politics, and impartiality. His theory of mixed government passed through Cicero and Machiavelli to Montesquieu and the American founders.
