## Biography

Theodor Mommsen was born in 1817 in Garding in Schleswig, then ruled by Denmark, the son of a pastor. He studied law at Kiel and travelled in Italy and France collecting Roman inscriptions. A liberal, he lost his professorship at Leipzig for taking part in the revolution of 1848. He later taught at Zurich, Breslau and, from 1861, Berlin. He served as a liberal member of the Prussian and German parliaments and was a fierce critic of Bismarck and of antisemitism. He died in 1903.

Mommsen's History of Rome (1854–56), a vivid, partisan narrative that portrayed Caesar as the hero who swept away a corrupt aristocracy, made him famous and won him the Nobel Prize. His more lasting achievements were scholarly: he directed the Corpus of Latin Inscriptions, wrote a monumental study of Roman constitutional law, and organised collaborative research on a vast scale. He set the model for large-scale scholarly projects in the humanities.

## Key histories & documents

### History of Rome (1854–1856 (vol. 5, 1885))

- **Date:** 1854–1856 (vol. 5, 1885)
- **Type:** History (vols 1–3 and 5)
- **Written / held:** Written in Zurich and Breslau; published in Leipzig and Berlin
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=History%20of%20Rome%20Theodor%20Mommsen)

The first three volumes of Mommsen's Römische Geschichte, published in 1854–56, cover Rome from its origins to Caesar's victory at Thapsus in 46 BC. Mommsen wrote with the energy of a political pamphleteer, treating the Roman aristocracy as a decadent oligarchy and Caesar as a genius who created a new order; he described the politics of the Republic in the vocabulary of nineteenth-century parties. The planned fourth volume on the empire was never written, but in 1885 he published a fifth on the provinces of the empire, a masterpiece of scholarly synthesis. The Nobel committee in 1902 described him as the greatest living master of the art of historical writing, with special reference to this work.
