# Gaius Julius Caesar

**100 BC – 44 BC** · Roman  
Born: Rome · Died: Rome  
Traditions & eras: [Roman historiography & biography](../movements/roman-historiography.md)  

> Roman general, dictator and writer, Caesar recorded his conquest of Gaul and his civil war in terse commentaries that are also brilliant pieces of political self-presentation.

## Biography

Gaius Julius Caesar was born in Rome in 100 BC into an old patrician family. He rose through the offices of the Republic, formed an alliance with Pompey and Crassus, and as governor of Gaul from 58 BC conquered the whole of the region between the Pyrenees and the Rhine, also crossing into Britain and Germany. In 49 BC he led his army across the Rubicon into Italy, beginning a civil war against Pompey and the Senate. Victorious, he was made dictator for life, and was assassinated by a group of senators on the Ides of March, 44 BC.

Caesar wrote his commentaries in a famously clean, unadorned Latin, referring to himself in the third person. The form, a general's notes ostensibly offered as raw material for future historians, gave his account an air of objectivity while presenting his actions in the best light; his contemporary Cicero praised their bare elegance. They are the main source for the Gallic Wars and the civil war, and they have long been used to teach Latin. His name became a title for emperors, from Kaiser to Tsar.

### Commentaries on the Gallic War (De Bello Gallico) (c. 58–50 BC)

- **Date:** c. 58–50 BC
- **Type:** War commentary (7 books, plus an 8th by Hirtius)
- **Written / held:** Written in Gaul and Rome
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Commentaries%20on%20the%20Gallic%20War%20%28De%20Bello%20Gallico%29%20Gaius%20Julius%20Caesar)

Caesar wrote seven books of commentaries covering his campaigns in Gaul from 58 to 52 BC, probably sending them to Rome year by year or publishing them together around 51 BC, while his enemies in the Senate attacked his conduct. An eighth book was added after his death by his officer Aulus Hirtius. The work opens with the famous statement that all Gaul is divided into three parts, and describes his wars against the Helvetii, the Belgae and the Germans, two expeditions to Britain, and the great revolt led by Vercingetorix that ended at the siege of Alesia. Its descriptions of Gallic and Germanic society are among the earliest we have. Its plain, controlled style presents Caesar's actions as necessary and successful, and it has long served as a primer of Latin and of military history.

### Commentaries on the Civil War (De Bello Civili) (c. 49–45 BC)

- **Date:** c. 49–45 BC
- **Type:** War commentary (3 books)
- **Written / held:** Written in Italy and on campaign
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Commentaries%20on%20the%20Civil%20War%20%28De%20Bello%20Civili%29%20Gaius%20Julius%20Caesar)

The Civil War covers the years 49 and 48 BC: Caesar's invasion of Italy, his campaigns in Spain, the siege of Massilia, the war in Greece culminating in the battle of Pharsalus and Pompey's flight to Egypt, where he was murdered. Caesar presents himself as a moderate, merciful leader forced to defend his dignity and the rights of the tribunes against a narrow faction, and his opponents as arrogant and divided. The work is unfinished and may not have been published in his lifetime. It is a key source for the fall of the Roman Republic and a classic example of political self-justification.
