# Johan Huizinga

**1872 – 1945** · Dutch  
Born: Groningen, Netherlands · Died: De Steeg, near Arnhem, Netherlands  
Traditions & eras: [Cultural history](../movements/cultural-history.md)  

> A Dutch cultural historian, Huizinga wrote The Autumn of the Middle Ages and Homo Ludens, a study of play in culture.

## Biography

Johan Huizinga was born in Groningen in 1872, the son of a professor of physiology. He studied Dutch literature and Sanskrit, and taught history at a secondary school before becoming professor at Groningen in 1905 and at Leiden in 1915. He was one of the most respected intellectuals in the Netherlands between the wars and wrote critically of the cultural crisis of his time. After the German occupation closed Leiden University in 1942 he was held hostage by the Nazis for several months and then exiled to a village near Arnhem, where he died in February 1945, shortly before the liberation.

Huizinga approached history through images, forms and emotions rather than institutions, reading paintings, chronicles and courtly rituals to recover the mentality of an age. His Autumn of the Middle Ages portrayed fourteenth- and fifteenth-century Burgundy and France as an age of ripeness and decline, not the dawn of the Renaissance. His Homo Ludens (1938) argued that play is a fundamental element of culture. Both books continue to influence cultural history and other fields.

### The Autumn of the Middle Ages (1919)

- **Date:** 1919
- **Type:** Cultural history
- **Written / held:** Written in Leiden; first published in Haarlem by H. D. Tjeenk Willink
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Autumn%20of%20the%20Middle%20Ages%20Johan%20Huizinga)

Herfsttij der Middeleeuwen, published in 1919 and first translated into English as The Waning of the Middle Ages in 1924, began from Huizinga's wish to understand the painting of Jan van Eyck and his contemporaries. He found in the culture of the Burgundian court and its world not the dawn of the Renaissance but the overripe end of the Middle Ages: violent contrasts of emotion, elaborate rituals of chivalry and love, obsession with death, and a tendency to give every thought a visible form. He drew on chronicles, poetry, sermons and art. The book's evocative style and focus on sensibility made it a classic of cultural history and an ancestor of the history of mentalities.

### Homo Ludens (1938)

- **Date:** 1938
- **Type:** Cultural history / theory
- **Written / held:** Published in Haarlem by H. D. Tjeenk Willink
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Homo%20Ludens%20Johan%20Huizinga)

In Homo Ludens: A Study of the Play-Element in Culture, Huizinga argued that play is older than culture and that civilisation arises and unfolds in and as play. He defined play as a free activity, standing outside ordinary life, with its own rules, time and space, and traced the play-element in contests, law courts, warfare, poetry, philosophy and the arts across many cultures. In the final chapters he lamented the decline of genuine play in modern mass society and politics, a warning written in the shadow of totalitarianism. The book has influenced anthropology, the study of games and media theory.
