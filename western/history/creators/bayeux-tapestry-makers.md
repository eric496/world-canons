# Makers of the Bayeux Tapestry

**11th century – 11th century** · English (anonymous)  
Born: probably Canterbury, England · Died: England  
Traditions & eras: [Medieval chronicles & charters](../movements/medieval-chronicles.md)  

> The anonymous designers and embroiderers, probably English, who made the Bayeux Tapestry for, it is widely believed, Bishop Odo of Bayeux.

## Biography

Nothing is known for certain of the people who made the Bayeux Tapestry. Most scholars believe it was commissioned by Bishop Odo of Bayeux, William the Conqueror's half-brother, perhaps for the consecration of Bayeux Cathedral in 1077, and that it was designed and stitched in England, probably at Canterbury, within a few years of the Norman Conquest. English needlework was famous across Europe, and Old English spellings in the inscriptions support this view, though other theories have been proposed.

Whoever they were, the designers composed a continuous narrative of about seventy metres, combining a main story told largely from the Norman point of view with borders of fables, animals and everyday scenes that sometimes comment on the action. They worked in coloured wools on linen using a limited palette. The result is both a historical source and one of the great works of medieval art.

## Key histories & documents

### Bayeux Tapestry (c. 1070s)

[![Bayeux Tapestry](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Bayeux_Tapestry_scene57_Harold_death.jpg/1280px-Bayeux_Tapestry_scene57_Harold_death.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bayeux_Tapestry_scene57_Harold_death.jpg)

<sub>Scene 57: the death of King Harold at Hastings Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bayeux_Tapestry_scene57_Harold_death.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1070s
- **Type:** Embroidered narrative (wool on linen, c. 70 m)
- **Written / held:** Bayeux Museum (Musée de la Tapisserie de Bayeux), Normandy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Bayeux%20Tapestry%20Makers%20of%20the%20Bayeux%20Tapestry)

Though called a tapestry, it is an embroidery in coloured wools on linen, about 70 metres long and 50 centimetres high, probably made in England in the decade after the Conquest for Bishop Odo of Bayeux. In some fifty scenes with Latin captions it shows Harold Godwinson's journey to Normandy and his oath to Duke William, the death of Edward the Confessor and Harold's coronation, Halley's comet, William's preparations and Channel crossing, and the battle of Hastings in 1066, ending with Harold's death and the flight of the English; the end is lost. Its borders contain fables, animals and scenes of daily life. It is a unique visual source for armour, ships, horses and buildings of the eleventh century, and its version of events largely justifies the Norman claim. It has been displayed in Bayeux for most of its history.
