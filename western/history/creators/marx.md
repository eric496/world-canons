## Biography

Karl Marx was born in Trier in 1818 into a family of Jewish descent that had converted to Lutheranism. He studied law and philosophy in Bonn and Berlin, where he joined the Young Hegelians, and became a radical journalist. Expelled from Germany, France and Belgium in turn, he settled in London in 1849 and lived there for the rest of his life, often in poverty, supported by his friend and collaborator Friedrich Engels. He worked in the British Museum reading room on Capital, whose first volume appeared in 1867, and helped lead the International Working Men's Association. He died in London in 1883.

Marx argued that the way societies produce their material life shapes their politics, law and ideas, and that history moves through conflicts between classes. Besides his economic works he wrote incisive histories of his own time, such as The Eighteenth Brumaire and The Civil War in France. His ideas inspired socialist and labour movements worldwide and, in the twentieth century, communist states whose record of repression is part of his contested legacy; his influence on the writing of social and economic history has been profound.

### The Communist Manifesto (1848)

[![The Communist Manifesto](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/86/Communist-manifesto.png/960px-Communist-manifesto.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Communist-manifesto.png)

<sub>Cover of the first edition, London, February 1848 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Communist-manifesto.png).</sub>

- **Date:** 1848
- **Type:** Political manifesto
- **Written / held:** Written in Brussels with Friedrich Engels; first printed in London, February 1848
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Communist%20Manifesto%20Karl%20Marx)

The Manifest der Kommunistischen Partei was commissioned by the Communist League, a small group of German émigré workers, and written by Marx with Friedrich Engels in Brussels at the turn of 1847–48; it was printed in London in February 1848, just as revolution broke out across Europe. It opens by declaring that a spectre is haunting Europe and that the history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles. It describes the bourgeoisie as a revolutionary class that has transformed the world through capitalism, and predicts that the proletariat it has created will overthrow it, abolishing private property in the means of production. It ends with a call for workers of all countries to unite. Little noticed at first, it became one of the most widely read political documents in history, and it is admired by some and condemned by others for what was done in its name.

### The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte (1852)

[![The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8a/Marx_EighteenthBrumaire.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Marx_EighteenthBrumaire.JPG)

<sub>Cover of an early edition of The Eighteenth Brumaire Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Marx_EighteenthBrumaire.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1852
- **Type:** Contemporary history / political analysis
- **Written / held:** Written in London; first published in New York in the journal Die Revolution
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Eighteenth%20Brumaire%20of%20Louis%20Bonaparte%20Karl%20Marx)

Written between December 1851 and March 1852, immediately after Louis-Napoleon's coup d'état, this essay analyses the French revolution of 1848 and its collapse into a new Bonapartist dictatorship. Marx famously observes that men make their own history, but not under circumstances of their own choosing, and that history happens twice, the first time as tragedy, the second as farce. He traces the shifting alliances of classes and parties and explains how Louis-Napoleon could rise by balancing between them, with the support of the small-holding peasantry. It is considered his most brilliant piece of historical writing and a classic of political analysis.
