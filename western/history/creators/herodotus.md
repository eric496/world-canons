## Biography

Herodotus was born around 484 BC in Halicarnassus, a Greek city on the coast of Asia Minor then under Persian rule. Little is securely known of his life. He seems to have been exiled after political troubles in his home city, and he travelled widely, by his own account visiting Egypt, the Black Sea, Phoenicia and Babylon. He spent time in Athens, where he is said to have given public readings, and joined the Athenian colony at Thurii in southern Italy, where he probably died some time after 430 BC.

His Histories open by saying that he publishes his 'inquiry' (historia) so that great deeds should not be forgotten, and so that the cause of the conflict between Greeks and barbarians should be known. He gathered oral traditions, compared accounts and often reported versions he did not believe, letting the reader judge. Critics from Thucydides onwards called him credulous, a 'father of lies', but modern archaeology and ethnography have confirmed much of what he recorded, and his curiosity about other peoples makes him the founder of ethnography as well as of history.

## Key histories & documents

### Histories (c. 430 BC)

<sub>Fragment of Herodotus' Histories, book 8, on papyrus from Oxyrhynchus (P. Oxy. 2099), 2nd century AD Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:POxy_v0017_n2099_a_01_hires.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 430 BC
- **Type:** Prose history (9 books)
- **Written / held:** Written in Greece and southern Italy; earliest complete manuscripts medieval (e.g. Laurentian Library, Florence)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Histories%20Herodotus)

Herodotus composed his Histories in the third quarter of the fifth century BC, probably performing parts of it aloud before it took its final form; later scholars divided it into nine books, each named after a Muse. The first half travels outward, describing the rise of Persia and the customs, geography and wonders of Lydia, Egypt, Scythia and other lands, often in long digressions. The second half narrows to the invasions of Greece by Darius and Xerxes, culminating in Marathon, Thermopylae, Salamis and Plataea. Herodotus compares competing oral accounts, admits uncertainty and searches for causes in human motives as well as divine retribution. His word historia, meaning inquiry, gave history its name, and his combination of narrative, ethnography and analysis set the agenda for the discipline.
