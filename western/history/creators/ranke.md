# Leopold von Ranke

**1795 – 1886** · German  
Born: Wiehe, Electorate of Saxony · Died: Berlin  
Traditions & eras: [Professional & scientific history](../movements/scientific-history.md)  

> Often called the father of modern scientific history, Ranke made the critical study of archival sources the basis of the historical profession.

## Biography

Leopold Ranke was born in 1795 in Wiehe in Thuringia, into a family of Lutheran pastors and lawyers, and studied classics and theology at Leipzig. While teaching at a school in Frankfurt an der Oder he wrote his first book, whose success won him a post at the University of Berlin in 1825. He then spent years in the archives of Vienna, Venice and Rome, notably studying the reports of Venetian ambassadors. He taught at Berlin for half a century, was appointed historiographer of Prussia, and was ennobled in 1865. He kept writing into his nineties and died in Berlin in 1886.

In the preface to his first book Ranke said that he wished only to show the past 'as it actually was', a phrase that has been endlessly quoted and debated; it was meant less as naive objectivity than as a refusal to judge the past by the present. His research seminars trained generations of historians and spread the practice of archival research and footnoted critical scholarship to Europe and America. His own works focused on states, churches and great powers, an emphasis later historians challenged.

### Histories of the Latin and Germanic Peoples, 1494–1514 (1824)

- **Date:** 1824
- **Type:** History
- **Written / held:** Published in Leipzig and Berlin by Reimer
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Histories%20of%20the%20Latin%20and%20Germanic%20Peoples%2C%201494%E2%80%931514%20Leopold%20von%20Ranke)

Ranke's Geschichten der romanischen und germanischen Völker von 1494 bis 1514 described the struggles over Italy at the beginning of the Italian Wars, treating the Latin and Germanic nations as a single cultural unit. In a famous preface he wrote that history had been assigned the office of judging the past and instructing the present for the benefit of future ages, but that his work aspired only to show what actually happened. In an appendix, published as a separate volume, he subjected earlier historians, including Guicciardini, to rigorous criticism, arguing that only contemporary documents and eyewitness reports could be trusted. The book earned him a chair at Berlin and set out the method of critical history.

### The History of the Popes (1834–1836)

- **Date:** 1834–1836
- **Type:** History (3 volumes)
- **Written / held:** Published in Berlin; based on research in Venetian and Roman archives
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20History%20of%20the%20Popes%20Leopold%20von%20Ranke)

Die römischen Päpste, ihre Kirche und ihr Staat im sechzehnten und siebzehnten Jahrhundert was based on Ranke's research in Venice, Vienna and Rome, where the Vatican archives were closed to him, so he relied on the reports of Venetian ambassadors and on private collections. It describes the Catholic Reformation and the papacy as a political power during the age of the Reformation and the Counter-Reformation. Though a Lutheran, Ranke wrote without polemic, which was widely admired, though the book was placed on the Catholic Index. Macaulay's essay on it popularised it in England. It is a classic example of Ranke's cool, document-based narrative of states and churches.
