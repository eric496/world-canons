# Fernand Braudel

**1902 – 1985** · French  
Born: Luméville-en-Ornois, Meuse, France · Died: Cluses, Haute-Savoie, France  
Traditions & eras: [Annales school](../movements/annales-school.md)  

> The leading figure of the second generation of the Annales school, Braudel wrote The Mediterranean and developed the idea of history's 'long duration'.

## Biography

Fernand Braudel was born in 1902 in a village in Lorraine and studied history in Paris. He taught in Algeria and in São Paulo, and began research in the Spanish archives on Philip II. Captured as a French soldier in 1940, he spent five years in German prisoner-of-war camps, where he wrote much of his great book from memory. After the war he succeeded Lucien Febvre as the leader of the Annales school, editing the journal, teaching at the Collège de France and directing the sixth section of the École pratique des hautes études. He was elected to the Académie française in 1984 and died in 1985.

Braudel distinguished three speeds of historical time: the almost motionless history of geography and environment, the slow rhythms of economies and societies, and the fast-moving surface of events and politics, which he considered the least significant. His Mediterranean and his three-volume Civilization and Capitalism applied this vision on a vast scale. His approach influenced world history and world-systems theory, though critics noted that it left little room for human agency and politics.

## Key histories & documents

### The Mediterranean and the Mediterranean World in the Age of Philip II (1949)

- **Date:** 1949
- **Type:** Total history
- **Written / held:** Largely drafted in German prisoner-of-war camps; published in Paris by Armand Colin
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Mediterranean%20and%20the%20Mediterranean%20World%20in%20the%20Age%20of%20Philip%20II%20Fernand%20Braudel)

Braudel began research on Philip II's Mediterranean policy in the 1920s, but turned the sea itself into his subject. He wrote much of the book from memory as a prisoner of war in Germany from 1940 to 1945, defending it as a thesis in 1947 and publishing it in 1949; a revised edition followed in 1966. Its three parts move at three speeds of time: the almost unchanging environment of mountains, plains, seas and climate; the slow rhythms of economies, states, societies and civilisations; and the fast-moving history of events, culminating in the war between the Spanish and Ottoman empires and the battle of Lepanto. It is the classic work of the Annales school and of the 'long duration'.
