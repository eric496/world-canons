# Abraham Lincoln

**1809 – 1865** · American  
Born: near Hodgenville, Kentucky · Died: Washington, D.C.  
Traditions & eras: [Reform, emancipation & revolution](../movements/nineteenth-century-documents.md)  

> The sixteenth President of the United States led the Union through the Civil War, issued the Emancipation Proclamation and gave the Gettysburg Address.

## Biography

Abraham Lincoln was born in a log cabin in Kentucky in 1809 and grew up on the frontier in Indiana and Illinois with little formal schooling. He worked as a shopkeeper, postmaster and surveyor, taught himself law and became a successful lawyer in Springfield, Illinois. After a term in Congress he rose to national prominence by opposing the expansion of slavery in debates with Stephen Douglas in 1858. His election as President in 1860 prompted the secession of Southern states. He led the Union through the Civil War and was assassinated by John Wilkes Booth in April 1865, days after the Confederate surrender at Appomattox.

Lincoln's position on slavery moved from opposing its expansion to emancipation as a war measure and, finally, to backing the Thirteenth Amendment, which abolished it. Historians continue to debate the pace and motives of that change. He was a writer of exceptional economy and moral seriousness, and his speeches, especially the Gettysburg Address and the Second Inaugural, are among the most studied texts in American history.

### Emancipation Proclamation (1863)

- **Date:** 1863
- **Type:** Executive order
- **Written / held:** Signed in Washington, D.C., 1 January 1863; original in the National Archives
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Emancipation%20Proclamation%20Abraham%20Lincoln)

Lincoln issued a preliminary proclamation after the battle of Antietam in September 1862 and signed the final proclamation on 1 January 1863. Issued as a war measure under his powers as commander-in-chief, it declared that all persons held as slaves in areas then in rebellion against the United States were and henceforward shall be free. It did not apply to slave states loyal to the Union or to Union-occupied areas, so it freed few people immediately, but every advance of Union armies thereafter brought freedom, and it authorised the enlistment of Black soldiers, nearly 200,000 of whom served. It transformed the war into a war against slavery and deterred European recognition of the Confederacy. Slavery was abolished throughout the United States by the Thirteenth Amendment in 1865.

### Gettysburg Address (1863)

- **Date:** 1863
- **Type:** Speech
- **Written / held:** Delivered at the Soldiers' National Cemetery, Gettysburg, Pennsylvania, 19 November 1863; five autograph copies survive, including in the Library of Congress
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Gettysburg%20Address%20Abraham%20Lincoln)

Lincoln spoke at the dedication of the national cemetery at Gettysburg, four and a half months after the battle, following a two-hour oration by Edward Everett. In about two minutes he recalled that the nation had been conceived in liberty and dedicated to the proposition that all men are created equal, said that the living could not consecrate the ground that the dead had already hallowed, and called for a new birth of freedom so that government of the people, by the people, for the people shall not perish from the earth. Newspaper reactions divided along party lines. Five manuscript copies in Lincoln's hand survive, which differ slightly; the Bliss copy is the one usually reproduced. The address became one of the most quoted speeches in history.
