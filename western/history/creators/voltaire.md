# Voltaire (François-Marie Arouet)

**1694 – 1778** · French  
Born: Paris · Died: Paris  
Traditions & eras: [Enlightenment history](../movements/enlightenment-history.md)  
Also in: [Literature](../../literature/creators/voltaire.md)  

> The leading writer of the French Enlightenment, Voltaire also reinvented history as the story of civilisation, manners and the human mind.

## Biography

François-Marie Arouet was born in Paris in 1694, the son of a notary, and educated by the Jesuits. He took the name Voltaire after imprisonment in the Bastille for satirical verses. A stay in England from 1726 to 1728 made him an admirer of Locke, Newton and English liberty. He lived for years at Cirey with the scientist Émilie du Châtelet, spent a difficult period at the court of Frederick the Great, and finally settled at Ferney near the Swiss border, from where he campaigned against religious intolerance. He returned to Paris in triumph in 1778 and died there that year.

As well as plays, poems, Candide and countless pamphlets, Voltaire wrote major histories: of Charles XII of Sweden, the Age of Louis XIV, and the Essay on the Manners and Spirit of Nations. He wanted history to describe arts, sciences, commerce and customs rather than only kings and wars, and he included China, India and Islam in his story. He was sceptical, witty and often partisan, especially against the church, and he helped coin the phrase 'philosophy of history'.

### The Age of Louis XIV (1751)

- **Date:** 1751
- **Type:** History
- **Written / held:** Written in France and at Potsdam; first published in Berlin, 1751
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Age%20of%20Louis%20XIV%20Voltaire%20%28Fran%C3%A7ois-Marie%20Arouet%29)

Voltaire worked on Le Siècle de Louis XIV for some twenty years, publishing it in Berlin in 1751 while at the court of Frederick the Great. He declared that he intended to depict not the actions of one man but the spirit of men in the most enlightened age there had ever been. Along with the wars and politics of the reign, the book devotes chapters to government, commerce, law, the church, religious disputes, science, literature and the fine arts, and to anecdotes of court life. Voltaire consulted surviving witnesses and documents. The work admires the king's patronage of culture while condemning religious persecution, including the revocation of the Edict of Nantes. It is a founding work of cultural history.

### Essay on the Manners and Spirit of Nations (1756)

- **Date:** 1756
- **Type:** Universal history
- **Written / held:** First published in Geneva, 1756; expanded editions to 1769
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Essay%20on%20the%20Manners%20and%20Spirit%20of%20Nations%20Voltaire%20%28Fran%C3%A7ois-Marie%20Arouet%29)

The Essai sur les mœurs et l'esprit des nations grew out of Voltaire's wish to give Émilie du Châtelet a history worth reading, and was published in 1756 and repeatedly enlarged. Deliberately answering Bossuet's Discourse on Universal History, which centred on the Jews and Christianity, Voltaire opened with China, India and Persia and the Islamic world before turning to Europe, treating Christianity as one civilisation among others. He was interested in laws, customs, arts and beliefs rather than battles and dynasties, and he relentlessly attacked fanaticism and superstition. Later editions included an introduction he called the Philosophy of History. The work's scope was revolutionary, though its scholarship was uneven and its judgments often polemical.
