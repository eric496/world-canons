## Biography

Alexis de Tocqueville was born in Paris in 1805 into a Norman aristocratic family; several of his relatives had been guillotined during the Terror. Trained as a magistrate, he travelled in the United States in 1831–32 with his friend Gustave de Beaumont, officially to study prisons. The first volume of Democracy in America appeared in 1835 and made him famous. He was elected to the Chamber of Deputies in 1839, helped write the constitution of the Second Republic in 1848, and was briefly foreign minister in 1849. Retiring after Louis-Napoleon's coup of 1851, he wrote The Old Regime and the Revolution. He died of tuberculosis at Cannes in 1859.

Tocqueville saw the advance of social equality as an irresistible historical force and asked how liberty could be preserved within it. He warned of the tyranny of the majority and a soft despotism of centralised administration, and praised local self-government and civil associations as antidotes. In his study of the Revolution he argued, from archival research, that it continued the centralising work of the monarchy more than it broke with it. Both books are still widely read.

### Democracy in America (1835 and 1840)

- **Date:** 1835 and 1840
- **Type:** Political and social analysis (2 volumes)
- **Written / held:** Written in Paris after travels in the United States in 1831–32; published by Gosselin
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Democracy%20in%20America%20Alexis%20de%20Tocqueville)

After nine months in the United States in 1831–32, Tocqueville published the first volume of De la démocratie en Amérique in 1835 and the second in 1840. The first describes American institutions: townships, federalism, the courts, the press, parties and the power of the majority, which he feared could become a new kind of tyranny. It ends with a long chapter on the three races of America, analysing the destruction of Native Americans and slavery, which he predicted would lead to catastrophe. The second volume asks how equality of conditions shapes ideas, feelings and manners, warning of individualism and of a mild despotism of administration, and praising voluntary associations, local liberty and religion as counterweights. It is still read as the most perceptive book ever written about the United States.

### The Old Regime and the Revolution (1856)

[![The Old Regime and the Revolution](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Alexis_de_Tocqueville_-_L%27Ancien_R%C3%A9gime_et_la_R%C3%A9volution%2C_L%C3%A9vy%2C_1866.djvu/page1-960px-Alexis_de_Tocqueville_-_L%27Ancien_R%C3%A9gime_et_la_R%C3%A9volution%2C_L%C3%A9vy%2C_1866.djvu.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Alexis_de_Tocqueville_-_L%27Ancien_R%C3%A9gime_et_la_R%C3%A9volution%2C_L%C3%A9vy%2C_1866.djvu)

<sub>L'Ancien Régime et la Révolution, Michel Lévy edition, 1866 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Alexis_de_Tocqueville_-_L%27Ancien_R%C3%A9gime_et_la_R%C3%A9volution%2C_L%C3%A9vy%2C_1866.djvu).</sub>

- **Date:** 1856
- **Type:** Historical analysis
- **Written / held:** Written in Paris and Tours; published by Michel Lévy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Old%20Regime%20and%20the%20Revolution%20Alexis%20de%20Tocqueville)

In L'Ancien Régime et la Révolution Tocqueville used provincial archives, especially those of the intendants, to study France before 1789. He argued that the Revolution was less a break than a continuation of trends, above all the centralisation of administration under the absolute monarchy, which had already deprived the nobility of its political role while leaving its privileges, making them hated. He observed that revolutions tend to come not when conditions are at their worst but when a bad government begins to reform. The book was planned as the first part of a larger work that his death prevented. It is a pioneering example of archival social and administrative history.
