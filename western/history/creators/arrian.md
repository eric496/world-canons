## Biography

Lucius Flavius Arrianus was born around AD 86 in Nicomedia in Bithynia. As a young man he studied with the Stoic philosopher Epictetus, whose teachings he recorded as the Discourses and the Handbook. He then entered Roman service, reached the consulship around 130 under Hadrian, and governed Cappadocia, where he repelled an invasion by the Alans. In later life he lived in Athens, where he held the chief magistracy, and devoted himself to writing.

Arrian consciously modelled himself on Xenophon, calling himself a new Xenophon. His Anabasis of Alexander is based on lost accounts by Ptolemy and Aristobulus, who had served with Alexander, and he explains his choice of sources in a way modern historians admire. He also wrote on India, on tactics, on hunting and on the Black Sea coast. Because the contemporary histories of Alexander are lost, Arrian remains the foundation of most modern narratives of the conqueror's life.

## Key histories & documents

### Anabasis of Alexander (c. AD 130s–140s)

- **Date:** c. AD 130s–140s
- **Type:** Military history (7 books)
- **Written / held:** Written in the Roman East or Athens
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Anabasis%20of%20Alexander%20Arrian%20of%20Nicomedia)

Arrian's Anabasis of Alexander, named after Xenophon's work, follows Alexander from his accession in 336 BC through the conquest of the Persian Empire, the campaigns in Central Asia and India, and the return to Babylon, where he died in 323 BC. Arrian explains that he relied principally on Ptolemy, Alexander's general and later king of Egypt, and on Aristobulus, an engineer on the expedition, because both had taken part and wrote after Alexander's death, when they had less reason to flatter him. He clearly marks other material as hearsay. Though admiring, he criticises Alexander's excesses, such as the killing of Cleitus. Because the contemporary sources are lost, Arrian is the foundation of most modern narratives of Alexander.
