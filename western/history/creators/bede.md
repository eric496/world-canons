## Biography

Bede was born around 673 on lands belonging to the twin monastery of Wearmouth-Jarrow in Northumbria and was given to the monastery at the age of seven. He spent almost his whole life there, as monk and priest, studying in one of the finest libraries in western Europe, assembled by the abbots Benedict Biscop and Ceolfrith. He died at Jarrow in 735, reportedly while finishing a translation of the Gospel of John. He was later called 'the Venerable' and was declared a Doctor of the Church in 1899.

Bede wrote biblical commentaries, works on grammar, poetry, science and time-reckoning, saints' lives and a martyrology. His Ecclesiastical History, completed in 731, names his informants and quotes documents, and is the foundation of early English history. His use of anno Domini dating, in this work and his treatise on time, did much to spread the system across Europe. He is often called the father of English history.

## Key histories & documents

### Ecclesiastical History of the English People (731)

[![Ecclesiastical History of the English People](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/68/Beda_Petersburgiensis_f3v.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Beda_Petersburgiensis_f3v.jpg)

<sub>The St Petersburg Bede, an 8th-century manuscript of the Ecclesiastical History (National Library of Russia) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Beda_Petersburgiensis_f3v.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 731
- **Type:** Church and national history (5 books)
- **Written / held:** Written at Jarrow, Northumbria; early copies include the St Petersburg Bede and the Moore Bede (Cambridge University Library)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ecclesiastical%20History%20of%20the%20English%20People%20Bede)

Bede completed the Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum in 731. It runs from Caesar's invasion of Britain to Bede's own day, focusing on the conversion of the Anglo-Saxon kingdoms by missionaries from Rome and from Ireland and Iona, the decision at the Synod of Whitby in 664 to follow Roman customs, and the lives of saints such as Aidan and Cuthbert. In his preface Bede names the people who supplied him with information, and he quotes letters and documents, an unusual practice that makes his work exceptionally valuable. Memorable passages include Pope Gregory's pun on Angles and angels and the image of human life as a sparrow flying through a lighted hall. Bede's use of anno Domini dating helped spread the system. Two manuscripts copied within a few years of his death survive.
