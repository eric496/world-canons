# Leonardo Bruni

**c. 1370 – 1444** · Italian (Florentine)  
Born: Arezzo, Tuscany · Died: Florence  
Traditions & eras: [Renaissance humanist history](../movements/renaissance-history.md)  

> Humanist and chancellor of Florence, Bruni wrote a history of the Florentine people that is often called the first modern work of history.

## Biography

Leonardo Bruni was born around 1370 in Arezzo and studied in Florence, where he learned Greek from the Byzantine scholar Manuel Chrysoloras. He served as a papal secretary for a decade before returning to Florence, where he was chancellor of the republic from 1427 until his death in 1444. He was given a state funeral and a magnificent tomb in Santa Croce, sculpted by Bernardo Rossellino, with his History laid on his breast.

Bruni translated Plato, Aristotle and Plutarch into elegant Latin and wrote lives of Dante and Petrarch. In his History of the Florentine People he rejected legend and divine providence as explanations, used archival documents, and told the story of the republic as one of civic liberty. He described the period after the fall of Rome as a long decline and his own age as a revival, helping to create the threefold division of history into ancient, medieval and modern.

## Key histories & documents

### History of the Florentine People (1415–1442)

- **Date:** 1415–1442
- **Type:** Humanist history (12 books)
- **Written / held:** Written in Florence
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=History%20of%20the%20Florentine%20People%20Leonardo%20Bruni)

Bruni began his Historiae Florentini populi around 1415 and worked on it until shortly before his death; it runs from the city's founding to 1402. Written in elegant classical Latin modelled on Livy, it rejects the legends of earlier chroniclers about Florence's origins, explains events through human causes rather than divine providence, and draws on documents from the city's archives. Its theme is Florentine liberty, which Bruni traced to the city's foundation under the Roman Republic rather than the empire. The Florentine government granted him tax exemptions for his work, and it was translated into Italian in 1473. It set the pattern for humanist histories of cities and states across Europe.
