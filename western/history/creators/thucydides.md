> An Athenian general turned historian, Thucydides wrote the history of the Peloponnesian War with a rigour and analytical power that have made him a model for historians and political thinkers.

## Biography

Thucydides was born in Athens around 460 BC into a wealthy family with connections to Thrace, where it owned gold-mining rights. He survived the plague that struck Athens in 430 BC and described its symptoms precisely. In 424 BC, as one of the city's generals, he failed to prevent the Spartan commander Brasidas from taking Amphipolis and was exiled for twenty years. He used his exile to gather information from both sides. His history breaks off in the middle of the events of 411 BC, and he probably died around 400 BC.

Thucydides set out to write a 'possession for all time' rather than a performance to please an audience. He rejected myth and hearsay, compared eyewitness reports, and sought the underlying causes of events, famously identifying Spartan fear of growing Athenian power as the truest cause of the war. The speeches he composed for his characters, such as Pericles' Funeral Oration and the Melian Dialogue, set out the logic of power and justice with stark clarity. He became a founding text for the study of international relations, and is often cited in debates about realism in politics.

## Key histories & documents

### History of the Peloponnesian War (c. 431–400 BC)

[![History of the Peloponnesian War](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Thucydides_Manuscript.jpg/1280px-Thucydides_Manuscript.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Thucydides_Manuscript.jpg)

<sub>Medieval Greek manuscript of Thucydides Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Thucydides_Manuscript.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 431–400 BC
- **Type:** Prose history (8 books, unfinished)
- **Written / held:** Written in Athens and in exile; medieval manuscripts (e.g. Laurentian Library, Florence)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=History%20of%20the%20Peloponnesian%20War%20Thucydides)

Thucydides began writing at the outbreak of the war in 431 BC, convinced it would be the greatest conflict the Greeks had known. His account, divided by later editors into eight books, follows the war year by year, summer and winter, until it breaks off in 411 BC. He explains his method at the outset: he checked eyewitness reports and composed speeches to convey what the situation demanded. Set pieces include Pericles' Funeral Oration, the plague of Athens, the civil war at Corcyra, the Melian Dialogue, in which Athenian envoys tell the weak that they must suffer what they must, and the catastrophic expedition to Sicily. The work became a model of political history and a foundational text for theorists of international relations, from Hobbes, who translated it, to modern realists.
