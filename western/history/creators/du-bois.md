# W. E. B. Du Bois

**1868 – 1963** · American (later Ghanaian)  
Born: Great Barrington, Massachusetts · Died: Accra, Ghana  
Traditions & eras: [20th-century history & human-rights documents](../movements/twentieth-century-history.md)  

> Sociologist, historian and civil-rights leader, Du Bois wrote The Souls of Black Folk and Black Reconstruction and helped found the NAACP.

## Biography

William Edward Burghardt Du Bois was born in Great Barrington, Massachusetts, in 1868. He studied at Fisk University, at Berlin and at Harvard, where in 1895 he became the first African American to earn a doctorate there, with a dissertation on the suppression of the African slave trade. He pioneered empirical sociology with The Philadelphia Negro and taught at Atlanta University. In 1909 he co-founded the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People and edited its magazine, The Crisis, for a quarter of a century. He was also a leader of the Pan-African movement. In later life he moved leftward, was harassed by the US government, joined the Communist Party in 1961 and moved to Ghana, where he died in 1963.

Du Bois's Souls of Black Folk (1903) introduced the idea of 'double consciousness' and declared that the problem of the twentieth century was the problem of the colour line. His Black Reconstruction in America (1935) challenged the prevailing view that Reconstruction had failed because of Black incompetence, emphasising instead the agency of freed people; it was largely ignored by white historians at the time but is now seen as a landmark. He is one of the central figures of American intellectual history.

### The Souls of Black Folk (1903)

- **Date:** 1903
- **Type:** Essays
- **Written / held:** Published in Chicago by A. C. McClurg
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Souls%20of%20Black%20Folk%20W.%20E.%20B.%20Du%20Bois)

The Souls of Black Folk gathers fourteen essays, several previously published, that combine history, sociology, autobiography and fiction. Du Bois declares that the problem of the twentieth century is the problem of the colour line and describes the 'double consciousness' of Black Americans, always seeing themselves through the eyes of others. Chapters examine the Freedmen's Bureau and Reconstruction, criticise Booker T. Washington's strategy of accommodation, describe rural Georgia and the death of Du Bois's infant son, and celebrate the 'sorrow songs', the spirituals, with musical epigraphs heading each chapter. The book was a landmark in African American literature and political thought and helped inspire the civil-rights movement.

### Black Reconstruction in America (1935)

- **Date:** 1935
- **Type:** History
- **Written / held:** Published in New York by Harcourt, Brace
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Black%20Reconstruction%20in%20America%20W.%20E.%20B.%20Du%20Bois)

Black Reconstruction in America: An Essay Toward a History of the Part Which Black Folk Played in the Attempt to Reconstruct Democracy in America, 1860–1880 challenged the dominant 'Dunning school', which portrayed Reconstruction as a disastrous period of corruption caused by Black suffrage. Du Bois argued that enslaved people had freed themselves by a 'general strike', leaving the plantations to work for and fight with the Union, and that Reconstruction governments had achieved real advances such as public schools before being overthrown by violence. His use of Marxist categories and his final chapter, 'The Propaganda of History', attacking white historians, ensured it was largely ignored by the profession at the time. From the 1960s historians increasingly vindicated his interpretation, and it is now regarded as a classic.
