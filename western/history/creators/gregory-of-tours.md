## Biography

Georgius Florentius, later called Gregory, was born around 538 in Clermont into a Gallo-Roman senatorial family that had produced many bishops. He was educated by churchmen, and in 573 he became bishop of Tours, home to the shrine of St Martin, one of the most important churches in Gaul. As bishop he dealt directly with the quarrelsome Frankish kings, defending his church and his own position, and on one occasion stood trial on a charge of slandering the queen. He died in 594.

Gregory's Ten Books of Histories run from the Creation to 591, but most of them describe his own time in dense, anecdotal detail: murders, feuds, miracles, plagues and royal intrigue. He wrote in a Latin he himself apologised for as rustic. Older readers saw a credulous chronicler of a barbarous age; modern scholars stress his careful moral and theological design. He also wrote several books on miracles and saints' lives.

## Key histories & documents

### History of the Franks (Ten Books of Histories) (c. 575–594)

- **Date:** c. 575–594
- **Type:** Chronicle / history (10 books)
- **Written / held:** Written in Tours, Gaul
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=History%20of%20the%20Franks%20%28Ten%20Books%20of%20Histories%29%20Gregory%20of%20Tours)

Gregory called his work simply the Ten Books of Histories; the title History of the Franks was added later. It begins with the Creation and a summary of world history, but from the fifth book onward it describes Gregory's own time in great detail: the struggles between the sons and grandsons of Clovis, the feud between queens Brunhild and Fredegund, plagues, famines and portents, and the day-to-day affairs of the Church. Clovis's conversion and baptism, told in book 2, became a founding moment in French national memory. Gregory writes as a bishop interpreting events morally, and he often appears in his own pages. Without him the history of sixth-century Gaul would be almost unknown.
