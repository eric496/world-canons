## Biography

Gaius Sallustius Crispus was born in 86 BC at Amiternum in the Sabine country, to a plebeian family. He was tribune of the people in 52 BC, was expelled from the Senate in 50 BC, allegedly for immorality, and then sided with Caesar in the civil war. As governor of the province of Africa Nova he was accused of extortion but escaped trial, and he retired very rich to his famous gardens in Rome, the Horti Sallustiani. After Caesar's murder he withdrew from politics and devoted himself to writing.

Sallust wrote two complete monographs, on the conspiracy of Catiline and on the war against Jugurtha, and a larger Histories that survives only in fragments. His style is compressed, archaic and abrupt, deliberately modelled on Thucydides, and his theme is the corruption of Roman virtue by wealth, ambition and faction after the fall of Carthage. Readers have long noted the gap between his moralising and his own career. He was much admired by Tacitus and by Renaissance and early-modern political writers.

### The Conspiracy of Catiline (Bellum Catilinae) (c. 42 BC)

- **Date:** c. 42 BC
- **Type:** Historical monograph
- **Written / held:** Written in Rome
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Conspiracy%20of%20Catiline%20%28Bellum%20Catilinae%29%20Sallust%20%28Gaius%20Sallustius%20Crispus%29)

Sallust's first monograph tells how the bankrupt aristocrat Lucius Sergius Catilina plotted to seize power in Rome in 63 BC, and how the consul Cicero exposed the conspiracy, leading to the execution of several conspirators without trial and Catiline's death in battle. Sallust frames the story with a meditation on how Rome's virtue was corrupted by wealth and ambition after the fall of Carthage. The centrepiece is a pair of speeches in the Senate debate on the conspirators' fate by Julius Caesar, urging clemency, and Cato the Younger, demanding death. The work is notable for giving Cicero less credit than he gave himself. It became a model of compact political history.

### The Jugurthine War (Bellum Jugurthinum) (c. 41 BC)

- **Date:** c. 41 BC
- **Type:** Historical monograph
- **Written / held:** Written in Rome
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Jugurthine%20War%20%28Bellum%20Jugurthinum%29%20Sallust%20%28Gaius%20Sallustius%20Crispus%29)

This monograph narrates the war of 112–105 BC against Jugurtha, a ruthless prince who seized the throne of Numidia in North Africa and repeatedly bribed Roman senators and commanders. Sallust chose the subject, he said, because it was the first time the arrogance of the nobility was challenged. The war was finally won by Gaius Marius, a 'new man' from outside the aristocracy, with the help of his quaestor Sulla, whose later rivalry would plunge Rome into civil war. The work combines geography of Africa, battle narrative and speeches, and was much read by Renaissance and early-modern writers on politics.
