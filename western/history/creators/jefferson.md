## Biography

Thomas Jefferson was born in 1743 at Shadwell in Virginia, the son of a planter and surveyor, and studied at the College of William and Mary before becoming a lawyer. Elected to the Second Continental Congress, he drafted the Declaration of Independence in 1776. He later served as governor of Virginia, minister to France, the first Secretary of State, Vice-President and, from 1801 to 1809, President, when he made the Louisiana Purchase. In retirement he founded the University of Virginia. He died on 4 July 1826, fifty years to the day after the Declaration was adopted.

Jefferson asked to be remembered for three things: the Declaration of Independence, the Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom and the University of Virginia. He wrote eloquently of human equality, yet enslaved more than six hundred people over his lifetime, and historians now broadly accept that he fathered children with Sally Hemings, an enslaved woman. This contradiction is central to modern assessments of him and of the American founding.

### Declaration of Independence (1776)

[![Declaration of Independence](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8f/United_States_Declaration_of_Independence.jpg/1280px-United_States_Declaration_of_Independence.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:United_States_Declaration_of_Independence.jpg)

<sub>The signed engrossed Declaration of Independence (National Archives, Washington) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:United_States_Declaration_of_Independence.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1776
- **Type:** Declaration
- **Written / held:** Adopted in Philadelphia; signed engrossed copy in the National Archives, Washington, D.C.
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Declaration%20of%20Independence%20Thomas%20Jefferson)

On 2 July 1776 the Second Continental Congress voted for independence from Britain, and on 4 July it adopted a declaration drafted mainly by Thomas Jefferson, with edits by John Adams, Benjamin Franklin and the Congress itself, which cut a passage blaming George III for the slave trade. Its preamble states that all men are created equal and endowed with unalienable rights to life, liberty and the pursuit of happiness, that governments derive their just powers from the consent of the governed, and that people may alter or abolish a government that destroys these ends. A long list of grievances against the king follows. The engrossed parchment was signed by most delegates on 2 August. The gap between its language of equality and the reality of slavery and the exclusion of women and Native Americans was pointed out from the start, and abolitionists, suffragists and civil-rights leaders invoked it to demand that its promise be fulfilled. It inspired many later declarations of independence.

### Virginia Statute for Religious Freedom (1777 (enacted 1786))

- **Date:** 1777 (enacted 1786)
- **Type:** State law
- **Written / held:** Drafted in Virginia; enacted by the Virginia General Assembly in Richmond, 1786
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Virginia%20Statute%20for%20Religious%20Freedom%20Thomas%20Jefferson)

Jefferson drafted the bill in 1777, but it was enacted by the Virginia General Assembly only in January 1786, steered through by James Madison while Jefferson was in France. It declared that no one should be compelled to attend or support any religious worship or suffer for their religious opinions, and that such opinions should not affect civil capacities. Its preamble argued that truth is great and will prevail if left free. The statute ended the established church in Virginia and was a direct precursor of the religion clauses of the First Amendment. Jefferson listed it among the three achievements to be engraved on his tombstone.
