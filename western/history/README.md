Two intertwined canons: the books in which Western writers tried to record and explain the past, and the charters, declarations and constitutions that themselves changed its course. It runs from Herodotus and Thucydides through the medieval chroniclers, the humanists and the Enlightenment to the professional historians of the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, alongside documents from Magna Carta to the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. Manuscripts, original documents and early editions are shown from Wikimedia Commons; recent books still under copyright are described without images.

## Notes on this section

- **Two canons in one.** Histories (books that interpret the past) sit alongside documents (charters, declarations, constitutions) that made history. Documents are credited to their drafters or to the body that issued them, such as the Constitutional Convention or the UN Commission on Human Rights.
- **Scope.** This is the Western tradition. Great historians from other traditions, such as Sima Qian, Ibn Khaldun (whose *Muqaddimah* of 1377 anticipates much of modern social history) and Rashid al-Din, belong to sibling traditions in this repository.
- **Shared people.** Machiavelli, Hume, Voltaire, Tocqueville, Marx, Burke, Paine, Jefferson, Josephus, Bede and others also appear in the philosophy, literature, architecture and religion sections, under the same ids.
- **Images.** Manuscripts, original documents, early editions and period paintings come from Wikimedia Commons. Portraits are paintings, engravings or sculptures, never photographs, so several modern historians have none. Twentieth-century books still in copyright are described without images.
