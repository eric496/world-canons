# Jonathan Edwards

**1703 – 1758** · American (colonial)  
Born: East Windsor, Connecticut · Died: Princeton, New Jersey  
Movements & eras: [Evangelical revivals](../movements/evangelical-revivals.md)  

> New England Congregationalist preacher and philosopher, the leading theologian of the Great Awakening.

## Biography

Edwards was the son of a minister and entered Yale at thirteen, absorbing the works of Locke and Newton. In 1729 he succeeded his grandfather Solomon Stoddard as pastor of Northampton, Massachusetts, where revivals broke out in 1734–35 and again during the Great Awakening of 1740–42, when he preached his famous sermon 'Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God'. A dispute over admission to communion led to his dismissal in 1750. He became a missionary to Mohican and Mohawk people at Stockbridge, where he wrote his major treatises, and in 1758 was made president of the College of New Jersey (Princeton), dying weeks later after a smallpox inoculation.

In works such as A Treatise Concerning Religious Affections and Freedom of the Will, Edwards defended Calvinist theology with philosophical rigour and analysed the psychology of religious experience. He is widely regarded as the most important American theologian and an original philosopher. Edwards, like many in colonial New England, owned enslaved people, a fact now part of the assessment of his legacy.

### Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God (1741)

- **Date:** 1741
- **Type:** Sermon
- **Where written / held:** Preached at Enfield, Connecticut; printed in Boston, 1741
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sinners%20in%20the%20Hands%20of%20an%20Angry%20God%20Jonathan%20Edwards)

Edwards preached this sermon on 8 July 1741 at Enfield, Connecticut, during the Great Awakening. Taking a verse from Deuteronomy, 'their foot shall slide in due time', he argues that only God's mercy keeps unconverted sinners from falling into hell at any moment, using the image of a spider held over a fire. A witness recorded that cries and groans interrupted the preaching. The sermon is often taken as typical of Puritan fire-and-brimstone preaching, though historians note that it was unusual among Edwards's sermons, many of which dwell on the beauty and love of God. It is one of the most anthologised texts of American literature.

### A Treatise Concerning Religious Affections (1746)

- **Date:** 1746
- **Type:** Theological treatise
- **Where written / held:** Boston
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Treatise%20Concerning%20Religious%20Affections%20Jonathan%20Edwards)

Faced with both enthusiasm and scepticism after the revivals, Edwards asked what signs show that religious feelings come from God. He argues that true religion consists largely in 'holy affections', yet that intense emotion, bodily effects or fluent religious talk prove nothing either way. He then sets out twelve signs of genuine grace, culminating in a life of Christian practice. Combining Calvinist theology with Lockean psychology, the Religious Affections is a classic of the psychology of religion and influenced later evangelical thinking about conversion, as well as William James.
