# Philipp Jakob Spener

**1635 – 1705** · German

> Lutheran pastor in Frankfurt whose Pia Desideria launched the Pietist movement, often called the father of Pietism.

<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Philipp Jakob Spener · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Philipp_Jakob_Spener.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Spener studied theology at Strasbourg, Basel and Geneva, where he encountered Reformed piety, and in 1666 became senior pastor in Frankfurt. There from 1670 he organised small groups, the collegia pietatis, meeting in homes to read the Bible and pray. He later served as court preacher in Dresden and as a church leader in Berlin, where he helped to found the University of Halle, which under August Hermann Francke became the centre of Pietism.

Pietism emphasised personal conversion, Bible study by lay people, a practical Christian life and charity over doctrinal controversy. Critics among orthodox Lutherans accused it of subjectivism. Through Halle, the Moravians of Count Zinzendorf and its influence on John Wesley, it shaped evangelical Christianity worldwide.

## Key texts & works

### Pia Desideria (1675)

- **Date:** 1675
- **Type:** Reform programme
- **Where written / held:** Frankfurt am Main
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pia%20Desideria%20Philipp%20Jakob%20Spener)

Pia Desideria was first published as a preface to a new edition of the sermons of Johann Arndt, author of True Christianity, and soon issued separately. Spener describes the corruption of the church, among rulers, clergy and people, and expresses hope for better times. He proposes six reforms: more extensive use of scripture, including in small groups; the exercise of the priesthood of all believers; an emphasis on practical Christian life over knowledge; charity in religious controversy; reform of theological education to cultivate piety; and preaching aimed at edification rather than display. The programme gave rise to Pietism in Germany and influenced the Moravians and Methodists.
