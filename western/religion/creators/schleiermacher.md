# Friedrich Schleiermacher

**1768 – 1834** · German  
Born: Breslau, Silesia · Died: Berlin  
Movements & eras: [19th-century theology](../movements/nineteenth-century-religion.md)  
Also in: [Philosophy](../../philosophy/eras/04-19th-century/friedrich-schleiermacher.md)  

> German Reformed theologian and philosopher who grounded religion in feeling and became the father of modern liberal Protestant theology.

## Biography

Schleiermacher was the son of a Reformed army chaplain and was educated by the Moravian Brethren, whose warm piety marked him even after he lost his belief in their doctrines. He studied at Halle and in the 1790s, as a preacher in Berlin, moved in the circle of the early Romantics around Friedrich Schlegel. He became professor at Halle and, from 1810, at the new University of Berlin, which he helped to found, while also preaching at the Trinity Church. He worked for the union of Lutheran and Reformed churches in Prussia and translated Plato into German.

In On Religion: Speeches to its Cultured Despisers (1799) he argued that religion is neither metaphysics nor morality but a 'sense and taste for the infinite'; in The Christian Faith (1821–22) he defined it as the feeling of absolute dependence and reconstructed doctrine on that basis. He also founded modern hermeneutics, the theory of interpretation. Karl Barth, his greatest critic, still regarded him as the towering figure of 19th-century theology.

### On Religion: Speeches to its Cultured Despisers (1799)

- **Date:** 1799
- **Type:** Theological essays
- **Where written / held:** Berlin
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20Religion%3A%20Speeches%20to%20its%20Cultured%20Despisers%20Friedrich%20Schleiermacher)

Written while Schleiermacher was a hospital chaplain in Berlin among the early Romantics, the five speeches address educated people who had rejected religion as superstition or as mere morality. He argues that religion is neither knowledge nor action, but intuition and feeling of the universe, a sense of the infinite in the finite. True religion is found in many forms and in living communities rather than in dogmas or institutions, and Christianity is a supreme but not exclusive expression of it. Revised in later editions, the Speeches marked a new beginning for Protestant theology, shifting attention to religious experience, and influenced the study of religion as a distinct human phenomenon.

### The Christian Faith (Der christliche Glaube) (1821–1822; revised 1830–31)

- **Date:** 1821–1822; revised 1830–31
- **Type:** Systematic theology
- **Where written / held:** Berlin
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Christian%20Faith%20%28Der%20christliche%20Glaube%29%20Friedrich%20Schleiermacher)

In The Christian Faith Schleiermacher defines religion, or piety, as the 'feeling of absolute dependence', consciousness of being in relation with God, and Christianity as the form of piety in which everything is related to the redemption accomplished by Jesus of Nazareth. Doctrines are treated as descriptions of Christian religious self-consciousness, and some traditional teachings, such as the Trinity, which he placed in a conclusion, are reinterpreted. Its method made it the founding work of liberal Protestant theology, and it was the main target of Karl Barth's critique a century later.
