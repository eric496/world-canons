# Albert Schweitzer

**1875 – 1965** · Alsatian (German, later French)  
Born: Kaysersberg, Alsace · Died: Lambaréné, Gabon  
Movements & eras: [19th-century theology](../movements/nineteenth-century-religion.md), [20th-century theology & ecumenism](../movements/twentieth-century-theology.md)  

> Alsatian theologian, organist and physician whose Quest of the Historical Jesus transformed New Testament scholarship; later a medical missionary in Africa.

## Biography

Schweitzer studied theology and philosophy at Strasbourg, became a renowned organist and Bach scholar, and taught New Testament at the university. At thirty he decided to study medicine, and in 1913 he founded a hospital at Lambaréné in what is now Gabon, where he spent most of the rest of his life. He received the Nobel Peace Prize for 1952.

His Quest of the Historical Jesus (1906) reviewed a century of attempts to write the life of Jesus, arguing that each had projected its own values onto him, and presenting Jesus as an apocalyptic prophet expecting the imminent end of the world. His ethic of 'reverence for life' was widely admired, while later critics have questioned the paternalism of his approach to Africa.

## Key texts & works

### The Quest of the Historical Jesus (1906)

- **Date:** 1906
- **Type:** Theological scholarship
- **Where written / held:** Published as Von Reimarus zu Wrede, Tübingen
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Quest%20of%20the%20Historical%20Jesus%20Albert%20Schweitzer)

Originally titled From Reimarus to Wrede, the book reviews German and other scholarship from H. S. Reimarus in the 18th century to William Wrede in 1901, showing how each liberal 'life of Jesus' had made him in the image of its author's ideals. Schweitzer argued that the historical Jesus was dominated by the expectation of the imminent kingdom of God and, when it did not come, went to his death to bring it about, a figure strange to modern people. The book effectively ended the 19th-century quest and set the agenda for later debates on eschatology, and the 'new quest' and 'third quest' of the historical Jesus took up its questions.
