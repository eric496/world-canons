# Samson Raphael Hirsch

**1808 – 1888** · German Jewish  
Born: Hamburg · Died: Frankfurt am Main  
Movements & eras: [19th-century theology](../movements/nineteenth-century-religion.md)  

> German rabbi who founded Neo-Orthodoxy, combining strict observance of Jewish law with engagement in modern European culture.

## Biography

Hirsch was born in Hamburg, studied briefly at the University of Bonn, and served as rabbi in Oldenburg, Emden and Moravia before becoming, in 1851, rabbi of a small separatist Orthodox congregation in Frankfurt, which he led for nearly forty years and built into a model community with its own schools.

Against the Reform movement, which was revising Jewish law and liturgy, Hirsch taught that the Torah is divine and binding, but that observant Jews should embrace secular education and citizenship, a principle summed up as Torah im Derech Eretz. His Nineteen Letters on Judaism (1836) and his commentary on the Pentateuch remain influential in modern Orthodox Judaism.

## Key texts & works

### Nineteen Letters on Judaism (1836)

- **Date:** 1836
- **Type:** Epistolary treatise
- **Where written / held:** Altona (Hamburg)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Nineteen%20Letters%20on%20Judaism%20Samson%20Raphael%20Hirsch)

Published under the pseudonym Ben Uziel, the Nineteen Letters respond to Benjamin, a young Jew drawn to European culture who doubts the value of the commandments. Hirsch's rabbi, Naphtali, explains Judaism as a divinely given way of life whose laws educate humanity toward justice and service of God, argues that the Jewish mission in exile is to witness to God among the nations, and calls for a renewal of Jewish education rooted in the Torah but open to general culture. Appearing as Reform Judaism was gaining ground in Germany, it became a manifesto of Neo-Orthodoxy.
