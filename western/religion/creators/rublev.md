# Andrei Rublev

**c. 1360s – c. 1428** · Russian  
Born: Muscovy · Died: Moscow  
Movements & eras: [Byzantine Orthodoxy](../movements/byzantine-orthodoxy.md)  

> Russian monk and icon painter, the most celebrated master of Russian iconography, best known for his Trinity icon.

## Biography

Almost nothing is known of Rublev's early life. He was a monk, associated with the Trinity monastery of St Sergius and later with the Andronikov monastery in Moscow. Chronicles record him working in 1405 with the Byzantine-born Theophanes the Greek on the Annunciation Cathedral in the Moscow Kremlin, and in 1408 with Daniil Chorny on the frescoes of the Dormition Cathedral in Vladimir. He died in the Andronikov monastery, probably in 1428.

Rublev's style, with its soft colours, flowing lines and calm, gentle figures, is regarded as the high point of Russian icon painting. Many works have been attributed to him, but only a few are documented, above all the Trinity. A Russian church council of 1551 held up his icons as a model. The Russian Orthodox Church canonised him in 1988, and Andrei Tarkovsky's film Andrei Rublev (1966) made him widely known.

## Key texts & works

### Trinity (c. 1411 or 1425–27)

[![Trinity](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Andrey_Rublev_-_%D0%A1%D0%B2._%D0%A2%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Andrey_Rublev_-_%D0%A1%D0%B2._%D0%A2%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Andrey_Rublev_-_%D0%A1%D0%B2._%D0%A2%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

- **Date:** c. 1411 or 1425–27
- **Type:** Icon (tempera on panel)
- **Where written / held:** State Tretyakov Gallery, Moscow
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Trinity%20Andrei%20Rublev)

The icon depicts the three angels who visited Abraham at Mamre (Genesis 18), a scene traditionally read by Christians as a revelation of the Trinity. Rublev left out Abraham and Sarah, who usually appear, and concentrated on the three figures seated around a table bearing a chalice, their heads inclined toward each other in a circle of silent communion. Behind them are a house, a tree and a mountain. The soft blues, greens and golds and the balance of the composition have made it a symbol of harmony and peace. Painted for the Trinity monastery of St Sergius, perhaps in his memory, it was transferred to the Tretyakov Gallery in 1929. The Russian Church council of 1551 held it up as a model for representing the Trinity.
