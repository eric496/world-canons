# Pope John XXIII

**1881 – 1963** · Italian  
Born: Sotto il Monte, Lombardy · Died: Vatican City  
Movements & eras: [20th-century theology & ecumenism](../movements/twentieth-century-theology.md)  

> Italian pope who called the Second Vatican Council and wrote the encyclical Pacem in terris; canonised in 2014.

## Biography

Angelo Giuseppe Roncalli was the son of sharecroppers near Bergamo. Ordained in 1904, he was secretary to the bishop of Bergamo, an army chaplain in the First World War, and from 1925 a Vatican diplomat in Bulgaria, Turkey and Greece, where during the Second World War he helped Jewish refugees, and then nuncio to France. He became patriarch of Venice in 1953 and was elected pope in 1958, aged seventy-six, widely expected to be a caretaker.

Within three months he announced an ecumenical council to bring 'aggiornamento', updating, to the church. The Second Vatican Council opened in 1962; he died of cancer in June 1963 before its second session. His encyclicals Mater et magistra and Pacem in terris addressed social justice, human rights and peace. He removed language offensive to Jews from the Good Friday liturgy. Known as 'Good Pope John', he was canonised in 2014.

## Key texts & works

### Pacem in terris (1963)

- **Date:** 1963
- **Type:** Papal encyclical
- **Where written / held:** Vatican City, 11 April 1963
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pacem%20in%20terris%20Pope%20John%20XXIII)

Issued in April 1963, two months before his death and months after the Cuban missile crisis, Pacem in terris sets out a vision of peace founded on truth, justice, charity and freedom. It lists universal human rights, including freedom of religion and conscience, and corresponding duties, discusses relations between individuals and states and among nations, calls for disarmament and the banning of nuclear weapons, and supports the United Nations. It was the first encyclical addressed not only to Catholics but to all people of good will, and it was widely welcomed, influencing the Second Vatican Council and later Catholic social teaching.
