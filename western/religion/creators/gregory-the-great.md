## Biography

Gregory came from a senatorial family in Rome and served as prefect of the city before giving away his wealth and turning his family home into a monastery. He spent several years in Constantinople as papal envoy and was elected pope in 590, amid plague, Lombard invasion and the weakness of imperial government in Italy. He managed the church's estates to feed Rome, negotiated with the Lombards, and in 596 sent the monk Augustine with a mission to the Anglo-Saxons of Kent.

Gregory's Pastoral Rule, a guide for bishops, his Moralia on the Book of Job, his homilies and his Dialogues on the saints of Italy were among the most read books of the Middle Ages. He is counted as one of the four Latin doctors of the church. Medieval tradition associated him with the chant of the Roman liturgy, hence the name Gregorian chant, though musicologists consider the repertory to have taken shape later, largely in the Carolingian era.

### Pastoral Rule (Regula pastoralis) (c. 590)

- **Date:** c. 590
- **Type:** Pastoral treatise
- **Where written / held:** Rome
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pastoral%20Rule%20%28Regula%20pastoralis%29%20Pope%20Gregory%20I%20%28Gregory%20the%20Great%29)

Written at the start of his pontificate, the Pastoral Rule explains who should accept the office of shepherd of souls, how a pastor should live, and above all how to adapt teaching to different listeners: the poor and the rich, the joyful and the sad, the proud and the timid, the married and the celibate, and so on through some forty contrasting pairs. It closes by warning the pastor against pride in his own success. The book was sent to bishops throughout the West; Alfred the Great translated it into Old English in the 890s, with a preface lamenting the decline of learning, and Byzantine emperors had it translated into Greek. It shaped the ideal of the bishop throughout the Middle Ages.

### Dialogues (c. 593–594)

- **Date:** c. 593–594
- **Type:** Hagiography
- **Where written / held:** Rome
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dialogues%20Pope%20Gregory%20I%20%28Gregory%20the%20Great%29)

Framed as conversations between Gregory and his deacon Peter, the Dialogues collect miracle stories of holy men of 6th-century Italy, to show that sanctity had not disappeared from Italy in an age of war and plague. The second book is devoted entirely to Benedict and is the main source for his life. The fourth book discusses the fate of souls after death, with visions that contributed to the development of the Western doctrine of purgatory. A minority of scholars has argued that the Dialogues were not written by Gregory, but most accept them as his. They were immensely popular, translated into Greek by Pope Zacharias and into Old English at Alfred's court.
