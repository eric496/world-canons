# John Chrysostom

**c. 347 – 407** · Syrian Greek  
Born: Antioch, Syria · Died: Comana, Pontus

> Preacher of Antioch and archbishop of Constantinople, the 'golden-mouthed', whose sermons and liturgy made him the most revered preacher of the Greek church.

<sub>Portrait: St John Chrysostom, Byzantine depiction · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Johnchrysostom.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

John was born in Antioch, studied rhetoric under the pagan orator Libanius, and lived for some years as a hermit before ill health brought him back to the city. As a priest in Antioch from 386 he became famous for his preaching, delivering long series of sermons on books of the Bible. In 398 he was made archbishop of Constantinople, where his attacks on luxury and his reforms of the clergy offended the empress Eudoxia and Archbishop Theophilus of Alexandria. Deposed and exiled, he died on a forced march in 407. The name Chrysostom, 'golden mouth', was given to him after his death.

His hundreds of surviving homilies are models of practical, literal biblical interpretation and moral exhortation, especially on wealth and care for the poor. A series of sermons he preached against Christians attending synagogue in Antioch contain harsh anti-Jewish language that historians regard as an important and troubling chapter in the history of Christian anti-Judaism. The Divine Liturgy most often celebrated in the Orthodox churches bears his name, though it developed over time.

### On the Priesthood (c. 386–391)

- **Date:** c. 386–391
- **Type:** Dialogue
- **Where written / held:** Antioch
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20the%20Priesthood%20John%20Chrysostom)

In six books, Chrysostom imagines a conversation with his friend Basil (not Basil of Caesarea), whom he had tricked into accepting ordination while himself avoiding it. Justifying his evasion, he describes the awesome responsibility of the priest, who offers the eucharist and must guide souls, and the qualities a priest needs: wisdom, patience, eloquence, and resistance to vanity and to the pressures of powerful laypeople. It is a classic of pastoral literature, much read in the Middle Ages and the Renaissance, and a source for the high view of priesthood in both Eastern and Western churches.

### Divine Liturgy of St John Chrysostom (c. 4th–10th century (traditionally attributed))

- **Date:** c. 4th–10th century (traditionally attributed)
- **Type:** Liturgy
- **Where written / held:** Constantinople; celebrated in Orthodox and Eastern Catholic churches
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Divine%20Liturgy%20of%20St%20John%20Chrysostom%20John%20Chrysostom)

The Divine Liturgy of St John Chrysostom is the standard eucharistic service of the Byzantine rite, used on most Sundays and weekdays in Orthodox and Eastern Catholic churches; the longer Liturgy of St Basil is used on certain feasts. Its central prayer, the anaphora, may go back to Chrysostom's time in Antioch or Constantinople, and scholars think he may have adapted an earlier anaphora; much of the rest of the service grew around it over the following centuries in the imperial capital. It includes litanies, the Little and Great Entrances, the Cherubic Hymn, the Creed, the consecration and communion. It has been translated into many languages and set to music by composers such as Tchaikovsky and Rachmaninoff.
