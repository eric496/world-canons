# William James

**1842 – 1910** · American  
Born: New York City · Died: Chocorua, New Hampshire  
Movements & eras: [19th-century theology](../movements/nineteenth-century-religion.md)  
Also in: [Philosophy](../../philosophy/eras/04-19th-century/william-james.md)  

> American psychologist and pragmatist philosopher whose Varieties of Religious Experience founded the modern psychology of religion.

## Biography

William James, elder brother of the novelist Henry James, grew up in a cosmopolitan family shaped by his father's Swedenborgian religious interests. He trained in medicine at Harvard, where he taught for most of his career, first physiology, then psychology and philosophy. His Principles of Psychology (1890) was a founding text of the discipline, and with C. S. Peirce he developed pragmatism.

His Gifford Lectures, published as The Varieties of Religious Experience (1902), studied conversion, saintliness and mysticism through first-hand accounts, judging religion by its fruits in human life rather than by doctrine. The book remains a classic of religious studies.

## Key texts & works

### The Varieties of Religious Experience (1902)

- **Date:** 1902
- **Type:** Psychology of religion
- **Where written / held:** Gifford Lectures, University of Edinburgh, 1901–02; published New York and London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Varieties%20of%20Religious%20Experience%20William%20James)

Delivered as Gifford Lectures in Edinburgh, the Varieties defines religion, for his purposes, as the feelings, acts and experiences of individuals in relation to whatever they consider the divine. Drawing on autobiographies of saints, mystics, converts and ordinary people, James contrasts the 'healthy-minded' with the 'sick soul' and the 'twice-born', and analyses conversion, saintliness and mysticism, proposing four marks of mystical experience: ineffability, a noetic quality, transiency and passivity. Judging religion pragmatically by its fruits, he concludes that it is open to the reality of a 'more' beyond conscious life. The book has remained continuously in print.
