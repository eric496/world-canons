# Hesiod

**fl. c. 700 BC** · Greek  
Born: Ascra, Boeotia · Died:   
Movements & eras: [Greco-Roman religion](../movements/greco-roman-religion.md)  

> Greek poet whose Theogony gave the classic account of the birth of the gods, and whose Works and Days joined myth to farming, justice and piety.

## Biography

Hesiod is one of the two earliest Greek poets whose works survive, usually dated around 700 BC, a little after or roughly contemporary with the Homeric epics. Almost everything known about him comes from his own poems: he describes himself as a farmer and shepherd in Ascra, a village below Mount Helicon in Boeotia, where the Muses appeared to him and gave him the gift of song. He mentions a dispute with his brother Perses over their inheritance and a victory in a poetry contest at Chalcis. Ancient scholars debated which poems were really his, and modern scholars generally accept only the Theogony and Works and Days as substantially his own.

Hesiod's importance for the history of religion is that he systematised Greek beliefs about the gods into a genealogy, from Chaos and Earth through the Titans to the rule of Zeus. Herodotus later said that Homer and Hesiod had given the Greeks their gods, their names and their shapes. His stories of Prometheus, Pandora and the ages of humankind became permanent parts of Western culture, and his insistence that Zeus upholds justice shaped later Greek moral and religious thought.

### Theogony (c. 700 BC)

- **Date:** c. 700 BC
- **Type:** Epic poem
- **Where written / held:** Boeotia, Greece; survives in medieval Byzantine manuscripts
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Theogony%20Hesiod)

The Theogony, about a thousand lines of hexameter verse, opens with Hesiod's encounter with the Muses on Mount Helicon and then tells how the world came to be: first Chaos, then Earth (Gaia), Tartarus and Eros, followed by the generations of gods born from Earth and Sky (Ouranos). It narrates the castration of Ouranos by his son Kronos, Kronos's swallowing of his children, and the victory of Zeus and the Olympians over the Titans, along with the story of Prometheus and the first woman. Scholars have noted close parallels with Near Eastern succession myths, such as the Hittite Kumarbi cycle and the Babylonian Enuma Elish, suggesting cultural contact. The poem became the standard Greek genealogy of the gods, used by later poets, mythographers and philosophers, and remains the starting point for the study of Greek mythology.

### Works and Days (c. 700 BC)

- **Date:** c. 700 BC
- **Type:** Didactic poem
- **Where written / held:** Boeotia, Greece
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Works%20and%20Days%20Hesiod)

Works and Days is addressed to Hesiod's brother Perses, who had apparently cheated him over their inheritance with the help of corrupt judges. Hesiod urges him to honest labour and justice, which Zeus protects. Along the way he tells the myth of Pandora, who released evils into the world, and the myth of the five ages of humankind, from the golden race to the present iron age. The second half gives practical instruction on farming and sailing through the seasons, ending with a list of days of the month favourable or unfavourable for various tasks. The poem is a key witness to the everyday piety of archaic Greece, in which the gods are bound up with work, weather and fairness, and it was the model for later didactic poetry such as Virgil's Georgics.
