# Flavius Josephus

**AD 37 – c. 100** · Judean  
Born: Jerusalem · Died: Rome  
Movements & eras: [Second Temple Judaism](../movements/second-temple-judaism.md)  
Also in: [History](../../history/creators/josephus.md)  

> Jewish priest, general and historian whose histories of the Jewish revolt and of the Jewish people are central sources for Judaism and the world of the New Testament.

## Biography

Joseph son of Matthias was born in Jerusalem into a priestly family. In the Jewish revolt against Rome that began in AD 66 he commanded rebel forces in Galilee, but surrendered after the siege of Jotapata in 67. By his own account he predicted that the Roman general Vespasian would become emperor; when this came true he was freed, took the family name Flavius, and accompanied Titus at the siege and destruction of Jerusalem in 70. He spent the rest of his life in Rome under imperial patronage, writing in Greek.

His Jewish War and Jewish Antiquities, together with his autobiography and the apologetic Against Apion, are the main sources for Jewish history in the late Second Temple period, describing Pharisees, Sadducees and Essenes, Herod the Great and the fall of Masada. The Antiquities contains brief references to John the Baptist, to James 'the brother of Jesus', and to Jesus himself; the passage on Jesus, the Testimonium Flavianum, is widely believed to have been expanded by later Christian copyists, though many scholars think an authentic core lies beneath it. Jews long regarded him as a turncoat, while Christians preserved and treasured his works.

### The Jewish War (c. AD 75)

- **Date:** c. AD 75
- **Type:** History
- **Where written / held:** Rome; preserved in Greek manuscripts and an early Latin translation
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Jewish%20War%20Flavius%20Josephus)

Josephus wrote the Jewish War in Rome in the 70s AD, first, he says, in Aramaic for Jews of the east and then in Greek. After surveying Jewish history from the Maccabees through Herod the Great, it narrates the revolt from its outbreak in 66, Josephus's own command in Galilee, the factional strife in Jerusalem, the siege and burning of the Temple by Titus in 70, and the fall of Masada, where he reports the defenders' mass suicide. Its description of the Temple, the Jewish sects and the Roman army are invaluable. Josephus writes as a client of the Flavian emperors, blaming the disaster on rebel fanatics rather than on the Jewish people or on Rome, and historians weigh his account with that in mind. Christians read the fall of Jerusalem as fulfilling the prophecies of Jesus, and the book was widely copied and translated in the Middle Ages.

### Jewish Antiquities (c. AD 93–94)

- **Date:** c. AD 93–94
- **Type:** History
- **Where written / held:** Rome
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jewish%20Antiquities%20Flavius%20Josephus)

In twenty books, the Antiquities retells the biblical story, adding traditions found elsewhere in Jewish sources, and continues the history through the Persian, Hellenistic and Roman periods, with extensive detail on the Hasmoneans, Herod and the Roman governors of Judea. Josephus's aim was to present Judaism as an ancient and honourable nation to a gentile audience. Book 18 contains a brief account of Jesus, the Testimonium Flavianum; in its transmitted form it calls Jesus the Messiah, which most scholars consider a Christian interpolation, though many think Josephus wrote a shorter, neutral notice. A passage on the death of 'James, the brother of Jesus who was called Christ' in Book 20 is generally accepted as authentic. The work is a major source for the historical context of the New Testament.
