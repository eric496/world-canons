## Biography

Giovanni di Pietro di Bernardone, called Francesco, was the son of a prosperous merchant of Assisi. After a carefree youth and a spell as a prisoner of war, he underwent a conversion in his early twenties, renouncing his inheritance in a famous public scene before the bishop of Assisi. He lived as a penitent, rebuilt ruined churches and cared for lepers, and attracted followers whose simple way of life Pope Innocent III approved around 1209. Clare of Assisi founded a parallel order of women. Francis travelled to Egypt during the Fifth Crusade in 1219 and met Sultan al-Kamil. According to his early biographers, in 1224 he received the stigmata, the wounds of Christ. He died at the Porziuncola below Assisi in 1226 and was canonised two years later.

Francis's ideal of following the poor Christ literally, owning nothing, preaching penance and peace, and seeing all creatures as brothers and sisters, inspired a rapid spread of the friars and a lasting tension within the order over how strictly to observe poverty. His Canticle of the Sun is among the first works of Italian literature. The cycle of his life in the basilica at Assisi and countless later images made him a central figure of European art, and in 1979 he was named patron saint of ecology.

### Rule of St Francis (Regula bullata) (1223)

- **Date:** 1223
- **Type:** Religious rule
- **Where written / held:** Approved by Pope Honorius III; papal bull preserved in the Sacro Convento, Assisi
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Rule%20of%20St%20Francis%20%28Regula%20bullata%29%20Francis%20of%20Assisi)

Francis's first, very simple rule of about 1209 consisted mainly of Gospel passages; a longer 'Rule without a bull' followed in 1221. The definitive rule, approved by Honorius III's bull Solet annuere in November 1223, is shorter and more juridical, prescribing that the friars 'observe the holy Gospel of our Lord Jesus Christ, living in obedience, without anything of their own, and in chastity'. Friars were to own nothing, not to receive money, to work or beg, and to preach briefly. How strictly to keep this poverty became a bitter issue after Francis's death, splitting the order into Conventuals and Spirituals and later Observants and Capuchins, and drawing in the papacy. The rule is still professed by Franciscan friars.

### Canticle of the Sun (1224–1226)

- **Date:** 1224–1226
- **Type:** Hymn (Umbrian Italian)
- **Where written / held:** San Damiano, Assisi; earliest text in Assisi, Biblioteca del Sacro Convento, Cod. 338
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Canticle%20of%20the%20Sun%20Francis%20of%20Assisi)

According to early sources, Francis composed the Canticle of the Creatures, also called the Canticle of Brother Sun, in 1225 while ill and nearly blind at San Damiano. In Umbrian Italian rather than Latin, it praises the Lord 'through' (or 'for', the Italian per being ambiguous) Brother Sun, Sister Moon and the stars, Brothers Wind and Fire, Sister Water and Mother Earth. Francis later added a stanza on pardon and peace, reportedly to reconcile the bishop and mayor of Assisi, and a final one welcoming Sister Bodily Death. It is among the earliest literary works in Italian. Its vision of creation as a family has made it one of the best-loved Christian texts, and Pope Francis took its opening words, Laudato si', as the title of his 2015 encyclical on the environment.
