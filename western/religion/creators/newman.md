# John Henry Newman

**1801 – 1890** · English  
Born: London · Died: Edgbaston, Birmingham  
Movements & eras: [19th-century theology](../movements/nineteenth-century-religion.md)  

> Anglican leader of the Oxford Movement who became a Roman Catholic and a cardinal, author of the Apologia Pro Vita Sua and The Idea of a University.

## Biography

Newman was a fellow of Oriel College, Oxford, and vicar of the university church of St Mary's, where his sermons drew large audiences. From 1833 he was a leader of the Oxford Movement, which sought to recover the Catholic heritage of the Church of England, and wrote many of its Tracts for the Times. His study of the early church led him to doubt the Anglican position, and in 1845 he was received into the Roman Catholic Church. He founded the Birmingham Oratory, was the first rector of the Catholic University of Ireland, and was made a cardinal in 1879.

His Essay on the Development of Christian Doctrine argued that doctrine grows organically over time, and his Grammar of Assent explored how people come to believe. His Apologia, written in reply to an attack by Charles Kingsley, is a classic of autobiography. He also wrote the poem The Dream of Gerontius and hymns such as 'Lead, Kindly Light'. He was canonised in 2019 and declared a doctor of the church in 2025.

### An Essay on the Development of Christian Doctrine (1845)

- **Date:** 1845
- **Type:** Theological treatise
- **Where written / held:** London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=An%20Essay%20on%20the%20Development%20of%20Christian%20Doctrine%20John%20Henry%20Newman)

Newman wrote the Essay while trying to decide whether the Roman Catholic Church was the true heir of the early church, despite doctrines not explicitly found in scripture or the early Fathers. He argued that living ideas unfold over time, as an acorn becomes an oak, and proposed seven 'notes' for distinguishing true developments from corruptions, such as preservation of type, continuity of principles and chronic vigour. He finished it before his reception into the Catholic Church in October 1845. Controversial at first among both Protestants and Catholics, the idea of doctrinal development became widely influential, and is reflected in the Second Vatican Council.

### Apologia Pro Vita Sua (1864)

- **Date:** 1864
- **Type:** Spiritual autobiography
- **Where written / held:** Published in weekly parts, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Apologia%20Pro%20Vita%20Sua%20John%20Henry%20Newman)

In January 1864 the novelist Charles Kingsley wrote that truth for its own sake had never been a virtue of the Roman clergy, citing Newman. Newman responded first with an exchange of letters and then, in seven weekly pamphlets written at great speed, with a history of his religious opinions: his evangelical conversion as a boy, his Oxford years, the Oxford Movement and the Tracts for the Times, his growing doubts about the Anglican claim, and his reception into the Catholic Church in 1845. The candour and beauty of the book won wide admiration in Victorian England and restored his reputation. It is a classic of English autobiography and of religious literature.
