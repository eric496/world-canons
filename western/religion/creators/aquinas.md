## Biography

Thomas was born into a noble family at Roccasecca between Rome and Naples and educated at the abbey of Monte Cassino and the university of Naples. Against fierce family opposition, which included confining him for a year, he joined the new Dominican order of preachers. He studied under Albert the Great in Paris and Cologne and taught in Paris, Naples and at Dominican houses in Italy. In December 1273 he stopped writing after an experience during Mass, reportedly saying that everything he had written seemed like straw. He died on his way to the Council of Lyon in 1274 and was canonised in 1323.

In the Summa contra Gentiles and the unfinished Summa Theologiae, Aquinas argued that faith and reason cannot contradict each other, offered his 'five ways' of arguing for God's existence, and set out a theology of nature, grace, natural law, virtue and the sacraments. Some of his propositions were condemned in Paris in 1277, but he was later held up as the church's common doctor. In 1879 Pope Leo XIII made his thought the basis of Catholic theological education, and Thomism remains a living philosophical tradition. He also wrote the hymns for the feast of Corpus Christi.

### Summa contra Gentiles (c. 1259–1265)

- **Date:** c. 1259–1265
- **Type:** Philosophical theology
- **Where written / held:** Paris and Orvieto; autograph manuscript in the Vatican Library
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Summa%20contra%20Gentiles%20Thomas%20Aquinas)

The Summa contra Gentiles ('against the gentiles') treats God, creation, providence and human happiness by philosophical argument in its first three books, and then truths known only by revelation, such as the Trinity and incarnation, in the fourth. A 14th-century tradition says it was written at the request of the Dominican Raymond of Peñafort as a manual for missionaries among Muslims and Jews in Spain; modern scholars debate this and see it more as an exposition of the wisdom of Christian faith in dialogue with Aristotle and his Arabic and Jewish commentators. Its third book's account of human happiness as the vision of God is a classic. Much of the autograph, in Aquinas's notoriously difficult handwriting, survives.

### Summa Theologiae (1265–1274 (unfinished))

[![Summa Theologiae](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/78/SummaTheologiae.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SummaTheologiae.jpg)

<sub>An early copy of the Summa Theologiae Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SummaTheologiae.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1265–1274 (unfinished)
- **Type:** Systematic theology
- **Where written / held:** Rome, Paris and Naples
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Summa%20Theologiae%20Thomas%20Aquinas)

Aquinas intended the Summa as a guide for beginners in theology. Its three parts treat God and creation; the human being's movement toward God through action, law, virtue and grace; and Christ and the sacraments, as the way to God. It is divided into some 500 questions and about 2,600 articles, each following a fixed method: a question, objections, an authority 'on the contrary', Aquinas's own answer and replies to each objection. It includes the 'five ways' of arguing for God's existence and an influential treatment of natural law. Aquinas stopped writing in December 1273, leaving the third part unfinished; his assistants completed it from his earlier writings. After the Council of Trent it replaced Peter Lombard's Sentences as the main textbook of Catholic theology, and it remains central to Catholic thought and widely studied by philosophers.
