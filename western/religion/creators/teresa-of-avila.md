## Biography

Teresa Sánchez de Cepeda y Ahumada was born in Ávila; her paternal grandfather was a converso, a Jewish convert to Christianity, a fact later concealed. She entered the Carmelite convent of the Incarnation in Ávila in 1535. After years of illness and spiritual struggle she experienced a deepening of prayer in her late thirties, with visions and raptures, including the piercing of her heart by an angel. In 1562, despite strong opposition, she founded the small convent of St Joseph in Ávila under a stricter 'discalced' (barefoot) rule, and went on to found sixteen more convents across Spain, working with John of the Cross to extend the reform to friars.

Teresa's Life, The Way of Perfection and The Interior Castle describe the stages of prayer with psychological insight, humour and practical sense, and are classics of Spanish prose. Her writings were examined by the Inquisition but approved. She was canonised in 1622 and in 1970 became, with Catherine of Siena, the first woman declared a doctor of the church. Bernini's sculpture The Ecstasy of St Teresa made her vision famous.

### The Book of Her Life (Libro de la vida) (1562–1565)

- **Date:** 1562–1565
- **Type:** Spiritual autobiography
- **Where written / held:** Ávila; autograph in the library of El Escorial
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Book%20of%20Her%20Life%20%28Libro%20de%20la%20vida%29%20Teresa%20of%20%C3%81vila)

Teresa wrote her Life at the request of her confessors, who wanted to test the authenticity of her mystical experiences. It recounts her childhood, her entry into the Carmelites, years of illness and lukewarm prayer, and her conversion before an image of the wounded Christ. In a famous middle section she compares the stages of prayer to four ways of watering a garden, from laboriously drawing water from a well to rain falling from heaven. It also describes her vision of an angel piercing her heart with a golden spear, which Bernini sculpted in the Cornaro Chapel in Rome. The Inquisition held the manuscript for years but did not condemn it, and it was printed in 1588 in an edition by Fray Luis de León.

### Interior Castle (El castillo interior) (1577)

- **Date:** 1577
- **Type:** Mystical treatise
- **Where written / held:** Toledo and Ávila; autograph in the convent of the Discalced Carmelites, Seville
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Interior%20Castle%20%28El%20castillo%20interior%29%20Teresa%20of%20%C3%81vila)

Written in 1577 at the request of her superiors, The Interior Castle (also called The Mansions) uses the image of a castle made of a single diamond or crystal, with many rooms grouped into seven dwelling places. The soul enters through prayer and moves inward through stages of self-knowledge, struggle, the 'prayer of quiet', and the experiences of mystical union that Teresa compares to a silkworm becoming a butterfly, culminating in 'spiritual marriage' with God in the innermost room. She combines lofty experience with practical advice and humour addressed to her nuns. It is considered her masterpiece and a classic of Christian mysticism and of Spanish prose.
