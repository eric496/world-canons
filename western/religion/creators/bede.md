# Bede

**c. 673 – 735** · Anglo-Saxon  
Born: Northumbria · Died: Jarrow, Northumbria  
Movements & eras: [Monasticism](../movements/monasticism.md)  
Also in: [History](../../history/creators/bede.md)  

> Northumbrian monk and scholar, known as the Venerable Bede, whose Ecclesiastical History of the English People is the foundation of English history.

## Biography

Bede was given at the age of seven to the twin monastery of Wearmouth and Jarrow in Northumbria, then one of the great centres of learning in Europe, with a library brought from Rome by its founder Benedict Biscop. He spent his whole life there as a monk and priest, rarely travelling, devoted to study, teaching and writing. He died at Jarrow in 735, reportedly while finishing an English translation of the Gospel of John.

Bede wrote biblical commentaries, works on grammar, natural science and chronology, saints' lives and his Ecclesiastical History. His work on calculating the date of Easter popularised the system of dating years from the birth of Christ (AD), devised in the 6th century by Dionysius Exiguus. Called 'Venerable' from the 9th century, he is the only Englishman named a doctor of the church.

## Key texts & works

### Ecclesiastical History of the English People (731)

- **Date:** 731
- **Type:** History
- **Where written / held:** Jarrow, Northumbria; early manuscripts include the St Petersburg Bede and the Moore Bede
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ecclesiastical%20History%20of%20the%20English%20People%20Bede)

Completed in 731, Bede's Historia ecclesiastica gentis Anglorum tells, in five books, the story of Britain from the Roman invasion to his own day, centred on the conversion of the Anglo-Saxon kingdoms through Pope Gregory's mission to Kent in 597 and the Irish mission from Iona to Northumbria. It records the Synod of Whitby of 664, the lives of saints such as Aidan and Cuthbert, the cowherd poet Cædmon and many miracles. Bede named his sources and used documents obtained from Canterbury and Rome, and he dated events by the year of the Lord, popularising AD dating. Historians value it highly while noting its Northumbrian and Roman sympathies. It survives in over 150 manuscripts and was translated into Old English in the reign of Alfred.
