# John of Damascus

**c. 675 – 749** · Syrian (Arab Christian)  
Born: Damascus · Died: Mar Saba, near Jerusalem  
Movements & eras: [Byzantine Orthodoxy](../movements/byzantine-orthodoxy.md)  

> Monk and theologian under Umayyad rule who wrote the classic defence of icons and a summary of Orthodox doctrine that shaped both Eastern and Western theology.

## Biography

John was born in Damascus, the Umayyad capital, into a prominent Christian family, the Mansur, who served the caliphs as financial administrators; he himself appears to have held such a post. Early in the 8th century he left public life to become a monk at the monastery of Mar Saba in the Judean desert, where he spent the rest of his life writing, preaching and composing hymns. He died there around 749.

When the Byzantine emperor Leo III began to attack the veneration of images in the 720s, John, living safely outside the empire, wrote three treatises in defence of icons, arguing that because God had become visible in Christ he could be depicted, and that honour paid to an image passes to its prototype. His Fount of Knowledge, whose third part is the Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith, summed up Greek patristic doctrine and was translated into Latin in the 12th century. He is also counted among the great hymnographers of the Orthodox Church.

- [On the Divine Images](#on-the-divine-images-c-726750) (c. 726–750)

### On the Divine Images (c. 726–750)

- **Date:** c. 726–750
- **Type:** Theological treatises
- **Where written / held:** Mar Saba monastery, near Jerusalem
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20the%20Divine%20Images%20John%20of%20Damascus)

When Emperor Leo III moved against religious images, John, writing under Muslim rule beyond the emperor's reach, composed three apologies for icons. He argues that while the invisible God cannot be depicted, God became visible in Christ, so that to refuse images of Christ is to deny the reality of the incarnation. He distinguishes worship (latreia), due only to God, from veneration (proskynesis) given to icons, and notes that honour paid to an image passes to its prototype. He gathers quotations from scripture and the Fathers in support. His arguments shaped the decrees of the Second Council of Nicaea in 787, which restored icon veneration, and remain the foundation of Orthodox iconography.

### An Exact Exposition of the Orthodox Faith (c. 743)

- **Date:** c. 743
- **Type:** Systematic theology
- **Where written / held:** Mar Saba monastery, near Jerusalem
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=An%20Exact%20Exposition%20of%20the%20Orthodox%20Faith%20John%20of%20Damascus)

The Exposition forms the third part of John's Fount of Knowledge, after a section on philosophy and one on heresies, including a chapter on Islam that is among the earliest Christian discussions of it. In a hundred chapters, it gathers the teachings of the Greek Fathers, above all the Cappadocians and Maximus the Confessor, on God, the Trinity, creation, angels, humanity, Christ, the sacraments, icons and the last things. Translated into Latin by Burgundio of Pisa around 1150, it was used by Peter Lombard and Thomas Aquinas, making John an important link between Eastern and Western theology.
