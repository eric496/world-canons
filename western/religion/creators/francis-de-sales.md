## Biography

François de Sales was born into a noble Savoyard family and studied in Paris with the Jesuits and law at Padua before being ordained in 1593 against his father's wishes. He worked as a missionary in the Chablais region, which had become Calvinist, and in 1602 became Catholic bishop of Geneva, residing at Annecy because Geneva was Protestant. With Jane Frances de Chantal he founded the Order of the Visitation in 1610.

His Introduction to the Devout Life (1609) and Treatise on the Love of God (1616) taught that holiness is possible in every walk of life, not only in the cloister, in a gentle and accessible style. He was canonised in 1665, declared a doctor of the church in 1877 and named patron of writers and journalists.

## Key texts & works

### Introduction to the Devout Life (1609)

- **Date:** 1609
- **Type:** Devotional guide
- **Where written / held:** First printed in Lyon
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Introduction%20to%20the%20Devout%20Life%20Francis%20de%20Sales)

The Introduction grew from letters of spiritual direction Francis de Sales wrote to Madame de Charmoisy, a relative by marriage. Addressed to 'Philothea', it argues that devotion is possible and necessary for people of every condition, the soldier, the craftsman, the courtier and the married woman, not only for monks and nuns. In five parts it guides the reader from a resolution to amend her life, through prayer and the sacraments, the practice of virtues, and resisting temptations, to an annual renewal of resolutions. Its gentle, gracious style made it popular across Europe; it went through many editions in his lifetime and has been translated into many languages.
