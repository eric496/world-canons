## Biography

What is known of Benedict comes almost entirely from the second book of Gregory the Great's Dialogues, written some fifty years after his death and full of miracle stories. According to Gregory, Benedict was born at Nursia to a good family, studied in Rome, and fled its corruption to live as a hermit in a cave at Subiaco. Disciples gathered around him and he organised them into small monasteries. Around 529 he founded the monastery of Monte Cassino, between Rome and Naples, where he wrote his Rule and where he died. His sister Scholastica is also venerated as a saint.

Benedict's Rule is notable for its moderation, balance between prayer, work and reading, and its picture of the abbot as a father. Over the following centuries, with the support of popes and Carolingian rulers, it became the standard rule for monasteries across Latin Europe, and Benedictine houses became centres of learning, agriculture and book copying. In 1964 Pope Paul VI named Benedict a patron saint of Europe.

## Key texts & works

### Rule of Saint Benedict (c. 530–540)

[![Rule of Saint Benedict](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c9/MS._Hatton_48_fol._6v-7r.jpg/1280px-MS._Hatton_48_fol._6v-7r.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MS._Hatton_48_fol._6v-7r.jpg)

<sub>The oldest surviving copy of the Rule, c. 700, Bodleian Library, Oxford (MS Hatton 48) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:MS._Hatton_48_fol._6v-7r.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 530–540
- **Type:** Monastic rule
- **Where written / held:** Monte Cassino; oldest surviving copy (c. 700) in the Bodleian Library, Oxford (MS Hatton 48)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Rule%20of%20Saint%20Benedict%20Benedict%20of%20Nursia)

The Rule, in a prologue and seventy-three short chapters, sets out how a community of monks should live under an abbot. It orders the day around the Divine Office, the eight services of communal prayer in which the Psalms are sung each week, alternating with manual labour and lectio divina, the slow prayerful reading of scripture. It covers obedience, silence and humility, which it describes as a ladder of twelve steps, as well as food, sleep, clothing, the reception of guests 'as Christ', discipline and the election of the abbot. Benedict drew on earlier rules, especially the anonymous Rule of the Master, but his is notably moderate, calling itself 'a little rule for beginners'. Promoted by Charlemagne and Louis the Pious, it became the norm for Western monasteries, and is still followed by Benedictines, Cistercians and Trappists. The oldest surviving copy, written in England around 700, is in the Bodleian Library in Oxford, while a 9th-century manuscript at St Gallen preserves the text most faithfully.
