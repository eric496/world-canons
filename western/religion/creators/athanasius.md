# Athanasius of Alexandria

**c. 296–298 – 373** · Egyptian Greek  
Born: Alexandria, Egypt · Died: Alexandria  
Movements & eras: [Church Fathers & the Councils](../movements/church-fathers.md), [Monasticism](../movements/monasticism.md)  

> Bishop of Alexandria and champion of the Nicene Creed against Arianism, author of On the Incarnation and the influential Life of Antony.

## Biography

Athanasius attended the Council of Nicaea in 325 as a young deacon and secretary to Bishop Alexander, whom he succeeded as bishop of Alexandria in 328. For the next forty-five years he fought for the council's teaching that the Son is 'of one substance' with the Father against the followers of Arius, who held that the Son was a created being. Shifting imperial politics sent him into exile five times, once hiding among the monks of the Egyptian desert. He died in office in 373.

Athanasius's treatise On the Incarnation argues that God became human so that humans might share in the divine life. His Life of Antony, about the Egyptian hermit, became a model for Christian biography and spread the monastic ideal across the Roman world; Augustine records its effect on his conversion. His Easter letter of 367 is the earliest known list of the twenty-seven books of the New Testament exactly as they now stand. He is venerated as a saint and doctor of the church in East and West.

### On the Incarnation (De Incarnatione) (c. 318–337)

- **Date:** c. 318–337
- **Type:** Theological treatise
- **Where written / held:** Alexandria
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20the%20Incarnation%20%28De%20Incarnatione%29%20Athanasius%20of%20Alexandria)

On the Incarnation forms the second part of a two-part work, following Against the Gentiles. Athanasius argues that humanity, created in God's image through the Word, was falling back into corruption and nothingness through sin, and that only the Word himself, by becoming human, dying and rising, could renew that image and overcome death. He also argues from the spread of Christianity and the courage of martyrs that Christ is alive. Its date is debated, some placing it before the Arian controversy around 318 and others in the 330s. The phrase often summarised from it, that God became human so that humans might become divine, expresses the Eastern doctrine of deification. C. S. Lewis's introduction to a 1944 English translation helped make it widely read today.

### Life of Antony (c. 360)

- **Date:** c. 360
- **Type:** Hagiography
- **Where written / held:** Alexandria
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Life%20of%20Antony%20Athanasius%20of%20Alexandria)

Written shortly after Antony's death in 356, the Life tells how the young Egyptian, hearing the Gospel command to sell all and give to the poor, gave away his property, lived as an ascetic near his village and then withdrew into the desert, first to an abandoned fort and later to the 'inner mountain' near the Red Sea. It describes his battles with demons, his counsel to visitors, his miracles and his support for Nicene orthodoxy. Translated into Latin within a few years, it spread the monastic ideal throughout the West. In his Confessions Augustine describes how hearing the story of Antony moved him on the eve of his conversion. Its scenes of temptation inspired painters from Bosch and Grünewald to Ernst and Dalí, and Flaubert's novel The Temptation of Saint Anthony.
