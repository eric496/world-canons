# Ignatius of Loyola

**1491 – 1556** · Spanish (Basque)

> Basque soldier turned mystic who founded the Society of Jesus (the Jesuits) and wrote the Spiritual Exercises.

<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Ignatius of Loyola · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ignatius_Loyola.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Íñigo López de Loyola was born into a Basque noble family and served as a courtier and soldier until a cannonball shattered his leg at the siege of Pamplona in 1521. During a long convalescence he read lives of Christ and the saints and resolved to serve God. At Manresa, near Montserrat, he spent months in prayer and penance and began the notes that became the Spiritual Exercises. After a pilgrimage to Jerusalem he studied at Alcalá, Salamanca and Paris, where he gathered companions including Francis Xavier. In 1540 Pope Paul III approved their Society of Jesus, and Ignatius led it from Rome until his death. He was canonised in 1622.

The Jesuits, bound by a special vow of obedience to the pope regarding missions, became the most dynamic order of the Catholic Reformation. They founded hundreds of schools and colleges and sent missionaries to Asia, Africa and the Americas. Ignatius's Spiritual Exercises, with their method of imaginative prayer and the discernment of spirits, remain widely used by Catholics and others for retreats.

## Key texts & works

### Spiritual Exercises (1522–1548)

[![Spiritual Exercises](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/bc/Exercitia_Spiritualia_1ed2.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Exercitia_Spiritualia_1ed2.jpg)

<sub>The first edition of the Spiritual Exercises, Rome, 1548 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Exercitia_Spiritualia_1ed2.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1522–1548
- **Type:** Manual of prayer and retreat
- **Where written / held:** Manresa and Rome; first printed Rome, 1548
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Spiritual%20Exercises%20Ignatius%20of%20Loyola)

The Spiritual Exercises is not a book to be read straight through but a manual for a director guiding someone through a retreat, usually of about thirty days, divided into four 'weeks'. The first focuses on sin and God's mercy; the second on the life of Christ, with the famous meditations on the Two Standards and the call of the King; the third on the Passion; and the fourth on the resurrection, ending with the 'Contemplation to Attain Love'. It teaches methods of prayer using the imagination to place oneself within Gospel scenes, daily examination of conscience, and rules for 'discernment of spirits' in making decisions. Ignatius drafted it at Manresa in 1522–23 and refined it for decades; Pope Paul III approved it in 1548. It formed every Jesuit and remains widely used by Catholics and others.
