# Poets of the Homeric Hymns

**c. 7th–5th century BC** · Greek  
Born: Greek world · Died:   
Movements & eras: [Greco-Roman religion](../movements/greco-roman-religion.md)  

> The anonymous Greek poets of the Homeric Hymns, a collection of hymns to the gods composed in the epic style and once attributed to Homer.

## Biography

The thirty-three Homeric Hymns are poems addressed to individual gods, composed in the same dactylic hexameter and formulaic language as the Iliad and Odyssey. Ancient readers attributed them to Homer, but scholars now regard them as the work of many anonymous poets, the longest probably dating from the 7th and 6th centuries BC and some shorter ones much later. Several may have served as preludes sung by rhapsodes before epic recitations at festivals.

The four long hymns, to Demeter, Apollo, Hermes and Aphrodite, are among the most important sources for Greek religion, telling how gods founded their sanctuaries and cults. The Hymn to Demeter, which narrates the abduction of Persephone and the founding of the Eleusinian Mysteries, is a key text for the study of Greek mystery religion. The collection survived in Byzantine manuscripts, and the Hymn to Demeter was rediscovered only in 1777 in a single manuscript in Moscow.

## Key texts & works

### Homeric Hymn to Demeter (c. 650–550 BC)

[![Homeric Hymn to Demeter](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/77/Great_Eleusinian_relief_%28NAMA_126%29.jpg/1280px-Great_Eleusinian_relief_%28NAMA_126%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Great_Eleusinian_relief_%28NAMA_126%29.jpg)

<sub>The Great Eleusinian Relief: Demeter, Persephone and the youth Triptolemus, c. 440–430 BC (National Archaeological Museum, Athens) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Great_Eleusinian_relief_%28NAMA_126%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 650–550 BC
- **Type:** Hymn
- **Where written / held:** Rediscovered in a manuscript in Moscow, 1777; now in Leiden University Library
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Homeric%20Hymn%20to%20Demeter%20Poets%20of%20the%20Homeric%20Hymns)

This hymn of nearly five hundred lines tells how Hades, with Zeus's consent, seized Persephone, daughter of the grain goddess Demeter, and carried her to the underworld. Demeter wanders the earth in mourning, disguised as an old woman, and takes service as a nurse in the royal house of Eleusis. When her divinity is revealed she orders a temple built there and withholds the growth of crops until Zeus forces Hades to return Persephone. Because Persephone has eaten a pomegranate seed she must spend part of each year below. Before returning to Olympus, Demeter teaches the Eleusinians her secret rites, which bring blessing to initiates in this life and the next. The Eleusinian Mysteries were celebrated for about a thousand years until they were suppressed at the end of the 4th century AD, and the hymn is one of the principal sources for them, although it guards their secrets.
