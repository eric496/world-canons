# Origen of Alexandria

**c. 185 – c. 253** · Egyptian Greek  
Born: Alexandria, Egypt · Died: Tyre or Caesarea  
Movements & eras: [Church Fathers & the Councils](../movements/church-fathers.md)  

> The most prolific scholar of the early church, a pioneer of biblical textual criticism and allegorical interpretation whose bold speculations were later condemned.

<sub>Portrait: Imaginary engraved portrait of Origen · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Origen3.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Origen was born to a Christian family in Alexandria; his father was martyred in the persecution of 202, and the young Origen took over the city's catechetical school. He lived ascetically and, according to Eusebius, castrated himself in literal obedience to a saying of Jesus, a report some historians doubt. After conflict with Bishop Demetrius of Alexandria he moved around 231 to Caesarea in Palestine, where he founded a school and library. Arrested and tortured in the Decian persecution of 250, he died a few years later.

Origen wrote commentaries and homilies on almost every book of the Bible, the Hexapla comparing Hebrew and Greek texts, the apologetic Against Celsus, and On First Principles, the first systematic Christian theology. He read scripture on literal, moral and spiritual levels, and speculated on the pre-existence of souls and the final restoration of all things. Some of these ideas were condemned in the 6th century, and many of his works are lost or survive only in Latin translation. He nevertheless shaped the exegesis and spirituality of both the Greek and Latin churches.

### On First Principles (De principiis) (c. 220–230)

- **Date:** c. 220–230
- **Type:** Systematic theology
- **Where written / held:** Alexandria; survives mainly in Rufinus's Latin translation
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20First%20Principles%20%28De%20principiis%29%20Origen%20of%20Alexandria)

Written in Alexandria, On First Principles sets out in four books what the church teaches clearly and explores more speculatively questions it leaves open: the Trinity, the creation of rational spirits, the fall, free will, the end of all things and how scripture should be read. Origen suggests that souls existed before bodies, that the material world was created as a place of education for fallen spirits, and that all might ultimately be restored to God, ideas that became controversial. His fourth book explains reading scripture according to body, soul and spirit, the literal, moral and spiritual senses. The Greek original was lost after Origen's condemnation in 553, and the main text is Rufinus's Latin translation of 398, which softened some passages, along with Greek fragments. The work set the pattern for later systematic theology.

### Hexapla (c. 230–245)

- **Date:** c. 230–245
- **Type:** Biblical edition (six-column synopsis)
- **Where written / held:** Library of Caesarea (lost); fragments survive, e.g. in palimpsests in Milan and Cambridge
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Hexapla%20Origen%20of%20Alexandria)

The Hexapla ('sixfold') set out the Old Testament in parallel columns: the Hebrew text, a Greek transliteration of the Hebrew, and the Greek versions of Aquila, Symmachus, the Septuagint and Theodotion, with extra columns for some books. In the Septuagint column Origen used critical signs borrowed from Alexandrian scholars of Homer to mark where the Greek had more or less than the Hebrew. The enormous work, perhaps some 6,000 leaves, was kept in the library at Caesarea, where Jerome consulted it, and was probably destroyed in the 7th century. Only fragments survive, in quotations, a Syriac translation of the Septuagint column and a few palimpsest leaves. It established a model for comparing biblical texts and shaped the Septuagint text used by later copyists.
