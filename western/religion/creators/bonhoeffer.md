# Dietrich Bonhoeffer

**1906 – 1945** · German  
Born: Breslau, Silesia · Died: Flossenbürg concentration camp  
Movements & eras: [20th-century theology & ecumenism](../movements/twentieth-century-theology.md)  

> German Lutheran pastor and theologian of the Confessing Church, executed by the Nazis, whose prison writings reshaped modern theology.

## Biography

Bonhoeffer came from a distinguished Berlin academic family and earned his doctorate in theology at twenty-one. A year at Union Theological Seminary in New York (1930–31), including time at the Abyssinian Baptist Church in Harlem, deepened his concern for social justice. From 1933 he opposed the Nazis and the pro-Nazi 'German Christians', led an illegal Confessing Church seminary at Finkenwalde, and in 1939 returned from America to share his country's fate. Through his brother-in-law Hans von Dohnanyi he joined the resistance circle in military intelligence. Arrested in 1943, he was hanged at Flossenbürg on 9 April 1945.

His Discipleship (in English The Cost of Discipleship) contrasts 'cheap grace' with costly grace, and Life Together describes Christian community. His letters from Tegel prison, published after the war, speculated about a 'religionless Christianity' for a world 'come of age' and have been widely debated. He is commemorated by many churches as a martyr.

### Discipleship (The Cost of Discipleship) (1937)

- **Date:** 1937
- **Type:** Theological meditation
- **Where written / held:** Munich (Chr. Kaiser Verlag)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Discipleship%20%28The%20Cost%20of%20Discipleship%29%20Dietrich%20Bonhoeffer)

Written from his teaching at the illegal Confessing Church seminary at Finkenwalde, Nachfolge ('Discipleship') opens with the famous distinction between cheap grace, 'grace without discipleship', which the church sells cheaply as an assurance without demands, and costly grace, which calls a person to follow Jesus at the cost of one's life. Much of the book expounds the Sermon on the Mount. Published in 1937, the year the Gestapo closed the seminary, it was translated into English in 1948 as The Cost of Discipleship, and Bonhoeffer's execution gave its words exceptional authority.

### Letters and Papers from Prison (1943–1945; published 1951)

- **Date:** 1943–1945; published 1951
- **Type:** Letters and theological fragments
- **Where written / held:** Written in Tegel prison, Berlin; edited by Eberhard Bethge, Munich
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Letters%20and%20Papers%20from%20Prison%20Dietrich%20Bonhoeffer)

After his arrest in April 1943, Bonhoeffer wrote letters to his family and above all to his friend Eberhard Bethge, smuggled out by sympathetic guards, as well as poems such as 'Who am I?' and 'By gracious powers'. From April 1944 the letters explore new theological ideas: that the world has 'come of age' and no longer needs God as a stopgap, that Christianity must be 'religionless', and that the church must exist for others. Bethge published them in 1951 as Widerstand und Ergebung ('Resistance and Submission'). Their fragmentary, provocative thoughts stimulated the 'secular theology' of the 1960s and continue to be debated.
