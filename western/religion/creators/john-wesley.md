## Biography

John Wesley was the fifteenth child of Samuel Wesley, rector of Epworth, and his formidable wife Susanna. At Oxford he and his brother Charles led a group of earnest students mockingly called the 'Holy Club' or 'Methodists'. After an unhappy mission to Georgia, he experienced an assurance of salvation at a meeting in Aldersgate Street, London, on 24 May 1738, where, he wrote, he felt his heart 'strangely warmed'. From 1739, following George Whitefield, he took to preaching in the open air, and for over fifty years travelled some quarter of a million miles on horseback across Britain and Ireland, preaching tens of thousands of sermons and organising converts into societies and classes.

Wesley taught that salvation was open to all and that believers could grow toward 'Christian perfection' in love. He remained an Anglican clergyman, but his ordination of ministers for America led after his death to the separation of the Methodists from the Church of England. Methodism became one of the largest Protestant movements, and his concern for the poor, education and the abolition of slavery influenced social reform.

### Journal of John Wesley (1735–1790)

- **Date:** 1735–1790
- **Type:** Journal
- **Where written / held:** Published in instalments, London, 1740–1791
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Journal%20of%20John%20Wesley%20John%20Wesley)

Wesley kept a private diary and from 1740 published extracts as his Journal in twenty-one instalments. It records his voyage to Georgia and encounters with the Moravians, his experience at Aldersgate Street on 24 May 1738, when he felt his heart 'strangely warmed', and decades of field preaching across Britain and Ireland, with accounts of mobs, conversions, illnesses and the conditions of the poor. It also reveals his wide reading and interests in medicine and electricity. It is a major source for the history of the revival and of 18th-century social life.

### Sermons on Several Occasions (1746–1760)

- **Date:** 1746–1760
- **Type:** Sermons
- **Where written / held:** London and Bristol
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sermons%20on%20Several%20Occasions%20John%20Wesley)

Wesley issued his sermons in volumes from 1746 onward, writing in plain style 'for plain people'. They include 'Salvation by Faith', preached at Oxford in 1738, 'The Scripture Way of Salvation', 'Christian Perfection', 'The Witness of the Spirit', 'Catholic Spirit' on tolerance between Christians, and 'The Use of Money', with its advice to gain all you can, save all you can and give all you can. The first collections, together with his Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament, became the official doctrinal standards of Methodist preachers and are still referred to by Methodist churches.
