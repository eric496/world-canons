# Basil of Caesarea

**c. 330 – 379** · Cappadocian Greek  
Born: Caesarea, Cappadocia · Died: Caesarea  
Movements & eras: [Church Fathers & the Councils](../movements/church-fathers.md), [Monasticism](../movements/monasticism.md)  

> Bishop, theologian and monastic organiser, one of the three Cappadocian Fathers, whose ascetic rules remain the basis of Orthodox monasticism.

## Biography

Basil, called 'the Great', came from a wealthy and devout family of Cappadocia in Asia Minor; his brother Gregory of Nyssa and sister Macrina were also revered as saints. He studied in Constantinople and Athens, where he befriended Gregory of Nazianzus, then turned to the ascetic life and visited monks in Egypt, Syria and Palestine. Ordained priest and, in 370, bishop of Caesarea, he built a complex of hospital, hostel and poorhouse, the Basileias, and defended Nicene orthodoxy against the emperor Valens.

With the two Gregories, Basil shaped the classic doctrine of the Trinity as one essence in three persons, and his On the Holy Spirit defended the full divinity of the Spirit. His Asketikon, the rules he wrote for communities of monks, favoured communal life over solitary hermitage and remains the foundation of Eastern monasticism. A eucharistic liturgy bearing his name is still celebrated on certain days of the Orthodox year.

### Asketikon (Rules of St Basil) (c. 358–370s)

- **Date:** c. 358–370s
- **Type:** Monastic rule
- **Where written / held:** Pontus and Cappadocia
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Asketikon%20%28Rules%20of%20St%20Basil%29%20Basil%20of%20Caesarea)

Basil's Asketikon developed in several editions from his visits to monks and his own community in Pontus. Its 'Longer Rules' and 'Shorter Rules' answer questions about Christian life in community: obedience, prayer, work, poverty, the care of children, and how to live with others in love. Basil favoured cenobitic, communal monasticism over solitary hermit life, asking how a hermit could wash anyone's feet. He also expected monasteries to serve their neighbours, an ideal realised in his hospital complex at Caesarea. A Latin version by Rufinus influenced Benedict of Nursia, who recommends 'the Rule of our holy father Basil' in his own Rule. In the Orthodox world, monks are often described as following the tradition of St Basil.

### On the Holy Spirit (c. 375)

- **Date:** c. 375
- **Type:** Theological treatise
- **Where written / held:** Caesarea, Cappadocia
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20the%20Holy%20Spirit%20Basil%20of%20Caesarea)

Basil wrote On the Holy Spirit after being criticised for using a doxology that glorified the Father 'with the Son, together with the Holy Spirit', implying their equality. Against the Pneumatomachi ('Spirit-fighters'), who denied the Spirit's divinity, he argues from scripture, from the baptismal formula and from the church's unwritten tradition of worship that the Spirit is to be honoured together with the Father and the Son. He is careful not to call the Spirit 'God' directly, a caution that reflects the politics of the time. The treatise was a major influence on the Council of Constantinople and on all later Trinitarian theology, and is also an early witness to the idea of authoritative unwritten tradition.
