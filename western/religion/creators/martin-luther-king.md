# Martin Luther King Jr.

**1929 – 1968** · American  
Born: Atlanta, Georgia · Died: Memphis, Tennessee  
Movements & eras: [20th-century theology & ecumenism](../movements/twentieth-century-theology.md)  

> Baptist minister and leader of the American civil rights movement, whose nonviolent campaigns and sermons drew on Christian theology and the Hebrew prophets.

## Biography

King was the son and grandson of Baptist pastors of the Ebenezer Baptist Church in Atlanta. He studied at Morehouse College, Crozer Theological Seminary and Boston University, where he earned a doctorate in systematic theology. As pastor of Dexter Avenue Baptist Church in Montgomery, Alabama, he led the bus boycott of 1955–56 and then the Southern Christian Leadership Conference, organising campaigns in Albany, Birmingham, Selma and elsewhere. He received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1964 and was assassinated in Memphis in 1968.

King's thought combined the Black church tradition, the social gospel, the personalist philosophy he studied in Boston, Reinhold Niebuhr's realism and Gandhi's nonviolence. His sermons and writings, such as Strength to Love and the Letter from Birmingham Jail, made a theological case for justice and love of enemies that influenced liberation movements worldwide.

### Letter from Birmingham Jail (1963)

- **Date:** 1963
- **Type:** Open letter
- **Where written / held:** Written in Birmingham City Jail, Alabama
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Letter%20from%20Birmingham%20Jail%20Martin%20Luther%20King%20Jr.)

Arrested on 12 April 1963 during the Birmingham campaign against segregation, King read a statement by eight white Alabama clergymen urging Black citizens to stop demonstrating and wait for the courts. Writing at first in the margins of a newspaper, he answered that injustice anywhere is a threat to justice everywhere, that nonviolent direct action aims to create tension that forces negotiation, and that one has a moral duty to disobey unjust laws, citing Augustine and Aquinas. He expressed disappointment with the 'white moderate' and with churches that stayed silent. The letter, widely published that summer, became one of the most important documents of the civil rights movement and of Christian social ethics. It remains under copyright and is reproduced by The King Center and the Martin Luther King Jr. Research and Education Institute at Stanford.

### Strength to Love (1963)

- **Date:** 1963
- **Type:** Sermons
- **Where written / held:** New York (Harper & Row)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Strength%20to%20Love%20Martin%20Luther%20King%20Jr.)

Strength to Love gathers sixteen sermons, many preached at Dexter Avenue Baptist Church in Montgomery and Ebenezer Baptist Church in Atlanta, and revised partly while King was in jail. They include 'A Tough Mind and a Tender Heart', 'Loving Your Enemies', 'Antidotes for Fear' and 'Pilgrimage to Nonviolence', in which he describes his intellectual journey through the social gospel, Niebuhr and Gandhi. The book shows the theological foundations of King's activism in the Black church preaching tradition. It remains under copyright.
