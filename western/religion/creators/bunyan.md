# John Bunyan

**1628 – 1688** · English  
Born: Elstow, Bedfordshire · Died: London  
Movements & eras: [Puritanism & Pietism](../movements/puritanism-pietism.md)  

> English tinker and Puritan preacher who wrote The Pilgrim's Progress while imprisoned for unlicensed preaching.

## Biography

Bunyan was the son of a brazier at Elstow near Bedford and followed his father's trade as a tinker. He served in the Parliamentary army during the Civil War. After his marriage he passed through years of spiritual anguish before joining an independent congregation in Bedford and becoming a popular preacher. After the Restoration of 1660, unlicensed preaching was outlawed, and Bunyan spent most of the next twelve years in Bedford gaol, refusing to promise to stop. Released in 1672, he became pastor of the Bedford congregation, and died in London in 1688.

Bunyan wrote some sixty works, including his spiritual autobiography Grace Abounding to the Chief of Sinners. The Pilgrim's Progress, an allegory of the Christian life as a journey, became one of the most widely read books in English, translated into more than two hundred languages. Its plain, vivid English and memorable characters influenced writers from Defoe to Dickens.

### Grace Abounding to the Chief of Sinners (1666)

- **Date:** 1666
- **Type:** Spiritual autobiography
- **Where written / held:** London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Grace%20Abounding%20to%20the%20Chief%20of%20Sinners%20John%20Bunyan)

Written in prison and published in 1666, Grace Abounding tells Bunyan's story as a spiritual drama: a youth of swearing and games, his marriage and first stirrings of piety, and then years of agonising doubt in which he feared he had committed the unforgivable sin, tormented by verses of scripture that seemed to condemn or save him by turns. He finally finds assurance in Christ, and then describes his call to preach and his imprisonment. It is one of the most psychologically intense religious autobiographies in English and a key text for understanding Puritan experience of conversion.

### The Pilgrim's Progress (1678 (Part I); 1684 (Part II))

- **Date:** 1678 (Part I); 1684 (Part II)
- **Type:** Allegory
- **Where written / held:** Printed by Nathaniel Ponder, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Pilgrim%27s%20Progress%20John%20Bunyan)

Bunyan began The Pilgrim's Progress during his imprisonment in Bedford gaol. The narrator dreams of a man called Christian, burdened with sin, who leaves the City of Destruction for the Celestial City. On the way he loses his burden at the cross, passes through the Slough of Despond, Vanity Fair, where his companion Faithful is martyred, and Doubting Castle, kept by Giant Despair, and meets characters such as Evangelist, Mr Worldly Wiseman and Hopeful, before crossing the river of death. The second part, of 1684, follows his wife Christiana and their children. Written in plain, vivid prose drawing on the Bible and popular storytelling, it was an immediate success and has been translated into some two hundred languages, carried worldwide by missionaries. It has been called one of the first English novels and influenced writers from Defoe to Thackeray and Louisa May Alcott.
