## Biography

Augustine was born at Thagaste in Roman North Africa to a pagan father and a Christian mother, Monica. He studied and taught rhetoric in Carthage, Rome and Milan, and for nine years followed the Manichaean religion. In Milan, influenced by Neoplatonist philosophy and by the sermons of Bishop Ambrose, he converted to Christianity in 386 and was baptised in 387. Returning to Africa, he was ordained priest at Hippo Regius and in 395 or 396 became its bishop, a post he held until his death in 430 while the city was besieged by the Vandals.

Augustine wrote prodigiously: autobiography, sermons, letters, biblical commentaries, and controversial works against Manichaeans, Donatists and Pelagians. His teachings on original sin, grace, predestination, the Trinity and the church, and his idea of history as the story of two cities, became the framework of Western theology. Medieval scholastics, Luther and Calvin, Catholic Jansenists and modern philosophers have all claimed him in different ways.

### Confessions (c. 397–400)

[![Confessions](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5d/Augustine_Confessiones.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Augustine_Confessiones.jpg)

<sub>Augustine's Confessions in a manuscript Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Augustine_Confessiones.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 397–400
- **Type:** Autobiography and prayer
- **Where written / held:** Hippo Regius, North Africa
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Confessions%20Augustine%20of%20Hippo)

In thirteen books addressed to God, Augustine recounts his childhood in Thagaste, including the theft of pears that he analyses as a sin done for its own sake, his studies in Carthage, his years as a Manichaean and a teacher of rhetoric, and his mother Monica's prayers. In Milan, influenced by Neoplatonism and by Ambrose, he struggles with his attachment to sexual desire and worldly success until, in a garden in 386, he hears a child's voice say 'take up and read', opens Paul's letters and is converted. After describing his mother's death, he turns to meditations on memory, time and the creation story in Genesis. The book's famous opening declares that the human heart is restless until it rests in God. The Confessions profoundly influenced Christian spirituality and the Western tradition of self-examination, from Petrarch to Rousseau and modern autobiography.

### On the Trinity (De Trinitate) (c. 400–420)

- **Date:** c. 400–420
- **Type:** Theological treatise
- **Where written / held:** Hippo Regius, North Africa
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20the%20Trinity%20%28De%20Trinitate%29%20Augustine%20of%20Hippo)

Augustine worked on On the Trinity for some twenty years. The first books set out the scriptural basis and the vocabulary of the doctrine of one God in three persons, defending the equality of the Son and Spirit with the Father. The later books turn inward, looking for analogies of the Trinity in the human mind, made in God's image, most famously the triad of memory, understanding and will. The work became the foundation of Latin Trinitarian theology; its account of the Spirit as the love between Father and Son underlies the Western filioque. Medieval theologians from Anselm to Aquinas built on it.

### The City of God (413–426)

[![The City of God](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/52/City_of_God_Manuscript.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:City_of_God_Manuscript.jpg)

<sub>A medieval illuminated manuscript of The City of God Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:City_of_God_Manuscript.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 413–426
- **Type:** Theological and historical treatise
- **Where written / held:** Hippo Regius, North Africa
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20City%20of%20God%20Augustine%20of%20Hippo)

When the Visigoths sacked Rome in 410, pagans blamed the disaster on the abandonment of the old gods. In twenty-two books written over thirteen years, Augustine first argues that the gods had never protected Rome and that pagan religion and philosophy cannot give true happiness, then traces through history two 'cities', defined by two loves: the earthly city of self-love, and the city of God, a community of the faithful on pilgrimage through this world toward its fulfilment beyond history. Along the way he discusses creation, the fall, the Roman Empire, providence, peace and the last things. The work shaped medieval ideas of church and state and Western philosophies of history, and it was one of the most copied books of the Middle Ages.
