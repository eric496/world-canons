# John of the Cross

**1542 – 1591** · Spanish  
Born: Fontiveros, Castile · Died: Úbeda, Andalusia  
Movements & eras: [Catholic Reformation](../movements/catholic-reformation.md)  

> Spanish Carmelite friar, mystic and poet, co-founder of the Discalced Carmelites and author of The Dark Night of the Soul.

## Biography

Juan de Yepes y Álvarez grew up in poverty after the early death of his father, worked in a hospital and studied with the Jesuits and at the University of Salamanca. He joined the Carmelites in 1563 and in 1567 met Teresa of Ávila, who recruited him to extend her reform to the friars. Opponents of the reform within the order imprisoned him in Toledo in 1577 for some nine months, in a tiny cell, where he composed much of his poetry before escaping. He later served as prior and in other offices, but fell out of favour shortly before his death in 1591.

His few poems, including 'The Dark Night', 'The Spiritual Canticle' and 'The Living Flame of Love', are counted among the finest lyrics in Spanish, and he wrote long prose commentaries on them that map the soul's purification and union with God. The phrase 'dark night of the soul' has passed into common speech. He was canonised in 1726 and declared a doctor of the church in 1926.

### Spiritual Canticle (1578–1584)

- **Date:** 1578–1584
- **Type:** Poem and commentary
- **Where written / held:** Begun in prison in Toledo; completed in Andalusia
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Spiritual%20Canticle%20John%20of%20the%20Cross)

John began the Spiritual Canticle while imprisoned in a cell in the Carmelite priory of Toledo in 1577–78, composing the first thirty-one stanzas in his head. The poem, inspired by the Song of Songs, is a dialogue in which the bride, the soul, searches for her wounded and hidden beloved through woods and meadows until they are united. He completed it later and wrote a long commentary explaining its stages of spiritual life. Two versions of the poem and commentary exist, with a different order of stanzas, and scholars debate whether the second is his revision.

### The Dark Night of the Soul (c. 1578–1585)

- **Date:** c. 1578–1585
- **Type:** Poem and commentary
- **Where written / held:** Composed in Granada and Andalusia; first printed in Alcalá, 1618
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Dark%20Night%20of%20the%20Soul%20John%20of%20the%20Cross)

The poem 'En una noche oscura', 'On a dark night', in eight stanzas, tells of a woman slipping out of her quiet house at night, guided only by the light in her heart, to meet her beloved and rest with him. John wrote two prose works explaining it, The Ascent of Mount Carmel and The Dark Night, which describe the 'night of the senses' and the deeper 'night of the spirit', in which God purifies the soul by taking away consolations and certainties, leading it through apparent emptiness to union with God. The prose commentaries were left unfinished. The phrase 'dark night of the soul' has come into general use for times of spiritual desolation.
