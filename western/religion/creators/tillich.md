# Paul Tillich

**1886 – 1965** · German-American  
Born: Starzeddel, Brandenburg (now Starosiedle, Poland) · Died: Chicago  
Movements & eras: [20th-century theology & ecumenism](../movements/twentieth-century-theology.md)  

> German-American Lutheran theologian who interpreted Christianity in dialogue with existentialist philosophy, psychology and culture.

## Biography

Tillich was the son of a Lutheran pastor, served as an army chaplain in the First World War, and taught at Berlin, Marburg, Dresden, Leipzig and Frankfurt. A religious socialist, he was dismissed by the Nazis in 1933 and emigrated to the United States, where he taught at Union Theological Seminary, Harvard and the University of Chicago, becoming widely known beyond academic circles.

His Systematic Theology used a 'method of correlation', relating existential questions to theological answers, and described God as 'being itself' and faith as 'ultimate concern'. The Courage to Be and Dynamics of Faith reached a broad public, and his thought shaped the dialogue between theology, art and psychotherapy.

## Key texts & works

### Systematic Theology (1951–1963)

- **Date:** 1951–1963
- **Type:** Systematic theology
- **Where written / held:** University of Chicago Press
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Systematic%20Theology%20Paul%20Tillich)

In three volumes, Tillich uses a 'method of correlation' in which philosophy formulates the questions arising from human existence, such as finitude, estrangement and ambiguity, and theology answers with the symbols of Christian revelation. Its five parts treat reason and revelation, being and God, existence and the Christ, life and the Spirit, and history and the kingdom of God. Tillich describes God not as a being among others but as 'being itself' or the 'ground of being', and Christ as the 'New Being'. The work was influential in mid-century American theology and in dialogue with psychology and the arts.
