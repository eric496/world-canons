## Biography

Kierkegaard was the youngest child of a wealthy, melancholic Copenhagen merchant and studied theology at the University of Copenhagen. In 1841 he broke off his engagement to Regine Olsen, an event that haunted his writing. Over the next decade he published a stream of books, many under pseudonyms, such as Either/Or, Fear and Trembling, The Concept of Anxiety and Concluding Unscientific Postscript, alongside 'upbuilding discourses' under his own name. In his last years he waged a public campaign against the Danish state church, accusing it of having abolished true Christianity. He collapsed in the street and died in 1855.

Kierkegaard insisted that faith is a passionate, personal decision before God that cannot be reached by reason or social conformity, and he analysed the stages of existence as aesthetic, ethical and religious. Little known outside Denmark in his lifetime, he was rediscovered in the early 20th century and became a major influence on existentialism and on dialectical theology, including Barth.

### Fear and Trembling (1843)

[![Fear and Trembling](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/76/Fear_and_Trembling.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fear_and_Trembling.jpg)

<sub>Title page of Frygt og Bæven, 1843 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fear_and_Trembling.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1843
- **Type:** Philosophical-religious essay
- **Where written / held:** Published under the pseudonym Johannes de Silentio, Copenhagen
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Fear%20and%20Trembling%20S%C3%B8ren%20Kierkegaard)

Kierkegaard's pseudonymous author, Johannes de Silentio, retells the story of the binding of Isaac (Genesis 22) in several versions and confesses that he cannot understand Abraham. He argues that if Abraham is the father of faith, faith cannot be the same as ethics, the universal duty that would forbid killing one's son; it must involve a 'teleological suspension of the ethical' and an absolute relation of the individual to God, held 'by virtue of the absurd'. He contrasts the 'knight of faith', who gives up everything and receives it back, with the 'knight of infinite resignation'. The book reflects Kierkegaard's own broken engagement and his polemic against Hegelian philosophy and comfortable Christianity, and is a key text for existentialism and for debates in Jewish and Christian thought on the Akedah.

### The Sickness unto Death (1849)

[![The Sickness unto Death](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/31/The_Sickness_Unto_Death.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Sickness_Unto_Death.jpg)

<sub>Title page of Sygdommen til Døden, 1849 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Sickness_Unto_Death.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1849
- **Type:** Philosophical-religious treatise
- **Where written / held:** Published under the pseudonym Anti-Climacus, Copenhagen
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Sickness%20unto%20Death%20S%C3%B8ren%20Kierkegaard)

Published under the pseudonym Anti-Climacus, whom Kierkegaard described as a Christian of an extraordinarily high degree, The Sickness unto Death takes its title from Jesus's words about Lazarus. It defines the self as a relation that relates itself to itself and is grounded in the power that established it, and analyses the forms of despair: not being conscious of having a self, not willing to be oneself, and willing defiantly to be oneself. Despair before God is sin, and its opposite is faith. The work's psychological depth influenced Heidegger, Sartre and theologians such as Tillich and Barth.
