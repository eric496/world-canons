## About the images

All pictures come from Wikimedia Commons and are shown as thumbnails linked from there. We use only files hosted on Commons; images that exist only on English Wikipedia are not free and are left out.

**Artist portraits** are always artworks, never photographs: a self-portrait where one survives (Dürer, Rembrandt, Van Gogh), otherwise a portrait painted, drawn, engraved or sculpted by a contemporary (Melzi's drawing of Leonardo, Fantin-Latour's Manet, Camille Claudel's bust of Rodin). For the earliest Italians we use the woodcut portraits from Giorgio Vasari's *Lives of the Artists* (1568), and for some Netherlandish painters the engraved portraits published by Dominicus Lampsonius in 1572. These are imagined or traditional likenesses rather than records of a face, and captions say when a self-portrait is only presumed. Ancient sculptors and artists of whom no free portrait exists have none.

**Copyright.** A work is reproduced only when it is in the public domain, which in practice means its maker died more than about 70 years ago and Commons hosts it. Works by artists still in copyright, such as Picasso, Dalí, Kahlo, Magritte, Pollock, Rothko and Warhol, are not reproduced. Instead each one links to the museum page where it can be seen. This also applies to their self-portraits, so these artists have no portrait here, and the movements they define (Abstract Expressionism, Pop Art, Neo-Expressionism) have no illustration.

**Movement images** reuse a representative public-domain work, usually one already in the gallery. Dada is shown through Alfred Stieglitz's 1917 photograph of Duchamp's *Fountain*, published in *The Blind Man* and in the public domain.
