## Biography

Francisco de Goya was born in Fuendetodos near Zaragoza and trained in Zaragoza and Madrid, with a visit to Italy. He made his name designing cartoons for the Royal Tapestry Factory, cheerful scenes of Spanish life, and rose to become First Court Painter to Charles IV in 1799. A severe illness in 1792–93 left him completely deaf, and his art grew darker and more personal. His print series Los Caprichos (1799) satirised superstition and corruption. The French invasion and the brutal Peninsular War (1808–14) produced his etchings The Disasters of War, published only after his death. In 1819–23 he painted the 'Black Paintings' on the walls of his house outside Madrid, and in 1824 he went into self-imposed exile in Bordeaux, where he died in 1828.

Goya was a brilliant court portraitist and painter of the Spanish people. He was also an unsparing witness to war, cruelty, madness and the irrational, and he anticipated Romanticism, Expressionism and even Surrealism. His Family of Charles IV, his Majas and his prints made him a crucial link between Velázquez and modern artists such as Manet and Picasso.

### The Third of May 1808 (1814)

[![The Third of May 1808](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/El_Tres_de_Mayo%2C_by_Francisco_de_Goya%2C_from_Prado_thin_black_margin.jpg/1280px-El_Tres_de_Mayo%2C_by_Francisco_de_Goya%2C_from_Prado_thin_black_margin.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Tres_de_Mayo%2C_by_Francisco_de_Goya%2C_from_Prado_thin_black_margin.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Tres_de_Mayo%2C_by_Francisco_de_Goya%2C_from_Prado_thin_black_margin.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1814
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Museo del Prado, Madrid
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Third%20of%20May%201808%20Francisco%20Goya)

On 2 May 1808 the people of Madrid rose up against French occupation troops. The next night the French executed hundreds of civilians in reprisal. In 1814, after the French were expelled, Goya painted this scene and its companion, The Second of May. Under a dark sky, a faceless line of soldiers aims rifles at a group of prisoners lit by a large lantern. At the centre, a man in a white shirt flings his arms wide in a Christ-like pose, and marks resembling the stigmata appear on his palm. Others cover their eyes, pray or lie dead in blood. It is one of the first great paintings to show the horror of war without glory, and it influenced Manet's Execution of Maximilian and Picasso's Guernica. It is in the Prado.

### Saturn Devouring His Son (c. 1819–23)

[![Saturn Devouring His Son](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Francisco_de_Goya%2C_Saturno_devorando_a_su_hijo_%281819-1823%29.jpg/1280px-Francisco_de_Goya%2C_Saturno_devorando_a_su_hijo_%281819-1823%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Francisco_de_Goya%2C_Saturno_devorando_a_su_hijo_%281819-1823%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Francisco_de_Goya%2C_Saturno_devorando_a_su_hijo_%281819-1823%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1819–23
- **Type:** Oil mural transferred to canvas
- **Where to see it:** Museo del Prado, Madrid
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Saturn%20Devouring%20His%20Son%20Francisco%20Goya)

Between 1819 and 1823, Goya, now old, deaf and disillusioned, painted 14 dark murals directly on the plaster walls of his house outside Madrid, known as the Quinta del Sordo ('House of the Deaf Man'). This image, painted in the dining room, shows the Roman god Saturn (Kronos), who devoured his children for fear that one of them would overthrow him. Out of the black, a wild-eyed giant tears into the headless, bloody body of a child. The scene has been read as a symbol of time, of the destruction of Spain by its rulers, or of Goya's own despair. The titles were given by others after his death. In the 1870s the murals were transferred to canvas, and they were donated to the Prado in 1881.
