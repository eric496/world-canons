## Biography

Peter Paul Rubens was born in Siegen in Germany, where his Protestant father had fled, and grew up in Antwerp after the family returned and converted to Catholicism. After his training he spent 1600–08 in Italy, mainly at the Gonzaga court in Mantua, studying Michelangelo, Titian and ancient sculpture. Back in Antwerp he became court painter to the Habsburg governors Archduke Albert and Isabella and built a grand house and studio, now the Rubenshuis museum. He ran a huge workshop, with assistants such as the young Anthony van Dyck, and produced altarpieces, mythologies, portraits, tapestry designs and vast decorative cycles, including the life of Marie de' Medici (Louvre) and the ceiling of the Banqueting House in Whitehall.

Rubens was also a highly educated humanist and a diplomat. He helped negotiate peace between Spain and England in 1629–30 and was knighted by both Charles I and Philip IV. His paintings are full of energy, swirling movement, warm colour and abundant flesh, which gave English the word 'Rubenesque'. He was the most influential painter of the northern Baroque, and later painters from Watteau to Delacroix and Renoir looked to him.

## Key artworks

### The Elevation of the Cross (1610–11)

[![The Elevation of the Cross](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/ca/Peter_Paul_Rubens_-_Raising_of_the_Cross_%28Antwerp_Cathedral%29.JPG/1280px-Peter_Paul_Rubens_-_Raising_of_the_Cross_%28Antwerp_Cathedral%29.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Peter_Paul_Rubens_-_Raising_of_the_Cross_%28Antwerp_Cathedral%29.JPG)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Peter_Paul_Rubens_-_Raising_of_the_Cross_%28Antwerp_Cathedral%29.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1610–11
- **Type:** Oil on panel (triptych)
- **Where to see it:** Cathedral of Our Lady, Antwerp
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Elevation%20of%20the%20Cross%20Peter%20Paul%20Rubens)

Rubens painted this huge triptych in 1610–11, soon after returning from Italy, for the high altar of the church of St Walburga in Antwerp. It was moved to the Cathedral of Our Lady in 1816, after being taken to Paris under Napoleon and returned. Across all three panels, muscular men strain to haul the cross with Christ upright along a dramatic diagonal. In the side wings, mourners grieve at left and Roman officers watch at right. The heroic bodies show what Rubens learned from Michelangelo's nudes and Hellenistic sculpture, and the dramatic light owes something to Caravaggio. With his Descent from the Cross in the same cathedral, it established him as the leading painter of the Southern Netherlands.
