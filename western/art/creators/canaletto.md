## Biography

Giovanni Antonio Canal, called Canaletto ('little canal'), was the son of a Venetian theatrical scene painter and began his career painting stage sets. Around 1720 he turned to painting views of Venice. These became the most coveted souvenirs of British travellers on the Grand Tour, many sold through his agent Joseph Smith, the British consul, whose own huge collection was later bought by George III for the Royal Collection. When the War of the Austrian Succession cut off the tourist trade, he moved to England in 1746 and spent most of the next decade painting London and country houses.

Canaletto's vedute (view paintings) combine topographical accuracy with careful composition, crisp light and lively figures. He often adjusted viewpoints and combined several perspectives into one picture. He owned a camera obscura, now in the Museo Correr in Venice, and used drawing aids to construct his views. His early work is loose and atmospheric. His later, more mechanical production was supported by a busy workshop and many imitators, including his nephew Bernardo Bellotto.

## Key artworks

### The Stonemason's Yard (c. 1725)

[![The Stonemason's Yard](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/45/Canaletto_-_The_Stonemason%27s_Yard.jpg/1280px-Canaletto_-_The_Stonemason%27s_Yard.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Canaletto_-_The_Stonemason%27s_Yard.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Canaletto_-_The_Stonemason%27s_Yard.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1725
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** National Gallery, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Stonemason%27s%20Yard%20Canaletto)

Painted around 1725, early in his career, this view in the National Gallery, London, shows not a famous monument but the everyday Campo San Vidal in Venice, temporarily turned into a stonemasons' workshop. In the foreground, masons dress blocks of stone for the rebuilding of the church of San Vidal. A mother rushes to a toddler who has fallen, women gossip at a window, and laundry hangs from a balcony. Across the Grand Canal, sunlit, rises the church and bell tower of Santa Maria della Carità. The warm light, the strong shadows and the sense of real working life make it one of Canaletto's finest paintings.
