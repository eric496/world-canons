> A goldsmith and sculptor who won the 1401 competition for the Florence Baptistery doors, a contest often taken as the start of the Renaissance. He spent most of his life on two sets of bronze doors.

## Biography

Lorenzo Ghiberti trained as a goldsmith. In 1401, at about 23, he won the competition to make the second set of bronze doors for the Florence Baptistery, beating Filippo Brunelleschi among others. Both trial panels, showing the Sacrifice of Isaac, survive in the Bargello. The doors with 28 scenes took him until 1424. He then spent most of the next three decades on a second pair, which Michelangelo reputedly called worthy to be the Gates of Paradise.

Ghiberti's workshop was a training ground for the next generation, including Donatello, Michelozzo and Paolo Uccello. He also wrote the Commentarii, which contains the first autobiography by a European artist and an early history of Italian art. His work bridges the graceful International Gothic style and the new Renaissance interest in perspective, classical architecture and the nude.

## Key artworks

### Gates of Paradise (1425–52)

[![Gates of Paradise](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/Lorenzo_Ghiberti-The_Gates_of_Paradise_-_Original-Museo_dell%27Opera_del_Duomo_%28Florence%29.jpg/1280px-Lorenzo_Ghiberti-The_Gates_of_Paradise_-_Original-Museo_dell%27Opera_del_Duomo_%28Florence%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lorenzo_Ghiberti-The_Gates_of_Paradise_-_Original-Museo_dell%27Opera_del_Duomo_%28Florence%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lorenzo_Ghiberti-The_Gates_of_Paradise_-_Original-Museo_dell%27Opera_del_Duomo_%28Florence%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1425–52
- **Type:** Gilded bronze doors
- **Where to see it:** Museo dell'Opera del Duomo, Florence (copies on the Baptistery)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Gates%20of%20Paradise%20Lorenzo%20Ghiberti)

In 1425 Ghiberti was commissioned to make a third set of bronze doors for the Florence Baptistery, the east doors facing the cathedral. He worked on them until 1452. Abandoning the Gothic four-lobed frames of earlier doors, he made ten large square panels of gilded bronze telling Old Testament stories from Adam and Eve to Solomon and the Queen of Sheba. Each panel uses the new linear perspective to create deep architectural and landscape settings, with figures modelled from almost fully round in front to faint low relief at the back. Small heads in the frame include a self-portrait. Michelangelo reportedly said the doors were worthy to be the Gates of Paradise. After flood damage in 1966 and long restoration, the originals are now in the cathedral museum, and copies hang on the Baptistery.
