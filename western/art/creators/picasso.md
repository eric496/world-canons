# Pablo Picasso

**1881 – 1973** · Spanish  
Born: Málaga, Spain · Died: Mougins, France  
Movements: [Cubism](../movements/cubism.md)  

> The most influential artist of the 20th century, co-inventor of Cubism and endlessly reinventive across painting, sculpture, ceramics and print.

## Biography

Pablo Picasso was born in Málaga, the son of the painter and drawing teacher José Ruiz Blasco. He grew up in A Coruña and Barcelona, where he was a child prodigy. He settled in Paris in 1904. After the melancholy Blue Period (1901–04), triggered partly by the suicide of his friend Carles Casagemas, and the Rose Period of circus performers, he painted Les Demoiselles d'Avignon (1907). With Georges Braque he then invented Cubism and collage. He designed for the Ballets Russes, married the dancer Olga Khokhlova, passed through a Neoclassical phase and joined the margins of Surrealism. His mural Guernica (1937) became a universal anti-war image. He stayed in Paris during the German occupation, joined the French Communist Party in 1944, and later lived in the south of France, working in ceramics at Vallauris. He died at Mougins in 1973, aged 91.

Picasso is the most influential artist of the 20th century. He constantly reinvented his style and worked in painting, sculpture, printmaking, ceramics, stage design and assemblage, producing an estimated tens of thousands of works. His tumultuous relationships with a succession of women, including Marie-Thérèse Walter, Dora Maar, Françoise Gilot and Jacqueline Roque, shaped his art and are now critically examined. Museums dedicated to him exist in Paris, Barcelona, Málaga and Antibes.

### Les Demoiselles d'Avignon (1907)

> 🖼️ **[View it at MoMA →](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/79766)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1907
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Les%20Demoiselles%20d%27Avignon%20Pablo%20Picasso)

Picasso worked on this large canvas in his Paris studio over several months in 1907, making hundreds of preparatory drawings. It shows five nude women, prostitutes in a brothel on the Carrer d'Avinyó in Barcelona, confronting the viewer, with a still life of fruit at the bottom. Their bodies are made of sharp, angular planes. Some faces recall ancient Iberian sculpture, and the two at the right resemble African and Oceanic masks, which Picasso saw at the Trocadéro ethnographic museum in Paris. Even Picasso's friends were shocked, and he did not exhibit it publicly until 1916. It has been called a turning point in modern art and a starting point for Cubism. The Museum of Modern Art in New York acquired it in 1939.

### Guernica (1937)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Museo Reina Sofía →](https://www.museoreinasofia.es/en/collections/artwork/guernica-0/)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1937
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Museo Reina Sofía, Madrid
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Guernica%20Pablo%20Picasso)

In January 1937 the government of the Spanish Republic, then fighting General Franco's Nationalists in the Spanish Civil War, commissioned Picasso to paint a mural for its pavilion at the Paris World's Fair. On 26 April 1937 German and Italian aircraft bombed the Basque town of Guernica in support of Franco. Picasso painted the huge canvas, nearly 8 metres wide, in about five weeks, and his partner Dora Maar photographed its progress. In a monochrome palette of black, white and grey that evokes newspaper photographs, a bull, a screaming horse, a mother holding her dead child, a dismembered soldier and figures in flames are crowded under a harsh electric light. At Picasso's request it was kept at the Museum of Modern Art in New York until democracy was restored in Spain, and it arrived in Madrid in 1981. Since 1992 it has been in the Museo Reina Sofía.
