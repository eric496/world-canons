## Biography

Paul Gauguin was born in Paris and spent part of his childhood in Lima, Peru, with his mother's family. After serving in the merchant navy and the navy, he became a successful stockbroker, married the Danish Mette Gad and had five children. He collected Impressionist paintings and began painting as an amateur under Pissarro, exhibiting in the Impressionist shows. After the stock market crash of 1882 he decided to paint full time, and his family life collapsed. He worked in Brittany at Pont-Aven, in Martinique and, briefly and disastrously, with Van Gogh in Arles in 1888. In search of an unspoiled 'primitive' paradise he went to Tahiti in 1891, returned to France, and left again in 1895. He spent his last years in the Marquesas Islands, where he died in 1903, in conflict with the colonial authorities and the Church.

Gauguin developed a style of flat areas of bold, non-naturalistic colour enclosed by dark outlines, influenced by Japanese prints, stained glass and folk art. His aim was to express ideas, emotions and spiritual meaning rather than to record appearances. He was also an innovative woodcut artist and sculptor and a crucial influence on the Nabis, Fauvism and Expressionism. Today his colonial fantasy and his sexual relationships with very young Tahitian girls are the subject of critical debate, often discussed alongside his art.

### Vision after the Sermon (1888)

[![Vision after the Sermon](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/56/La_vision_apr%C3%A8s_le_sermon_%28Paul_Gauguin%29.jpg/1280px-La_vision_apr%C3%A8s_le_sermon_%28Paul_Gauguin%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_vision_apr%C3%A8s_le_sermon_%28Paul_Gauguin%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_vision_apr%C3%A8s_le_sermon_%28Paul_Gauguin%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1888
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Scottish National Gallery, Edinburgh
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Vision%20after%20the%20Sermon%20Paul%20Gauguin)

Painted in Pont-Aven in 1888, this is one of the founding works of Symbolism and of Gauguin's mature style. A group of Breton women in white headdresses, having just heard a sermon, have a vision of the biblical story of Jacob wrestling with the angel. The boundary between the real women in the foreground and their vision is a diagonal tree trunk. The ground is a flat, unnatural field of vermilion. Its bold outlines, flat colour and cropped composition show the influence of Japanese prints and of Émile Bernard. Gauguin offered it to a local church, which refused it. It is in the Scottish National Gallery, Edinburgh.

### Where Do We Come From? What Are We? Where Are We Going? (1897–98)

[![Where Do We Come From? What Are We? Where Are We Going?](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0a/Gauguin_-_Where_Do_We_Come_From%3F_What_Are_We%3F_Where_Are_We_Going%3F_%281897-98%29.jpg/1280px-Gauguin_-_Where_Do_We_Come_From%3F_What_Are_We%3F_Where_Are_We_Going%3F_%281897-98%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gauguin_-_Where_Do_We_Come_From%3F_What_Are_We%3F_Where_Are_We_Going%3F_%281897-98%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gauguin_-_Where_Do_We_Come_From%3F_What_Are_We%3F_Where_Are_We_Going%3F_%281897-98%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1897–98
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Museum of Fine Arts, Boston
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Where%20Do%20We%20Come%20From%3F%20What%20Are%20We%3F%20Where%20Are%20We%20Going%3F%20Paul%20Gauguin)

Gauguin painted this huge frieze, nearly four metres wide, in Tahiti in 1897–98, during a period of illness, poverty and grief after the death of his daughter Aline. He considered it his testament. It is meant to be read from right to left: a sleeping baby and young women at the right represent the beginning of life; a central figure picking fruit represents adult life and experience; and an old woman crouched in despair at the far left represents the approach of death. A mysterious blue idol presides in the background. In his letters, Gauguin said he attempted suicide after finishing it. It is in the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston.
