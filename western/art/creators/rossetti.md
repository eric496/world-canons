# Dante Gabriel Rossetti

**1828 – 1882** · British  
Born: London · Died: Birchington-on-Sea, England  
Movements: [Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood](../movements/pre-raphaelites.md)  

> Poet and painter, co-founder of the Pre-Raphaelites, whose later images of brooding women shaped Aestheticism and Symbolism.

## Biography

Dante Gabriel Rossetti was born in London, the son of Gabriele Rossetti, an Italian political exile and Dante scholar. His sister was the poet Christina Rossetti. In 1848 he co-founded the Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood with Millais and Hunt. He was as much a poet as a painter and also made translations from early Italian poetry. In 1860 he married his model Elizabeth Siddal, who died of a laudanum overdose in 1862. Grief-stricken, he buried a manuscript of his poems with her, and later had it recovered from her grave. From the 1860s his principal muse was Jane Morris, wife of his friend William Morris, with whom he shared Kelmscott Manor. He became dependent on chloral and died in 1882.

Rossetti's early works are medieval scenes in bright, flat colour. From the 1860s he painted mostly sensuous half-length images of women with abundant hair, full lips and strong necks, surrounded by flowers and symbolic objects, often paired with his own sonnets. These became the defining images of Aestheticism and influenced the Symbolists and Art Nouveau.

## Key artworks

### Proserpine (1874)

[![Proserpine](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/37/Dante_Gabriel_Rossetti_-_Proserpine_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dante_Gabriel_Rossetti_-_Proserpine_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dante_Gabriel_Rossetti_-_Proserpine_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1874
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Tate Britain, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Proserpine%20Dante%20Gabriel%20Rossetti)

In Roman myth, Proserpine (Persephone) was abducted by Pluto, god of the underworld. She was allowed to return to her mother, Ceres, only for part of each year, because she had eaten a few pomegranate seeds in the underworld. Rossetti shows her in a dim palace corridor, holding the bitten pomegranate, a sliver of daylight from the world above falling on the wall behind her. An incense burner and a trailing ivy branch, a symbol of clinging memory, are beside her, and Rossetti's own sonnet on the subject is inscribed in Italian on a scroll at top right. The model was Jane Morris, the wife of William Morris and Rossetti's great love, herself trapped in an unhappy marriage. Rossetti painted at least eight versions. This one, of 1874, is in Tate Britain.
