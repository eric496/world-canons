# Anthony van Dyck

**1599 – 1641** · Flemish  
Born: Antwerp · Died: London  
Movements: [Baroque](../movements/baroque.md)  

> Rubens's most gifted assistant, who became principal painter to Charles I of England. His elegant aristocratic portraits set the model for British portraiture.

## Biography

Anthony van Dyck was a prodigy in Antwerp, running his own workshop as a teenager and becoming Rubens's most gifted assistant. From 1621 to 1627 he worked in Italy, above all in Genoa, where he painted elegant full-length portraits of the city's aristocracy and studied Titian closely. After a few years back in Antwerp he moved to London in 1632 as principal painter to Charles I, who knighted him. He painted the king, Queen Henrietta Maria, their children and the English nobility until his early death in London in 1641.

Van Dyck created a new kind of aristocratic portrait: relaxed yet dignified, elegant, refined, with shimmering silks, slender hands and poetic landscape backgrounds. His images of Charles I shaped the king's legend, and his style became the standard for British portraiture for two centuries, through Lely, Kneller, Gainsborough and Reynolds. He also left a famous series of etched portraits of his fellow artists, the Iconography.

- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Musée du Louvre, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Charles%20I%20at%20the%20Hunt%20Anthony%20van%20Dyck)

In this portrait of around 1635, Van Dyck shows King Charles I not in armour or royal robes but as a gentleman out hunting. He has dismounted and stands casually with his hand on a cane, looking down at the viewer with an air of effortless superiority. A groom and a page attend his horse, which bows its head as if paying homage. The relaxed pose conceals Charles's short stature and conveys his belief in his natural, divine right to rule. The painting later entered the French royal collection and is now in the Louvre.
