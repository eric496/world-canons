## Biography

Gian Lorenzo Bernini was born in Naples, the son of the sculptor Pietro Bernini, and moved to Rome as a child. By his early twenties he had carved a series of dazzling marble groups for Cardinal Scipione Borghese: Pluto and Proserpina, Apollo and Daphne and a David. His great patron Pope Urban VIII put him in charge of St Peter's, where he created the bronze baldacchino over the high altar, the Cathedra Petri, and the vast colonnaded piazza. He also designed the Fountain of the Four Rivers in Piazza Navona, the church of Sant'Andrea al Quirinale and many papal tombs and busts. In 1665 Louis XIV invited him to Paris to design the Louvre, but his plans were not adopted.

Bernini made marble appear to breathe, soften and move: flesh dimples under fingers, hair streams in the wind, and stone becomes leaves. He fused sculpture, architecture, painting and hidden light into total theatrical experiences, and he also wrote and staged plays. More than any other artist he defined the look of Baroque Rome, and his long rivalry with the architect Francesco Borromini is one of the great stories of art history.

### Apollo and Daphne (1622–25)

[![Apollo and Daphne](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Apollo_and_Daphne_%28Bernini%29_%28cropped%29.jpg/1280px-Apollo_and_Daphne_%28Bernini%29_%28cropped%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Apollo_and_Daphne_%28Bernini%29_%28cropped%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Apollo_and_Daphne_%28Bernini%29_%28cropped%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1622–25
- **Type:** Marble sculpture
- **Where to see it:** Galleria Borghese, Rome
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Apollo%20and%20Daphne%20Gian%20Lorenzo%20Bernini)

Carved in 1622–25 for Cardinal Scipione Borghese, this life-size marble group, which remains in the Galleria Borghese, illustrates a story from Ovid's Metamorphoses. The god Apollo, struck by Cupid's arrow, chases the nymph Daphne, who prays to her father, a river god, to save her. At the very moment Apollo touches her, she turns into a laurel tree: bark encloses her legs, her toes become roots, and leaves sprout from her fingers and streaming hair. Bernini, working with his assistant Giuliano Finelli on the delicate leaves, made hard marble look like moving flesh and fluttering foliage. Cardinal Maffeo Barberini, later Pope Urban VIII, wrote a moralising Latin couplet for the base to make the pagan love story acceptable.

### Ecstasy of Saint Teresa (1647–52)

[![Ecstasy of Saint Teresa](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1a/Ecstasy_of_St._Teresa_HDR.jpg/1280px-Ecstasy_of_St._Teresa_HDR.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ecstasy_of_St._Teresa_HDR.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ecstasy_of_St._Teresa_HDR.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1647–52
- **Type:** Marble sculpture in architectural setting
- **Where to see it:** Cornaro Chapel, Santa Maria della Vittoria, Rome
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ecstasy%20of%20Saint%20Teresa%20Gian%20Lorenzo%20Bernini)

Made in 1647–52 for the Cornaro Chapel in the church of Santa Maria della Vittoria in Rome, commissioned by the Venetian cardinal Federico Cornaro, this ensemble stages a vision described by the Spanish mystic Teresa of Ávila. An angel pierced her heart with a golden spear, leaving her 'on fire with a great love of God'. Teresa swoons on a cloud, her eyes half closed and her lips parted, while the smiling angel prepares to strike again. Natural light from a hidden window above falls down gilded bronze rays. On the side walls, members of the Cornaro family sit in theatre boxes as if watching the scene. The combination of sculpture, architecture, coloured marble and light is the supreme example of Bernini's theatrical Baroque.
