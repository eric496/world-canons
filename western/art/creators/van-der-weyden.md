# Rogier van der Weyden

**c. 1399 – 1464** · Flemish  
Born: Tournai · Died: Brussels  
Movements: [Northern Renaissance](../movements/northern-renaissance.md)  

> City painter of Brussels and the most influential Netherlandish artist of his age, known for intense emotion and sculptural figures.

## Biography

Rogier de le Pasture, known in Flemish as Rogier van der Weyden, was born in Tournai. He trained there in the workshop of Robert Campin, whose robust realism he refined. By 1436 he was official painter to the city of Brussels, where he ran a large and influential workshop. He visited Italy, probably around the Jubilee of 1450, and worked for the Burgundian court, the Medici and the Este. His great polyptych of the Last Judgment still stands in the Hospices de Beaune.

Where van Eyck was the cool observer of light and texture, Rogier was the painter of emotion. His figures are elegant and sculptural, arranged in clear rhythmic compositions, and they express grief and devotion with intense, restrained feeling. His portraits of the Burgundian nobility set a model for courtly portraiture. Through his workshop and the many copies of his designs, he became the most widely imitated painter in northern Europe in the later 15th century.

## Key artworks

### The Descent from the Cross (c. 1435)

[![The Descent from the Cross](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/El_Descendimiento%2C_by_Rogier_van_der_Weyden%2C_from_Prado_in_Google_Earth.jpg/1280px-El_Descendimiento%2C_by_Rogier_van_der_Weyden%2C_from_Prado_in_Google_Earth.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Descendimiento%2C_by_Rogier_van_der_Weyden%2C_from_Prado_in_Google_Earth.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Descendimiento%2C_by_Rogier_van_der_Weyden%2C_from_Prado_in_Google_Earth.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1435
- **Type:** Oil on panel
- **Where to see it:** Museo del Prado, Madrid
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Descent%20from%20the%20Cross%20Rogier%20van%20der%20Weyden)

Painted around 1435 for the chapel of the Crossbowmen's Guild in Leuven, this panel compresses ten life-size figures into a shallow gold box, like a carved and painted altar shrine come to life. Small crossbows in the upper corners refer to the patrons. Christ's pale body is lowered from the cross, and the Virgin collapses in a faint that exactly echoes her son's pose, expressing her compassion: she suffers with him. Each mourner shows grief differently, and the tears are painted as glassy drops on their cheeks. The painting so impressed the Habsburgs that it was acquired by Mary of Hungary and later Philip II, which is how it reached the Prado.
