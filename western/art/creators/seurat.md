# Georges Seurat

**1859 – 1891** · French  
Born: Paris · Died: Paris  
Movements: [Post-Impressionism](../movements/post-impressionism.md)  

> Seurat developed Pointillism, building images from small dots of pure colour based on optical theory. He died at 31.

## Biography

Georges Seurat was born in Paris to a well-off family. He studied at the École des Beaux-Arts with Henri Lehmann, a pupil of Ingres, and absorbed scientific theories of colour by Michel-Eugène Chevreul and Ogden Rood. He also made remarkable velvety drawings in black conté crayon. When the Salon rejected his Bathers at Asnières in 1884, he helped found the Society of Independent Artists. He showed A Sunday on La Grande Jatte at the last Impressionist exhibition in 1886, where the critic Félix Fénéon coined the term Neo-Impressionism. With Paul Signac he led a group of followers. He died suddenly in 1891, aged 31, probably of an infection, leaving a secret partner, Madeleine Knobloch, and their infant son.

Seurat developed a technique, later called Pointillism or Divisionism, of placing small dots or strokes of pure colour side by side so that they would mix in the viewer's eye, aiming at greater luminosity than mixed paint. His carefully planned, almost geometric compositions and stiff, stylised figures brought order and science to Impressionism's spontaneity, and they influenced Fauvism, Futurism and abstract art.

## Key artworks

### A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte (1884–86)

[![A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/A_Sunday_on_La_Grande_Jatte%2C_Georges_Seurat%2C_1884.jpg/1280px-A_Sunday_on_La_Grande_Jatte%2C_Georges_Seurat%2C_1884.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:A_Sunday_on_La_Grande_Jatte%2C_Georges_Seurat%2C_1884.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:A_Sunday_on_La_Grande_Jatte%2C_Georges_Seurat%2C_1884.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1884–86
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Art Institute of Chicago
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Sunday%20Afternoon%20on%20the%20Island%20of%20La%20Grande%20Jatte%20Georges%20Seurat)

Seurat worked on this huge canvas, about 2 by 3 metres, from 1884 to 1886, making some 60 drawings and oil studies on the spot. It shows Parisians of different classes relaxing on La Grande Jatte, an island in the Seine on the outskirts of Paris: strolling couples, a woman with a monkey on a leash, a girl skipping, soldiers, boaters and a man playing a horn. Seurat painted it with tiny dots and dashes of contrasting colour, according to scientific colour theories, and later added a painted border of dots. The stiff, statue-like figures give the scene a strange, frozen solemnity. It caused a sensation at the last Impressionist exhibition of 1886, and it is in the Art Institute of Chicago. It inspired Stephen Sondheim's musical Sunday in the Park with George (1984).
