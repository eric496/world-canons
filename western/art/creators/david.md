## Biography

Jacques-Louis David was born in Paris in 1748. He trained with Joseph-Marie Vien and, after several failed attempts, won the Prix de Rome in 1774. In Rome (1775–80) he immersed himself in ancient sculpture and Poussin, and his Oath of the Horatii caused a sensation at the Paris Salon of 1785. During the Revolution he became a radical Jacobin deputy to the National Convention, voted for the execution of Louis XVI and staged great public festivals. After Robespierre's fall in 1794 he was imprisoned for a time. He then became the official painter of Napoleon, recording his coronation in a vast canvas (1805–07). After the restoration of the Bourbons he was exiled as a regicide to Brussels, where he died in 1825.

David was the leader of Neoclassicism. His paintings use clear, sculptural drawing, severe compositions, restrained colour and subjects of civic virtue and self-sacrifice drawn from Greek and Roman history. He also painted penetrating portraits. His studio trained a whole generation, including Gros, Girodet, Gérard and Ingres, and his work shows how art could serve politics and propaganda.

### Oath of the Horatii (1784)

[![Oath of the Horatii](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bd/Le_Serment_des_Horaces_-_Jacques-Louis_David_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Peintures_INV_3692_%3B_MR_1432.jpg/1280px-Le_Serment_des_Horaces_-_Jacques-Louis_David_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Peintures_INV_3692_%3B_MR_1432.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Le_Serment_des_Horaces_-_Jacques-Louis_David_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Peintures_INV_3692_%3B_MR_1432.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Le_Serment_des_Horaces_-_Jacques-Louis_David_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Peintures_INV_3692_%3B_MR_1432.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1784
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Musée du Louvre, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Oath%20of%20the%20Horatii%20Jacques-Louis%20David)

Commissioned by the French royal government and painted in Rome in 1784, this canvas illustrates a legend from early Roman history. Rome and Alba Longa decided to settle a war by a fight between three brothers from each side: the Horatii and the Curiatii. In a stark hall with three Doric arches, the three Horatii brothers stretch out their arms to swear an oath on the swords their father holds up, pledging to fight to the death for Rome. At the right, the women of the family collapse in grief. One sister is engaged to one of the Curiatii, and another woman is a Curiatius married to a Horatius. The contrast between the rigid, straight lines of the men and the soft curves of the women dramatises the conflict between duty to the state and personal feeling. Shown at the 1785 Salon, it became the manifesto of Neoclassicism. It is in the Louvre.

### The Death of Marat (1793)

[![The Death of Marat](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Death_of_Marat_by_David.jpg/1280px-Death_of_Marat_by_David.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Death_of_Marat_by_David.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Death_of_Marat_by_David.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1793
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Royal Museums of Fine Arts of Belgium, Brussels
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Death%20of%20Marat%20Jacques-Louis%20David)

On 13 July 1793 the radical revolutionary journalist Jean-Paul Marat was stabbed to death in his bath, where he spent hours soaking to soothe a skin disease, by Charlotte Corday, a supporter of the rival Girondin faction. His friend David painted him within months as a martyr of the Revolution. Marat slumps in the bath, his arm hanging down like Christ's in a Deposition, with a quill still in his hand and Corday's deceitful letter in the other. The wooden crate beside him bears the dedication 'À Marat, David'. The upper half of the canvas is empty and dark. Stripped of almost all detail, it is a powerful piece of political propaganda and a deeply moving image. It is now in the Royal Museums of Fine Arts of Belgium in Brussels.
