# Phidias

**c. 480 BC – c. 430 BC** · Ancient Greek  
Born: Athens · Died: unknown  
Movements: [Classical Antiquity (Greece & Rome)](../movements/classical-antiquity.md)  

> The most famous sculptor of Classical Athens, who supervised the sculptural programme of the Parthenon under Pericles. His colossal gold-and-ivory statues of Athena and Zeus are lost.

## Biography

Phidias (Pheidias) was the most famous artist of Classical Athens. His friend the statesman Pericles put him in charge of the artistic programme on the Acropolis after the Persian Wars. He oversaw the sculpture of the Parthenon and made its cult statue, the Athena Parthenos, a figure about 12 metres high built of gold and ivory over a wooden core. He later made the colossal seated Zeus at Olympia, counted among the Seven Wonders of the ancient world. Excavators at Olympia found his workshop, including a cup scratched with the words 'I belong to Pheidias'.

Ancient sources say Phidias was accused of embezzling gold or of impiety, and his last years are obscure. All of his famous cult statues are lost and are known only from descriptions, coins and small copies. Even so, the Parthenon sculptures made under his direction became the standard of 'classical' beauty: calm, idealised and full of life. Through Renaissance and Neoclassical artists, his style shaped how the West imagined Greek art.

## Key artworks

### Parthenon Sculptures (447–432 BC)

[![Parthenon Sculptures](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/23/Elgin-marbles-jan-2024.jpg/1280px-Elgin-marbles-jan-2024.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Elgin-marbles-jan-2024.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Elgin-marbles-jan-2024.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 447–432 BC
- **Type:** Marble (frieze, metopes, pediments)
- **Where to see it:** Acropolis Museum, Athens, and British Museum, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Parthenon%20Sculptures%20Phidias)

The Parthenon, built 447–432 BC as the temple of Athena, carried the most ambitious sculpture programme in Greek history, designed and supervised by Phidias. There were 92 carved metopes showing mythical battles; a continuous frieze about 160 metres long showing a procession in honour of Athena, probably the Panathenaic festival; and two pediments, showing Athena's birth and her contest with Poseidon for Athens. The figures combine idealised calm with a remarkable sense of breathing flesh under clinging drapery. Between 1801 and 1812 agents of Lord Elgin removed about half of the surviving sculpture to Britain, and it has been in the British Museum since 1816. Greece has long sought its return, and the Acropolis Museum, opened in 2009, displays its own portion with spaces left for the missing pieces.
