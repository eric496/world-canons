## Biography

Edvard Munch was born in Løten, Norway, and grew up in Kristiania (now Oslo), the son of a devout army doctor. His mother died of tuberculosis when he was five, and his favourite sister Sophie when he was 13, and illness, death and anxiety haunted his work. He joined the bohemian circle of Kristiania, studied in Paris, and in 1892 an exhibition of his work in Berlin was closed after a scandal, which made him famous in Germany. In the 1890s he developed his cycle of paintings The Frieze of Life, on love, anxiety and death. After a breakdown in 1908 and treatment in a Copenhagen clinic, his art became brighter. He painted murals for the Oslo University aula and lived at Ekely outside Oslo. The Nazis declared his work 'degenerate'. He died in January 1944 and left his remaining works to the city of Oslo, now in the Munch Museum.

Munch used simplified, flowing forms, symbolic colour and expressive distortion to depict inner psychological states. He was also a hugely innovative printmaker in woodcut and lithography. A crucial bridge between Symbolism and Expressionism, he is Norway's most famous artist.

## Key artworks

### The Scream (1893)

[![The Scream](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Edvard_Munch%2C_1893%2C_The_Scream%2C_oil%2C_tempera_and_pastel_on_cardboard%2C_91_x_73_cm%2C_National_Gallery_of_Norway.jpg/1280px-Edvard_Munch%2C_1893%2C_The_Scream%2C_oil%2C_tempera_and_pastel_on_cardboard%2C_91_x_73_cm%2C_National_Gallery_of_Norway.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edvard_Munch%2C_1893%2C_The_Scream%2C_oil%2C_tempera_and_pastel_on_cardboard%2C_91_x_73_cm%2C_National_Gallery_of_Norway.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edvard_Munch%2C_1893%2C_The_Scream%2C_oil%2C_tempera_and_pastel_on_cardboard%2C_91_x_73_cm%2C_National_Gallery_of_Norway.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1893
- **Type:** Tempera and crayon on cardboard
- **Where to see it:** National Museum, Oslo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Scream%20Edvard%20Munch)

Munch described an experience while walking at sunset along a road above the Oslo fjord: the sky turned blood-red, and he felt what he called an infinite scream passing through nature. In The Scream, a skull-like figure on a bridge clasps its face and seems to scream, or to hear a scream, while two figures walk away behind it. The blood-red sky and the blue fjord swirl in rhythmic lines. Munch made several versions: this painting in tempera and crayon on cardboard of 1893, in the National Museum in Oslo; a crayon drawing and a later painting in the Munch Museum; a pastel of 1895, sold in 2012 for nearly $120 million; and a lithograph. Research in 2021 confirmed that a pencil note on this version saying it could only have been painted by a madman was written by Munch himself. Two versions have been stolen and recovered, in 1994 and 2004. It has become a universal image of modern anxiety.
