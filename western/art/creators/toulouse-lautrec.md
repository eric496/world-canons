# Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec

**1864 – 1901** · French  
Born: Albi, France · Died: Château Malromé, France  
Movements: [Post-Impressionism](../movements/post-impressionism.md)  

> The chronicler of Montmartre's cabarets, dance halls and brothels, whose bold posters transformed graphic design.

## Biography

Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec was born in Albi into an ancient aristocratic family. His parents were first cousins, and he probably had a congenital bone disorder. After breaking both thigh bones in his early teens, his legs stopped growing, and he reached an adult height of about 1.5 metres. He studied painting in Paris with Léon Bonnat and Fernand Cormon, where he met Van Gogh and Émile Bernard, and he settled in Montmartre. His poster Moulin Rouge – La Goulue (1891) made him famous overnight. He spent his nights in cabarets, dance halls, circuses and brothels. Alcoholism ruined his health, and after a stay in a sanatorium in 1899 he died in 1901, aged 36, at his mother's château.

Toulouse-Lautrec captured the performers and customers of Montmartre's nightlife, including Jane Avril, Aristide Bruant, Yvette Guilbert and La Goulue, with sharp, economical line, bold colour and a sympathetic but unflinching eye. His lithographic posters, influenced by Japanese prints and Degas, revolutionised graphic design and advertising. The Musée Toulouse-Lautrec in Albi holds the world's largest collection of his work.

## Key artworks

### At the Moulin Rouge (1892–95)

[![At the Moulin Rouge](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e4/Henri_de_Toulouse-Lautrec%2C_At_the_Moulin_Rouge.jpg/1280px-Henri_de_Toulouse-Lautrec%2C_At_the_Moulin_Rouge.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Henri_de_Toulouse-Lautrec%2C_At_the_Moulin_Rouge.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Henri_de_Toulouse-Lautrec%2C_At_the_Moulin_Rouge.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1892–95
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Art Institute of Chicago
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=At%20the%20Moulin%20Rouge%20Henri%20de%20Toulouse-Lautrec)

Painted in 1892–95, this painting in the Art Institute of Chicago shows a group of regulars at the Moulin Rouge dance hall in Montmartre. A group of men and women sit around a table drinking. In the background the dancer La Goulue arranges her hair in front of a mirror, and Toulouse-Lautrec himself walks past with his much taller cousin, Gabriel Tapié de Céleyran. At the right edge, a woman's face is cut off by the frame and lit in a ghostly green by the gaslight. The strange angle and lighting give the scene an unsettling, almost sinister mood.
