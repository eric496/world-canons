# Georgia O'Keeffe

**1887 – 1986** · American  
Born: Sun Prairie, Wisconsin · Died: Santa Fe, New Mexico  
Movements: [American Modernism](../movements/american-modernism.md)  

> A key figure of American modernism, known for magnified flowers, New York skyscrapers and New Mexico landscapes.

## Biography

Georgia O'Keeffe was born on a farm near Sun Prairie, Wisconsin. She studied at the Art Institute of Chicago and the Art Students League in New York, and she worked as an art teacher in Texas and elsewhere. The ideas of Arthur Wesley Dow led her in 1915 to a series of radical charcoal abstractions, which the photographer and gallery owner Alfred Stieglitz exhibited in 1916. They married in 1924, and Stieglitz photographed her in hundreds of portraits. In the 1920s she painted her famous magnified flowers and New York skyscrapers. From 1929 she spent much of each year in New Mexico, moving there permanently after Stieglitz's death in 1946 and living at Ghost Ranch and Abiquiú. Macular degeneration largely ended her painting in the 1970s. She died in Santa Fe in 1986, aged 98.

O'Keeffe is one of the founding figures of American modernism. Her close-up flowers, bleached animal bones and desert landscapes are painted with smooth surfaces, bold colour and a strong sense of abstraction. She firmly rejected the Freudian interpretations critics imposed on her flowers. The Georgia O'Keeffe Museum in Santa Fe opened in 1997.

## Key artworks

### Jimson Weed/White Flower No. 1 (1932)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Crystal Bridges Museum →](https://crystalbridges.org/blog/five-american-artworks-you-should-see-during-earth-month)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1932
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art, Bentonville
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Jimson%20Weed/White%20Flower%20No.%201%20Georgia%20O%27Keeffe)

O'Keeffe painted this canvas in 1932. It shows a single white jimson weed (datura) blossom, a flower that grows around her home in New Mexico and opens at dusk. She enlarged the flower to fill the canvas, so that its petals become an almost abstract form against a soft blue-grey and green background. She said she painted flowers big so that busy people would take time to see them. In 2014 it sold at auction for $44.4 million, a record for a work by a woman artist at the time, and it went to the Crystal Bridges Museum of American Art in Bentonville, Arkansas.
