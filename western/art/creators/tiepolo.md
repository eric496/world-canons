## Biography

Giovanni Battista Tiepolo was born in Venice and married Cecilia Guardi, sister of the view painters Francesco and Gianantonio Guardi. He became the most sought-after decorative painter in Europe, covering ceilings and walls with frescoes in Venetian palaces and churches, in the Villa Pisani at Stra and in northern Italy. In 1750–53 he worked in Würzburg for the Prince-Bishop. In 1762 King Charles III summoned him to Madrid to paint ceilings in the new Royal Palace, and he died there in 1770. His sons Giandomenico and Lorenzo were his assistants.

Tiepolo's frescoes open ceilings into dazzling skies filled with gods, allegories and exotic figures, foreshortened from below, in luminous pastel colour and with tremendous speed and confidence. He also made witty etchings and brilliant oil sketches. By the end of his life, Neoclassical taste was turning against his flamboyant style, but he is now seen as the last great master of the Italian grand decorative tradition.

## Key artworks

### Würzburg Residence staircase ceiling (1752–53)

[![Würzburg Residence staircase ceiling](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9b/Tiepolo%2C_Giovanni_-_Deckenfresko_im_Treppenhaus_der_W%C3%BCrzburger_Residenz_-_Detail_Amerika.jpg/1280px-Tiepolo%2C_Giovanni_-_Deckenfresko_im_Treppenhaus_der_W%C3%BCrzburger_Residenz_-_Detail_Amerika.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tiepolo%2C_Giovanni_-_Deckenfresko_im_Treppenhaus_der_W%C3%BCrzburger_Residenz_-_Detail_Amerika.jpg)

<sub>Detail: the allegory of America. Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tiepolo%2C_Giovanni_-_Deckenfresko_im_Treppenhaus_der_W%C3%BCrzburger_Residenz_-_Detail_Amerika.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1752–53
- **Type:** Fresco
- **Where to see it:** Würzburg Residence, Germany
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=W%C3%BCrzburg%20Residence%20staircase%20ceiling%20Giovanni%20Battista%20Tiepolo)

Between 1752 and 1753, Tiepolo painted the vault over the grand staircase of the Würzburg Residence, the palace of the Prince-Bishop Karl Philipp von Greiffenclau. The vault was designed by the architect Balthasar Neumann and is one of the largest ceiling frescoes ever painted, around 600 square metres. As visitors climb the stairs, the ceiling unfolds: at the centre, the sun god Apollo rises in a radiant sky, and around the cornice are allegories of the four known continents, Europe, Asia, Africa and America, each crowded with people, animals and exotic costumes. In the Europe section Tiepolo included portraits of the prince-bishop, of Neumann sitting on a cannon with his dog, and of himself and his son Giandomenico. The vault survived the bombing of Würzburg in 1945.
