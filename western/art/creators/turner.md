## Biography

Joseph Mallord William Turner was born in Covent Garden, London, the son of a barber. He entered the Royal Academy Schools at 14, exhibited there at 15, and was elected a full Academician in 1802, aged only 26. He travelled tirelessly through Britain and Europe, the Alps, the Rhine and above all Venice, filling hundreds of sketchbooks, and was also a master of watercolour and an important printmaker. Eccentric and secretive, he lived his last years in Chelsea under the name 'Mr Booth'. He died in 1851 and left the nation his studio contents: around 300 oil paintings and some 30,000 works on paper, now mostly at Tate Britain.

Turner began in the tradition of Claude Lorrain and the Dutch marine painters, but he increasingly dissolved landscape into light, colour, storm, fire, steam and atmosphere. His late works were mocked as incomprehensible by some critics and championed by the young John Ruskin. Turner is seen as a forerunner of Impressionism and even abstraction. Britain's leading contemporary art prize is named after him, and his self-portrait and The Fighting Temeraire appear on the £20 note.

### The Fighting Temeraire (1839)

[![The Fighting Temeraire](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/The_Fighting_Temeraire%2C_JMW_Turner%2C_National_Gallery.jpg/1280px-The_Fighting_Temeraire%2C_JMW_Turner%2C_National_Gallery.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Fighting_Temeraire%2C_JMW_Turner%2C_National_Gallery.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Fighting_Temeraire%2C_JMW_Turner%2C_National_Gallery.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1839
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** National Gallery, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Fighting%20Temeraire%20J.%20M.%20W.%20Turner)

HMS Temeraire was a 98-gun warship that fought heroically at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805. In 1838 the decommissioned ship was towed by a steam tug up the Thames to Rotherhithe to be broken up. Turner exhibited his painting of the scene in 1839. The ghostly old ship glides upriver behind a squat, black, smoke-belching tug, while the sun sets in a blaze of red and gold on the right. It is a poetic elegy for the age of sail and of Britain's naval glory, giving way to the age of steam. Turner called it his 'darling' and refused to sell it. It came to the National Gallery with the Turner Bequest and was voted Britain's greatest painting in a 2005 BBC poll.

### The Slave Ship (1840)

[![The Slave Ship](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/26/Slave-ship.jpg/1280px-Slave-ship.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Slave-ship.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Slave-ship.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1840
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Museum of Fine Arts, Boston
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Slave%20Ship%20J.%20M.%20W.%20Turner)

Turner exhibited this painting in 1840 with the title Slavers Throwing Overboard the Dead and Dying – Typhoon Coming On. It was inspired by accounts of the Zong massacre of 1781, when the crew of a British slave ship threw more than 130 enslaved Africans overboard to claim insurance. In the foreground, shackled legs and arms sink into a sea of blood-red and gold, surrounded by fish and gulls. The ship sails away into a furious storm and sunset. It was shown at a time of continuing campaigns against slavery worldwide. The critic John Ruskin owned it for many years. It is now in the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston.

### Rain, Steam and Speed – The Great Western Railway (1844)

[![Rain, Steam and Speed – The Great Western Railway](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/35/Rain_Steam_and_Speed_the_Great_Western_Railway.jpg/1280px-Rain_Steam_and_Speed_the_Great_Western_Railway.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rain_Steam_and_Speed_the_Great_Western_Railway.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rain_Steam_and_Speed_the_Great_Western_Railway.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1844
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** National Gallery, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Rain%2C%20Steam%20and%20Speed%20%E2%80%93%20The%20Great%20Western%20Railway%20J.%20M.%20W.%20Turner)

Exhibited in 1844, this painting shows a steam train on Isambard Kingdom Brunel's Maidenhead Railway Bridge over the Thames, rushing towards the viewer through rain and mist. The engine's black funnel and the bridge are almost the only solid forms in a swirling haze of golden and grey light. A hare runs on the tracks in front of the train, perhaps a symbol of natural speed outpaced by the machine. A ploughman works in a field and boaters drift on the river below. Turner captured modern technology and the sensation of speed in an almost abstract way. It is in the National Gallery, London.
