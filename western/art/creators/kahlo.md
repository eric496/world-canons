# Frida Kahlo

**1907 – 1954** · Mexican  
Born: Coyoacán, Mexico City · Died: Coyoacán  
Movements: [Dada & Surrealism](../movements/dada-surrealism.md)  

> Kahlo's intense self-portraits draw on Mexican folk art, her physical suffering and her identity. The Surrealists claimed her, though she rejected the label.

## Biography

Frida Kahlo was born in Coyoacán, Mexico City, in the 'Blue House' (Casa Azul), the daughter of a German-born photographer and a Mexican mother of mixed Indigenous and Spanish descent. She contracted polio at six, and at 18 she was gravely injured in a bus accident that broke her spine and pelvis and left her in pain for the rest of her life. She began painting while confined to bed, using a mirror fixed above her. In 1929 she married the muralist Diego Rivera. They divorced in 1939 and remarried in 1940. She lived in the United States in the early 1930s, and in 1939 the Louvre bought her painting The Frame. She taught at the La Esmeralda art school, and her first solo exhibition in Mexico, in 1953, she attended in her bed. She died in 1954, aged 47.

Kahlo painted around 200 works, about a third of them self-portraits, combining Mexican folk art, ex-voto paintings and pre-Columbian imagery with her own experiences of pain, love, the body, miscarriage and identity. André Breton claimed her as a Surrealist, but she said she painted her own reality, not dreams. Since the 1980s she has become a global icon of Mexican identity and feminism. Her house is a museum, and in Mexico her works are protected as national artistic monuments.

## Key artworks

### The Two Fridas (1939)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Museo de Arte Moderno, Mexico City →](https://mam.inba.gob.mx/destacadas.html)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1939
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Museo de Arte Moderno, Mexico City
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Two%20Fridas%20Frida%20Kahlo)

Kahlo painted this large double self-portrait, about 1.7 metres square, in 1939, around the time of her divorce from Diego Rivera. Two Fridas sit side by side holding hands under a stormy sky. At the right, the Frida in a Tehuana dress, which Rivera loved, holds a tiny portrait of him. At the left, the Frida in a white European Victorian dress tries to stop the bleeding from a severed artery with surgical forceps, and blood drips onto her skirt. Their exposed hearts are connected by a single vein. The painting expresses her divided identity, Mexican and European, loved and abandoned. It was shown at the International Surrealist Exhibition in Mexico City in 1940 and is in the Museo de Arte Moderno in Mexico City.
