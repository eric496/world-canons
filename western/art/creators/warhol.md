# Andy Warhol

**1928 – 1987** · American  
Born: Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania · Died: New York  
Movements: [Pop Art](../movements/pop-art.md)  

> The leading figure of Pop Art, Warhol used silkscreen to mass-produce images of consumer goods and celebrities from his studio, the Factory.

## Biography

Andy Warhol was born Andrew Warhola in Pittsburgh, the son of Carpatho-Rusyn immigrants from present-day Slovakia. As a sickly child he spent long periods in bed drawing and collecting pictures of film stars. He studied commercial art at the Carnegie Institute of Technology and moved to New York in 1949, where he became a highly successful illustrator, especially of shoe advertisements. In 1962 his exhibition of Campbell's Soup Can paintings in Los Angeles and his Marilyn Monroe and Coca-Cola silkscreens made him the leading figure of Pop Art. His studio, the Factory, became a celebrated hub of artists, musicians, actors and hangers-on. He made experimental films, managed the rock band The Velvet Underground and founded Interview magazine. In 1968 he was shot and severely wounded by the writer Valerie Solanas. In the 1980s he collaborated with Jean-Michel Basquiat. He died in 1987 after routine gallbladder surgery.

Warhol erased the boundary between fine art, commerce and celebrity. He used commercial silkscreen printing and assistants to mass-produce images of consumer products, film stars, disasters and political figures, exploring fame, repetition, death and the media. He famously predicted that in the future everyone would be world-famous for 15 minutes. The Andy Warhol Museum in Pittsburgh opened in 1994.

### Campbell's Soup Cans (1962)

> 🖼️ **[View it at MoMA →](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/79809)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1962
- **Type:** Synthetic polymer paint on 32 canvases
- **Where to see it:** Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Campbell%27s%20Soup%20Cans%20Andy%20Warhol)

In July 1962 Warhol's first solo exhibition of paintings, at the Ferus Gallery in Los Angeles, showed 32 canvases, one for each variety of soup then sold by Campbell's. The paintings were displayed side by side on a narrow shelf, like products in a supermarket. Each was painted by hand with the help of projection and a stamp for the fleur-de-lis pattern, but looks mass-produced, and only the label changes. The gallery owner Irving Blum bought the whole set for $1,000, paid in instalments. The paintings provoked debate about whether consumer goods could be the subject of fine art, and they made Warhol famous. The Museum of Modern Art acquired them in 1996.

### Marilyn Diptych (1962)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Tate →](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/warhol-marilyn-diptych-t03093)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1962
- **Type:** Acrylic and silkscreen on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Tate Modern, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Marilyn%20Diptych%20Andy%20Warhol)

Marilyn Monroe died in August 1962. Within weeks, Warhol began silkscreening a publicity photograph of her from the 1953 film Niagara onto canvas. In the Marilyn Diptych, 50 images of her face are arranged in a grid across two panels. On the left, 25 images are printed in garish colours of yellow hair, pink skin and turquoise eye shadow. On the right, 25 are printed in black and white, increasingly smeared or faded, as if fading away. The work comments on celebrity, mass media, repetition and death. It was bought by the collectors Burton and Emily Tremaine, and Tate acquired it in 1980.
