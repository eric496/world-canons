## Biography

William Hogarth was born in London and trained as an engraver of silver before taking up painting. He eloped with the daughter of the court painter Sir James Thornhill. His first great success, the series A Harlot's Progress (1732), was sold to the public as engravings, and its popularity led to pirated copies. Hogarth campaigned for the Engraving Copyright Act of 1735, known as 'Hogarth's Act', which protected artists' rights in their prints. He was a founding governor of the Foundling Hospital, created famous prints such as Gin Lane and Beer Street (1751), and wrote a treatise, The Analysis of Beauty (1753), arguing for the S-shaped 'line of beauty'.

Hogarth called his narrative series 'modern moral subjects'. They are stories told in sequences of pictures, read like a novel or play, satirising greed, vanity, corruption, cruelty and fashionable folly in every class of London society. He was also a lively portraitist and a fierce promoter of a native British school of painting against fashionable foreign imports. His work is a forerunner of the political cartoon and the comic strip.

## Key artworks

### Marriage A-la-Mode (c. 1743)

[![Marriage A-la-Mode](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/Marriage_A-la-Mode_1%2C_The_Marriage_Settlement_-_William_Hogarth.jpg/1280px-Marriage_A-la-Mode_1%2C_The_Marriage_Settlement_-_William_Hogarth.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Marriage_A-la-Mode_1%2C_The_Marriage_Settlement_-_William_Hogarth.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Marriage_A-la-Mode_1%2C_The_Marriage_Settlement_-_William_Hogarth.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1743
- **Type:** Oil on canvas (series of six)
- **Where to see it:** National Gallery, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Marriage%20A-la-Mode%20William%20Hogarth)

This series of six paintings of around 1743, in the National Gallery, London, was engraved and sold to the public in 1745. It tells the story of an arranged marriage between the son of the bankrupt, gout-ridden Earl Squanderfield and the daughter of a rich, miserly city merchant, a deal trading money for rank. The couple are bored and unfaithful from the start. The young viscount catches syphilis, and his wife takes the lawyer Silvertongue as her lover. In the end, the husband is killed by the lawyer in a duel, the lawyer is hanged, and the countess poisons herself while her father pulls the ring from her finger. Every scene is crammed with satirical detail about fashion, art collecting, quack medicine and moral decay.
