> Trained as an icon painter in Crete, then in Venice and Rome, El Greco settled in Toledo. His elongated, flickering figures and visionary colour were rediscovered by early modernists.

<sub>Portrait: Portrait of a Man, c. 1595–1600, probably a self-portrait · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Greco_-_Portrait_of_a_Man_-_WGA10554.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Domenikos Theotokopoulos was born in Candia on Crete, then ruled by Venice, and was already a master painter of icons there. Around 1567 he moved to Venice, where he absorbed Titian and Tintoretto, and then to Rome. In 1577 he went to Spain, hoping for the patronage of Philip II at the Escorial, but the king disliked his work. He settled in Toledo, where he lived until his death in 1614, painting altarpieces, saints and portraits for the city's churches and intellectual elite. He always signed his works in Greek.

El Greco fused Byzantine spirituality, Venetian colour and Mannerist elongation into one of the most personal styles in art. His figures are flame-like and stretched, set in flickering light and unearthly colours. After his death he was largely dismissed as eccentric. He was rediscovered in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when artists such as Cézanne, Picasso and the German Expressionists saw him as a forerunner of modern art.

### The Burial of the Count of Orgaz (1586–88)

[![The Burial of the Count of Orgaz](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/ff/El_Greco_-_The_Burial_of_the_Count_of_Orgaz.JPG/1280px-El_Greco_-_The_Burial_of_the_Count_of_Orgaz.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Greco_-_The_Burial_of_the_Count_of_Orgaz.JPG)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Greco_-_The_Burial_of_the_Count_of_Orgaz.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1586–88
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Church of Santo Tomé, Toledo
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Burial%20of%20the%20Count%20of%20Orgaz%20El%20Greco%20%28Domenikos%20Theotokopoulos%29)

Commissioned in 1586 for the parish church of Santo Tomé in Toledo, where it still hangs, this painting illustrates a local legend. At the burial of the pious nobleman Don Gonzalo Ruiz, lord of Orgaz, in 1323, St Stephen and St Augustine came down from heaven to lay him in his tomb with their own hands. In the lower half, the saints in gold vestments lower the armoured body before a row of solemn Toledan gentlemen dressed in the black of the 1580s, many of them probably real portraits. A boy at left points to the scene; he is thought to be El Greco's son, Jorge Manuel, and the handkerchief in his pocket bears the year of his birth, 1578. Above, the count's soul is carried up to Christ, the Virgin and the saints in a swirling, luminous heaven.

### View of Toledo (c. 1596–1600)

[![View of Toledo](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/02/El_Greco_View_of_Toledo.jpg/1280px-El_Greco_View_of_Toledo.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Greco_View_of_Toledo.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:El_Greco_View_of_Toledo.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1596–1600
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=View%20of%20Toledo%20El%20Greco%20%28Domenikos%20Theotokopoulos%29)

One of only two surviving landscapes by El Greco, this painting of around 1596–1600 shows his adopted city under a stormy sky of churning blue-black clouds. Toledo spreads over its hill above the River Tagus, its buildings glowing an eerie silver-green. El Greco took liberties: he moved the cathedral bell tower to sit beside the Alcázar for dramatic effect. The scene is less a topographical record than a spiritual vision of the city, and its expressive distortion and colour made it an icon for modern painters. It is in the Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York.
