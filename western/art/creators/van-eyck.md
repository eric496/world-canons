Born: probably Maaseik, Low Countries · Died: Bruges  
Movements: [Northern Renaissance](../movements/northern-renaissance.md)  

> Court painter to the Duke of Burgundy, van Eyck brought oil painting to an unmatched level of luminous detail. His portraits and altarpieces fuse close observation with layered religious symbolism.

## Biography

Jan van Eyck is first recorded working for John of Bavaria in The Hague. From 1425 he was court painter and valet de chambre to Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy, one of the richest rulers in Europe. Philip valued him highly enough to send him on confidential diplomatic missions, including a voyage to Portugal in 1428–29 to paint the portrait of the duke's prospective bride, Isabella. He settled in Bruges, where he died in 1441. He sometimes signed with the punning motto 'Als ich can' ('As I can').

Van Eyck did not invent oil paint, as later legend claimed, but he perfected it. By building up thin, translucent glazes he achieved glowing colour and an unprecedented level of detail: individual hairs, reflections in metal and pearls, the texture of fur and brocade. His portraits are among the first independent portraits in Western art, and his religious works pack everyday objects with symbolic meaning. Italian collectors prized his work, and he shaped the whole tradition of Netherlandish painting.

### Ghent Altarpiece (Adoration of the Mystic Lamb) (completed 1432)

[![Ghent Altarpiece (Adoration of the Mystic Lamb)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/50/Ghent_Altarpiece_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Ghent_Altarpiece_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ghent_Altarpiece_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ghent_Altarpiece_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** completed 1432
- **Type:** Oil on panel (polyptych)
- **Where to see it:** St Bavo's Cathedral, Ghent
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ghent%20Altarpiece%20%28Adoration%20of%20the%20Mystic%20Lamb%29%20Jan%20van%20Eyck)

This huge folding altarpiece was commissioned by the Ghent merchant Joos Vijd and his wife Elisabeth Borluut for their chapel in what is now St Bavo's Cathedral. An inscription says it was begun by Hubert van Eyck and completed by his brother Jan in 1432. When open, the upper register shows God enthroned between the Virgin and John the Baptist, flanked by singing angels and the strikingly realistic nude figures of Adam and Eve. Below, crowds of saints, prophets and martyrs converge on a meadow to adore the Lamb of God on an altar. It has been called the most stolen artwork in history: looted by Napoleon's troops, sold off in parts, and hidden by the Nazis in an Austrian salt mine. One panel, the Just Judges, was stolen in 1934 and has never been recovered. A restoration begun in 2012 removed later overpaint and revealed the Lamb's startlingly human original face.

### Arnolfini Portrait (1434)

[![Arnolfini Portrait](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/The_Arnolfini_portrait_%281434%29.jpg/1280px-The_Arnolfini_portrait_%281434%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Arnolfini_portrait_%281434%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Arnolfini_portrait_%281434%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1434
- **Type:** Oil on oak panel
- **Where to see it:** National Gallery, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Arnolfini%20Portrait%20Jan%20van%20Eyck)

Dated 1434, this small oak panel shows a wealthy Italian merchant, usually identified as Giovanni di Nicolao Arnolfini, and a woman, standing in a room of their house in Bruges. Every detail is rendered with astonishing precision: the dog, the oranges on the sill, the brass chandelier with a single lit candle, the discarded clogs. A convex mirror on the back wall reflects the couple from behind and two more figures in the doorway. Above it Van Eyck wrote in elaborate script, 'Johannes de eyck fuit hic 1434' ('Jan van Eyck was here'). It was long read as a wedding contract with the painter as witness. Scholars now debate whether it records a betrothal, celebrates a marriage, or even commemorates a dead wife. The National Gallery in London bought it in 1842.
