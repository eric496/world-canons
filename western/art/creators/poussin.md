## Biography

Nicolas Poussin was born in Normandy and trained in Paris. In 1624 he went to Rome, where he spent almost all the rest of his life. There he found learned patrons such as the antiquarian Cassiano dal Pozzo and painted for a circle of connoisseurs who appreciated his serious, intellectual approach. Louis XIII and Cardinal Richelieu summoned him back to Paris in 1640 as First Painter to the King, but he disliked court intrigue and the large decorative commissions, and returned to Rome in 1642.

Poussin painted mythological, historical and biblical subjects with carefully planned compositions, clear drawing, measured gesture and a stoic moral seriousness inspired by antiquity and Raphael. He believed a painting should be read like a text, with each figure's expression conveying meaning. His late landscapes are among the most profound in art. Through the French Royal Academy, where the 'Poussinistes' (championing drawing) famously argued against the 'Rubénistes' (championing colour), he became the founding father of French classicism and a model for David and Cézanne.

## Key artworks

### Et in Arcadia ego (c. 1637–38)

[![Et in Arcadia ego](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/df/Nicolas_Poussin_-_Et_in_Arcadia_ego_%28deuxi%C3%A8me_version%29.jpg/1280px-Nicolas_Poussin_-_Et_in_Arcadia_ego_%28deuxi%C3%A8me_version%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nicolas_Poussin_-_Et_in_Arcadia_ego_%28deuxi%C3%A8me_version%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nicolas_Poussin_-_Et_in_Arcadia_ego_%28deuxi%C3%A8me_version%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1637–38
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Musée du Louvre, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Et%20in%20Arcadia%20ego%20Nicolas%20Poussin)

In this painting of around 1637–38 in the Louvre, three shepherds in an idyllic Arcadian landscape have discovered a tomb. One kneels to trace with his finger the inscription 'Et in Arcadia ego' ('Even in Arcadia, I am here'), meaning that Death is present even in paradise. The others reflect quietly, and a calm, classically dressed woman at right rests her hand on a shepherd's shoulder. This is a second, more meditative version of a subject Poussin had painted more dramatically a decade earlier (now at Chatsworth). The figures are arranged like a classical relief, and the mood is serene, philosophical acceptance of mortality. Louis XIV bought it in 1685.
