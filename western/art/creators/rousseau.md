# Henri Rousseau

**1844 – 1910** · French  
Born: Laval, France · Died: Paris  
Movements: [Post-Impressionism](../movements/post-impressionism.md)  

> A self-taught customs official ('Le Douanier') whose flat, dreamlike jungles were mocked at first and then embraced by the avant-garde.

## Biography

Henri Rousseau was born in Laval in western France, the son of a tinsmith. After army service he worked for many years as a toll collector at the gates of Paris, which earned him the nickname 'Le Douanier' (the customs officer). He was entirely self-taught and began exhibiting at the Salon des Indépendants in 1886, where critics and the public mocked his work for decades. He retired in 1893 to paint full time, supporting himself by teaching and playing music. He never left France, and his famous jungles were drawn from visits to the Paris botanical gardens and zoo, illustrated magazines and his imagination. He died in Paris in 1910.

Rousseau's flat, meticulously detailed, dreamlike pictures, with lush vegetation, staring animals and naive perspective, were championed by avant-garde artists and poets. In 1908 Picasso and friends held a famous banquet in his honour at the Bateau-Lavoir studio, half in jest and half in homage. He became a hero of modernism and the best-known 'naive' painter, and he influenced the Surrealists.

## Key artworks

### The Sleeping Gypsy (1897)

[![The Sleeping Gypsy](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/06/La_Boh%C3%A9mienne_endormie.jpg/1280px-La_Boh%C3%A9mienne_endormie.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_Boh%C3%A9mienne_endormie.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_Boh%C3%A9mienne_endormie.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1897
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Sleeping%20Gypsy%20Henri%20Rousseau)

In this painting of 1897, a mandolin player in a striped robe lies asleep on a desert under a full moon, a water jug beside her. A lion approaches and sniffs at her, but seems not to attack. The scene is utterly still and mysterious, painted in Rousseau's smooth, flat style with crisp outlines and pale colours. Rousseau offered it to the town council of his home town, Laval, which turned it down. It disappeared for years, was rediscovered in 1923 and acclaimed by the Surrealists for its dreamlike quality. It is in the Museum of Modern Art, New York.
