# Kazimir Malevich

**1879 – 1935** · Ukrainian-Russian  
Born: Kyiv, Russian Empire · Died: Leningrad  
Movements: [Early Abstraction](../movements/abstraction.md)  

> The founder of Suprematism, an abstraction of pure geometric form and 'the supremacy of pure feeling'.

## Biography

Kazimir Malevich was born in Kyiv to a Polish family and grew up in rural Ukraine before moving to Moscow in 1904. He passed rapidly through Impressionism, Symbolism and a Russian 'Cubo-Futurism'. In 1913 he designed the costumes and sets for the Futurist opera Victory over the Sun, including a curtain with a black and white square. At the exhibition 0,10 in Petrograd in December 1915 he unveiled Suprematism, an art of pure geometric forms, with the Black Square as its emblem. After the Revolution he taught in Vitebsk, where he founded the UNOVIS group, and later in Petrograd. Under Stalin's regime he was attacked as a formalist and briefly arrested in 1930. In his last years he returned to stylised figure painting. He died in Leningrad in 1935, and a black square was painted on his coffin.

Malevich sought the 'supremacy of pure feeling' in art, free from the depiction of objects. His White on White (1918) took this further than anyone at the time. He was a major influence on Constructivism, the Bauhaus and later Minimalism. In recent years there has been growing emphasis on his Ukrainian roots.

## Key artworks

### Black Square (1915)

- **Date:** 1915
- **Type:** Oil on linen
- **Where to see it:** State Tretyakov Gallery, Moscow
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Black%20Square%20Kazimir%20Malevich)

Malevich first exhibited this painting, a black square on a white ground, at the Last Futurist Exhibition of Paintings 0,10 in Petrograd in December 1915. It hung high in the corner of the room, the place where an icon traditionally hangs in Russian homes. He presented it as the 'zero point' of painting, the beginning of Suprematism, an art of pure feeling free from depicting objects. The paint of the original has cracked badly. In 2015 researchers at the Tretyakov Gallery found two earlier colourful compositions underneath and a handwritten inscription referring to an old joke picture by the French humorist Alphonse Allais. Malevich painted three later versions.
