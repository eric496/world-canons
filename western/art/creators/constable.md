# John Constable

**1776 – 1837** · British  
Born: East Bergholt, Suffolk, England · Died: London  
Movements: [Romanticism](../movements/romanticism.md)  

> Constable painted the English countryside from direct observation, with fresh skies and flecks of light. He was more admired in France than in Britain during his lifetime.

## Biography

John Constable was born in East Bergholt, Suffolk, the son of a prosperous mill-owner and corn merchant whose mills at Flatford and Dedham stood on the River Stour. He entered the Royal Academy Schools in 1799 but found recognition slowly. His long courtship of Maria Bicknell was opposed by her family until they married in 1816. He spent summers in Hampstead, where he made famous cloud studies in 1821–22, which he called 'skying'. His work won a gold medal in Paris in 1824, but he was elected a full Royal Academician only in 1829, aged 52, the year after Maria's death.

Constable painted the familiar landscape of the Stour valley ('Constable Country'), Salisbury, Hampstead and Brighton with fresh observation, broken touches of colour, and flecks of white to capture glittering light, which critics called 'Constable's snow'. He made full-size oil sketches for his large 'six-footer' exhibition canvases. He was more admired in France than in England during his lifetime, and he influenced Delacroix, the Barbizon School and the Impressionists.

## Key artworks

### The Hay Wain (1821)

[![The Hay Wain](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/John_Constable_-_The_Hay_Wain_%281821%29.jpg/1280px-John_Constable_-_The_Hay_Wain_%281821%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:John_Constable_-_The_Hay_Wain_%281821%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:John_Constable_-_The_Hay_Wain_%281821%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1821
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** National Gallery, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Hay%20Wain%20John%20Constable)

Exhibited at the Royal Academy in 1821 as Landscape: Noon, this large canvas shows a horse-drawn hay cart (wain) crossing a ford in the River Stour at Flatford Mill in Suffolk. Constable knew the spot intimately since childhood. At the left stands the cottage of the farmer Willy Lott, and on the right, beyond the trees, haymakers work in sunlit meadows under a vast sky of clouds. Constable worked up the composition from a full-size oil sketch, and his flickering white highlights convey sparkling light on water and leaves. The painting failed to sell in England, but at the Paris Salon of 1824 it won a gold medal from Charles X and inspired Delacroix and other French painters. It is in the National Gallery, London.
