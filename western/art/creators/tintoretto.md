# Tintoretto

**1518 – 1594** · Italian  
Born: Venice · Died: Venice  
Movements: [Mannerism](../movements/mannerism.md)  

> A fast, dramatic Venetian painter who aimed to combine 'the drawing of Michelangelo and the colour of Titian', with dynamic diagonals and theatrical light.

## Biography

Jacopo Robusti was nicknamed Tintoretto ('the little dyer') after his father's trade. He spent almost his entire life in Venice, where his speed and energy earned him the nickname 'il Furioso'. His greatest project was the decoration of the Scuola Grande di San Rocco, a religious confraternity's meeting house, which he filled with more than fifty paintings over some 23 years. He also painted the enormous Paradise in the Doge's Palace. His daughter Marietta and son Domenico worked in his busy studio.

Tintoretto reportedly aimed to combine 'the drawing of Michelangelo and the colour of Titian'. His paintings are full of drama, with plunging diagonals, sudden shifts of scale, theatrical lighting and figures in violent motion. He sometimes worked out compositions with small wax figures in model stages. His visionary religious painting influenced El Greco and anticipated the Baroque.

## Key artworks

### The Last Supper (San Giorgio Maggiore) (1592–94)

[![The Last Supper (San Giorgio Maggiore)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/Jacopo_Tintoretto_-_The_Last_Supper_-_WGA22649.jpg/1280px-Jacopo_Tintoretto_-_The_Last_Supper_-_WGA22649.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jacopo_Tintoretto_-_The_Last_Supper_-_WGA22649.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jacopo_Tintoretto_-_The_Last_Supper_-_WGA22649.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1592–94
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** San Giorgio Maggiore, Venice
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Last%20Supper%20%28San%20Giorgio%20Maggiore%29%20Tintoretto)

Tintoretto painted this canvas in 1592–94, at the end of his life, for the chancel of Palladio's church of San Giorgio Maggiore in Venice, where it still hangs. Rather than the calm frontal arrangement used by Leonardo, he set the table on a steep diagonal running into the dark depths of a tavern-like room. Christ, at the centre, gives bread to the apostles, his halo blazing. Servants busy themselves with food and dishes, and a cat pokes into a basket. Smoke from a hanging oil lamp turns into the ghostly forms of angels swirling overhead, merging the everyday and the supernatural in one dramatic image of the Eucharist.
