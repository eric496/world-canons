## Biography

Very little is known for certain about Giorgione ('Big George'), born Giorgio da Castelfranco. He probably trained with Giovanni Bellini, became famous in Venice for poetic small paintings made for private collectors, and in 1508 painted frescoes, now almost entirely lost, on the façade of the German merchants' warehouse (the Fondaco dei Tedeschi) with the young Titian. He died of plague in 1510, still in his early thirties.

Only a handful of paintings are securely attributed to him, including The Tempest, The Three Philosophers in Vienna, and the Sleeping Venus in Dresden, which Titian finished. Yet his influence was immense. He made mood, landscape and atmosphere the true subjects of painting, often leaving the narrative deliberately mysterious, and he painted directly in oil without detailed preparatory drawing. The 'Giorgionesque' style of soft light and dreamy, enigmatic pastoral reshaped Venetian art.

## Key artworks

### The Tempest (c. 1506–08)

[![The Tempest](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8a/Giorgione_-_Das_Gewitter.jpg/1280px-Giorgione_-_Das_Gewitter.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Giorgione_-_Das_Gewitter.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Giorgione_-_Das_Gewitter.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1506–08
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Gallerie dell'Accademia, Venice
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Tempest%20Giorgione)

This small painting of around 1506–08, in the Gallerie dell'Accademia in Venice, is one of the most mysterious in Western art. At the left a young man with a staff stands by broken columns. At the right a half-naked woman sits nursing a baby on the river bank and looks straight out at us. Between them, under a dark sky split by lightning, lies a town. X-rays show that Giorgione originally painted a bathing woman where the young man now stands, which suggests he invented as he went. In 1530 a Venetian collector described it simply as a 'little landscape with the storm, a gypsy and a soldier'. Interpretations range from Adam and Eve to classical myth, but its real subject may be the stormy atmosphere itself, which makes it a pioneer of landscape as mood.
