# Albrecht Dürer

**1471 – 1528** · German  
Born: Nuremberg, Holy Roman Empire · Died: Nuremberg  
Movements: [Northern Renaissance](../movements/northern-renaissance.md)  

> Painter, printmaker and theorist who carried Italian Renaissance ideas north of the Alps. His woodcuts and engravings circulated across Europe and made him the first internationally famous artist through print.

## Biography

Albrecht Dürer was the son of a Hungarian-born goldsmith in Nuremberg, a wealthy imperial city and printing centre. He trained as a goldsmith and then with the painter Michael Wolgemut, travelled as a journeyman, and made two journeys to Italy (1494–95 and 1505–07), where he absorbed Venetian colour and Italian theory. His woodcut series the Apocalypse (1498) made him famous across Europe. He later worked for Emperor Maximilian I and travelled to the Netherlands in 1520–21, leaving a vivid diary of the journey.

Dürer was painter, draughtsman, engraver, theorist and a shrewd businessman. He published his own prints and protected his famous 'AD' monogram, and late in life he wrote treatises on measurement, fortification and human proportion. He made self-portraiting a serious art and raised printmaking to the level of painting. He was the first northern artist to absorb the Italian Renaissance fully, and he became the model of the artist as intellectual.

### Self-Portrait at Twenty-Eight (1500)

[![Self-Portrait at Twenty-Eight](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/D%C3%BCrer_Alte_Pinakothek.jpg/1280px-D%C3%BCrer_Alte_Pinakothek.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:D%C3%BCrer_Alte_Pinakothek.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:D%C3%BCrer_Alte_Pinakothek.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1500
- **Type:** Oil on panel
- **Where to see it:** Alte Pinakothek, Munich
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Self-Portrait%20at%20Twenty-Eight%20Albrecht%20D%C3%BCrer)

In 1500, a year many expected to bring the end of the world, Dürer painted himself in strict frontal view. Such symmetry was normally reserved for images of Christ. His long curled hair frames his face, and his hand touches the fur collar of his coat in a gesture recalling a blessing. The Latin inscription reads: 'I, Albrecht Dürer of Nuremberg, painted myself thus, with undying colours, at the age of 28 years.' The painting is not blasphemous. It asserts that the artist's creative gift comes from God and gives the painter a dignity once reserved for scholars and princes. It is in the Alte Pinakothek, Munich.

### Young Hare (1502)

[![Young Hare](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_-_Hare%2C_1502_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_-_Hare%2C_1502_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_-_Hare%2C_1502_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_-_Hare%2C_1502_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1502
- **Type:** Watercolour and gouache
- **Where to see it:** Albertina, Vienna
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Young%20Hare%20Albrecht%20D%C3%BCrer)

This watercolour and gouache study of 1502, in the Albertina in Vienna, is one of the most famous nature studies ever made. The hare crouches, ears alert, and every hair is built up with fine strokes of brush, from the soft underbelly to the stiff whiskers. Look closely and you can see a window reflected in the animal's eye, suggesting it was studied indoors. Dürer probably worked from a live or dead hare together with sketches, combining close observation with studio skill. Studies like this fed into the animals in his prints and paintings.

### Melencolia I (1514)

[![Melencolia I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7a/Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_-_Melencolia_I_-_Google_Art_Project_%28_AGDdr3EHmNGyA%29.jpg/1280px-Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_-_Melencolia_I_-_Google_Art_Project_%28_AGDdr3EHmNGyA%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_-_Melencolia_I_-_Google_Art_Project_%28_AGDdr3EHmNGyA%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Albrecht_D%C3%BCrer_-_Melencolia_I_-_Google_Art_Project_%28_AGDdr3EHmNGyA%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1514
- **Type:** Engraving
- **Where to see it:** Impressions in many collections
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Melencolia%20I%20Albrecht%20D%C3%BCrer)

One of Dürer's three 'Master Engravings' of 1513–14, Melencolia I shows a brooding winged figure seated amid scattered tools: a compass, plane, saw, nails, a large polyhedron, a sphere, an hourglass, scales, a bell and a ladder. A starved dog sleeps at her feet and a comet blazes over the sea. On the wall is a 'magic square' whose rows, columns and diagonals all add up to 34, with the date 1514 in the bottom row. The print draws on Renaissance ideas that melancholy, one of the four temperaments, was the condition of creative genius and also of paralysing doubt. It has been interpreted endlessly and is one of the most discussed images in Western art.
