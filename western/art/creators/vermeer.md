> Vermeer painted quiet domestic interiors bathed in pearly daylight. Only about 35 works are attributed to him, and he was largely forgotten until the 19th century.

<sub>Portrait: Figure in The Procuress, 1656, often thought to be a self-portrait · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cropped_version_of_Jan_Vermeer_van_Delft_002.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Johannes Vermeer spent his whole life in Delft, where his father was a weaver, innkeeper and art dealer. He joined the painters' Guild of St Luke in 1653 and later served as its head. He married Catharina Bolnes, a Catholic, and they lived in the house of her well-off mother, Maria Thins. They had 15 children, 11 of whom survived him. He seems to have worked slowly, producing perhaps two or three paintings a year, and around 35 survive. In 1672 the French invasion of the Dutch Republic wrecked the art market, and he died in 1675 leaving his family in debt.

Vermeer painted quiet interiors, mostly of women reading letters, playing music, pouring milk or making lace, by a window that floods the room with soft daylight. He used expensive natural ultramarine, sparkling dots of light (pointillé) and precise perspective, and some scholars believe he used optical devices such as the camera obscura. He was almost forgotten until the French critic Théophile Thoré-Bürger championed him in the 1860s. Today he is one of the most admired painters in the world.

### The Milkmaid (c. 1657–58)

[![The Milkmaid](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6e/Johannes_Vermeer_-_Het_melkmeisje_-_Google_Art_Project.png/1280px-Johannes_Vermeer_-_Het_melkmeisje_-_Google_Art_Project.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Johannes_Vermeer_-_Het_melkmeisje_-_Google_Art_Project.png)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Johannes_Vermeer_-_Het_melkmeisje_-_Google_Art_Project.png).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1657–58
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Milkmaid%20Johannes%20Vermeer)

In this small painting of about 1657–58, in the Rijksmuseum, a sturdy kitchen maid pours a thin stream of milk from a jug into an earthenware bowl, absorbed in her task. On the table are bread, a blue cloth and a jug, painted with sparkling dots of light. The bare wall behind her shows nail holes and a nail with its shadow. On the floor stand a foot-warmer and Delft tiles, one showing Cupid, perhaps a hint at the love associations of milkmaids in Dutch art. Vermeer used costly natural ultramarine in her apron. He turns an everyday action into an image of calm, almost sacred concentration.

### View of Delft (c. 1660–61)

[![View of Delft](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a2/Vermeer-view-of-delft.jpg/1280px-Vermeer-view-of-delft.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vermeer-view-of-delft.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vermeer-view-of-delft.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1660–61
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Mauritshuis, The Hague
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=View%20of%20Delft%20Johannes%20Vermeer)

This cityscape of around 1660–61, in the Mauritshuis in The Hague, shows Delft seen from across the harbour to the south. Clouds cast shadows over the old city walls and gates in the foreground, while sunlight falls on the roofs and the tower of the New Church beyond. Vermeer used thick, granular paint, even mixing in sand, to suggest the texture of brick and stone, and pointillé dots on the boats. The novelist Marcel Proust considered it the most beautiful painting in the world. In his novel In Search of Lost Time, the writer Bergotte dies in front of it while admiring a 'little patch of yellow wall'.

### Girl with a Pearl Earring (c. 1665)

[![Girl with a Pearl Earring](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0f/1665_Girl_with_a_Pearl_Earring.jpg/1280px-1665_Girl_with_a_Pearl_Earring.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:1665_Girl_with_a_Pearl_Earring.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:1665_Girl_with_a_Pearl_Earring.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1665
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Mauritshuis, The Hague
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Girl%20with%20a%20Pearl%20Earring%20Johannes%20Vermeer)

Painted around 1665, this is not a portrait of a known sitter but a tronie, a Dutch study of a face or character type. A young woman in an exotic blue and yellow turban turns over her shoulder to look at us, lips parted. A large pearl, painted with just a few strokes, glints at her ear. The background, now dark, was originally a deep green curtain, as a research project in 2018 revealed, along with fine eyelashes. The painting was sold at auction in 1881 for just a couple of guilders, and its buyer later bequeathed it to the Mauritshuis. Tracy Chevalier's 1999 novel and the 2003 film made it one of the most famous images in the world.

### The Art of Painting (c. 1666–68)

[![The Art of Painting](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5e/Jan_Vermeer_-_The_Art_of_Painting_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Jan_Vermeer_-_The_Art_of_Painting_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jan_Vermeer_-_The_Art_of_Painting_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jan_Vermeer_-_The_Art_of_Painting_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1666–68
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Kunsthistorisches Museum, Vienna
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Art%20of%20Painting%20Johannes%20Vermeer)

In this painting of around 1666–68, in the Kunsthistorisches Museum in Vienna, a heavy tapestry curtain is pulled back to reveal a painter, seen from behind in a fine slashed doublet, at work. His model is dressed as Clio, the Muse of history, with a laurel wreath, a trumpet and a book. On the wall hangs a large map of the Seventeen Provinces of the Netherlands, with a crease that some see as dividing the Dutch Republic from the Spanish Netherlands. An elaborate chandelier displays the double-headed Habsburg eagle. Vermeer seems to have valued the painting greatly: after his death his widow tried to keep it from his creditors. Adolf Hitler bought it in 1940, and after the war it went to the Vienna museum. A restitution claim by heirs of the former owners was rejected by an Austrian advisory board in 2011.
