# Michelangelo Buonarroti

**1475 – 1564** · Italian  
Born: Caprese, Republic of Florence · Died: Rome  
Movements: [High Renaissance](../movements/high-renaissance.md), [Mannerism](../movements/mannerism.md)  

> Sculptor first, but also painter, architect and poet. His heroic, muscular figures dominate the High Renaissance, and his late work points toward Mannerism.

## Biography

Michelangelo Buonarroti was born in Caprese and raised in Florence. He was briefly apprenticed to the painter Domenico Ghirlandaio and then studied sculpture in the circle of Lorenzo de' Medici. Before he was 30 he had carved the Rome Pietà and the David. Pope Julius II then called him to Rome for a vast tomb project that dragged on for 40 years, and made him paint the Sistine Chapel ceiling. He went on to design the Medici Chapel and Laurentian Library in Florence and, from 1546, served as chief architect of St Peter's Basilica, designing its great dome. He died in Rome in 1564, aged 88.

Michelangelo saw himself above all as a sculptor who released figures from the stone. His art is centred on the heroic male nude, charged with muscular energy and spiritual struggle, and he also wrote passionate poetry, including sonnets to Vittoria Colonna and Tommaso de' Cavalieri. Contemporaries called him 'il divino'. Vasari's Lives presented him as the summit of all art, and his towering reputation shaped the modern idea of artistic genius.

### Pietà (1498–99)

[![Pietà](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Pieta_de_Michelangelo_-_Vaticano.jpg/1280px-Pieta_de_Michelangelo_-_Vaticano.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pieta_de_Michelangelo_-_Vaticano.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pieta_de_Michelangelo_-_Vaticano.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1498–99
- **Type:** Marble sculpture
- **Where to see it:** St Peter's Basilica, Vatican City
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Piet%C3%A0%20Michelangelo%20Buonarroti)

The French cardinal Jean de Bilhères commissioned this marble Pietà, the Virgin mourning the dead Christ on her lap, for his tomb. Michelangelo carved it in 1498–99, when he was in his early twenties. He solved the problem of holding an adult man on a woman's lap by making Mary's body larger and her robes deep and voluminous. He gave her a young, serene face, explaining, according to his biographer Condivi, that purity preserves youth. It is the only work Michelangelo ever signed: his name is carved across the strap on Mary's breast, reportedly after he heard others credited for it. In 1972 a man attacked it with a hammer, and after restoration it has been shown behind glass in St Peter's Basilica.

### David (1501–04)

[![David](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/%27David%27_by_Michelangelo_Fir_JBU004.jpg/1280px-%27David%27_by_Michelangelo_Fir_JBU004.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%27David%27_by_Michelangelo_Fir_JBU004.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%27David%27_by_Michelangelo_Fir_JBU004.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1501–04
- **Type:** Marble sculpture
- **Where to see it:** Galleria dell'Accademia, Florence
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=David%20Michelangelo%20Buonarroti)

In 1501 the cathedral works in Florence gave the 26-year-old Michelangelo a huge, narrow block of marble that earlier sculptors had begun and abandoned. By 1504 he had carved from it a David over 5 metres tall. Instead of showing the hero triumphant over Goliath's head, he chose the tense moment before the fight. David stands in relaxed contrapposto with his sling over his shoulder, but his brow is furrowed and his gaze fixed on the enemy. The oversized head and hands make him read clearly from below. A committee of artists, including Leonardo and Botticelli, decided to place it outside the Palazzo della Signoria as a symbol of the Florentine Republic's defiance of tyranny. It was moved indoors to the Accademia in 1873, and a copy now stands in its place.

### Sistine Chapel Ceiling (1508–12)

[![Sistine Chapel Ceiling](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Sistine_Chapel_ceiling_02_%28brightened%29.jpg/1280px-Sistine_Chapel_ceiling_02_%28brightened%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sistine_Chapel_ceiling_02_%28brightened%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sistine_Chapel_ceiling_02_%28brightened%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1508–12
- **Type:** Fresco
- **Where to see it:** Sistine Chapel, Vatican City
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sistine%20Chapel%20Ceiling%20Michelangelo%20Buonarroti)

Pope Julius II persuaded a reluctant Michelangelo, who considered himself a sculptor, to paint the ceiling of the papal chapel. Between 1508 and 1512, working on scaffolding he designed himself, he covered some 500 square metres with more than 300 figures. Down the centre run nine scenes from Genesis, from the Separation of Light and Darkness to the Drunkenness of Noah, including the Creation of Adam, in which God's finger reaches towards Adam's. Around them sit monumental prophets and sibyls, the ancestors of Christ, and the energetic nude youths called ignudi. Contrary to legend, he painted standing and bending backwards rather than lying down. A restoration completed in 1994 removed centuries of grime and revealed brilliant colours.

### The Last Judgment (1536–41)

[![The Last Judgment](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/18/Last_Judgement_%28Michelangelo%29.jpg/1280px-Last_Judgement_%28Michelangelo%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Last_Judgement_%28Michelangelo%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Last_Judgement_%28Michelangelo%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1536–41
- **Type:** Fresco
- **Where to see it:** Sistine Chapel, Vatican City
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Last%20Judgment%20Michelangelo%20Buonarroti)

More than twenty years after the ceiling, Michelangelo returned to the Sistine Chapel to paint the entire altar wall (1536–41) for Pope Paul III. A powerful, beardless Christ raises his arm in judgement at the centre of a swirling vortex of more than 300 figures. The saved rise towards heaven on the left, and the damned are dragged down to hell on the right, where Charon beats them from his boat. St Bartholomew holds his own flayed skin, whose sagging face is generally read as Michelangelo's self-portrait. The many nudes provoked fierce criticism, and after his death the painter Daniele da Volterra was hired to paint drapery over the genitals, earning the nickname 'Il Braghettone' ('the breeches-maker').
