## Biography

Egon Schiele was born in Tulln on the Danube, where his father was stationmaster. His father died when Egon was 14. He entered the Vienna Academy of Fine Arts at 16 but left after three years, rebelling against its conservatism. Gustav Klimt became his mentor and supporter. With his companion and model Wally Neuzil he moved to the small town of Neulengbach, where in 1912 he was arrested and briefly imprisoned. Most charges were dropped, but he was convicted for exhibiting erotic drawings where children could see them. In 1915 he married Edith Harms and was called up for military service. His exhibition at the Vienna Secession in 1918 was a great success, but that autumn Edith, six months pregnant, died of influenza, and Schiele died three days later, aged 28.

Schiele's art is defined by its nervous, angular line and its raw, often contorted and sexual images of the human body, including dozens of unsparing self-portraits. He also painted townscapes and landscapes. He is a central figure of Austrian Expressionism, and the Leopold Museum in Vienna has the world's largest collection of his work.

## Key artworks

### Self-Portrait with Physalis (1912)

[![Self-Portrait with Physalis](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b8/Egon_Schiele_-_Self-Portrait_with_Physalis_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Egon_Schiele_-_Self-Portrait_with_Physalis_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Egon_Schiele_-_Self-Portrait_with_Physalis_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Egon_Schiele_-_Self-Portrait_with_Physalis_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1912
- **Type:** Oil and gouache on panel
- **Where to see it:** Leopold Museum, Vienna
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Self-Portrait%20with%20Physalis%20Egon%20Schiele)

Painted in 1912, the year of Schiele's brief imprisonment, this self-portrait in the Leopold Museum in Vienna shows the artist's head and shoulders, turning sharply. His head tilts towards his shoulder and he looks out at the viewer with a fixed, intense gaze. Beside him are a sprig of physalis (Chinese lantern plant) with red fruits and a few leaves, set against a bare white background. The sharp outlines, flat white space and twisted pose create tension. It was painted as a companion to his portrait of Wally Neuzil, which is the same size.
