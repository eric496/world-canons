# Mark Rothko

**1903 – 1970** · American  
Born: Dvinsk, Russian Empire (now Daugavpils, Latvia) · Died: New York  
Movements: [Abstract Expressionism](../movements/abstract-expressionism.md)  

> A leading Colour Field painter whose soft-edged rectangles of colour aim at contemplative, almost religious experience.

## Biography

Mark Rothko was born Markus Rothkowitz in Dvinsk (now Daugavpils, Latvia), in the Russian Empire, to a Jewish family. In 1913 the family emigrated to Portland, Oregon. He won a scholarship to Yale but left without graduating, moved to New York and studied art. In the 1940s he painted mythological, Surrealist-influenced images, then 'multiforms' of soft colour patches. From around 1949 he arrived at his classic format: two or three soft-edged rectangles of luminous colour floating on a coloured field. In 1958–59 he painted a series of dark murals for the Four Seasons restaurant in the Seagram Building, then withdrew from the commission and gave some of the paintings to Tate. He later painted the murals for Harvard and the Rothko Chapel in Houston. Suffering from depression and ill health, he took his own life in his New York studio in February 1970.

Rothko rejected being called a colourist or an abstract artist. He said he was interested only in expressing basic human emotions such as tragedy, ecstasy and doom, and he wanted viewers to stand close to his large canvases and be enveloped by them. He became one of the leading figures of Colour Field painting and Abstract Expressionism.

## Key artworks

### Rothko Chapel paintings (1964–67)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Rothko Chapel →](https://www.rothkochapel.org/learn/about/)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1964–67
- **Type:** Oil on canvas (cycle of 14)
- **Where to see it:** Rothko Chapel, Houston
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Rothko%20Chapel%20paintings%20Mark%20Rothko)

In 1964 the art collectors and philanthropists John and Dominique de Menil commissioned Rothko to create paintings for a non-denominational chapel in Houston. He worked on the 14 large canvases from 1964 to 1967 and was closely involved in the design of the octagonal building, although he died before it opened in 1971. The paintings, some single panels and some triptychs, are either black rectangles on deep maroon fields or monochrome purple tones, so dark that they reveal themselves only slowly to the eye. Together they create an atmosphere of meditation for people of all faiths. Barnett Newman's sculpture Broken Obelisk stands on the plaza outside. Several panels were damaged by Hurricane Beryl in 2024 and have been undergoing conservation.
