# Filippo Brunelleschi

**1377 – 1446** · Italian  
Born: Florence · Died: Florence  
Movements: [Early Italian Renaissance](../movements/early-renaissance.md)  

> Architect and engineer credited with the first systematic demonstration of linear perspective. His dome for Florence Cathedral was an engineering feat without precedent since antiquity.

## Biography

Filippo Brunelleschi was trained as a goldsmith. After losing the 1401 Baptistery doors competition to Ghiberti, he turned increasingly to architecture and engineering. Tradition says he went to Rome with Donatello to measure and study the ancient ruins. Around 1415–20 he made a celebrated experiment with painted panels of the Baptistery and a mirror. It demonstrated the rules of linear perspective, which the theorist Leon Battista Alberti soon codified for painters.

His buildings in Florence are the founding works of Renaissance architecture: the Ospedale degli Innocenti, the churches of San Lorenzo and Santo Spirito, and the Pazzi Chapel. They are built from clear, mathematically related proportions, round arches and classical columns in grey pietra serena stone against white walls. His crowning achievement was the cathedral dome, and he was given the rare honour of burial inside the cathedral itself.

## Key artworks

### Dome of Florence Cathedral (1420–36)

- **Date:** 1420–36
- **Type:** Architecture
- **Where to see it:** Santa Maria del Fiore, Florence
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dome%20of%20Florence%20Cathedral%20Filippo%20Brunelleschi)

Florence Cathedral's octagonal crossing, about 45 metres across, had been planned at a size no one knew how to cover: it was too wide for the timber centring normally used to support a vault while it was built. In 1418 Brunelleschi won the competition with a radical proposal, and work ran from 1420 to 1436. His dome is made of two shells, an inner and an outer, linked by ribs. The bricks are laid in a herringbone pattern so the courses lock together and support themselves as the dome rises. He also invented new hoisting machines to lift around four million bricks and huge stones. The marble lantern on top was completed after his death to his design, and Verrocchio's workshop, where the young Leonardo was training, added the gilded ball in 1471. It remains the largest masonry dome ever built.
