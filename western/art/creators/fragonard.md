# Jean-Honoré Fragonard

**1732 – 1806** · French  
Born: Grasse, France · Died: Paris  
Movements: [Rococo](../movements/rococo.md)  

> A brilliant, rapid painter of love scenes and gardens for aristocratic patrons. The French Revolution ended demand for his work.

## Biography

Jean-Honoré Fragonard was born in Grasse in Provence and grew up in Paris. He briefly studied with Chardin and then with Boucher. He won the Prix de Rome in 1752 and spent several years in Italy, sketching the gardens of Roman villas. In 1765 the Academy acclaimed his grand history painting Coresus and Callirhoë, but he then largely abandoned official careers to work for wealthy private clients. His series The Progress of Love, made for Madame du Barry, was returned to him and is now in the Frick Collection, New York.

Fragonard painted playful scenes of love, gardens, children and reading with dazzling, rapid brushwork, as well as his virtuoso 'fantasy figures', each painted in about an hour. The French Revolution destroyed his aristocratic market. Jacques-Louis David helped him find a post in the administration of the new Louvre museum, but he died in 1806 almost forgotten, and he was rediscovered by 19th-century collectors and painters.

## Key artworks

### The Swing (1767)

[![The Swing](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/83/The_Swing_%28P430%29.jpg/1280px-The_Swing_%28P430%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Swing_%28P430%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Swing_%28P430%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1767
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Wallace Collection, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Swing%20Jean-Honor%C3%A9%20Fragonard)

According to a contemporary memoir, a nobleman asked the painter Gabriel-François Doyen to paint his mistress on a swing pushed by a bishop, with himself placed where he could see her legs. The shocked Doyen passed the commission to Fragonard, who painted it in 1767. In a lush, overgrown garden, a young woman in a frothy pink dress swings high, kicking off her slipper towards a statue of Cupid, who holds a finger to his lips. Her lover hides in the bushes below, gazing up her skirts, while an elderly man, not a bishop in the final version, pushes the swing from the shadows. The painting is the quintessence of Rococo flirtation. A 2021 restoration at the Wallace Collection in London removed yellowed varnish and revealed its original bright colours.
