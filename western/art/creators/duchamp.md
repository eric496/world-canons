# Marcel Duchamp

**1887 – 1968** · French-American  
Born: Blainville-Crevon, France · Died: Neuilly-sur-Seine  
Movements: [Dada & Surrealism](../movements/dada-surrealism.md)  

> Duchamp's 'readymades' — ordinary objects declared art — shifted art from the eye to the idea. He is a founding figure of conceptual art.

## Biography

Marcel Duchamp was born in Blainville-Crevon in Normandy into an artistic family: his brothers Jacques Villon and Raymond Duchamp-Villon and his sister Suzanne were also artists. His Cubist painting Nude Descending a Staircase, No. 2 was withdrawn from a Paris exhibition in 1912 at the request of fellow Cubists, but it caused a sensation at the 1913 Armory Show in New York. Soon after, he gave up conventional painting and began making readymades, everyday objects selected and presented as art. He moved to New York in 1915 and worked for years on The Bride Stripped Bare by Her Bachelors, Even (The Large Glass). He devoted much of his time to chess, playing for France in international tournaments, and created a female alter ego, Rrose Sélavy. He became an American citizen in 1955. After his death in 1968 it was discovered that he had spent 20 years secretly building a final work, Étant donnés, now in the Philadelphia Museum of Art.

Duchamp rejected what he called 'retinal' art, made to please the eye, in favour of art addressed to the mind. His readymades, his puns and his idea that the artist's choice and context make something art laid the foundations of Dada, Pop Art, Conceptual Art and much contemporary art.

### Nude Descending a Staircase, No. 2 (1912)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Philadelphia Museum of Art →](https://philamuseum.org/collection/object/51449)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1912
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Philadelphia Museum of Art
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Nude%20Descending%20a%20Staircase%2C%20No.%202%20Marcel%20Duchamp)

Painted in 1912, this painting shows a figure walking down a spiral staircase broken into overlapping, mechanical-looking planes, like a sequence of photographs. It combines the fragmentation of Cubism with the analysis of motion in the chronophotographs of Étienne-Jules Marey and the Futurists, in browns and ochres. Fellow Cubists asked Duchamp to remove it from the 1912 Salon des Indépendants in Paris, perhaps because of its title and its suggestion of motion. At the 1913 Armory Show in New York it became the most notorious work, and a critic described it as 'an explosion in a shingle factory'. It is in the Philadelphia Museum of Art.

### Fountain (1917)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Tate →](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/duchamp-fountain-t07573)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1917
- **Type:** Readymade (porcelain urinal)
- **Where to see it:** Original lost; 1964 replicas in several museums, incl. Tate
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Fountain%20Marcel%20Duchamp)

In April 1917 the Society of Independent Artists in New York announced an exhibition open to anyone who paid the fee. Duchamp, a member of its board, submitted a standard porcelain urinal, laid on its back and signed 'R. Mutt 1917'. The committee refused to show it, and it was hidden during the exhibition. The photographer Alfred Stieglitz photographed it, and the photograph appeared in a small magazine, The Blind Man, with an article arguing that the artist's choice is what makes something art. The original was lost. In 1964 Duchamp authorised a small edition of replicas, now in museums including Tate. In 2004 a poll of 500 art experts named it the most influential work of modern art. Some scholars have suggested that the Dada artist Elsa von Freytag-Loringhoven may have been involved in the idea, though this is debated.
