## Biography

Rembrandt Harmenszoon van Rijn was the son of a Leiden miller. He trained there and briefly in Amsterdam with the history painter Pieter Lastman, then moved permanently to Amsterdam in the early 1630s. The Anatomy Lesson of Dr Nicolaes Tulp (1632) made his name. He married Saskia van Uylenburgh, bought a grand house and collected art and curiosities extravagantly. Saskia died in 1642, the year of The Night Watch, and of their children only their son Titus survived infancy. Lavish spending and the economic climate led to his insolvency in 1656, and he lost his house and collection. His later years were marked by the deaths of his partner Hendrickje Stoffels and of Titus. He died in Amsterdam in 1669.

Rembrandt was a supreme painter, draughtsman and etcher. His art is famed for dramatic chiaroscuro, rich, sometimes thickly built-up paint and a deep empathy for human emotion, whether in biblical scenes, portraits of Amsterdam burghers or the more than 80 self-portraits in paint, etching and drawing that trace his face from youth to old age. Scholars have long debated attributions, and the Rembrandt Research Project spent decades sorting his works from those of his many pupils.

### The Night Watch (1642)

[![The Night Watch](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3a/La_ronda_de_noche%2C_por_Rembrandt_van_Rijn.jpg/1280px-La_ronda_de_noche%2C_por_Rembrandt_van_Rijn.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_ronda_de_noche%2C_por_Rembrandt_van_Rijn.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_ronda_de_noche%2C_por_Rembrandt_van_Rijn.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1642
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Rijksmuseum, Amsterdam
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Night%20Watch%20Rembrandt%20van%20Rijn)

Officially titled Militia Company of District II under the Command of Captain Frans Banninck Cocq, this huge canvas of 1642 was commissioned to hang in the hall of Amsterdam's arquebusiers' guild. Instead of the usual rows of portraits, Rembrandt showed the company bursting into action as the captain, in black with a red sash, orders his lieutenant in pale yellow to march them out. Around them, muskets are loaded and fired, a drum beats, and a mysterious girl in gold carries a chicken, the company's emblem. The popular name came later, when darkened varnish made the daylight scene look nocturnal. In 1715 the painting was cut down on all sides to fit a wall in the town hall. It has survived three vandal attacks, and since 2019 the Rijksmuseum's Operation Night Watch has studied and restored it in public view.

### Self-Portrait with Two Circles (c. 1665–69)

[![Self-Portrait with Two Circles](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Rembrandt_Self-portrait_%28Kenwood%29.jpg/1280px-Rembrandt_Self-portrait_%28Kenwood%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rembrandt_Self-portrait_%28Kenwood%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rembrandt_Self-portrait_%28Kenwood%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1665–69
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Kenwood House, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Self-Portrait%20with%20Two%20Circles%20Rembrandt%20van%20Rijn)

Painted in the last years of his life, around 1665–69, this self-portrait shows Rembrandt standing in his working clothes and a white cap, holding his palette, brushes and maulstick. He looks out at us with a steady gaze. Behind him on the wall are two mysterious partial circles. They have been interpreted as references to world maps, to symbols of perfection and theory, or to the legend of the ancient painter Apelles drawing a perfect circle freehand. The face is built up with thick, energetic paint, while the hands are barely sketched. It is in Kenwood House in London.

### The Return of the Prodigal Son (c. 1668)

[![The Return of the Prodigal Son](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Rembrandt_Harmensz_van_Rijn_-_Return_of_the_Prodigal_Son_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Rembrandt_Harmensz_van_Rijn_-_Return_of_the_Prodigal_Son_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rembrandt_Harmensz_van_Rijn_-_Return_of_the_Prodigal_Son_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rembrandt_Harmensz_van_Rijn_-_Return_of_the_Prodigal_Son_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1668
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** State Hermitage Museum, St Petersburg
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Return%20of%20the%20Prodigal%20Son%20Rembrandt%20van%20Rijn)

This late painting, probably from around 1668, in the Hermitage in St Petersburg, illustrates Jesus's parable of the son who squanders his inheritance and returns home in disgrace, only to be welcomed with love by his father. The ragged son kneels with his shaven head buried against his father's chest, one sandal falling from his worn foot. The old, almost blind father bends over him and lays both hands on his back. Viewers have often noted that one hand looks larger and masculine and the other more slender. At the right, the stern elder brother watches, and other figures emerge from the deep warm shadows. It is one of the most moving images of forgiveness in art.
