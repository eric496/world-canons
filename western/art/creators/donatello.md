# Donatello

**c. 1386 – 1466** · Italian  
Born: Florence, Italy · Died: Florence  
Movements: [Early Italian Renaissance](../movements/early-renaissance.md)  

> The most inventive sculptor of the early Renaissance, Donatello revived the classical nude and free-standing bronze statue. He worked in marble, bronze, wood and terracotta with striking psychological realism.

## Biography

Donato di Niccolò di Betto Bardi, known as Donatello, trained in Ghiberti's bronze workshop before becoming the most inventive sculptor of the 15th century. His early marble saints for Orsanmichele and Florence Cathedral, such as St Mark and St George, gave figures a new psychological life and a believable stance. He also invented rilievo schiacciato ('flattened relief'), creating deep perspective in carving only millimetres deep. His friend and patron was Cosimo de' Medici.

Donatello revived antique types that had not been made for a thousand years, including the free-standing nude and the bronze equestrian monument (the Gattamelata in Padua, 1453). His late works, such as the gaunt wooden Penitent Magdalene, have a raw intensity that looks ahead to much later art. He was buried near Cosimo in the church of San Lorenzo.

## Key artworks

### David (c. 1440s)

- **Date:** c. 1440s
- **Type:** Bronze sculpture
- **Where to see it:** Museo Nazionale del Bargello, Florence
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=David%20Donatello)

Donatello's bronze David is the first free-standing life-size nude statue made in Europe since antiquity. Its date is debated, usually placed between the 1430s and the 1450s. It was made for the Medici, probably to stand in the courtyard of their palace. The slender young hero wears only a wide-brimmed shepherd's hat and boots, and he rests one foot on the severed head of Goliath, his sword at his side. His relaxed contrapposto stance, soft adolescent body and dreamy expression have prompted endless discussion of its meaning, from a symbol of Florence's freedom to an expression of sensual beauty. When the Medici were expelled in 1495 the statue was moved to the Palazzo della Signoria as a civic emblem.
