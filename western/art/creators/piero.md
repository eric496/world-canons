## Biography

Piero della Francesca spent much of his life in his small home town of Borgo San Sepolcro in Tuscany, but worked for the courts of Rimini, Ferrara and above all Urbino. In Urbino he painted the famous profile portraits of Duke Federico da Montefeltro and his wife Battista Sforza (Uffizi). His masterpiece in fresco is the Legend of the True Cross in the church of San Francesco at Arezzo. He also painted the Resurrection in Sansepolcro, which Aldous Huxley called 'the best picture in the world'.

Piero was also a serious mathematician. He wrote treatises on perspective (De prospectiva pingendi), arithmetic and the five regular solids, and his paintings have a still, geometric calm, pale cool colour and a clear daylight. He was largely forgotten for centuries. 20th-century critics and artists rediscovered him and saw in his ordered forms an anticipation of Cézanne and modern abstraction.

### The Baptism of Christ (c. 1450s)

[![The Baptism of Christ](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5a/Piero_della_Francesca_-_Baptism_of_Christ_-_WGA17595.jpg/1280px-Piero_della_Francesca_-_Baptism_of_Christ_-_WGA17595.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Piero_della_Francesca_-_Baptism_of_Christ_-_WGA17595.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Piero_della_Francesca_-_Baptism_of_Christ_-_WGA17595.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1450s
- **Type:** Tempera on panel
- **Where to see it:** National Gallery, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Baptism%20of%20Christ%20Piero%20della%20Francesca)

Probably painted in the 1450s for a chapel or priory in Piero's home town of Sansepolcro, this panel shows John the Baptist pouring water over Christ's head as the dove of the Holy Spirit hovers above. Christ stands perfectly still and upright, like a column, beside a walnut tree whose pale trunk echoes his body. Three angels watch at the left, and in the background a man pulls his shirt over his head before his own baptism. The figures' reflections appear in the still river, and the landscape is the hilly countryside around Sansepolcro. Its geometry, pale colour and calm make it one of the best-loved works of the Renaissance; it is in the National Gallery, London.

### The Flagellation of Christ (c. 1455–60)

[![The Flagellation of Christ](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Piero_della_Francesca_-_Flagelaci%C3%B3n_de_Cristo%2C_1468-1470_%28Galleria_Nazionale_delle_Marche%2C_Urbino%29.png/1280px-Piero_della_Francesca_-_Flagelaci%C3%B3n_de_Cristo%2C_1468-1470_%28Galleria_Nazionale_delle_Marche%2C_Urbino%29.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Piero_della_Francesca_-_Flagelaci%C3%B3n_de_Cristo%2C_1468-1470_%28Galleria_Nazionale_delle_Marche%2C_Urbino%29.png)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Piero_della_Francesca_-_Flagelaci%C3%B3n_de_Cristo%2C_1468-1470_%28Galleria_Nazionale_delle_Marche%2C_Urbino%29.png).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1455–60
- **Type:** Oil and tempera on panel
- **Where to see it:** Galleria Nazionale delle Marche, Urbino
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Flagellation%20of%20Christ%20Piero%20della%20Francesca)

This small panel of around 1455–60, in the Galleria Nazionale delle Marche in Urbino, is one of the most puzzling pictures of the Renaissance. In a classical hall at the left and rear, Christ is tied to a column and whipped while a seated ruler watches. In the right foreground, outside, three large figures stand talking, apparently paying no attention. Their identity has been debated for centuries: theories range from members of the Montefeltro court to an allusion to the fall of Constantinople in 1453. The architecture is built with such exact perspective that scholars have reconstructed its floor plan from the painting.
