# Cimabue

**c. 1240 – 1302** · Italian  
Born: Florence · Died: Pisa  
Movements: [Late Gothic & Proto-Renaissance](../movements/proto-renaissance.md)  

> A Florentine painter who began loosening Byzantine conventions toward greater naturalism. Tradition makes him Giotto's teacher.

## Biography

Cenni di Pepo, called Cimabue, was born in Florence around 1240 and is the earliest Florentine painter whose name is attached to major surviving works. He worked in Rome, at Assisi (where he painted frescoes in the Upper Church of San Francesco) and in Pisa, where he made the mosaic of St John in the cathedral apse shortly before his death. Tradition, followed by Vasari, makes him Giotto's teacher.

Cimabue still worked within the Byzantine 'Greek manner', with gold grounds and stylised drapery. He gave it new weight, emotion and a sense of real space, so his figures begin to feel like bodies rather than icons. Dante paid him an ambiguous tribute in Purgatory: 'Cimabue thought he held the field in painting, and now Giotto has the cry.' His great painted Crucifix in Santa Croce, badly damaged in the 1966 Florence flood, became a symbol of the city's lost heritage.

## Key artworks

### Santa Trinita Maestà (c. 1280–90)

[![Santa Trinita Maestà](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Cimabue_-_Maest%C3%A0_di_Santa_Trinita_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Cimabue_-_Maest%C3%A0_di_Santa_Trinita_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cimabue_-_Maest%C3%A0_di_Santa_Trinita_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cimabue_-_Maest%C3%A0_di_Santa_Trinita_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1280–90
- **Type:** Tempera on panel
- **Where to see it:** Uffizi Gallery, Florence
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Santa%20Trinita%20Maest%C3%A0%20Cimabue)

This towering altarpiece, more than 3.8 metres high, was painted for the church of Santa Trinita in Florence. The Virgin sits enthroned with the Christ Child, surrounded by eight angels stacked up either side of the throne. Below, four Old Testament prophets look out from arches under the throne. The gold ground and stylised features follow Byzantine tradition. Cimabue gives the throne a sense of depth, the faces gentle individual expressions and the composition a new monumental structure. In the Uffizi it hangs in the same room as Duccio's Rucellai Madonna and Giotto's Ognissanti Madonna, so visitors can see the change from Byzantine to early Renaissance painting side by side.
