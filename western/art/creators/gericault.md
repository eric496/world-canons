## Biography

Théodore Géricault was born in Rouen to a wealthy family and became a passionate horseman. He trained with Carle Vernet and Pierre-Narcisse Guérin and made a sensational debut at the 1812 Salon with The Charging Chasseur. After studying in Italy in 1816–17 he devoted 18 months to The Raft of the Medusa, exhibited in 1819. He visited England in 1820–21, painting horse races and the London poor. Around 1822 he painted a series of portraits of the mentally ill for a psychiatrist friend. Riding accidents and illness led to his death in 1824, aged just 32.

In his short career Géricault rejected the cool idealism of David's school for raw energy, dramatic contrast and contemporary subjects charged with suffering and political meaning. His studies of horses, corpses and severed limbs and his portraits of 'monomaniacs' show a new psychological and physical realism. He opened the way for French Romanticism and for Delacroix, who admired him deeply.

## Key artworks

### The Raft of the Medusa (1818–19)

- **Date:** 1818–19
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Musée du Louvre, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Raft%20of%20the%20Medusa%20Th%C3%A9odore%20G%C3%A9ricault)

In July 1816 the French frigate Méduse ran aground off the coast of Mauritania through the incompetence of its captain, a returned aristocrat appointed by the restored monarchy. About 150 people were left on a makeshift raft. During 13 days adrift, they suffered starvation, madness, murder and cannibalism, and only 15 survived. Géricault interviewed survivors, built a model of the raft, and studied corpses and severed limbs in hospitals. His huge canvas, about 5 by 7 metres, shows the moment the survivors sight a distant ship on the horizon. The figures rise in a pyramid from the dead and despairing to a Black man waving a cloth. Shown at the 1819 Salon, it was both a political scandal and a manifesto of Romanticism. It is in the Louvre.
