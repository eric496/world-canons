# Édouard Manet

**1832 – 1883** · French  
Born: Paris · Died: Paris  
Movements: [Realism](../movements/realism.md), [Impressionism](../movements/impressionism.md)  

> Manet's flat, frank pictures of modern Paris caused scandal and paved the way for Impressionism. He is often seen as the first modern painter.

## Biography

Édouard Manet was born in Paris to a wealthy family; his father was a senior magistrate. After failing to enter the naval academy and sailing as a trainee to Rio de Janeiro, he studied for six years with the academic painter Thomas Couture. He admired Velázquez, Goya and Hals, and his early works show Spanish influence. Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe (1863) and Olympia (1865) caused huge scandals and made him the reluctant leader of young avant-garde painters who met at the Café Guerbois. Yet he never exhibited at the Impressionists' independent exhibitions, still hoping for official success at the Salon. He was a friend of Baudelaire, Zola and Mallarmé. His health declined from the effects of syphilis, and he died in 1883 at 51, shortly after a leg amputation.

Manet painted modern Paris life, its cafés, boulevards, racecourses and people, with flat areas of colour, strong contrasts, little traditional modelling and bold black, often reworking old-master compositions in contemporary dress. His frankness and his emphasis on the painted surface broke with academic tradition and are seen as the starting point of modern painting. His sister-in-law was the Impressionist Berthe Morisot.

### Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe (1863)

[![Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/Edouard_Manet_-_Luncheon_on_the_Grass_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Edouard_Manet_-_Luncheon_on_the_Grass_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edouard_Manet_-_Luncheon_on_the_Grass_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edouard_Manet_-_Luncheon_on_the_Grass_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1863
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Musée d'Orsay, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Le%20D%C3%A9jeuner%20sur%20l%27herbe%20%C3%89douard%20Manet)

Rejected by the official Salon jury, this large painting was shown in 1863 at the Salon des Refusés, an exhibition of rejected works ordered by Napoleon III, where it caused a scandal. In a woodland clearing, a nude woman picnics with two fully dressed young men in modern clothes, and she gazes boldly at the viewer, while a second woman bathes in the background. The model was Victorine Meurent, and the men were Manet's brother and his future brother-in-law. The composition borrows from a Renaissance engraving after Raphael and from Titian's Pastoral Concert, but by putting the nude in a contemporary setting and painting her with flat light, Manet made the scene shockingly modern. It is in the Musée d'Orsay.

### Olympia (1863)

[![Olympia](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Edouard_Manet_-_Olympia_-_Google_Art_ProjectFXD.jpg/1280px-Edouard_Manet_-_Olympia_-_Google_Art_ProjectFXD.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edouard_Manet_-_Olympia_-_Google_Art_ProjectFXD.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edouard_Manet_-_Olympia_-_Google_Art_ProjectFXD.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1863
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Musée d'Orsay, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Olympia%20%C3%89douard%20Manet)

Painted in 1863 and shown at the 1865 Salon, Olympia reworks Titian's Venus of Urbino as a modern Parisian prostitute. The model, again Victorine Meurent, reclines naked on a bed wearing only a black ribbon at her throat, a bracelet, an orchid in her hair and a single slipper. She stares directly and unashamedly at the viewer as if at a client. A Black maid brings a bouquet, probably from an admirer, and a black cat, replacing Titian's faithful dog, arches its back at the foot of the bed. The flat, harshly lit body and frank subject outraged critics and the public, and guards had to protect the painting from attack. In 1890 Claude Monet raised a public subscription to buy it for the French state. It is in the Musée d'Orsay.

### A Bar at the Folies-Bergère (1882)

[![A Bar at the Folies-Bergère](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/%22Un_Bar_aux_Folies-Berg%C3%A8re%22_by_%C3%89douard_Manet_%281882%29.jpg/1280px-%22Un_Bar_aux_Folies-Berg%C3%A8re%22_by_%C3%89douard_Manet_%281882%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%22Un_Bar_aux_Folies-Berg%C3%A8re%22_by_%C3%89douard_Manet_%281882%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%22Un_Bar_aux_Folies-Berg%C3%A8re%22_by_%C3%89douard_Manet_%281882%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1882
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Courtauld Gallery, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Bar%20at%20the%20Folies-Berg%C3%A8re%20%C3%89douard%20Manet)

Manet's last major painting, shown at the Salon of 1882, is set in the famous Parisian music hall. A barmaid stands behind a marble counter loaded with champagne, bottles of English Bass beer, a bowl of oranges and flowers. Behind her, a mirror reflects the glittering crowded hall, a chandelier and the green-booted legs of a trapeze artist at the top left. But the reflection doesn't match. The barmaid's reflection appears too far to the right, talking to a man in a top hat who should be standing where we are. Her detached expression contrasts with the bustle, and the painting has been read as a meditation on modern alienation and commerce. The collector Samuel Courtauld bought it in 1926, and it is in the Courtauld Gallery, London.
