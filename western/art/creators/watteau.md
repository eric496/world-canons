# Antoine Watteau

**1684 – 1721** · French  
Born: Valenciennes, France · Died: Nogent-sur-Marne  
Movements: [Rococo](../movements/rococo.md)  

> Inventor of the fête galante, a scene of elegant figures flirting in dreamy parkland. His wistful mood set the tone for the Rococo.

## Biography

Antoine Watteau was born in Valenciennes, a town recently annexed to France, and arrived in Paris around 1702. He worked for the theatre painter Claude Gillot, who gave him a lifelong love of the commedia dell'arte. He then worked for the decorator Claude Audran, through whom he could study Rubens's Medici cycle in the Luxembourg Palace. He was admitted to the Royal Academy in 1712, although it took him five years to deliver his reception piece. He suffered from tuberculosis, visited London in 1719–20 to consult a doctor, and died in 1721 aged only 36.

Watteau invented a new genre, the fête galante: elegant men and women in silks lingering in dreamy parks, flirting, making music and dressed as theatre characters. His feathery brushwork, shimmering colour and the faint melancholy beneath the pleasure gave the Rococo its tone. His last great work, the shop sign for the art dealer Gersaint (Berlin), shows paintings being sold and packed. Its scene of Louis XIV's portrait being crated has been read as the end of an era.

## Key artworks

### Pilgrimage to Cythera (1717)

- **Date:** 1717
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Musée du Louvre, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pilgrimage%20to%20Cythera%20Antoine%20Watteau)

This is the painting Watteau submitted to the Royal Academy in 1717. Its theme did not fit any existing category, so the Academy created a new one for him: the fête galante. Elegant couples, guided by flying cupids, are on the island of Cythera, sacred to Venus, whose garlanded statue stands at right. From right to left the couples seem to pass through the stages of courtship: being persuaded, rising and finally heading down to a golden boat. Scholars debate whether they are arriving or reluctantly leaving the island of love. The hazy landscape and gentle melancholy make it the key image of the Rococo. Watteau painted a second version, now in Berlin.
