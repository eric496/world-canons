**c. 1525 – 1569** · Flemish  
Born: probably Breda, Low Countries · Died: Brussels  
Movements: [Northern Renaissance](../movements/northern-renaissance.md)  

> Bruegel painted peasant life, proverbs and landscapes with humour and humanity. His seasonal landscapes are among the first great pure landscape paintings in Europe.

## Biography

Pieter Bruegel the Elder probably came from the region of Breda. He became a master in the Antwerp painters' guild in 1551 and soon travelled to Italy, crossing the Alps, whose mountain scenery later filled his landscapes. Back in Antwerp he designed prints for the publisher Hieronymus Cock, including Bosch-like moral allegories. In 1563 he married Mayken Coecke, the daughter of his former master, and moved to Brussels. He died there in 1569. His sons Pieter Brueghel the Younger and Jan Brueghel the Elder founded a long dynasty of painters.

Bruegel painted peasant weddings and dances, children's games, proverbs, biblical scenes set in contemporary Flanders and the cycle of the seasons, with humour, sharp observation and deep humanity. Later legend made him 'Peasant Bruegel', but he was a learned city-dweller who moved in humanist circles and sold to wealthy collectors. His panoramic landscapes and his view of human folly within the vastness of nature are among the most original achievements of 16th-century art.

### The Tower of Babel (1563)

[![The Tower of Babel](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Pieter_Bruegel_the_Elder_-_The_Tower_of_Babel_%28Vienna%29_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg/1280px-Pieter_Bruegel_the_Elder_-_The_Tower_of_Babel_%28Vienna%29_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pieter_Bruegel_the_Elder_-_The_Tower_of_Babel_%28Vienna%29_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pieter_Bruegel_the_Elder_-_The_Tower_of_Babel_%28Vienna%29_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1563
- **Type:** Oil on panel
- **Where to see it:** Kunsthistorisches Museum, Vienna
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Tower%20of%20Babel%20Pieter%20Bruegel%20the%20Elder)

Bruegel painted the Tower of Babel at least three times. This large panel of 1563 is in Vienna, and a smaller version is in Rotterdam. The biblical tower, built by humans trying to reach heaven, rises in tiers inspired by the Roman Colosseum, which Bruegel had seen in Rome. It is already crumbling and leaning even as construction continues. Hundreds of tiny workers, cranes, scaffolds and ships at the harbour are shown in meticulous detail. In the foreground, King Nimrod visits the stonemasons, who kneel before him. In a busy commercial city like Antwerp, the painting could be read as a warning against pride and ambition and perhaps a comment on the city's many languages.

### The Hunters in the Snow (1565)

[![The Hunters in the Snow](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d8/Pieter_Bruegel_the_Elder_-_Hunters_in_the_Snow_%28Winter%29_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Pieter_Bruegel_the_Elder_-_Hunters_in_the_Snow_%28Winter%29_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pieter_Bruegel_the_Elder_-_Hunters_in_the_Snow_%28Winter%29_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pieter_Bruegel_the_Elder_-_Hunters_in_the_Snow_%28Winter%29_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1565
- **Type:** Oil on panel
- **Where to see it:** Kunsthistorisches Museum, Vienna
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Hunters%20in%20the%20Snow%20Pieter%20Bruegel%20the%20Elder)

This 1565 painting belongs to a series of six showing the times of the year, made for the Antwerp merchant Nicolaes Jongelinck. Five survive, three of them in Vienna. Weary hunters trudge back to their village with their hounds and only a single fox to show for their efforts. A sign hangs askew outside an inn where peasants are singeing a pig over a fire. Below, villagers skate and play on frozen ponds, and the landscape stretches out to jagged Alpine peaks under a cold greenish sky. Crows perch on bare trees. The painting captures the mood of a whole season, and it was painted during one of the harshest winters of the 'Little Ice Age'.
