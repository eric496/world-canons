# Hieronymus Bosch

> Bosch painted teeming moral allegories full of hybrid creatures, temptations and punishments. His imagination later made him a hero to the Surrealists.

## Biography

Jheronimus van Aken took the name Bosch from his home town of 's-Hertogenbosch in the Duchy of Brabant, where his family were painters and where he spent almost his whole life. Few documents about him survive. We know that he married into a wealthy family and was a prominent member of the religious Brotherhood of Our Lady, and that his patrons included the Burgundian and Habsburg nobility. Only around 25 paintings are firmly attributed to him today.

Bosch painted moral and religious allegories crowded with tiny figures, hybrid monsters, strange architecture and burning landscapes, warning of sin, folly and damnation. His meaning has been debated for centuries, with readings ranging from orthodox sermon to secret heresy. Philip II of Spain was an avid collector, which is why the Prado holds many of his greatest works. His teeming imagination influenced Bruegel and, centuries later, was claimed by the Surrealists.

## Key artworks

### The Garden of Earthly Delights (c. 1490–1510)

[![The Garden of Earthly Delights](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/96/The_Garden_of_earthly_delights.jpg/1280px-The_Garden_of_earthly_delights.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Garden_of_earthly_delights.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Garden_of_earthly_delights.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1490–1510
- **Type:** Oil on oak panel (triptych)
- **Where to see it:** Museo del Prado, Madrid
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Garden%20of%20Earthly%20Delights%20Hieronymus%20Bosch)

Painted around 1490–1510, probably for the Nassau family's palace in Brussels, this triptych shows the world's history from creation to damnation. Closed, its shutters show the world on the third day of creation, painted in grey tones inside a crystal sphere. Open, the left panel shows God presenting Eve to Adam in Eden. The vast central panel is a paradise of naked men and women frolicking with giant birds, oversized fruit and fantastic structures, perhaps humanity lost in sensual pleasure. The right panel is a nightmarish hell where sinners are tortured with giant musical instruments, gamblers are punished, and a pale 'Tree-Man' gazes out from among the flames. Philip II acquired it in 1593, and it is now the most famous work in the Prado.
