## Biography

Tiziano Vecellio came to Venice from the Alpine town of Pieve di Cadore as a boy. He trained with Giovanni Bellini, worked with Giorgione and, after Bellini's death in 1516, became the city's leading painter. His fame spread across Europe. He painted for the dukes of Ferrara, Mantua and Urbino, and above all for the Emperor Charles V, who made him a Count Palatine and a knight, and for Philip II of Spain. For Philip he painted a series of mythologies he called 'poesie'. He died in Venice in 1576 during a plague epidemic, in his late eighties.

Titian built his paintings from colour rather than drawing, and he excelled in every genre: altarpieces, mythologies, nudes and portraits that set the pattern for state portraiture. In old age his brushwork became extraordinarily loose. He reportedly painted with his fingers and returned to canvases again and again, creating flickering surfaces that seemed almost unfinished. Rubens, Velázquez, Rembrandt, Delacroix and the Impressionists all learned from him.

### Assumption of the Virgin (1516–18)

[![Assumption of the Virgin](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9e/Tizian_041.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tizian_041.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tizian_041.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1516–18
- **Type:** Oil on panel
- **Where to see it:** Basilica dei Frari, Venice
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Assumption%20of%20the%20Virgin%20Titian%20%28Tiziano%20Vecellio%29)

This enormous altarpiece, nearly 7 metres high, was installed on the high altar of the Franciscan church of Santa Maria Gloriosa dei Frari in Venice in 1518. It shows the Virgin Mary carried up to heaven. Below, the apostles reach up in amazement. In the middle, Mary rises on a cloud, borne by a swirl of cherubs, her red robe glowing. Above, God the Father leans down to receive her in a golden sky. The bold scale, energetic movement and blazing colour were unprecedented in Venice, and the picture announced Titian as the city's leading painter. It is still in place in the church for which it was made.

### Venus of Urbino (1538)

[![Venus of Urbino](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bb/Tiziano_-_Venere_di_Urbino_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Tiziano_-_Venere_di_Urbino_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tiziano_-_Venere_di_Urbino_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tiziano_-_Venere_di_Urbino_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1538
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Uffizi Gallery, Florence
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Venus%20of%20Urbino%20Titian%20%28Tiziano%20Vecellio%29)

Painted in 1538 and bought by Guidobaldo della Rovere, soon to be Duke of Urbino, this canvas shows a nude woman reclining on a white sheet, looking directly at the viewer with a small bouquet of roses in her hand. A little dog sleeps at her feet. In the background, two maids search a chest (cassone) for clothes. The painting has been read as a celebration of married love, with the dog symbolising fidelity and the chests a bride's trousseau, and also as a frankly sensual image for private pleasure. It transformed Giorgione's sleeping Venus into a wakeful, knowing woman, and it later inspired Manet's Olympia. It is in the Uffizi.
