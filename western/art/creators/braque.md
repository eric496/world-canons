# Georges Braque

**1882 – 1963** · French  
Born: Argenteuil, France · Died: Paris  
Movements: [Cubism](../movements/cubism.md)  

> Braque developed Cubism side by side with Picasso between 1908 and 1914 and introduced papier collé (pasted paper).

## Biography

Georges Braque was born in Argenteuil and grew up in Le Havre, training like his father and grandfather as a decorative house painter. In Paris he joined the Fauves in 1906–07. Meeting Picasso and seeing Les Demoiselles d'Avignon, together with the 1907 Cézanne retrospective, transformed his work. His landscapes of L'Estaque (1908) led a critic to speak of 'little cubes', and from 1909 to 1914 he and Picasso developed Cubism so closely together that their works can be hard to tell apart. Braque compared them to mountaineers roped together. Braque invented papier collé (pasted paper) in 1912 and introduced stencilled letters, imitation wood grain and sand into painting. Badly wounded in the head in 1915, he recovered slowly and went on to develop a calmer, more lyrical style.

Braque's later still lifes, his series of Studio interiors and his images of birds are meditative explorations of space and texture. In 1953 he painted the ceiling of the Henri II room in the Louvre. In 1961 he became the first living artist to have a retrospective at the Louvre, and he received a state funeral in 1963.

## Key artworks

### Violin and Candlestick (1910)

> 🖼️ **[View it at SFMOMA →](https://www.sfmoma.org/artwork/89.78/)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1910
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** San Francisco Museum of Modern Art
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Violin%20and%20Candlestick%20Georges%20Braque)

Painted in 1910 at the height of Analytic Cubism, this painting in the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art breaks a still life of a violin and a candlestick into overlapping fragments of planes and facets in muted browns and greys, so that object and space merge. Fragments of the violin's scroll, strings and f-holes can be made out at the lower left, and the candlestick's base and flame at the centre. The painting shows several viewpoints at once and invites the viewer to reconstruct the objects in the mind.
