# Salvador Dalí

**1904 – 1989** · Spanish  
Born: Figueres, Spain · Died: Figueres  
Movements: [Dada & Surrealism](../movements/dada-surrealism.md)  

> The most famous Surrealist, known for meticulously painted dreamscapes and a flamboyant public persona.

## Biography

Salvador Dalí was born in Figueres, Catalonia, into a prosperous family. An older brother, also named Salvador, had died before he was born, and Dalí later claimed this haunted him. He studied in Madrid, where he befriended the poet Federico García Lorca and the filmmaker Luis Buñuel, with whom he made the Surrealist film Un Chien Andalou (1929). In 1929 he met Gala, who became his muse, wife and manager, and he joined the Surrealist group in Paris. He was later expelled by André Breton for his politics and commercialism. He spent the years 1940–48 in the United States, where he became a celebrity, designed a dream sequence for Hitchcock's Spellbound and worked with Disney. He returned to Catalonia, living at Port Lligat. He created the Dalí Theatre-Museum in Figueres, which opened in 1974 and where he is buried. He died in 1989.

Dalí developed what he called the 'paranoiac-critical method', a way of tapping irrational associations and double images. He painted his hallucinatory dreamscapes with meticulous, old-master precision. Later he turned to religious, scientific and 'nuclear mysticism' subjects. His flamboyant persona, upturned moustache and showmanship made him one of the most famous artists of the 20th century.

## Key artworks

### The Persistence of Memory (1931)

> 🖼️ **[View it at MoMA →](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/79018)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1931
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Persistence%20of%20Memory%20Salvador%20Dal%C3%AD)

This small painting of 1931, only about 24 by 33 cm, is one of the most famous images of Surrealism. In a dreamlike landscape based on the rocky coast of Cap de Creus near Dalí's home, pocket watches melt and droop over a tree branch, the edge of a table and a strange fleshy creature lying on the ground, which resembles Dalí's own profile with long eyelashes. Ants crawl over a closed orange watch. Dalí said the soft watches were inspired by Camembert cheese melting in the sun. The painting suggests the fluidity and unreliability of time and memory in dreams. It has been in the Museum of Modern Art in New York since 1934.
