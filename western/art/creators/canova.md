## Biography

Antonio Canova was the grandson of a stonemason in Possagno, in the Venetian foothills. He trained in Venice and moved to Rome in 1781, where his tomb of Pope Clement XIV quickly made him famous. He became the most celebrated sculptor in Europe and worked for popes, the Napoleonic family, Russian tsars and British collectors. His works include Pauline Bonaparte Borghese as Venus Victrix, a colossal nude Napoleon as Mars the Peacemaker (now in Apsley House, London) and the Three Graces. In 1815 the Pope sent him to Paris to negotiate the return of artworks looted by Napoleon, and he was rewarded with a noble title.

Canova's marble is famous for its almost luminous smoothness, achieved with special polishing and a final wax patina, and for its ideal, graceful figures and delicate balance of sensuality and calm. He is the defining sculptor of Neoclassicism. He designed and paid for a temple in his home town, where he is buried, and his studio models are kept in a museum there.

## Key artworks

### Psyche Revived by Cupid's Kiss (1787–93)

[![Psyche Revived by Cupid's Kiss](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/0_Psych%C3%A9_ranim%C3%A9e_par_le_baiser_de_l%27Amour_-_Canova_-_Louvre_1.JPG/1280px-0_Psych%C3%A9_ranim%C3%A9e_par_le_baiser_de_l%27Amour_-_Canova_-_Louvre_1.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:0_Psych%C3%A9_ranim%C3%A9e_par_le_baiser_de_l%27Amour_-_Canova_-_Louvre_1.JPG)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:0_Psych%C3%A9_ranim%C3%A9e_par_le_baiser_de_l%27Amour_-_Canova_-_Louvre_1.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1787–93
- **Type:** Marble sculpture
- **Where to see it:** Musée du Louvre, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Psyche%20Revived%20by%20Cupid%27s%20Kiss%20Antonio%20Canova)

Commissioned in 1787 by the British collector Colonel John Campbell and completed in 1793, this marble group in the Louvre is based on the tale of Cupid and Psyche from Apuleius's Golden Ass. Psyche has opened a forbidden flask and fallen into a deathlike sleep. Cupid descends and revives her with a kiss. The two bodies form an elegant X: her arms rise to encircle his head, and his outspread wings frame the composition. The moment just before the kiss creates a delicate tension. The flawless polish of the marble makes skin appear soft and luminous. A second version is in the Hermitage in St Petersburg.
