# René Magritte

**1898 – 1967** · Belgian  
Born: Lessines, Belgium · Died: Brussels  
Movements: [Dada & Surrealism](../movements/dada-surrealism.md)  

> A Surrealist who painted ordinary objects with deadpan clarity in impossible combinations, questioning how images and words relate to reality.

## Biography

René Magritte was born in Lessines in Belgium. His mother died when he was 13. He studied at the Académie Royale des Beaux-Arts in Brussels and worked in a wallpaper factory and as a commercial and poster designer. Influenced by Giorgio de Chirico, he painted his first Surrealist work, The Lost Jockey, in 1926. From 1927 to 1930 he lived near Paris and took part in the Surrealist group around André Breton, but fell out with Breton and returned to Brussels, where he spent the rest of his life with his wife Georgette. He briefly experimented with a deliberately crude 'vache' style and an Impressionist-like style in the 1940s, then returned to his classic manner. He died in Brussels in 1967.

Magritte painted ordinary objects, such as bowler hats, apples, pipes, clouds, windows and curtains, with a precise, impersonal technique, but placed them in impossible combinations, scales and contexts. His aim was to make the familiar strange and to question the relationship between images, words and reality. His images have been endlessly adopted by advertising, album covers and film, and influenced Pop and Conceptual Art.

### The Treachery of Images ('Ceci n'est pas une pipe') (1929)

> 🖼️ **[View it at LACMA →](https://collections.lacma.org/object/31931)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1929
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Los Angeles County Museum of Art
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Treachery%20of%20Images%20%28%27Ceci%20n%27est%20pas%20une%20pipe%27%29%20Ren%C3%A9%20Magritte)

Magritte painted this image in 1929. A carefully drawn pipe, like an image in an advertisement or a schoolbook, floats against a plain background, and below it is a neat handwritten caption in French: 'This is not a pipe.' The paradox is simple and profound: of course it is not a pipe, but a painted image of a pipe. The painting questions the relationship between objects, images and words, and the philosopher Michel Foucault wrote a famous essay on it. It is in the Los Angeles County Museum of Art.

### The Son of Man (1964)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Wikipedia (private collection, no museum page) →](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Son_of_Man)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1964
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Private collection
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Son%20of%20Man%20Ren%C3%A9%20Magritte)

Magritte painted this self-portrait in 1964 for his friend and patron, the lawyer Harry Torczyner. A man in an overcoat, red tie and bowler hat stands in front of a low wall with the sea and a cloudy sky behind him. His face is almost entirely hidden by a green apple floating in front of it, and only the edge of one eye peeps out. Magritte said that everything we see hides something else, and that we always want to see what is hidden by what we see. The title and the apple suggest Adam and the Fall. It is in a private collection and is one of the most reproduced images of Surrealism.
