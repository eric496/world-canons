> Raphael synthesised the lessons of Leonardo and Michelangelo into a style of perfect clarity and grace. He ran a large workshop in Rome for the papacy before dying at 37.

## Biography

Raffaello Sanzio was the son of Giovanni Santi, a painter and poet at the cultivated court of Urbino. He was orphaned young and absorbed the graceful style of Pietro Perugino before moving to Florence around 1504, where he studied Leonardo and Michelangelo. In 1508 Julius II brought him to Rome to decorate the papal apartments (the Stanze). Under Leo X he became the most important artist in the city: painter, designer of tapestries for the Sistine Chapel, architect of St Peter's after Bramante, and superintendent of Roman antiquities. He died on his 37th birthday in 1520 and was buried in the Pantheon.

Raphael's art combines clarity, harmony and grace. His Madonnas became the most beloved religious images in Europe, and his large frescoes organise dozens of figures with effortless balance. He ran an efficient workshop and worked closely with printmakers, so his designs spread widely. For three centuries academies held him up as the model of perfect painting, which is why the 19th-century Pre-Raphaelites defined themselves against him.

### The School of Athens (1509–11)

[![The School of Athens](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/49/%22The_School_of_Athens%22_by_Raffaello_Sanzio_da_Urbino.jpg/1280px-%22The_School_of_Athens%22_by_Raffaello_Sanzio_da_Urbino.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%22The_School_of_Athens%22_by_Raffaello_Sanzio_da_Urbino.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%22The_School_of_Athens%22_by_Raffaello_Sanzio_da_Urbino.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1509–11
- **Type:** Fresco
- **Where to see it:** Stanza della Segnatura, Apostolic Palace, Vatican City
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20School%20of%20Athens%20Raphael%20%28Raffaello%20Sanzio%29)

Painted in 1509–11 in the Stanza della Segnatura, probably Julius II's library, this fresco celebrates philosophy and faces Raphael's Disputation of the Holy Sacrament, which celebrates theology. Under vast coffered vaults inspired by Bramante's designs for St Peter's, the greatest thinkers of antiquity gather. At the centre Plato, with Leonardo's features, points upward to the realm of ideas, while Aristotle gestures towards the earth. Around them are Socrates, Pythagoras, Euclid with a compass (probably given Bramante's face) and Diogenes sprawled on the steps. The brooding figure of Heraclitus in front is thought to portray Michelangelo, who was painting the Sistine ceiling nearby. Raphael included his own portrait looking out at the far right.

### Sistine Madonna (1512–13)

[![Sistine Madonna](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7a/RAFAEL_-_Madonna_Sixtina_%28Gem%C3%A4ldegalerie_Alter_Meister%2C_Dresden%2C_1513-14._%C3%93leo_sobre_lienzo%2C_265_x_196_cm%29.jpg/1280px-RAFAEL_-_Madonna_Sixtina_%28Gem%C3%A4ldegalerie_Alter_Meister%2C_Dresden%2C_1513-14._%C3%93leo_sobre_lienzo%2C_265_x_196_cm%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:RAFAEL_-_Madonna_Sixtina_%28Gem%C3%A4ldegalerie_Alter_Meister%2C_Dresden%2C_1513-14._%C3%93leo_sobre_lienzo%2C_265_x_196_cm%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:RAFAEL_-_Madonna_Sixtina_%28Gem%C3%A4ldegalerie_Alter_Meister%2C_Dresden%2C_1513-14._%C3%93leo_sobre_lienzo%2C_265_x_196_cm%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1512–13
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Gemäldegalerie Alte Meister, Dresden
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sistine%20Madonna%20Raphael%20%28Raffaello%20Sanzio%29)

Raphael painted this altarpiece in 1512–13 for the Benedictine church of San Sisto in Piacenza. The Virgin, carrying the Christ Child, walks forward on clouds as green curtains are drawn back, flanked by St Sixtus and St Barbara. Below, two winged cherubs lean on the ledge looking up dreamily. They have become some of the most reproduced images in the world, appearing on everything from postcards to stamps. Augustus III, King of Poland and Elector of Saxony, bought the painting in 1754 for Dresden. Soviet troops took it to Moscow in 1945, and it was returned to Dresden in 1955.
