# Masaccio

**1401 – 1428** · Italian  
Born: San Giovanni Valdarno, near Florence · Died: Rome  
Movements: [Early Italian Renaissance](../movements/early-renaissance.md)  

> In a career of barely six years, Masaccio applied perspective and solid, light-modelled figures to painting. He died at 26.

## Biography

Tommaso di Ser Giovanni, nicknamed Masaccio ('clumsy' or 'big Tom'), joined the Florentine painters' guild in 1422. He worked alongside the older painter Masolino, most famously on the Brancacci Chapel frescoes in Santa Maria del Carmine. He also painted the Pisa Polyptych of 1426, now divided among several museums. He went to Rome around 1428 and died there at only 26, in circumstances that remain unclear.

In barely six years Masaccio did for painting what Brunelleschi did for architecture and Donatello for sculpture. He gave figures real weight under a single consistent light, used scientific perspective, and showed emotion with sober dignity. Vasari wrote that the Brancacci Chapel became a school for later artists. The young Michelangelo copied its figures, and a fist fight there with a rival is said to have broken his nose.

### The Tribute Money (c. 1425)

[![The Tribute Money](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/The_Tribute_Money_by_Masaccio.jpg/1280px-The_Tribute_Money_by_Masaccio.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Tribute_Money_by_Masaccio.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Tribute_Money_by_Masaccio.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1425
- **Type:** Fresco
- **Where to see it:** Brancacci Chapel, Santa Maria del Carmine, Florence
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Tribute%20Money%20Masaccio)

This fresco in the Brancacci Chapel tells the story from Matthew's Gospel in which a tax collector asks for the temple tax. In the centre, Christ tells Peter to find a coin in the mouth of a fish. At the left, Peter crouches by the lake to take it out, and at the right he pays the collector. This is 'continuous narrative', with one figure appearing three times in a single scene. The apostles stand around Christ like solid statues, their robes falling with real weight. Light comes consistently from the direction of the chapel's actual window, and the vanishing point of the building at right lies at Christ's head. The mountains fade into hazy distance, an early use of atmospheric perspective.

### Holy Trinity (c. 1427)

[![Holy Trinity](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d2/Masaccio%2C_trinit%C3%A0.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Masaccio%2C_trinit%C3%A0.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Masaccio%2C_trinit%C3%A0.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1427
- **Type:** Fresco
- **Where to see it:** Santa Maria Novella, Florence
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Holy%20Trinity%20Masaccio)

Painted on a wall of Santa Maria Novella around 1427, the Holy Trinity creates the illusion of a deep chapel opening into the church. It has a coffered barrel vault and classical columns that closely follow Brunelleschi's architecture, and it is among the earliest paintings constructed with mathematically correct perspective. God the Father stands behind the crucified Christ with the dove of the Holy Spirit between them, flanked by the Virgin and St John. The two donors kneel outside the painted chapel, in the viewer's space. Below lies a painted tomb with a skeleton and an inscription warning: 'I was once what you are, and what I am you also will be.'
