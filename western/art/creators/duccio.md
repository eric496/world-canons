# Duccio di Buoninsegna

**c. 1255 – c. 1319** · Italian  
Born: Siena · Died: Siena  
Movements: [Late Gothic & Proto-Renaissance](../movements/proto-renaissance.md)  

> The founder of the Sienese school, combining Byzantine elegance and gold with new tenderness and narrative clarity.

## Biography

Duccio di Buoninsegna was the founding master of Sienese painting. He is first documented in 1278 and died around 1319. In 1285 he received the commission for the huge Rucellai Madonna for the Florentine church of Santa Maria Novella, now in the Uffizi. That a Sienese painter won a Florentine commission shows how high his reputation stood. Records suggest he was often fined and in debt, but his workshop shaped Sienese art for generations, through pupils such as Simone Martini and the Lorenzetti brothers.

Duccio kept the gold, line and elegance of Byzantine painting but added warmth, tender human gesture and clear, lively storytelling in small narrative scenes. Where Florentine painting after Giotto stressed sculptural mass, the Sienese tradition he founded valued colour, grace and lyrical detail. That contrast ran through Italian painting for the rest of the Gothic age.

## Key artworks

### Maestà (1308–11)

[![Maestà](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/Duccio_maesta1021.jpg/1280px-Duccio_maesta1021.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Duccio_maesta1021.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Duccio_maesta1021.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1308–11
- **Type:** Tempera and gold on panel
- **Where to see it:** Museo dell'Opera del Duomo, Siena
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Maest%C3%A0%20Duccio%20di%20Buoninsegna)

Siena was dedicated to the Virgin, and its cathedral commissioned Duccio in 1308 to paint a new high altarpiece. When it was finished in June 1311, the painting was carried from his workshop to the cathedral in a solemn procession with the whole city following, bells ringing and candles lit. The front shows the Virgin and Child enthroned among saints and angels, with Siena's four patron saints kneeling in front. The back held dozens of small scenes from the life and Passion of Christ, full of vivid storytelling. The altarpiece was sawn apart in 1771. Most of it is in Siena's cathedral museum, but some predella and back panels were sold and are now in London, New York, Washington and elsewhere.
