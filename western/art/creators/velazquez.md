## Biography

Diego Velázquez was born in Seville and trained with the painter and theorist Francisco Pacheco, whose daughter Juana he married. His early works include bodegones, kitchen scenes with figures painted with sober realism. In 1623 he became painter to the young King Philip IV in Madrid, where he spent the rest of his career. He took on increasingly important court offices, including palace chamberlain, organising royal events. He travelled to Italy twice, in 1629–31 and 1649–51, studying Titian and the antique and buying art for the royal collection. In 1659 he was admitted to the noble Order of Santiago, and he died in Madrid the following year.

Velázquez's art moved from early tight realism to an astonishingly free, economical brushwork. Seen up close his paintings dissolve into loose dabs of paint, but from a distance they resolve into silk, lace, flesh and air. He painted kings, the court's jesters and dwarfs, mythologies and the only surviving Spanish female nude of the period, the Rokeby Venus. Manet called him 'the painter of painters'. Goya, the Impressionists, Picasso and Francis Bacon all studied him.

### Portrait of Innocent X (1650)

[![Portrait of Innocent X](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Retrato_del_Papa_Inocencio_X._Roma%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg/1280px-Retrato_del_Papa_Inocencio_X._Roma%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Retrato_del_Papa_Inocencio_X._Roma%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Retrato_del_Papa_Inocencio_X._Roma%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1650
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Galleria Doria Pamphilj, Rome
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Portrait%20of%20Innocent%20X%20Diego%20Vel%C3%A1zquez)

Velázquez painted Pope Innocent X during his second visit to Rome in 1650. The elderly pope sits in a red armchair against a red curtain, his red cap and cape set against white lace. He fixes the viewer with a suspicious, shrewd glare. The brushwork is wonderfully loose, especially in the shimmering silks. According to tradition, the pope remarked that it was 'too true'. The portrait remains in the Doria Pamphilj family's gallery in Rome. In the 20th century it obsessed Francis Bacon, who painted dozens of 'screaming popes' based on it without ever seeing the original.

### Las Meninas (1656)

[![Las Meninas](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Las_Meninas%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez%2C_from_Prado_in_Google_Earth.jpg/1280px-Las_Meninas%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez%2C_from_Prado_in_Google_Earth.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Las_Meninas%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez%2C_from_Prado_in_Google_Earth.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Las_Meninas%2C_by_Diego_Vel%C3%A1zquez%2C_from_Prado_in_Google_Earth.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1656
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Museo del Prado, Madrid
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Las%20Meninas%20Diego%20Vel%C3%A1zquez)

This large painting of 1656, in the Prado, is one of the most analysed works in Western art. In a room of the Alcázar palace in Madrid, the five-year-old Infanta Margarita Teresa stands at the centre, attended by two maids of honour (the meninas), a chaperone, a bodyguard, the dwarfs Maribárbola and Nicolasito, and a sleepy mastiff. At the left, Velázquez himself stands at a huge canvas, brush in hand, wearing the red cross of the Order of Santiago, probably added after his knighthood in 1659. In a mirror on the back wall we see King Philip IV and Queen Mariana, apparently standing where we are. The picture raises endless questions about who is looking at whom and what is being painted. In 1957 Picasso painted 58 variations on it, now in the Museu Picasso in Barcelona.
