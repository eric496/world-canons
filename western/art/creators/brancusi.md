# Constantin Brâncuși

**1876 – 1957** · Romanian-French  
Born: Hobița, Romania · Died: Paris  
Movements: [Early Abstraction](../movements/abstraction.md)  

> A pioneer of modern sculpture who reduced forms to their pure essence in polished bronze, marble and carved wood.

## Biography

Constantin Brâncuși was born into a peasant family in the village of Hobița in Romania. He worked as a shepherd and a shop boy as a child, studied at the school of arts and crafts in Craiova and the Bucharest School of Fine Arts, and then made his way to Paris in 1904, partly on foot. He worked briefly as an assistant in Rodin's studio in 1907 but left after a short time, reportedly saying that nothing grows in the shadow of great trees. He spent the rest of his life in Paris. His ensemble at Târgu Jiu in Romania, including the Endless Column (1938), is a memorial to Romanian soldiers of the First World War. He bequeathed his studio and its contents to the French state, and it has been reconstructed beside the Centre Pompidou.

Brâncuși carved directly in stone and wood and refined his forms in polished bronze. Rejecting the expressive modelling of Rodin, he pursued the simplified essence of his subjects in a few recurring themes: The Kiss, the Sleeping Muse, the head, the bird and the column. His pure, abstract forms and his treatment of bases as part of the sculpture make him one of the founders of modern sculpture and a forerunner of Minimalism.

## Key artworks

### Bird in Space (1923 onward (series))

> 🖼️ **[View it at MoMA (1928 bronze) →](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/81033)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1923 onward (series)
- **Type:** Polished bronze and marble (series)
- **Where to see it:** Versions at MoMA (1928 bronze), the Met, Philadelphia Museum of Art, Seattle Art Museum and others
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Bird%20in%20Space%20Constantin%20Br%C3%A2ncu%C8%99i)

Brâncuși began the theme of the bird around 1910 with Maiastra, a magic bird from Romanian folklore. In 1923 he made the first Bird in Space, and he went on to make around 16 versions in marble and polished bronze. The bird's wings and feathers are eliminated, leaving a slender, tapering form that rises from a narrow foot and seems to soar, the essence of flight rather than an image of a bird. When a version was shipped to the United States in 1926, customs officials refused to classify it as art and taxed it as a manufactured metal object. In 1928 a court ruled in Brâncuși's favour, a landmark decision for modern art. Versions are in the Museum of Modern Art, the Metropolitan Museum, the Philadelphia Museum of Art and elsewhere.
