## Biography

Auguste Rodin was born in Paris to a working-class family. He failed three times to enter the École des Beaux-Arts and spent years as a decorative craftsman, including six years in Belgium. A trip to Italy in 1875 to see Michelangelo transformed him. His first major statue, The Age of Bronze (1877), was so lifelike that critics accused him of casting it from a living model. In 1880 he was commissioned to make a monumental portal, The Gates of Hell, based on Dante. He worked on it for the rest of his life and generated many of his independent sculptures from it. His other major works include The Burghers of Calais and the Monument to Balzac, which was rejected by its commissioners in 1898. His pupil and lover Camille Claudel was a brilliant sculptor in her own right. He donated his works to the state, and his Paris residence, the Hôtel Biron, opened as the Musée Rodin in 1919.

Rodin is considered the father of modern sculpture. He modelled clay with rough, expressive, light-catching surfaces, made fragments and partial figures into finished works, and reused and recombined forms. His sculptures convey intense inner emotion and physical energy, and his influence on Brâncuși, Matisse and later sculptors was enormous.

### The Thinker (conceived 1880)

[![The Thinker](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a4/Le_Penseur_by_Rodin_%28Kunsthalle_Bielefeld%29_2014-04-10.JPG/1280px-Le_Penseur_by_Rodin_%28Kunsthalle_Bielefeld%29_2014-04-10.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Le_Penseur_by_Rodin_%28Kunsthalle_Bielefeld%29_2014-04-10.JPG)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Le_Penseur_by_Rodin_%28Kunsthalle_Bielefeld%29_2014-04-10.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** conceived 1880
- **Type:** Bronze sculpture
- **Where to see it:** Musée Rodin, Paris (and many casts)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Thinker%20Auguste%20Rodin)

Rodin's Thinker was conceived around 1880 as a figure of the poet Dante, seated above the doorway of his monumental Gates of Hell and contemplating the circles of the Inferno below. A powerful nude man sits on a rock, his chin resting on the back of his hand and his whole body tense with the effort of thought. Rodin later exhibited it as an independent sculpture, and in 1902–04 he enlarged it to monumental size. In 1906 a cast was placed in front of the Panthéon in Paris. More than 20 large bronze casts are now in cities around the world, and one was placed on Rodin's own grave at Meudon. It has become a universal symbol of philosophy and intellectual effort.

### The Kiss (conceived 1882)

[![The Kiss](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Rodin_-_Le_Baiser_06.jpg/1280px-Rodin_-_Le_Baiser_06.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rodin_-_Le_Baiser_06.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rodin_-_Le_Baiser_06.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** conceived 1882
- **Type:** Marble sculpture
- **Where to see it:** Musée Rodin, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Kiss%20Auguste%20Rodin)

This sculpture of lovers in an embrace represents Paolo and Francesca from Dante's Inferno. Francesca, married to Paolo's brother, fell in love with him while they were reading together about Lancelot and Guinevere, and they were killed by her husband. Rodin originally designed the group for the Gates of Hell but removed it, perhaps because its happy sensuality did not fit the theme of suffering. The French state commissioned this large marble version, now in the Musée Rodin, in 1888. The book the lovers were reading is still visible in Paolo's hand. Other marble versions are in Tate and in Copenhagen.
