## Biography

Leonardo was the illegitimate son of a Florentine notary, born in the hill town of Vinci. He trained in the workshop of Andrea del Verrocchio, where, according to Vasari, the angel he painted in Verrocchio's Baptism of Christ outshone his master's work. In 1482 he moved to Milan in the service of Duke Ludovico Sforza, working as painter, engineer, pageant designer and sculptor of a colossal equestrian monument that was never cast. After the French invaded Milan in 1499 he moved between Florence, Milan and Rome. He spent his last years in France as the guest of King Francis I and died at Amboise in 1519.

Leonardo finished few paintings, but each changed the art. He invented sfumato, the soft smoky blending of light and shade, and gave his figures inner psychological life. His notebooks, of which around 7,000 pages survive, are written in mirror script. They record studies in anatomy, optics, geology, botany, flight, hydraulics and war machines, based on careful observation centuries ahead of their time. He has become the archetype of the 'Renaissance man'.

### Lady with an Ermine (c. 1489–91)

[![Lady with an Ermine](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Lady_with_an_Ermine_-_Leonardo_da_Vinci_%28adjusted_levels%29.jpg/1280px-Lady_with_an_Ermine_-_Leonardo_da_Vinci_%28adjusted_levels%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lady_with_an_Ermine_-_Leonardo_da_Vinci_%28adjusted_levels%29.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lady_with_an_Ermine_-_Leonardo_da_Vinci_%28adjusted_levels%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1489–91
- **Type:** Oil on walnut panel
- **Where to see it:** Czartoryski Museum (National Museum in Kraków)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Lady%20with%20an%20Ermine%20Leonardo%20da%20Vinci)

Painted around 1489–91 in Milan, this portrait shows Cecilia Gallerani, the teenage mistress of Duke Ludovico Sforza. She turns her head and body in opposite directions as if responding to someone outside the picture, a dynamic pose new to portraiture. She holds an ermine, a symbol of purity, which also puns on her name (the Greek for ermine is 'galê') and refers to Ludovico, who belonged to the Order of the Ermine. Her elegant hand and the animal's lithe body echo each other. The painting was bought by the Polish Czartoryski family around 1800, seized by the Nazis during the Second World War and recovered in 1946. It is now in the National Museum in Kraków.

### Vitruvian Man (c. 1490)

[![Vitruvian Man](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/22/Da_Vinci_Vitruve_Luc_Viatour.jpg/1280px-Da_Vinci_Vitruve_Luc_Viatour.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Da_Vinci_Vitruve_Luc_Viatour.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Da_Vinci_Vitruve_Luc_Viatour.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1490
- **Type:** Pen and ink on paper
- **Where to see it:** Gallerie dell'Accademia, Venice
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Vitruvian%20Man%20Leonardo%20da%20Vinci)

This pen-and-ink drawing of about 1490 illustrates a passage by the Roman architect Vitruvius, who said that a well-proportioned human body fits into both a circle and a square. Leonardo drew a nude man with two overlapping positions of arms and legs. Standing with arms outstretched, he fits the square. Spread-eagled, his navel becomes the centre of the circle. Leonardo's mirror-written notes around the drawing record the body's proportions. The image has become a universal symbol of science, harmony and the Renaissance belief that man is the measure of all things. It belongs to the Gallerie dell'Accademia in Venice and is exhibited only rarely because it is so fragile.

### The Last Supper (c. 1495–98)

[![The Last Supper](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/The_Last_Supper_-_Leonardo_Da_Vinci_-_High_Resolution_32x16.jpg/1280px-The_Last_Supper_-_Leonardo_Da_Vinci_-_High_Resolution_32x16.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Last_Supper_-_Leonardo_Da_Vinci_-_High_Resolution_32x16.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Last_Supper_-_Leonardo_Da_Vinci_-_High_Resolution_32x16.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1495–98
- **Type:** Tempera and oil on plaster (mural)
- **Where to see it:** Santa Maria delle Grazie, Milan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Last%20Supper%20Leonardo%20da%20Vinci)

Leonardo painted the Last Supper around 1495–98 on the end wall of the refectory of the Dominican convent of Santa Maria delle Grazie in Milan, for Ludovico Sforza. It captures the instant after Jesus says 'One of you will betray me.' The apostles react in four groups of three, with waves of shock, denial and questioning moving out from the calm central figure of Christ. All the lines of the perspective converge on his head. Leonardo rejected traditional fresco and painted with tempera and oil on dry plaster so he could work slowly, but the paint began to flake within his lifetime. The mural survived a 1943 bombing that destroyed much of the refectory and was restored over more than 20 years, finishing in 1999.

### Mona Lisa (c. 1503–1519)

[![Mona Lisa](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ec/Mona_Lisa%2C_by_Leonardo_da_Vinci%2C_from_C2RMF_retouched.jpg/1280px-Mona_Lisa%2C_by_Leonardo_da_Vinci%2C_from_C2RMF_retouched.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mona_Lisa%2C_by_Leonardo_da_Vinci%2C_from_C2RMF_retouched.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mona_Lisa%2C_by_Leonardo_da_Vinci%2C_from_C2RMF_retouched.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1503–1519
- **Type:** Oil on poplar panel
- **Where to see it:** Musée du Louvre, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Mona%20Lisa%20Leonardo%20da%20Vinci)

Leonardo began this portrait around 1503 of Lisa Gherardini, wife of the Florentine silk merchant Francesco del Giocondo, which is why it is also called La Gioconda. He never delivered it, kept working on it for years and took it to France, where it entered the royal collection of Francis I. The sitter's famously elusive smile, the soft sfumato shading around her eyes and mouth, her calm folded hands and the hazy imaginary landscape behind her made the painting a model for portraiture. It became world-famous after it was stolen from the Louvre in 1911 by the Italian handyman Vincenzo Peruggia. It was recovered in Florence in 1913, and today millions of visitors see it each year behind protective glass.
