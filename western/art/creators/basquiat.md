# Jean-Michel Basquiat

**1960 – 1988** · American  
Born: Brooklyn, New York · Died: New York  
Movements: [Neo-Expressionism](../movements/neo-expressionism.md)  

> Basquiat moved from street graffiti (as SAMO) to international fame, painting skulls, crowns, words and Black history. He died at 27.

## Biography

Jean-Michel Basquiat was born in Brooklyn to a Haitian father and a mother of Puerto Rican descent, who took him to museums as a child. At about seven he was hit by a car, and during his recovery his mother gave him a copy of the anatomy textbook Gray's Anatomy, which influenced his later imagery. As a teenager he left home and, with his friend Al Diaz, wrote enigmatic graffiti in Lower Manhattan under the tag SAMO. He showed at the Times Square Show in 1980 and the New York/New Wave exhibition in 1981, and by 1982 he was showing internationally, including at Documenta 7, as one of the youngest artists ever invited. He collaborated with Andy Warhol in 1984–85. He died of a drug overdose in his studio in August 1988, aged 27.

In just under a decade Basquiat produced around 1,000 paintings and more than 1,000 drawings. They combine raw, energetic figuration, skulls, crowns, words, lists, diagrams and cross-outs. They draw on African American and Caribbean history, jazz, comics, anatomy and art history, and confront racism, power and identity. In 2017 one of his skull paintings of 1982 sold at auction for $110.5 million, then a record for an American artist.

## Key artworks

### Untitled (Skull) (1981)

> 🖼️ **[View it at The Broad →](https://www.thebroad.org/art/jean-michel-basquiat/untitled)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1981
- **Type:** Acrylic and oilstick on canvas
- **Where to see it:** The Broad, Los Angeles
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Untitled%20%28Skull%29%20Jean-Michel%20Basquiat)

Basquiat painted this large canvas, about 2 metres high, in 1981, early in his career. It was shown at his first solo gallery exhibition in New York in 1982 and bought that year by the collectors Eli and Edythe Broad. A huge head fills the canvas: part skull, part mask, part anatomical diagram, with gaping eyes, a mouth full of teeth and a brain area filled with lines, colours and patterns. It seems at once alive and dead, revealing both the outside and the inside of the head. It has been read as a self-portrait and as an allusion to African masks and the history of Black identity. It is in The Broad in Los Angeles.
