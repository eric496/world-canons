# François Boucher

**1703 – 1770** · French  
Born: Paris · Died: Paris  
Movements: [Rococo](../movements/rococo.md)  

> The favourite painter of Madame de Pompadour and first painter to Louis XV, the embodiment of Rococo decoration and pastoral fantasy.

## Biography

François Boucher was born in Paris and trained with François Lemoyne. He made his name engraving Watteau's drawings and won the Prix de Rome, studying in Italy from 1727 to 1731. Back in Paris he rose rapidly at court. He designed tapestries for the Beauvais and Gobelins manufactories, becoming director of the Gobelins in 1755, as well as stage sets, porcelain and book illustrations. In 1765 he became First Painter to King Louis XV. His most important patron was Madame de Pompadour, the king's mistress, whose portraits he painted several times and whom he reportedly taught to etch.

Boucher embodied the Rococo: pastel-coloured mythologies of Venus and her nymphs, pastoral scenes of shepherds and shepherdesses, and frankly erotic nudes, painted with brilliant decorative fluency. His prodigious output decorated palaces across Europe. Critics like Diderot attacked him as artificial and immoral, and his reputation fell with the rise of Neoclassicism. His pupil Fragonard carried his style into the next generation.

## Key artworks

### Madame de Pompadour (1756)

[![Madame de Pompadour](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cb/Boucher_Marquise_de_Pompadour_-1756.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Boucher_Marquise_de_Pompadour_-1756.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Boucher_Marquise_de_Pompadour_-1756.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1756
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Alte Pinakothek, Munich
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Madame%20de%20Pompadour%20Fran%C3%A7ois%20Boucher)

Boucher painted this full-length portrait of Jeanne-Antoinette Poisson, Marquise de Pompadour, mistress of Louis XV, in 1756. She reclines on a sofa in a magnificent sea-green silk gown trimmed with pink ribbons and roses. Around her are signs of her intellectual and artistic patronage: books, a writing desk with letters and a quill, engravings and a mirror reflecting a bookcase. Her little spaniel lies at her feet. The portrait presents her as an enlightened patron of the arts and letters rather than just a royal favourite. It is in the Alte Pinakothek in Munich.
