# Mary Cassatt

**1844 – 1926** · American  
Born: Allegheny City (Pittsburgh), Pennsylvania · Died: Le Mesnil-Théribus, France  
Movements: [Impressionism](../movements/impressionism.md)  

> An American in Paris invited by Degas to join the Impressionists, best known for intimate scenes of women and children.

## Biography

Mary Cassatt was born near Pittsburgh into a wealthy family, studied at the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts and moved to Europe in 1866, eventually settling in Paris. After several Salon successes, Degas invited her in 1877 to exhibit with the Impressionists, and she showed with them four times. She was profoundly influenced by an 1890 exhibition of Japanese prints and produced a celebrated series of colour prints combining drypoint and aquatint. In 1893 she painted a large mural, Modern Woman, for the Woman's Building at the Chicago World's Fair, which is now lost. She advised American collectors, especially Louisine and Henry Havemeyer, whose collection went to the Metropolitan Museum. Her eyesight failed from about 1914, and she died in France in 1926.

Cassatt is best known for intimate scenes of women's daily lives: reading, taking tea, dressing, and above all caring for children. She depicted the bond between mother and child without sentimentality. She was a supporter of women's suffrage and the most important American member of the Impressionist movement.

## Key artworks

### The Child's Bath (1893)

[![The Child's Bath](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/72/Mary_Cassatt_-_The_Child%27s_Bath_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Mary_Cassatt_-_The_Child%27s_Bath_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mary_Cassatt_-_The_Child%27s_Bath_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mary_Cassatt_-_The_Child%27s_Bath_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1893
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Art Institute of Chicago
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Child%27s%20Bath%20Mary%20Cassatt)

In this painting of 1893, in the Art Institute of Chicago, a mother in a striped dress sits holding her small child on her lap and washing the child's feet in a basin. Both look down at the water, absorbed. Cassatt's high viewpoint, flat patterns on the carpet, wallpaper and dress, and strong outlines owe much to the Japanese prints she had seen in Paris in 1890. The scene is intimate and ordinary but monumental in its composition, and it is one of Cassatt's best-known works.
