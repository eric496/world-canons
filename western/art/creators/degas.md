# Edgar Degas

**1834 – 1917** · French  
Born: Paris · Died: Paris  
Movements: [Impressionism](../movements/impressionism.md)  

> A founder of the Impressionist exhibitions who preferred the studio to the outdoors. He is known for dancers, racecourses and bold, cropped compositions influenced by photography and Japanese prints.

## Biography

Edgar Degas was born into a wealthy Parisian banking family. He abandoned law to study art, deeply admired Ingres, and spent 1856–59 in Italy copying the old masters. He began as a history painter and portraitist, as in The Bellelli Family. In 1872–73 he visited relatives in New Orleans and painted A Cotton Office. He was a central organiser of the Impressionist exhibitions and showed in seven of the eight, but disliked the label and painting outdoors, preferring to call himself a realist. In later life his eyesight declined. He held fiercely anti-Dreyfusard views, which ended several friendships. He died in Paris in 1917.

Degas observed modern Paris: ballet dancers in rehearsal, racehorses and jockeys, laundresses, milliners, café life and women bathing. He used asymmetrical, cropped compositions and unusual viewpoints inspired by photography and Japanese prints. He was an innovator in pastel, monotype and photography, and his wax sculptures, found in his studio after his death, were cast in bronze. He is one of the great draughtsmen of the 19th century.

### The Dance Class (c. 1873–76)

[![The Dance Class](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Edgar_Degas_-_The_Ballet_Class_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Edgar_Degas_-_The_Ballet_Class_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edgar_Degas_-_The_Ballet_Class_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edgar_Degas_-_The_Ballet_Class_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1873–76
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Musée d'Orsay, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Dance%20Class%20Edgar%20Degas)

Degas painted this picture in 1873–76. It shows a rehearsal room at the Paris Opera, where the elderly ballet master Jules Perrot, leaning on his stick, conducts a lesson. Around him, dancers in white tutus with coloured sashes wait their turn, stretch, chat, adjust their costumes and scratch their backs. One sits on the piano at left, and a watering can on the floor was used to settle dust. The figures are scattered informally around the room, as if seen by an observer passing through. Degas showed it at the Impressionist exhibition of 1876. A similar version is in the Metropolitan Museum. This one is in the Musée d'Orsay.

### L'Absinthe (c. 1875–76)

[![L'Absinthe](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Edgar_Degas_-_In_a_Caf%C3%A9_-_Google_Art_Project_2.jpg/1280px-Edgar_Degas_-_In_a_Caf%C3%A9_-_Google_Art_Project_2.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edgar_Degas_-_In_a_Caf%C3%A9_-_Google_Art_Project_2.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edgar_Degas_-_In_a_Caf%C3%A9_-_Google_Art_Project_2.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1875–76
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Musée d'Orsay, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=L%27Absinthe%20Edgar%20Degas)

Painted in 1875–76, this scene of café life was posed by Degas's friends, the actress Ellen Andrée and the painter Marcellin Desboutin, at the Café de la Nouvelle Athènes in Montmartre, where the Impressionists met. A woman sits slumped and vacant-eyed in front of a glass of cloudy green absinthe beside a pipe-smoking man who looks away from her. The zigzag arrangement of the empty tables and the off-centre composition reinforce the mood of isolation. When it was shown in London in 1893, critics attacked it as a degrading lesson in drunkenness. It is in the Musée d'Orsay.

### Little Dancer Aged Fourteen (1878–81)

[![Little Dancer Aged Fourteen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/The_Little_Fourteen-Year-Old_Dancer_MET_DP-14939-005.jpg/1280px-The_Little_Fourteen-Year-Old_Dancer_MET_DP-14939-005.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Little_Fourteen-Year-Old_Dancer_MET_DP-14939-005.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Little_Fourteen-Year-Old_Dancer_MET_DP-14939-005.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1878–81
- **Type:** Wax sculpture with fabric and hair
- **Where to see it:** National Gallery of Art, Washington, D.C. (original wax)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Little%20Dancer%20Aged%20Fourteen%20Edgar%20Degas)

This is the only sculpture Degas ever exhibited. He showed it at the Impressionist exhibition of 1881. It shows Marie van Goethem, a young Belgian student of the Paris Opera ballet school, standing in the fourth position with her hands clasped behind her back and her chin raised. Degas modelled it in wax and dressed it in a real fabric bodice, a tulle tutu, satin slippers and a wig of real hair tied with a silk ribbon. Critics were shocked by its realism and called the girl ugly and coarse. After Degas's death, around 28 bronze casts were made from the wax, which is now in the National Gallery of Art, Washington.
