# Sandro Botticelli

**c. 1445 – 1510** · Italian  
Born: Florence, Italy · Died: Florence  
Movements: [Early Italian Renaissance](../movements/early-renaissance.md)  

> A Florentine painter close to the Medici circle, known for graceful line and mythological subjects drawn from Neoplatonic philosophy. His work fell out of favour for centuries before being rediscovered in the 19th century.

## Biography

Alessandro Filipepi, known as Sandro Botticelli, trained with Fra Filippo Lippi and became one of the favourite painters of the Medici circle under Lorenzo the Magnificent. In 1481–82 he was one of the artists summoned to Rome to fresco the walls of the new Sistine Chapel. He produced altarpieces, portraits, tondos (round paintings) and a long series of drawings illustrating Dante's Divine Comedy.

Botticelli's art is built on flowing line, graceful elongated figures and a melancholy beauty. His mythological paintings reflect the Neoplatonic philosophy of Lorenzo's circle, in which pagan myth could express Christian and moral truths. In the 1490s the friar Savonarola preached against luxury and pagan art in Florence. Botticelli's later work grows more severe and religious, and he died largely forgotten. The Pre-Raphaelites and Aesthetic movement of the 19th century revived him, and he is now among the most popular painters in the world.

### Primavera (c. 1480)

[![Primavera](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/3c/Botticelli-primavera.jpg/1280px-Botticelli-primavera.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Botticelli-primavera.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Botticelli-primavera.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1480
- **Type:** Tempera on panel
- **Where to see it:** Uffizi Gallery, Florence
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Primavera%20Sandro%20Botticelli)

Primavera ('Spring'), painted around 1480 for a member of the Medici family, is set in an orange grove on a flowery meadow where botanists have identified hundreds of plants. Venus stands at the centre with blindfolded Cupid aiming an arrow above her. At right, the blue wind god Zephyrus seizes the nymph Chloris, who turns into Flora, goddess of spring, scattering flowers. At left, the Three Graces dance in transparent veils, and Mercury raises his staff to disperse clouds. The painting probably celebrates love and marriage in Neoplatonic terms, though its exact meaning is still argued over. It hung in a Medici town house before entering the Uffizi.

### The Birth of Venus (c. 1484–86)

[![The Birth of Venus](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0b/Sandro_Botticelli_-_La_nascita_di_Venere_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg/1280px-Sandro_Botticelli_-_La_nascita_di_Venere_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sandro_Botticelli_-_La_nascita_di_Venere_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sandro_Botticelli_-_La_nascita_di_Venere_-_Google_Art_Project_-_edited.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1484–86
- **Type:** Tempera on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Uffizi Gallery, Florence
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Birth%20of%20Venus%20Sandro%20Botticelli)

Probably painted in the mid-1480s, this large canvas shows Venus, goddess of love, arriving at the shore of Cyprus on a scallop shell after her birth from the sea. At left, the wind god Zephyrus and a female companion blow her ashore amid falling roses, and at right a Hora (goddess of the seasons) rushes to cover her with a flowered cloak. Venus's pose, covering herself with her hand and hair, is borrowed from ancient statues of the modest Venus, but her elongated neck and sloping shoulders are pure Botticelli. It was one of the first large paintings on canvas in Florence and one of the first since antiquity to show a mythological nude on such a scale.
