## Biography

Born Guido di Pietro in the Mugello near Florence, he became a Dominican friar at Fiesole in the 1420s, taking the name Fra Giovanni. He was soon called 'Angelico' for the devotion of his painting and his life, and he was officially beatified by Pope John Paul II in 1982. When Cosimo de' Medici paid for the rebuilding of the convent of San Marco in Florence, Fra Angelico and his assistants frescoed the chapter house, corridors and the friars' cells. He later worked in Rome for Popes Eugenius IV and Nicholas V and decorated the Niccoline Chapel in the Vatican.

Fra Angelico combined the new Renaissance tools of perspective, solid figures and real light with a serene, luminous colour and clarity suited to prayer. The cell frescoes at San Marco are deliberately sparse so they could aid a friar's meditation, but his altarpieces are rich with gold and colour. He was one of the most admired painters in Italy in his lifetime.

## Key artworks

### Annunciation (San Marco) (c. 1440–45)

[![Annunciation (San Marco)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Fra_Angelico_-_Annunciation.jpg/1280px-Fra_Angelico_-_Annunciation.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fra_Angelico_-_Annunciation.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fra_Angelico_-_Annunciation.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1440–45
- **Type:** Fresco
- **Where to see it:** Museo di San Marco, Florence
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Annunciation%20%28San%20Marco%29%20Fra%20Angelico)

Fra Angelico painted this fresco around 1440–45 at the top of the staircase leading to the friars' dormitory in the convent of San Marco, where every friar would see it daily. The angel Gabriel, with rainbow-coloured wings, kneels before the Virgin, who sits on a simple stool and crosses her arms in humble acceptance. The setting is a light, airy loggia with slender columns close to the convent's real architecture by Michelozzo, opening onto an enclosed garden that symbolises her purity. An inscription below asks those passing to say a Hail Mary. The picture's quiet colours and clear space were designed for contemplation.
