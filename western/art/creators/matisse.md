# Henri Matisse

**1869 – 1954** · French  
Born: Le Cateau-Cambrésis, France · Died: Nice  
Movements: [Fauvism & Expressionism](../movements/fauvism-expressionism.md)  

> Leader of the Fauves and, with Picasso, the defining artist of early modernism. In old age he made radiant cut-paper compositions.

## Biography

Henri Matisse was born in Le Cateau-Cambrésis in northern France and trained as a lawyer. He began painting in 1889 while recovering from appendicitis, when his mother gave him a box of paints. He studied in Paris in the studio of the Symbolist painter Gustave Moreau. In the summer of 1905, working at Collioure with André Derain, he developed the explosive colour that led critics to call them the Fauves ('wild beasts'). Collectors including Gertrude and Leo Stein and the Russian Sergei Shchukin supported him. From 1917 he spent most of his time in Nice. After surgery for cancer in 1941 he was often confined to bed or a wheelchair. He then invented a new art of painted paper cut-outs, as in the book Jazz (1947), and designed the Chapel of the Rosary at Vence (1948–51). He died in Nice in 1954.

With Picasso, Matisse was the defining artist of early modernism. He used pure, expressive colour, simplified drawing and flattened space to create works of balance, joy and decorative harmony. He once said he dreamed of an art like a good armchair, offering rest to the tired mind. He worked in painting, sculpture, printmaking, book design and cut paper, and his influence on abstraction, colour field painting and design is incalculable.

### Woman with a Hat (1905)

[![Woman with a Hat](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Matisse-Woman-with-a-Hat.jpg/1280px-Matisse-Woman-with-a-Hat.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Matisse-Woman-with-a-Hat.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Matisse-Woman-with-a-Hat.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1905
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** San Francisco Museum of Modern Art
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Woman%20with%20a%20Hat%20Henri%20Matisse)

Matisse painted this portrait of his wife, Amélie, in 1905. She wears an elaborate hat and holds a fan. Her face is modelled not with natural flesh tones but with streaks of green, yellow, mauve and orange, and the background is an explosion of loose patches of colour. At the 1905 Salon d'Automne, it hung in a room with works by Derain, Vlaminck and others, and the critic Louis Vauxcelles called them 'fauves' (wild beasts). The painting caused a scandal, but it was bought by Leo and Gertrude Stein, which helped launch Matisse's career. It is now in the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art.

### Dance (1910)

[![Dance](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a7/Matissedance.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Matissedance.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Matissedance.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1910
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** State Hermitage Museum, St Petersburg
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dance%20Henri%20Matisse)

In 1909 the Moscow textile magnate Sergei Shchukin commissioned two large decorative panels, Dance and Music, for the staircase of his mansion. In Dance, completed in 1910, five red nude figures dance hand in hand in a circle on a green hill against a deep blue sky. The image is reduced to three colours and simplified, rhythmic outlines that seem to pulse with energy, recalling ancient vase painting and folk dances. When it was shown at the Paris Salon d'Automne in 1910, it was widely mocked. After the Russian Revolution Shchukin's collection was nationalised, and Dance is now in the Hermitage in St Petersburg. A preparatory version, Dance (I), is in the Museum of Modern Art, New York.
