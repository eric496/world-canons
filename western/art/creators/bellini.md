## Biography

Giovanni Bellini came from Venice's leading family of painters: his father Jacopo and brother Gentile were also prominent artists. For around half a century he ran the city's most important workshop, painting altarpieces for its churches, devotional Madonnas and later portraits, including the Doge Leonardo Loredan (National Gallery, London). His pupils included Giorgione and Titian, and when Dürer visited Venice in 1506 he wrote that Bellini, though very old, was 'still the best in painting'.

Bellini was among the first Venetians to master oil paint. He used it to create soft, glowing light, warm atmosphere and landscapes that seem to carry the emotion of the sacred figures. His great altarpieces, such as the San Zaccaria Madonna of 1505, fuse figures and architecture in golden light. That move from line towards colour and atmosphere became the defining trait of Venetian painting.

## Key artworks

### Saint Francis in the Desert (c. 1475–80)

[![Saint Francis in the Desert](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d7/Giovanni_Bellini_-_Saint_Francis_in_the_Desert_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Giovanni_Bellini_-_Saint_Francis_in_the_Desert_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Giovanni_Bellini_-_Saint_Francis_in_the_Desert_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Giovanni_Bellini_-_Saint_Francis_in_the_Desert_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1475–80
- **Type:** Oil on panel
- **Where to see it:** The Frick Collection, New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Saint%20Francis%20in%20the%20Desert%20Giovanni%20Bellini)

Painted around 1475–80 and now in the Frick Collection, New York, this panel shows St Francis of Assisi stepping out of his rocky cell. He stands barefoot with his arms spread and face lifted towards a radiant light at the upper left. Whether Bellini meant the moment he received the stigmata or simply an ecstatic hymn to Creation is debated. The landscape is observed with extraordinary care: a donkey, a heron, a rabbit peering from a hole, layered rocks, a walled town and distant blue hills. Everything is bathed in warm, golden light, and nature itself seems to share in the saint's spiritual experience.
