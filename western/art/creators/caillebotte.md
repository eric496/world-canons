## Biography

Gustave Caillebotte was born in Paris to a very wealthy family, earned a law degree and served in the Franco-Prussian War before studying painting with Léon Bonnat. After the Salon rejected The Floor Scrapers in 1875, he joined the Impressionists, exhibiting from 1876, and became a key organiser and financial backer of their shows. He also bought many of their paintings, sometimes to support struggling friends. He was a passionate yachtsman and boat designer, a stamp collector with his brother Martial and a keen gardener at Petit-Gennevilliers. He died there in 1894, aged 45. He left his collection of Impressionist works to the French state, which after much controversy accepted part of it. It became the core of the Musée d'Orsay's Impressionist collection.

Caillebotte's own painting combines Impressionist light with a more precise, realist approach. He was fascinated by the new Paris of Baron Haussmann's boulevards, steep plunging perspectives, views from balconies and windows, and urban labourers and bourgeois men. Long seen mainly as a patron, he has been reassessed since the 1970s as a major painter.

### The Floor Scrapers (1875)

[![The Floor Scrapers](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/Gustave_Caillebotte_-_The_Floor_Planers_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Gustave_Caillebotte_-_The_Floor_Planers_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gustave_Caillebotte_-_The_Floor_Planers_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gustave_Caillebotte_-_The_Floor_Planers_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1875
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Musée d'Orsay, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Floor%20Scrapers%20Gustave%20Caillebotte)

In this painting of 1875, three shirtless workmen kneel on the floor of an elegant, empty Parisian apartment, scraping the wooden floorboards to prepare them for varnishing. Light from the window and balcony behind them falls across their backs and arms and glistens on the scraped strips of wood, while curls of shavings pile up around them. A wine bottle and glass stand nearby. The Salon jury rejected it in 1875, probably because it showed urban labourers so frankly and without moral message. Caillebotte showed it at the second Impressionist exhibition in 1876. It is in the Musée d'Orsay.

### Paris Street; Rainy Day (1877)

[![Paris Street; Rainy Day](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/17/Gustave_Caillebotte_-_Paris_Street%3B_Rainy_Day_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Gustave_Caillebotte_-_Paris_Street%3B_Rainy_Day_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gustave_Caillebotte_-_Paris_Street%3B_Rainy_Day_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gustave_Caillebotte_-_Paris_Street%3B_Rainy_Day_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1877
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Art Institute of Chicago
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Paris%20Street%3B%20Rainy%20Day%20Gustave%20Caillebotte)

This large painting, shown at the third Impressionist exhibition in 1877 and now in the Art Institute of Chicago, depicts an intersection in the new Paris quartier de l'Europe, near the Gare Saint-Lazare. Elegantly dressed pedestrians carry umbrellas across the wet, shiny cobblestones, while Haussmann's uniform cream-coloured apartment buildings recede in a steep perspective. A green lamp post divides the composition in two. A fashionable couple walks towards us at the right, and their figures are sharply cut off by the picture's edge, almost like a photograph. The painting captures the modern experience of the city: anonymous, orderly and isolating.
