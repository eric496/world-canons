# Rosa Bonheur

**1822 – 1899** · French  
Born: Bordeaux · Died: Thomery  
Movements: [Realism](../movements/realism.md)  

> The most famous woman painter of the 19th century, celebrated for animal paintings based on close study. She was the first woman artist awarded the Legion of Honour.

## Biography

Rosa Bonheur was born in Bordeaux, the daughter of the painter and drawing teacher Raymond Bonheur, a follower of the utopian Saint-Simonian movement. He encouraged her to train as an artist. To study animal anatomy she visited slaughterhouses, horse fairs and veterinary schools, and she obtained a police permit allowing her to wear trousers for this work. She won a gold medal at the Salon in 1848, and Ploughing in the Nivernais (1849) and The Horse Fair made her internationally famous, especially in Britain and the United States. In 1865 Empress Eugénie presented her with the Legion of Honour, the first time it was given to a woman artist.

Bonheur lived for decades with her partner Nathalie Micas, and later with the American painter Anna Klumpke, at the Château de By near Fontainebleau, where she kept a menagerie including lions, deer and horses. Her animal paintings combine scientific accuracy with energy and grandeur. She was the most successful woman artist of the 19th century and has become an icon of independence and of lesbian history.

## Key artworks

### The Horse Fair (1852–55)

[![The Horse Fair](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/Rosa_Bonheur%2C_The_Horse_Fair%2C_1852%E2%80%9355.jpg/1280px-Rosa_Bonheur%2C_The_Horse_Fair%2C_1852%E2%80%9355.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rosa_Bonheur%2C_The_Horse_Fair%2C_1852%E2%80%9355.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rosa_Bonheur%2C_The_Horse_Fair%2C_1852%E2%80%9355.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1852–55
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Horse%20Fair%20Rosa%20Bonheur)

Bonheur spent around 18 months attending the horse market on the boulevard de l'Hôpital in Paris, near the Salpêtrière hospital, dressed in men's clothing to avoid attention. This huge canvas, about 5 metres wide, was first shown at the 1853 Salon and reworked over the next two years. It shows grooms struggling to control powerful Percheron horses as they are paraded and sold, in a whirl of movement and dust. Bonheur is said to have included herself among the riders. The painting toured Britain, where Queen Victoria saw it in a private viewing, and it was bought by the American railroad magnate Cornelius Vanderbilt, who gave it to the Metropolitan Museum of Art in 1887.
