## Biography

Paul Cézanne was born in Aix-en-Provence, the son of a prosperous banker. At school he became close friends with the future novelist Émile Zola, who encouraged him to go to Paris in 1861. His early works are dark and violent, and they were repeatedly rejected by the Salon. Under Pissarro's guidance in Pontoise in the early 1870s he brightened his palette and showed at the first and third Impressionist exhibitions. He then withdrew increasingly to Provence. His friendship with Zola ended in 1886 after Zola's novel L'Œuvre portrayed a failed painter apparently modelled on him. In 1895 the dealer Ambroise Vollard gave him his first solo exhibition, and young artists began to make pilgrimages to Aix. He died in 1906 after being caught in a storm while painting outdoors.

Cézanne aimed to 'make of Impressionism something solid and durable, like the art of the museums'. He built his paintings from patches of colour that model form, combined multiple viewpoints in a single image, and simplified nature into geometric structures. In a letter he advised treating nature through 'the cylinder, the sphere, the cone'. His still lifes, portraits, bathers and landscapes of Provence paved the way for Cubism and abstraction. Picasso and Matisse both called him 'the father of us all'.

### Mont Sainte-Victoire (series) (c. 1882–1906)

[![Mont Sainte-Victoire (series)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Paul_C%C3%A9zanne_-_Montagne_Saint-victoire_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Paul_C%C3%A9zanne_-_Montagne_Saint-victoire_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Paul_C%C3%A9zanne_-_Montagne_Saint-victoire_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Paul_C%C3%A9zanne_-_Montagne_Saint-victoire_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1882–1906
- **Type:** Oil on canvas (series)
- **Where to see it:** Various collections
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Mont%20Sainte-Victoire%20%28series%29%20Paul%20C%C3%A9zanne)

The limestone mountain of Sainte-Victoire rises east of Aix-en-Provence, Cézanne's home town. From the early 1880s until his death in 1906 he painted it in dozens of oils and watercolours, from various viewpoints, including his last studio at Les Lauves. In the early versions the mountain appears behind valleys, fields, viaducts and pine trees, rendered with careful patches of colour. In the late versions it dissolves into a mosaic of almost abstract blocks of green, ochre and blue. For Cézanne the mountain was a lifelong motif for exploring how to build space, structure and light through colour. Versions are in museums around the world, from Philadelphia to the Courtauld and Moscow.

### The Card Players (c. 1890–95 (series))

[![The Card Players](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/Les_Joueurs_de_cartes%2C_par_Paul_C%C3%A9zanne.jpg/1280px-Les_Joueurs_de_cartes%2C_par_Paul_C%C3%A9zanne.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Les_Joueurs_de_cartes%2C_par_Paul_C%C3%A9zanne.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Les_Joueurs_de_cartes%2C_par_Paul_C%C3%A9zanne.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1890–95 (series)
- **Type:** Oil on canvas (series of five)
- **Where to see it:** Versions at Musée d'Orsay, the Met, the Courtauld, the Barnes and a private collection
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Card%20Players%20Paul%20C%C3%A9zanne)

In the early 1890s Cézanne painted a series of five paintings of Provençal peasants playing cards. Some of them were workers on his family's estate, the Jas de Bouffan near Aix. The larger versions, in the Barnes Foundation and the Metropolitan Museum, show four or five figures. The three smaller versions, in the Musée d'Orsay, the Courtauld and a private collection, show just two men facing each other across a table, absorbed in the game. The figures are solid and still, like monuments. One version was sold privately around 2011, reportedly for more than $250 million, among the highest prices ever paid for a work of art.

### The Basket of Apples (c. 1893)

[![The Basket of Apples](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Paul_C%C3%A9zanne_-_The_Basket_of_Apples_-_1926.252_-_Art_Institute_of_Chicago.jpg/1280px-Paul_C%C3%A9zanne_-_The_Basket_of_Apples_-_1926.252_-_Art_Institute_of_Chicago.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Paul_C%C3%A9zanne_-_The_Basket_of_Apples_-_1926.252_-_Art_Institute_of_Chicago.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Paul_C%C3%A9zanne_-_The_Basket_of_Apples_-_1926.252_-_Art_Institute_of_Chicago.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1893
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Art Institute of Chicago
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Basket%20of%20Apples%20Paul%20C%C3%A9zanne)

In this still life of around 1893, in the Art Institute of Chicago, a basket tipped forward spills apples across a table covered with a rumpled white cloth. A wine bottle leans at an angle, and a plate of biscuits sits at the back. Nothing is quite stable: the front edge of the table does not line up from left to right, the bottle tilts, and the plate seems seen from above while the basket is seen from the side. Cézanne combined several viewpoints and adjusted forms to create a dynamic balance of colour and structure, an approach that led directly to Cubism.
