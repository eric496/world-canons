## Biography

Franz Marc was born in Munich, the son of a painter. After studying theology and then art at the Munich Academy, he visited Paris and was deeply affected by Van Gogh and the Impressionists. He studied animal anatomy in depth. His friendships with August Macke and Wassily Kandinsky led to the founding of the Blue Rider (Der Blaue Reiter) in 1911, and they published an influential almanac in 1912. Marc volunteered for the army in 1914 and was killed by a shell splinter at Verdun on 4 March 1916, aged 36.

Marc believed animals were purer and closer to nature and the spiritual than humans. He painted horses, deer, cows, tigers and foxes in symbolic colours: in his system blue stood for the masculine and spiritual, yellow for the feminine and gentle, and red for brutal matter. Under the influence of Cubism and Futurism his late works, such as The Fate of the Animals (1913), move towards abstraction.

## Key artworks

### The Large Blue Horses (1911)

[![The Large Blue Horses](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/aa/Franz_Marc_-_Die_gro%C3%9Fen_blauen_Pferde_1911.jpg/1280px-Franz_Marc_-_Die_gro%C3%9Fen_blauen_Pferde_1911.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Franz_Marc_-_Die_gro%C3%9Fen_blauen_Pferde_1911.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Franz_Marc_-_Die_gro%C3%9Fen_blauen_Pferde_1911.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1911
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Walker Art Center, Minneapolis
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Large%20Blue%20Horses%20Franz%20Marc)

In this painting of 1911, the year he co-founded the Blue Rider, three massive blue horses are packed tightly together, their bodies curving and overlapping in rhythmic arcs, set against rolling red and yellow hills and green and red trees. For Marc, blue symbolised the masculine and spiritual, and horses embodied a pure, innocent relationship with nature that humans had lost. The painting's harmony of bold colour and curving form makes it one of the icons of German Expressionism. It is in the Walker Art Center in Minneapolis.
