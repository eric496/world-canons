## Biography

Pierre-Auguste Renoir was born in Limoges to a working-class family who moved to Paris. As a teenager he was an apprentice painter of porcelain. He studied in Gleyre's studio, where he befriended Monet, and the two painted side by side at the bathing spot of La Grenouillère in 1869. He exhibited at the early Impressionist shows and found patrons such as the publisher Georges Charpentier. After a trip to Italy in 1881 he turned to a harder, more linear style inspired by Raphael and Ingres, and later to warm, rounded figures. He married Aline Charigot in 1890. One son, Jean Renoir, became a great film director. Despite severe rheumatoid arthritis, he continued to paint into old age at Cagnes-sur-Mer, where he died in 1919.

Renoir is the painter of the joyful side of Impressionism: Parisians dancing, boating, picnicking and chatting in dappled sunlight, children, flowers and voluptuous nudes. He painted with soft, feathery brushwork and warm iridescent colour. In his last years he also made sculptures with the help of an assistant.

### Bal du moulin de la Galette (1876)

[![Bal du moulin de la Galette](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/Renoir%2C_Pierre-Auguste_-_Dance_at_Le_Moulin_de_la_Galette%2C_1876.jpg/1280px-Renoir%2C_Pierre-Auguste_-_Dance_at_Le_Moulin_de_la_Galette%2C_1876.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Renoir%2C_Pierre-Auguste_-_Dance_at_Le_Moulin_de_la_Galette%2C_1876.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Renoir%2C_Pierre-Auguste_-_Dance_at_Le_Moulin_de_la_Galette%2C_1876.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1876
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Musée d'Orsay, Paris
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Bal%20du%20moulin%20de%20la%20Galette%20Pierre-Auguste%20Renoir)

In 1876 Renoir rented a studio in Montmartre so that he could paint on the spot at the Moulin de la Galette, an open-air dance hall and café beside one of the old windmills on the hill, where working-class Parisians came to dance on Sunday afternoons. His friends posed among the crowd. Sunlight filtered through the trees falls in patches on the dancers, hats and faces, dissolving forms into a shimmer of colour and movement. Gustave Caillebotte bought the painting, and it entered the French national collection with his bequest. It is now in the Musée d'Orsay. A smaller version sold at auction in 1990 for one of the highest prices ever paid for a painting at the time.

### Luncheon of the Boating Party (1880–81)

[![Luncheon of the Boating Party](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Pierre-Auguste_Renoir_-_Luncheon_of_the_Boating_Party_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Pierre-Auguste_Renoir_-_Luncheon_of_the_Boating_Party_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pierre-Auguste_Renoir_-_Luncheon_of_the_Boating_Party_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pierre-Auguste_Renoir_-_Luncheon_of_the_Boating_Party_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1880–81
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** The Phillips Collection, Washington, D.C.
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Luncheon%20of%20the%20Boating%20Party%20Pierre-Auguste%20Renoir)

Painted in 1880–81, this painting shows Renoir's friends relaxing after lunch on the balcony of the Maison Fournaise, a restaurant on an island in the Seine at Chatou, popular with boaters. At the left, the seamstress Aline Charigot, his future wife, plays with a small dog. At the right, the painter Gustave Caillebotte sits astride a chair in a white boating shirt. The tablecloth, glasses and bottles, the striped awning and the river beyond create a festive atmosphere. The American collector Duncan Phillips bought it in 1923, and it is the centrepiece of the Phillips Collection in Washington, D.C.
