## Biography

Ernst Ludwig Kirchner was born in Aschaffenburg in Bavaria and studied architecture in Dresden. In 1905, with fellow architecture students Erich Heckel, Karl Schmidt-Rottluff and Fritz Bleyl, he founded the artists' group Die Brücke ('The Bridge'), aiming to build a bridge to the future. They painted nudes in the studio and outdoors at the Moritzburg lakes, and they revived woodcut as a raw, expressive medium. In 1911 he moved to Berlin, where he painted his famous street scenes of prostitutes and passers-by. Drafted in the First World War, he suffered a breakdown and moved to Switzerland in 1917, settling near Davos, where he painted the mountains and their farmers. In 1937 the Nazis declared him 'degenerate' and removed over 600 of his works from German museums. He took his own life in 1938.

Kirchner's art, with its jagged forms, flattened space, clashing non-naturalistic colours and influences from African and Oceanic art and German Gothic woodcuts, expresses the excitement, alienation and nervous tension of modern life. He is one of the leading figures of German Expressionism.

## Key artworks

### Street, Dresden (1908 (reworked 1919))

[![Street, Dresden](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e8/Kirchner_-_Street%2C_Dresden%2C_1908%2C_12.1951.jpg/1280px-Kirchner_-_Street%2C_Dresden%2C_1908%2C_12.1951.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kirchner_-_Street%2C_Dresden%2C_1908%2C_12.1951.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kirchner_-_Street%2C_Dresden%2C_1908%2C_12.1951.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1908 (reworked 1919)
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Street%2C%20Dresden%20Ernst%20Ludwig%20Kirchner)

Kirchner painted this street scene in Dresden in 1908 and reworked it in 1919. Fashionably dressed women and other figures crowd the pavement in strident, unnatural colours of pink, orange and green, and their mask-like faces stare blankly. The pavement tilts upward, and a tram is visible in the background. The sense of crowding and isolation, the tension and the lurid colour express the anxiety of the modern city. It anticipates his famous Berlin street scenes of 1913–15. It is in the Museum of Modern Art, New York.
