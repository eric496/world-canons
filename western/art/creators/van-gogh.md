## Biography

Vincent van Gogh was born in Zundert in the southern Netherlands, the son of a Protestant pastor. He worked for the international art dealers Goupil & Cie in The Hague, London and Paris, then as a teacher and lay preacher, including among miners in the Borinage region of Belgium. In 1880, aged 27, he decided to become an artist, largely self-taught and supported by his younger brother Theo, an art dealer. After dark, earthy works such as The Potato Eaters (1885), he moved to Paris in 1886, where he discovered Impressionism and Japanese prints. In February 1888 he moved to Arles in Provence, dreaming of an artists' colony. Gauguin's stay there ended in December 1888 with a crisis in which Van Gogh cut off part of his own ear. He spent a year as a voluntary patient in an asylum at Saint-Rémy. In May 1890 he moved to Auvers-sur-Oise near Paris, where he shot himself in July and died two days later, aged 37.

In a career of about ten years Van Gogh produced around 860 oil paintings and more than 1,200 drawings and watercolours, most in his last two years. His vivid colour, thick paint and swirling, rhythmic brushstrokes express intense emotion, and he became a central influence on Expressionism. He sold very little during his lifetime. His more than 800 surviving letters, most to Theo, are among the most moving documents of any artist's life. Theo's widow, Jo van Gogh-Bonger, worked tirelessly to make his work known.

### Sunflowers (1888)

[![Sunflowers](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/Vincent_Willem_van_Gogh_127.jpg/1280px-Vincent_Willem_van_Gogh_127.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vincent_Willem_van_Gogh_127.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vincent_Willem_van_Gogh_127.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1888
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** National Gallery, London (one of several versions)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sunflowers%20Vincent%20van%20Gogh)

In August 1888 in Arles, Van Gogh painted a series of still lifes of sunflowers in a vase to decorate the room where Paul Gauguin would stay in his 'Yellow House'. He later made repetitions of them. This version, the fourth, in the National Gallery, London, shows 15 sunflowers at all stages of life, from fresh blooms to withered seed heads, painted in thick, textured strokes of yellow on yellow, with the vase signed 'Vincent'. Yellow, for Van Gogh, was the colour of happiness and the southern sun. Other versions are in Amsterdam, Munich, Philadelphia and Tokyo. The Tokyo version was bought at auction in 1987 for what was then a record price.

### The Bedroom (1888)

[![The Bedroom](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/76/Vincent_van_Gogh_-_De_slaapkamer_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Vincent_van_Gogh_-_De_slaapkamer_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vincent_van_Gogh_-_De_slaapkamer_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vincent_van_Gogh_-_De_slaapkamer_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1888
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam (first of three versions)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Bedroom%20Vincent%20van%20Gogh)

In October 1888 Van Gogh painted his own bedroom in the Yellow House in Arles. In a letter to Theo he explained that the colour was meant to suggest rest or sleep and to give a sense of calm. The room is shown in flat, bold colours: blue walls, a yellow bed and chairs, a red blanket, and a green window, with a skewed, steep perspective. The furniture, including two chairs and two pillows, has been read as a hope for companionship. When this first version was damaged by flooding, he painted two more at the asylum in Saint-Rémy, now in the Art Institute of Chicago and the Musée d'Orsay. This one is in the Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam.

### The Starry Night (1889)

[![The Starry Night](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Van_Gogh_-_Starry_Night_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg/1280px-Van_Gogh_-_Starry_Night_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Van_Gogh_-_Starry_Night_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Van_Gogh_-_Starry_Night_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1889
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Museum of Modern Art (MoMA), New York
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Starry%20Night%20Vincent%20van%20Gogh)

Van Gogh painted this canvas in June 1889 while a voluntary patient at the asylum of Saint-Paul-de-Mausole in Saint-Rémy-de-Provence. It is based on the view from his east-facing window before sunrise, but he painted it during the day in his studio, from memory and imagination. A tall, flame-like cypress tree rises in the foreground. Below, a quiet village sleeps, with a church spire more Dutch than Provençal. Above, the night sky churns with swirling spirals, a radiant crescent moon and glowing stars, including the morning star Venus. Van Gogh himself was not satisfied with it. It is now one of the most famous paintings in the world, in the Museum of Modern Art, New York.

### Wheatfield with Crows (1890)

[![Wheatfield with Crows](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a1/Korenveld_met_kraaien_-_s0149V1962_-_Van_Gogh_Museum.jpg/1280px-Korenveld_met_kraaien_-_s0149V1962_-_Van_Gogh_Museum.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Korenveld_met_kraaien_-_s0149V1962_-_Van_Gogh_Museum.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Korenveld_met_kraaien_-_s0149V1962_-_Van_Gogh_Museum.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1890
- **Type:** Oil on canvas
- **Where to see it:** Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Wheatfield%20with%20Crows%20Vincent%20van%20Gogh)

Van Gogh painted this canvas in July 1890 in Auvers-sur-Oise, in the last weeks of his life. Its unusual double-square format stretches out a field of wheat under a dark, stormy blue sky, with a flock of black crows flying low over the corn. Three paths lead into the field and seem to go nowhere. In a letter he described painting vast fields of wheat under troubled skies and trying to express sadness and extreme loneliness, but also the healthy and strengthening power of the countryside. Despite the legend, it was probably not his last painting. It is in the Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam.
