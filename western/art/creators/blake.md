## Biography

William Blake was born in London, the son of a hosier, and was apprenticed to the engraver James Basire, for whom he drew the tombs of Westminster Abbey. He studied briefly at the Royal Academy Schools, married Catherine Boucher (whom he taught to read and who helped him print and colour his books), and earned a modest living as an engraver. In 1788 he developed 'relief etching', which he said had been revealed to him in a vision by his dead brother Robert. With it he printed text and image together in illuminated books such as Songs of Innocence and of Experience (1789–94) and his prophetic books. In 1804 he was tried for sedition after a dispute with a soldier and acquitted. He died in London in 1827.

Blake created a private mythology of figures such as Urizen, Los and Orc to explore reason, imagination, tyranny, religion and revolution. He worked in watercolour, tempera, engraving and colour printing, with sinuous lines and muscular figures influenced by Michelangelo and medieval art. He was little known and often thought mad in his lifetime. The Pre-Raphaelites rediscovered him, and he is now celebrated as one of England's greatest poets and visionary artists. His lyric 'And did those feet in ancient time' became the hymn Jerusalem.

## Key artworks

### The Ancient of Days (1794)

[![The Ancient of Days](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/Europe_a_Prophecy_copy_K_plate_01.jpg/1280px-Europe_a_Prophecy_copy_K_plate_01.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Europe_a_Prophecy_copy_K_plate_01.jpg)

<sub>Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Europe_a_Prophecy_copy_K_plate_01.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1794
- **Type:** Relief etching with watercolour
- **Where to see it:** Copies in several collections, incl. the British Museum
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Ancient%20of%20Days%20William%20Blake)

This image is the frontispiece to Blake's illuminated book Europe a Prophecy (1794). Within a blazing circle, an old man with streaming white hair and beard crouches and reaches down into the darkness with a great golden compass to measure out the universe. It refers to a passage in the Book of Proverbs about God setting a compass on the face of the deep. In Blake's own mythology the figure is Urizen, the embodiment of reason and law, a creator but also a limiting tyrant. Blake printed it in relief and coloured it by hand. According to his friend Frederick Tatham, he was colouring a copy shortly before his death. Copies are in the British Museum, the Whitworth in Manchester and elsewhere.
