## Notes on this section

- **Scope.** The section follows the Western line of science, mathematics and medicine. Al-Khwarizmi, Ibn al-Haytham and Avicenna are included because Latin translations of their books shaped European science directly. Indian numerals, Chinese and Persian forms of the arithmetical triangle and other non-Western contributions are noted where they entered the story. Islamic, Indian and Chinese science will have their own sections.
- **Works and discoveries.** A "work" here can be a book, a paper, an experiment, an instrument or a discovery. Where a result came out of several publications (Cantor's set theory, Heisenberg's matrix mechanics), it appears as one entry with the range of years.
- **Shared credit.** Each entry has one listed creator, but many discoveries were joint. The double-helix paper is listed under Watson and was written with Crick. Photo 51 is listed under Rosalind Franklin and was taken with Raymond Gosling. The polonium and radium discoveries are listed under Marie Curie and were made with Pierre Curie. The descriptions name the collaborators.
- **Pictures.** Portraits are paintings, drawings, engravings, sculpture or coins only, never photographs, so several twentieth-century scientists have no portrait. Works are illustrated with first-edition title pages, manuscript pages, plates, diagrams and surviving instruments. Where no free image exists, the entry has no picture.
