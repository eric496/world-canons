# Benjamin Franklin

**1706 – 1790** · American  
Born: Boston, Massachusetts Bay · Died: Philadelphia, Pennsylvania  
Eras & fields: [Enlightenment chemistry & natural history](../movements/enlightenment-science.md)  

> Printer, statesman and natural philosopher who showed that lightning is electrical and invented the lightning rod. His theory of a single electrical fluid introduced the terms positive and negative charge.

## Biography

Franklin was the fifteenth child of a Boston candle maker and was apprenticed to his brother, a printer. He ran away to Philadelphia, where he built a successful printing and publishing business and founded a library, a fire company and the institutions that became the University of Pennsylvania and the American Philosophical Society. Retiring from business in 1748, he devoted himself to science, and later to politics, as colonial agent in London, signer of the Declaration of Independence, ambassador to France and delegate to the Constitutional Convention.

In the late 1740s Franklin's electrical experiments, reported in letters to London, led him to describe electricity as a single fluid that bodies could have in excess or deficit, which he called positive and negative, and to state the conservation of charge. He proposed that lightning is an electrical discharge and that pointed iron rods could protect buildings; the experiment was first carried out in France in 1752, and Franklin's own kite experiment is said to have followed. He also charted the Gulf Stream and invented bifocals and an efficient stove.

## Key works & discoveries

### Experiments and Observations on Electricity (1751)

- **Date:** 1751
- **Type:** Collection of letters on electricity
- **Published / where:** London: E. Cave
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Experiments%20and%20Observations%20on%20Electricity%20Benjamin%20Franklin)

Franklin's electrical letters, written to the London merchant and naturalist Peter Collinson from 1747, were collected and published in London in 1751, with later expanded editions, and translated into French in 1752. Franklin described experiments with Leyden jars, early capacitors, and explained electricity as a single subtle fluid present in all bodies, which could be in excess, which he called plus or positive, or in deficit, minus or negative, and which was never created or destroyed. He observed that pointed conductors draw off electricity silently and proposed that a pointed iron rod on a building, connected to the ground, could protect it from lightning, and that a rod on a high tower could test whether thunderclouds are electrified. In May 1752 Thomas-François Dalibard carried out the experiment at Marly near Paris, drawing sparks from a storm cloud, and Franklin's kite experiment in Philadelphia is said to have followed in June. The book made Franklin famous across Europe, and lightning rods spread rapidly.
