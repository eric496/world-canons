> Mathematician and astronomer who showed the stability of the solar system under Newtonian gravity and made probability theory a tool of science.

## Biography

Laplace came from a family of modest farmers in Normandy and was intended for the Church, but at Caen he discovered mathematics and went to Paris, where d'Alembert found him a teaching post at the military school. He was elected to the Academy of Sciences and collaborated with Lavoisier on heat. He survived the Revolution, helped design the metric system, briefly served Napoleon as minister of the interior, and was made a marquis under the restored monarchy.

In his five-volume Celestial Mechanics he showed that the apparent irregularities in the motions of the planets are periodic and that the solar system is stable, completing the Newtonian programme; he also suggested that it formed from a rotating nebula. His Analytical Theory of Probabilities and its popular companion set out the mathematics of chance and errors and applied it to astronomy, demography and judgement. He expressed the ideal of a strict determinism, imagining an intellect that could know the future from the present state of the universe.

## Key works & discoveries

### Traité de mécanique céleste (1799–1825)

[![Traité de mécanique céleste](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/52/Trait%C3%A9_de_m%C3%A9canique_c%C3%A9leste_%281798%29_%2814597824739%29.jpg/1280px-Trait%C3%A9_de_m%C3%A9canique_c%C3%A9leste_%281798%29_%2814597824739%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Trait%C3%A9_de_m%C3%A9canique_c%C3%A9leste_%281798%29_%2814597824739%29.jpg)

- **Date:** 1799–1825
- **Type:** Treatise on celestial mechanics
- **Published / where:** Paris: J. B. M. Duprat and Courcier
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Trait%C3%A9%20de%20m%C3%A9canique%20c%C3%A9leste%20Pierre-Simon%20Laplace)

Celestial Mechanics, published in five volumes between 1799 and 1825, gathered a century of work by Euler, Lagrange, Laplace himself and others into a systematic mathematical treatment of the solar system under Newton's law of gravitation, using the calculus rather than Newton's geometry. Laplace treated the shapes of the Earth and planets, the tides, the motion of the Moon, the satellites of Jupiter and Saturn's rings, and showed that the long-term inequalities in the motions of Jupiter and Saturn that had puzzled astronomers are periodic, so the solar system is stable. According to a well-known anecdote, Napoleon remarked that the work did not mention God and Laplace replied that he had had no need of that hypothesis; the story may have been embroidered. The book was translated into English with an extensive commentary by the American navigator Nathaniel Bowditch, and established the deterministic picture of a clockwork universe.
