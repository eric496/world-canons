# Alexander von Humboldt

> Naturalist and explorer whose travels in the Americas and vision of nature as a web of interconnections founded plant geography and inspired a generation of scientists, including Darwin.

## Biography

Humboldt was born into a wealthy Prussian family; his brother Wilhelm became a famous linguist and educational reformer. After studying at several universities and the Freiberg mining academy, he worked as a mining inspector. On his mother's death he used his inheritance to fund a five-year expedition with the botanist Aimé Bonpland through Venezuela, Cuba, the Andes and Mexico in 1799–1804, then spent over twenty years in Paris writing up the results, and lived his last decades in Berlin.

Humboldt measured everything he could, from temperature and altitude to magnetism, and he mapped how vegetation changes with climate and height, introducing isotherms to show climate on maps. He saw nature as a unified whole in which life, climate and geology interact, and he wrote about it with literary passion. His late work Kosmos sought to describe the entire physical universe. Darwin carried his travel narrative on the Beagle, and many places and species bear his name.

### Essay on the Geography of Plants (1807)

- **Date:** 1807
- **Type:** Treatise with illustrated profile
- **Published / where:** Paris and Tübingen: F. Schoell and J. G. Cotta
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Essay%20on%20the%20Geography%20of%20Plants%20Alexander%20von%20Humboldt)

The Essai sur la géographie des plantes, written with Aimé Bonpland, was the first publication from Humboldt's American expedition. It is accompanied by a large engraved plate, the Tableau physique or Naturgemälde, showing a cross-section of the volcanoes Chimborazo and Cotopaxi in the Andes, with the names of plants written at the heights where they grow, and columns on either side giving, for each altitude, the temperature, air pressure, humidity, the blueness of the sky, the boiling point of water and other measurements. The plate showed at a glance how zones of vegetation are linked to climate and altitude, and how similar zones recur from the equator to the poles. It founded the science of plant geography, or biogeography, and expressed Humboldt's view of nature as an interconnected whole. In 1802 Humboldt and Bonpland had climbed to about 5,900 metres on Chimborazo, then believed the highest mountain on Earth, a record for decades.

### Kosmos (1845–1862)

[![Kosmos](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7e/Atlas_zu_Alex._v._Humboldts_kosmos_1851_%28139502973%29.jpg/1280px-Atlas_zu_Alex._v._Humboldts_kosmos_1851_%28139502973%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Atlas_zu_Alex._v._Humboldts_kosmos_1851_%28139502973%29.jpg)

<sub>Plate from the atlas to Humboldt's Kosmos, 1851 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Atlas_zu_Alex._v._Humboldts_kosmos_1851_%28139502973%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1845–1862
- **Type:** Scientific survey in five volumes
- **Published / where:** Stuttgart and Tübingen: J. G. Cotta
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Kosmos%20Alexander%20von%20Humboldt)

Kosmos: Entwurf einer physischen Weltbeschreibung, Sketch of a Physical Description of the Universe, grew out of popular lectures Humboldt gave in Berlin in 1827–28 and occupied him for the rest of his life; the fifth volume appeared after his death. The first volume surveyed the whole of nature, from nebulae and stars to the Earth's interior, magnetism, oceans, climate, plants and animals, while the second traced the history of humanity's understanding of nature and its expression in poetry, landscape painting and gardens. Later volumes treated astronomy and the Earth in detail. Humboldt wanted to show the unity of nature and the interconnection of all its phenomena, and to combine scientific precision with aesthetic feeling. Kosmos was a best-seller, translated into most European languages, and inspired scientists, writers and artists including Darwin, Thoreau and Frederic Edwin Church.
