# Erwin Schrödinger

**1887 – 1961** · Austrian  
Born: Vienna, Austria-Hungary · Died: Vienna  
Eras & fields: [Relativity & quantum mechanics](../movements/relativity-quantum.md), [Genetics & molecular biology](../movements/molecular-biology.md)  

> Physicist whose wave equation became the central equation of quantum mechanics. His book What Is Life? inspired a generation of molecular biologists.

## Biography

Schrödinger studied at the University of Vienna and served as an artillery officer in the First World War. He was professor in Zurich from 1921, where he made his great discovery, and succeeded Planck in Berlin in 1927. Opposed to the Nazis, he left Germany in 1933, the year he shared the Nobel Prize in Physics with Paul Dirac, and after periods in Oxford and Graz settled at the Dublin Institute for Advanced Studies from 1939 to 1956, before returning to Vienna.

In 1926 Schrödinger described electrons as waves governed by an equation that gave the energy levels of the hydrogen atom, and showed that his wave mechanics was equivalent to Heisenberg's matrix mechanics. He was unhappy with the probabilistic interpretation, and in 1935 devised the thought experiment of a cat both alive and dead to show its strangeness. His 1944 book What Is Life? suggested that genes contain a coded message in an 'aperiodic crystal', an idea that Watson and Crick both acknowledged as an inspiration.

### Quantisation as an Eigenvalue Problem (Schrödinger equation) (1926)

- **Date:** 1926
- **Type:** Scientific papers
- **Published / where:** Annalen der Physik, Leipzig
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Quantisation%20as%20an%20Eigenvalue%20Problem%20%28Schr%C3%B6dinger%20equation%29%20Erwin%20Schr%C3%B6dinger)

Inspired by Louis de Broglie's suggestion that electrons have wave properties, Schrödinger sought an equation for these matter waves, and over the Christmas holiday of 1925, in a villa in Arosa in the Swiss Alps, found it. In four papers published in the first half of 1926 he showed that the allowed energy levels of the hydrogen atom follow naturally as the frequencies at which the electron wave can vibrate in a stable pattern, much as a guitar string can vibrate only at certain notes. Unlike Heisenberg's matrices, his wave mechanics used familiar mathematics of differential equations and was quickly adopted. He also showed that the two theories were mathematically equivalent. Schrödinger hoped that the wave represented a real smeared-out electron, but Max Born proposed in 1926 that the square of its size gives the probability of finding the particle at a place, the interpretation that prevailed. The Schrödinger equation is the basic equation of quantum chemistry, materials science and much of modern physics.

### What Is Life? (1944)

- **Date:** 1944
- **Type:** Book of lectures
- **Published / where:** Trinity College, Dublin (lectures, 1943); Cambridge University Press
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=What%20Is%20Life%3F%20Erwin%20Schr%C3%B6dinger)

In February 1943 Schrödinger gave three public lectures at Trinity College, Dublin, published the following year as What Is Life? The Physical Aspect of the Living Cell. Drawing on the work of the geneticist Max Delbrück and his colleagues on mutations caused by X-rays, he argued that the hereditary material must be a very small but extremely stable molecular structure, an 'aperiodic crystal' whose arrangement of atoms forms a 'code-script' determining the development of the organism. He also discussed how living things maintain their order against the tendency to disorder by drawing 'negative entropy' from their surroundings. Many of the ideas were not new, and biologists have criticised some of them, but the short book, written for a general audience, made the physical problem of heredity exciting to physicists. James Watson, Francis Crick and Maurice Wilkins all said it influenced their decision to work on genes.
