# Gregor Mendel

**1822 – 1884** · Austrian (Moravian)  
Born: Heinzendorf, Austrian Silesia · Died: Brno, Moravia  
Eras & fields: [Geology, evolution & heredity](../movements/evolution-biology.md)  

> Augustinian friar in Brno whose experiments on pea plants revealed the basic laws of inheritance. His work was overlooked until 1900 and is now the foundation of genetics.

## Biography

Johann Mendel was the son of German-speaking peasant farmers in Silesia. To pursue his education he entered the Augustinian abbey of St Thomas in Brno in 1843, taking the name Gregor, and the abbey sent him to the University of Vienna in 1851–53, where he studied physics, mathematics and botany. Back in Brno he taught natural science at a secondary school and from 1856 carried out breeding experiments in the abbey garden. In 1868 he was elected abbot, and administrative duties and a dispute over taxation of the monastery took up his later years.

Over eight years Mendel crossed and counted thousands of pea plants with contrasting traits, and found that the traits reappeared in fixed ratios in later generations. He explained this by hereditary factors that come in pairs, one from each parent, and are passed on independently. He presented the work in 1865 and published it in 1866, but it attracted little attention. In 1900 three botanists independently rediscovered his results, and his factors became the genes of the new science of genetics.

## Key works & discoveries

### Experiments on Plant Hybridization (1866)

[![Experiments on Plant Hybridization](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c3/Mendel_paper.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mendel_paper.jpg)

<sub>First page of Mendel's paper Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Mendel_paper.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1866
- **Type:** Scientific paper
- **Published / where:** Verhandlungen des naturforschenden Vereines in Brünn
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Experiments%20on%20Plant%20Hybridization%20Gregor%20Mendel)

Versuche über Pflanzenhybriden reported eight years of experiments in the abbey garden at Brno with some 28,000 pea plants. Mendel chose seven characters that appeared in two clear forms, such as round or wrinkled seeds and tall or short stems, crossed pure-breeding plants, and counted the offspring over several generations. In the first generation all plants showed one form, the dominant; in the next the recessive form reappeared in about a quarter of the plants, a ratio of three to one. He explained this by hereditary elements that come in pairs and separate when germ cells form, and showed that different characters are inherited independently of each other. He read the paper to the Natural History Society of Brno in 1865 and it was published in 1866, but its significance was not recognised. In 1900 Hugo de Vries, Carl Correns and Erich von Tschermak independently rediscovered it, and it became the foundation of genetics. Statisticians including R. A. Fisher later suggested that his data fit the expected ratios suspiciously well, a question still discussed.
