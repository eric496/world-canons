## Biography

Leonardo was the illegitimate son of a Florentine notary, trained in Verrocchio's workshop and worked for Lorenzo de' Medici, Ludovico Sforza in Milan, Cesare Borgia, the papal court and finally Francis I of France. Throughout his career he filled thousands of notebook pages with drawings and mirror-written notes, studying everything from the flight of birds and the flow of water to fossils, optics and machines.

In anatomy he dissected some thirty human bodies in Florence, Milan and Rome, producing drawings of the heart, skeleton, muscles and fetus of a quality not matched until Vesalius. He was guided by direct observation and by analogies between the body and the Earth. Because the notebooks were scattered after his death and not published, they had little influence on the science of his time; their rediscovery since the nineteenth century has made him the emblem of Renaissance curiosity.

### Codex Leicester (c. 1506–1510)

[![Codex Leicester](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/Vinci_-_Hammer_2A.jpg/1280px-Vinci_-_Hammer_2A.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vinci_-_Hammer_2A.jpg)

<sub>A page of the Codex Leicester Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vinci_-_Hammer_2A.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1506–1510
- **Type:** Notebook (72 pages)
- **Published / where:** Private collection (Bill Gates)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Codex%20Leicester%20Leonardo%20da%20Vinci)

The Codex Leicester is a set of eighteen double sheets written in Leonardo's mirror script with sketches in the margins, named after the Earl of Leicester who bought it in 1719. Its main subject is water: how it moves in rivers and seas, forms eddies and waves, erodes mountains and might be controlled by engineering. Leonardo argues that fossil shells found high in the mountains were left by living creatures in ancient seas, not by Noah's flood, an insight that anticipated modern geology. He also explains the faint light on the dark part of the crescent Moon, the earthshine, as sunlight reflected from the Earth, a correct explanation later given by Kepler's teacher Maestlin. The codex was bought by the industrialist Armand Hammer in 1980 and by Bill Gates in 1994, the most expensive manuscript sold at that time, and it has been exhibited around the world.

### Anatomical drawings (c. 1487–1513)

- **Date:** c. 1487–1513
- **Type:** Drawings in pen, ink and chalk
- **Published / where:** Royal Collection, Windsor Castle
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Anatomical%20drawings%20Leonardo%20da%20Vinci)

Leonardo's anatomical studies began in Milan in the late 1480s with drawings of the skull and became intensive around 1507–13, when he dissected human bodies at the hospital of Santa Maria Nuova in Florence and with the anatomist Marcantonio della Torre in Pavia. He drew the skeleton, muscles, heart, blood vessels, nerves and a fetus in the womb from several viewpoints, used cross-sections and exploded views, and made a wax cast of the ventricles of an ox brain. His studies of the heart describe the action of the valves and the eddies of blood in the aorta, in work that modern cardiologists have found remarkably perceptive, though he kept Galen's view of the blood's movement. He planned a treatise on anatomy that was never published. After his death the drawings passed to his pupil Francesco Melzi and later entered the English royal collection, where they remained largely unknown until the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.
