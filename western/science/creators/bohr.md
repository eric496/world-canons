# Niels Bohr

**1885 – 1962** · Danish  
Born: Copenhagen, Denmark · Died: Copenhagen  
Eras & fields: [X-rays, radioactivity & the atom](../movements/radioactivity-atom.md), [Relativity & quantum mechanics](../movements/relativity-quantum.md)  

> Physicist who applied quantum ideas to the structure of the atom and led the development and interpretation of quantum mechanics from his institute in Copenhagen.

## Biography

Bohr was the son of a physiology professor in Copenhagen and studied at the university there. In 1911–12 he worked in England, first with J. J. Thomson in Cambridge and then with Rutherford in Manchester. In 1921 he founded the Institute for Theoretical Physics in Copenhagen, which became the meeting place of the new quantum physics. He won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1922. In 1943 he escaped occupied Denmark and took part in the atomic bomb project, afterwards campaigning for openness and international control of nuclear weapons.

In 1913 Bohr proposed that electrons in atoms occupy only certain allowed orbits and emit or absorb light when they jump between them, explaining the spectrum of hydrogen. This model, though soon superseded, linked quantum theory to atomic structure. He then guided the younger physicists who created quantum mechanics and developed the principle of complementarity, at the heart of the Copenhagen interpretation, debating its meaning with Einstein for decades. He also explained nuclear fission with the liquid-drop model.

## Key works & discoveries

### On the Constitution of Atoms and Molecules (Bohr model) (1913)

[![On the Constitution of Atoms and Molecules (Bohr model)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/93/Bohr_atom_model.svg/1280px-Bohr_atom_model.svg.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bohr_atom_model.svg)

<sub>Diagram of the Bohr model of the atom Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bohr_atom_model.svg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1913
- **Type:** Scientific papers
- **Published / where:** Philosophical Magazine, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20the%20Constitution%20of%20Atoms%20and%20Molecules%20%28Bohr%20model%29%20Niels%20Bohr)

In a trilogy of papers published in 1913, Bohr combined Rutherford's nuclear atom with Planck's quantum. He postulated that the electron in a hydrogen atom can circle the nucleus only in certain stationary orbits, in which it does not radiate as classical physics would require, and that it emits or absorbs light of a single frequency when it jumps from one orbit to another, the energy of the light being the difference between the energies of the orbits. From these assumptions he derived the Balmer formula for the lines in the hydrogen spectrum, known empirically since 1885, and calculated the Rydberg constant from fundamental constants, a striking success. The model also explained spectra of ionised helium and the general structure of the periodic table. It could not explain atoms with more than one electron or the intensities of spectral lines, and was replaced by quantum mechanics in the 1920s, but its picture of an atom with electrons in shells remains a common teaching image.
