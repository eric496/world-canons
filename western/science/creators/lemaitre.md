# Georges Lemaître

**1894 – 1966** · Belgian  
Born: Charleroi, Belgium · Died: Leuven, Belgium  
Eras & fields: [Cosmology & the expanding universe](../movements/cosmology.md)  

> Belgian priest and physicist who proposed that the universe is expanding and that it began from a 'primeval atom', the idea now called the Big Bang.

## Biography

Lemaître studied engineering at Leuven, fought in the Belgian army in the First World War, then turned to mathematics and physics and was ordained a Catholic priest in 1923. He studied with Arthur Eddington in Cambridge and at Harvard and MIT, and from 1925 taught at the Catholic University of Leuven for the rest of his career. He later became president of the Pontifical Academy of Sciences, and was an early user of electronic computers.

In 1927 Lemaître published, in a little-read Belgian journal, a solution of Einstein's equations describing an expanding universe, with an estimate of the expansion rate from galaxy data, two years before Hubble. In 1931 he proposed that the universe began from a single 'primeval atom', whose decay produced everything since. The idea was later developed by George Gamow and others, and the discovery of the cosmic microwave background in 1965, shortly before Lemaître's death, gave it strong support. He kept his science and faith distinct.

## Key works & discoveries

### The primeval atom and the expanding universe (1927–1931)

- **Date:** 1927–1931
- **Type:** Scientific papers
- **Published / where:** Annales de la Société Scientifique de Bruxelles, 1927; Nature, 1931
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20primeval%20atom%20and%20the%20expanding%20universe%20Georges%20Lema%C3%AEtre)

In 1927 Lemaître published in a Belgian journal a paper showing that Einstein's equations of general relativity allow a universe of constant mass with a growing radius, and that this would account for the recession of the galaxies; using published distances and velocities, he derived an expansion rate close to the one Hubble found two years later. Einstein reportedly told him that his calculations were correct but his physics abominable. The paper was little noticed until Eddington arranged for an English translation in 1931, in which the passage estimating the expansion rate was omitted, a fact that later prompted speculation but was shown in 2011 to be Lemaître's own decision. In a letter to Nature in 1931, Lemaître went further and suggested that the universe began as a single quantum, a 'primeval atom', which split into the matter of the cosmos. The idea, mocked as the 'Big Bang' by Fred Hoyle in 1949, was developed by George Gamow and colleagues and confirmed by the discovery of the cosmic microwave background in 1965.
