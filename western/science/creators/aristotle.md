# Aristotle

**384 BC – 322 BC** · Greek  
Born: Stagira, Chalcidice · Died: Chalcis, Euboea  
Eras & fields: [Greek natural philosophy & medicine](../movements/greek-natural-philosophy.md)  
Also in: [Philosophy](../../philosophy/eras/01-ancient/aristotle.md)  

> Philosopher and naturalist whose physics, cosmology and biology dominated European and Islamic science until the seventeenth century. He was the first great field biologist.

## Biography

Aristotle was the son of a court physician in Macedon. He studied for twenty years in Plato's Academy in Athens, then spent several years on the island of Lesbos and around the Aegean, where he and his student Theophrastus carried out much of their biological research. He tutored the young Alexander the Great, and in 335 BC founded his own school in Athens, the Lyceum, which gathered collections and research on an unprecedented scale.

In natural science Aristotle described a universe of nested celestial spheres around a central Earth, explained motion by the natural places of four elements, and sought the purposes, or final causes, of things. Many of these ideas were later overturned, but his biological works, based on dissection and careful observation of hundreds of animals, were unmatched for two thousand years. Translated into Arabic and then Latin, his system framed the questions that the Scientific Revolution set out to answer differently.

### Physics (c. 350 BC)

- **Date:** c. 350 BC
- **Type:** Treatise on natural philosophy
- **Published / where:** Greek manuscripts; Latin translations from the 12th century
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Physics%20Aristotle)

Aristotle's Physics, from physis, nature, is a set of eight books of lectures on the principles of everything that changes. It defines nature as an inner source of motion and rest, distinguishes the four causes, material, formal, efficient and final, and analyses change, the infinite, place, void and time. Aristotle argues that a void is impossible, that a moving body needs a mover in contact with it, and, in the final books, that the eternal motion of the heavens requires an unmoved first mover. Together with On the Heavens, which described the four elements seeking their natural places at the centre of a spherical cosmos, it framed physics in the Islamic world and medieval Europe, where scholars wrote long commentaries on it and criticised its theory of projectiles. The new mechanics of Galileo and Newton was built largely in opposition to it.

### History of Animals (c. 343–330 BC)

- **Date:** c. 343–330 BC
- **Type:** Zoological treatise
- **Published / where:** Greek manuscripts; Latin translation by Theodorus Gaza, 1476
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=History%20of%20Animals%20Aristotle)

The History of Animals, in Greek Historia peri ta zoa, meaning inquiry into animals, collects observations on more than five hundred kinds of animal, many of them marine creatures studied around the lagoon of Pyrrha on Lesbos. Aristotle compares their parts, ways of life, habitats, feeding, breeding and character, drawing on dissection, the reports of fishermen and hunters, and earlier writers. Some observations were not confirmed for two thousand years, such as the placenta-like attachment of the young in the smooth dogfish and the arm used for mating by the octopus; others are mistaken. With its companion works On the Parts of Animals and On the Generation of Animals, it set out a programme of comparative biology. Through Arabic translations and Latin versions by Michael Scot and later Theodorus Gaza, it shaped natural history into the Renaissance, and Darwin praised Aristotle as one of the greatest observers.
