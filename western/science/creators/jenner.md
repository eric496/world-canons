# Edward Jenner

**1749 – 1823** · English  
Born: Berkeley, Gloucestershire · Died: Berkeley, Gloucestershire  
Eras & fields: [Enlightenment chemistry & natural history](../movements/enlightenment-science.md)  

> Country doctor who showed that inoculation with cowpox protects against smallpox, creating the first vaccine.

<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Edward Jenner · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edward_Jenner.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Jenner was the son of the vicar of Berkeley. He was apprenticed to a surgeon and then studied in London under the great surgeon-anatomist John Hunter, who encouraged him to experiment. He returned to Berkeley as a country practitioner, studied the nesting of cuckoos and the hibernation of hedgehogs, and was elected to the Royal Society for his work on the cuckoo.

Smallpox was then one of the great killers, and the risky practice of variolation, inoculating with smallpox matter itself, was common. Jenner investigated the country belief that milkmaids who caught cowpox were immune, and in 1796 inoculated a boy, James Phipps, with cowpox and later showed he could not be infected with smallpox. He published his results in 1798, and vaccination, from the Latin vacca for cow, spread rapidly around the world. Jenner spent much of his life promoting it; smallpox was declared eradicated in 1980.

## Key works & discoveries

### An Inquiry into the Causes and Effects of the Variolae Vaccinae (1798)

- **Date:** 1798
- **Type:** Medical treatise
- **Published / where:** London: Sampson Low
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=An%20Inquiry%20into%20the%20Causes%20and%20Effects%20of%20the%20Variolae%20Vaccinae%20Edward%20Jenner)

In May 1796 Jenner took matter from cowpox sores on the hand of a dairymaid, Sarah Nelmes, and inserted it into scratches on the arm of James Phipps, the eight-year-old son of his gardener. Six weeks later he inoculated the boy with smallpox matter and no disease followed. The Royal Society declined to publish his short paper, so Jenner gathered more cases and in 1798 published this 75-page book at his own expense, with coloured plates of cowpox pustules on Sarah Nelmes's hand. He argued that cowpox, variolae vaccinae, gave lasting protection against smallpox without the dangers of variolation with smallpox itself. Despite opposition and caricatures showing vaccinated people sprouting cows, vaccination spread across Europe and the Americas within a few years, Napoleon had his army vaccinated, and Spain sent a vaccination expedition around its empire. Jenner's work led to the global eradication of smallpox, declared by the World Health Organization in 1980. The word vaccine was later generalised by Pasteur.
