# James Watson

**1928 – 2025** · American  
Born: Chicago, Illinois · Died: East Northport, New York  
Eras & fields: [Genetics & molecular biology](../movements/molecular-biology.md)  

> Biologist who with Francis Crick proposed the double-helix structure of DNA in 1953, and later led the Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory and the early Human Genome Project.

## Biography

Watson entered the University of Chicago at fifteen, took his doctorate at Indiana University on viruses that infect bacteria, and went to Europe as a postdoctoral fellow. In 1951 he joined the Cavendish Laboratory in Cambridge, where he met Francis Crick. He later taught at Harvard, was director and later president of the Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory in New York, and was the first head of the National Institutes of Health genome effort. His late career was overshadowed by repeated public remarks about race and intelligence that led institutions to strip him of honours.

With Crick he built a model of DNA as two helical chains held together by paired bases, using the X-ray data of Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins, and published it in Nature in 1953. Watson, Crick and Wilkins shared the 1962 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. His candid memoir The Double Helix (1968) became a bestseller and was criticised for its portrayal of Franklin. His textbooks shaped the teaching of molecular biology.

## Key works & discoveries

### Molecular Structure of Nucleic Acids: A Structure for Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid (1953)

[![Molecular Structure of Nucleic Acids: A Structure for Deoxyribose Nucleic Acid](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b8/DNA-structure-and-bases.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:DNA-structure-and-bases.png)

<sub>Diagram of the DNA double helix and its base pairs Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:DNA-structure-and-bases.png).</sub>

- **Date:** 1953
- **Type:** Scientific paper (with Francis Crick)
- **Published / where:** Nature, London, 25 April 1953
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Molecular%20Structure%20of%20Nucleic%20Acids%3A%20A%20Structure%20for%20Deoxyribose%20Nucleic%20Acid%20James%20Watson)

Working at the Cavendish Laboratory in Cambridge, Watson and Crick built physical models from wire and metal plates to find a structure that fitted the chemical facts about DNA and the X-ray evidence. After a failed triple-helix model in 1951, and with the help of Franklin's Photo 51 and measurements and Erwin Chargaff's finding that DNA contains equal amounts of adenine and thymine and of guanine and cytosine, they arrived at the final model in early March 1953. In it two chains of sugars and phosphates wind around each other in opposite directions, with the bases on the inside joined in pairs, adenine with thymine and guanine with cytosine, by hydrogen bonds. The paper, little more than a page with a single diagram drawn by Crick's wife Odile, ends with a famously understated sentence noting that the specific pairing immediately suggests a possible copying mechanism for the genetic material. It appeared with supporting papers from Wilkins's and Franklin's groups. The structure explained how genes can be copied and how they can carry information, and it launched molecular biology. Watson, Crick and Wilkins shared the Nobel Prize in 1962.
