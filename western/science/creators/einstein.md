# Albert Einstein

**1879 – 1955** · German-born (Swiss, American)  
Born: Ulm, Kingdom of Württemberg · Died: Princeton, New Jersey  
Eras & fields: [Relativity & quantum mechanics](../movements/relativity-quantum.md), [Cosmology & the expanding universe](../movements/cosmology.md)  

> Theoretical physicist who created the special and general theories of relativity and helped found quantum theory. He became the most famous scientist of the twentieth century.

## Biography

Einstein grew up in Munich, left school without graduating and studied physics at the Zurich Polytechnic. Unable to find an academic post, he worked at the Swiss patent office in Bern, where in 1905 he published four papers that changed physics. He held professorships in Zurich and Prague and from 1914 in Berlin. The 1919 eclipse expedition confirming general relativity made him world-famous, and he won the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics for the photoelectric effect. Jewish and a pacifist, he left Germany in 1933 and settled at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton.

Special relativity showed that space and time depend on the observer's motion and that mass and energy are equivalent. General relativity, completed in 1915, explained gravity as the curvature of spacetime by matter and energy and predicted the bending of light, gravitational waves and black holes. His 1905 explanation of the photoelectric effect treated light as quanta, and he made many other contributions to quantum and statistical physics, though he never accepted that quantum mechanics was a complete description of reality. He spent his last decades seeking a unified field theory and speaking for peace and civil rights.

### On a Heuristic Point of View Concerning the Production and Transformation of Light (1905)

- **Date:** 1905
- **Type:** Scientific paper
- **Published / where:** Annalen der Physik, Leipzig
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20a%20Heuristic%20Point%20of%20View%20Concerning%20the%20Production%20and%20Transformation%20of%20Light%20Albert%20Einstein)

In this paper, received in March 1905, Einstein proposed that light, despite all the evidence that it is a wave, behaves in some situations as if it consists of independent packets, or quanta, of energy proportional to its frequency, extending Planck's quantum hypothesis from matter to radiation itself. He applied the idea to the photoelectric effect, in which light falling on a metal surface ejects electrons: experiments by Philipp Lenard had shown that the electrons' energy depends on the light's colour, not its brightness, which wave theory could not explain. Einstein predicted a simple linear relation between frequency and electron energy. The idea was received with great scepticism, even by Planck, and Robert Millikan set out to disprove the relation, but confirmed it precisely in 1914–16. It was for this work, rather than relativity, that Einstein received the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics. The light quanta, later called photons, were central to quantum mechanics and the idea of wave-particle duality.

### On the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies (special relativity) (1905)

- **Date:** 1905
- **Type:** Scientific paper
- **Published / where:** Annalen der Physik, Leipzig
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20the%20Electrodynamics%20of%20Moving%20Bodies%20%28special%20relativity%29%20Albert%20Einstein)

Zur Elektrodynamik bewegter Körper was received by the Annalen der Physik on 30 June 1905 and published in September, one of four papers that made 1905 Einstein's 'miracle year'. Starting from two postulates, that the laws of physics are the same for all observers moving uniformly, and that light always travels at the same speed in empty space regardless of the motion of its source, Einstein showed that the ideas of absolute time and simultaneity must be abandoned. Moving clocks run slow and moving rods are shortened in their direction of motion, as seen by a stationary observer, and the transformations between observers' measurements, found earlier by Lorentz in a different interpretation, follow simply. The ether, the supposed medium of light, becomes superfluous. In a short follow-up paper later that year Einstein showed that a body's mass is a measure of its energy content, the relation usually written E = mc². In 1908 Hermann Minkowski reformulated the theory as a geometry of four-dimensional spacetime.

### General theory of relativity (1915–1916)

- **Date:** 1915–1916
- **Type:** Scientific papers
- **Published / where:** Prussian Academy of Sciences, Berlin; Annalen der Physik, 1916
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=General%20theory%20of%20relativity%20Albert%20Einstein)

From 1907, when he realised that a person falling freely would not feel their own weight, Einstein worked to extend relativity to accelerated motion and gravity. With the help of his friend the mathematician Marcel Grossmann he learned Riemann's geometry and the tensor calculus, and after years of false starts presented the final field equations to the Prussian Academy in Berlin in November 1915, publishing a full account in the Annalen der Physik in 1916. In general relativity, matter and energy curve spacetime, and bodies move along the straightest possible paths in the curved geometry, which appears to us as gravitational attraction. The theory explained the long-standing anomaly in the orbit of Mercury and predicted that light passing the Sun would be deflected by twice the Newtonian amount; the British eclipse expeditions led by Arthur Eddington and Frank Dyson in 1919 reported confirmation, making Einstein world-famous. Its later predictions, the expansion of the universe, black holes, gravitational lensing and gravitational waves, first detected directly in 2015, have all been confirmed.
