# Christiaan Huygens

**1629 – 1695** · Dutch  
Born: The Hague, Dutch Republic · Died: The Hague  
Eras & fields: [Scientific Revolution](../movements/scientific-revolution.md)  

> Mathematician, astronomer and physicist who invented the pendulum clock, discovered Saturn's ring and its moon Titan and developed the wave theory of light.

## Biography

Huygens was the son of Constantijn Huygens, poet and secretary to the princes of Orange, and grew up among the cultural elite of the Dutch Republic; Descartes was a family friend. He studied law and mathematics at Leiden and Breda. With his brother he ground excellent telescope lenses, and in 1655 discovered Titan. From 1666 to 1681 he was the leading member of the new Académie Royale des Sciences in Paris, before returning to The Hague.

Huygens patented the pendulum clock in 1657, the first accurate timekeeper, and in Horologium Oscillatorium analysed the pendulum mathematically, with results on centrifugal force and curves that influenced Newton. He explained the strange appearance of Saturn as a ring, wrote an early treatise on probability, and proposed that light is a wave spreading through an ether, explaining reflection and refraction with the principle that still bears his name. He was perhaps the greatest scientist between Galileo and Newton.

### Horologium Oscillatorium (1673)

- **Date:** 1673
- **Type:** Treatise on clocks and mechanics
- **Published / where:** Paris: F. Muguet
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Horologium%20Oscillatorium%20Christiaan%20Huygens)

Huygens built the first pendulum clock in 1656 and had it made by the clockmaker Salomon Coster in The Hague, reducing the error of the best clocks from about fifteen minutes to well under a minute a day. In Horologium Oscillatorium, published in Paris and dedicated to Louis XIV, he described improved clocks and provided their mathematical theory. He showed that a pendulum swinging in a circular arc is not exactly regular, but one swinging along a cycloid is, and he showed how to make it follow that curve with curved metal cheeks. On the way he developed the theory of evolutes of curves, found the formula for the period of a pendulum, analysed the compound pendulum and stated the laws of centrifugal force. The book was one of the most important works of mechanics before Newton's Principia, which owed much to it.

### Treatise on Light (1690)

- **Date:** 1690
- **Type:** Treatise on optics
- **Published / where:** Leiden: Pieter van der Aa
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Treatise%20on%20Light%20Christiaan%20Huygens)

Huygens read his Traité de la lumière to the Paris Academy in 1679 but published it only in 1690. He proposed that light is a disturbance travelling at a finite speed through an elastic ether, and that each point on a wave front acts as the source of new spherical wavelets, whose common envelope forms the next wave front. This construction, Huygens's principle, gave elegant explanations of reflection and refraction, and he used it to explain the strange double refraction of Iceland spar, a crystal that splits light into two rays. His theory was eclipsed for a century by Newton's view that light consists of particles, but it was revived when Thomas Young and Augustin Fresnel showed in the early nineteenth century that light waves can interfere, and Huygens's principle remains part of optics today.
