# Georg Cantor

**1845 – 1918** · German  
Born: Saint Petersburg, Russia · Died: Halle, Germany  
Eras & fields: [Mathematics in the age of rigour](../movements/nineteenth-century-mathematics.md), [Logic & the foundations of mathematics](../movements/logic-foundations.md)  

> Mathematician who created set theory and showed that there are different sizes of infinity, some infinite sets being strictly larger than others.

## Biography

Cantor was born in Saint Petersburg to a merchant family that moved to Germany when he was eleven. He studied at Zurich, Berlin and Göttingen and spent his whole career at the University of Halle. His ideas met fierce opposition, especially from Leopold Kronecker, and he suffered recurring bouts of depression from 1884 onwards, spending periods in sanatoriums. He died in a Halle sanatorium during the First World War.

Starting from problems about trigonometric series, Cantor developed a theory of sets and their sizes. In 1874 he proved that the real numbers cannot be put in one-to-one correspondence with the whole numbers, and in 1891 gave his famous diagonal argument showing that for any set there is a larger one. He built an arithmetic of transfinite numbers and posed the continuum hypothesis. Hilbert called set theory a paradise from which no one would drive mathematicians, and it became the common language of modern mathematics.

## Key works & discoveries

### Cantor's diagonal argument and the theory of transfinite numbers (1874–1897)

[![Cantor's diagonal argument and the theory of transfinite numbers](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c8/Diagonal_argument_01.svg/960px-Diagonal_argument_01.svg.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Diagonal_argument_01.svg)

<sub>Diagram of the diagonal argument Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Diagonal_argument_01.svg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1874–1897
- **Type:** Mathematical papers
- **Published / where:** Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik; Jahresbericht der Deutschen Mathematiker-Vereinigung, 1891
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Cantor%27s%20diagonal%20argument%20and%20the%20theory%20of%20transfinite%20numbers%20Georg%20Cantor)

In an 1874 paper with a deliberately modest title, Cantor proved that although the algebraic numbers can be listed one by one, like the whole numbers, the real numbers cannot: there are, in a precise sense, more points on a line than there are whole numbers. In 1891 he gave a simpler and more general proof, the diagonal argument: given any list of infinite sequences of digits, one can construct a new sequence that differs from the first in its first digit, from the second in its second, and so on, so it cannot be in the list. The same idea shows that the set of all subsets of any set is always larger than the set itself, so there is no largest infinity. In papers up to 1897 Cantor built an arithmetic of infinite cardinal and ordinal numbers. His work was attacked by some mathematicians as meaningless, and paradoxes found around 1900 led to the axiomatic set theory that now underlies most of mathematics. The diagonal method was later used by Gödel and Turing.
