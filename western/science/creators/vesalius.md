# Andreas Vesalius

**1514 – 1564** · Flemish  
Born: Brussels, Habsburg Netherlands · Died: Zakynthos, Venetian Republic  
Eras & fields: [Renaissance anatomy & astronomy](../movements/renaissance-science.md)  

> Anatomist who founded modern human anatomy on dissection, correcting Galen in his magnificently illustrated Fabrica of 1543.

<sub>Portrait: Woodcut portrait of Vesalius from the Fabrica, 1543 · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Andreae_Vesali_%28BM_1877%2C1013.1171%29.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Vesalius came from a family of imperial physicians and apothecaries in Brussels. He studied at Leuven and Paris, where he collected bones from cemeteries and gallows, and in 1537 took his medical degree at Padua and was immediately appointed to teach surgery and anatomy. Unusually, he performed dissections himself before his students rather than leaving them to a barber-surgeon. After the Fabrica he became physician to Emperor Charles V and then Philip II of Spain; he died on the island of Zakynthos returning from a pilgrimage to Jerusalem.

By dissecting human bodies, Vesalius showed that many of Galen's descriptions were based on animals and did not apply to humans, and he insisted that anatomy should be learned from the body itself rather than from books. The Fabrica, with its large woodcuts from the workshop of Titian, set a new standard for scientific illustration. His approach of direct observation made Padua the leading medical school of Europe, where Harvey later studied.

## Key works & discoveries

### De humani corporis fabrica (1543)

[![De humani corporis fabrica](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Vesalius_Fabrica_fronticepiece.jpg/960px-Vesalius_Fabrica_fronticepiece.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vesalius_Fabrica_fronticepiece.jpg)

<sub>Title page of the Fabrica, 1543, showing Vesalius dissecting before a crowd Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vesalius_Fabrica_fronticepiece.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1543
- **Type:** Anatomical atlas
- **Published / where:** Basel: Johannes Oporinus
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=De%20humani%20corporis%20fabrica%20Andreas%20Vesalius)

The Fabrica, published in Basel in 1543, the same year as Copernicus' book, describes the human body in seven books on bones, muscles, blood vessels, nerves, abdominal organs, heart and lungs, and brain. It was based on Vesalius's own dissections in Padua, and he pointed out more than two hundred errors in Galen, showing that Galen had described animals rather than humans. Over two hundred woodcut illustrations, probably by artists in Titian's workshop including Jan Steven van Calcar, show skeletons and flayed 'muscle men' posed in Italian landscapes, and the title page depicts Vesalius dissecting before a crowd. The woodblocks were carefully cut and shipped across the Alps to the printer. The Fabrica, and the shorter Epitome for students, set a new standard for accuracy and illustration, and established the principle that anatomy must be learned from the body. The surviving blocks were destroyed in the bombing of Munich in 1944.
