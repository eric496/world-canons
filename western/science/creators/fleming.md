# Alexander Fleming

**1881 – 1955** · Scottish  
Born: Darvel, Ayrshire, Scotland · Died: London  
Eras & fields: [Public health, germ theory & modern medicine](../movements/germ-theory.md)  

> Bacteriologist who discovered penicillin in 1928, the first antibiotic, and the antibacterial enzyme lysozyme.

## Biography

Fleming grew up on a farm in Ayrshire, moved to London and worked in a shipping office before a legacy allowed him to study medicine at St Mary's Hospital Medical School. He spent his career at St Mary's, in the inoculation department of Sir Almroth Wright, and served in battlefield hospitals in France in the First World War, where he saw many soldiers die of infected wounds. He shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1945 with Howard Florey and Ernst Chain and was knighted in 1944.

In 1928 Fleming noticed that a mould contaminating a culture plate of staphylococci had killed the bacteria around it. He identified it as Penicillium and named its active substance penicillin, but could not purify it in useful quantities. A decade later Florey, Chain, Norman Heatley and colleagues in Oxford developed it into a drug, and it was mass-produced during the Second World War. Penicillin opened the age of antibiotics, and Fleming warned early that misuse would breed resistant bacteria.

### On a Remarkable Bacteriolytic Element Found in Tissues and Secretions (lysozyme) (1922)

- **Date:** 1922
- **Type:** Scientific paper
- **Published / where:** Proceedings of the Royal Society B, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20a%20Remarkable%20Bacteriolytic%20Element%20Found%20in%20Tissues%20and%20Secretions%20%28lysozyme%29%20Alexander%20Fleming)

In 1921, while suffering from a cold, Fleming added a drop of his own nasal mucus to a culture plate of bacteria and later noticed that the bacteria around it had dissolved. He went on to show that a similar substance was present in tears, saliva, egg white, blood and many tissues, and named it lysozyme, an enzyme that breaks down the cell walls of some bacteria. He published the discovery in 1922. Lysozyme mostly attacks harmless bacteria and was of little use as a drug, but the experience of looking for natural substances that kill bacteria prepared Fleming to recognise the significance of the contaminating mould he found in 1928. Lysozyme later became important in research: it was the first enzyme whose three-dimensional structure was determined, by David Phillips's group in 1965, and it is part of the body's innate defence against infection.

### History of penicillin: Fleming's discovery (1928; published 1929)

- **Date:** 1928; published 1929
- **Type:** Discovery of an antibiotic
- **Published / where:** St Mary's Hospital, London; published in the British Journal of Experimental Pathology, 1929
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=History%20of%20penicillin%3A%20Fleming%27s%20discovery%20Alexander%20Fleming)

Returning from holiday in September 1928, Fleming examined culture plates of Staphylococcus bacteria that he had left on his bench at St Mary's Hospital and noticed that on one of them a contaminating mould was surrounded by a clear zone where the bacteria had been destroyed. He grew the mould, identified as Penicillium notatum, now Penicillium rubens, found that its broth killed many disease-causing bacteria but was harmless to white blood cells, and named the active substance penicillin. He published his findings in 1929 and used it as a laboratory tool for separating bacteria, but its instability and the difficulty of purifying it discouraged him from developing it as a drug. In 1939–41 Howard Florey, Ernst Chain, Norman Heatley and their colleagues in Oxford purified it, showed its spectacular effects in mice and then in patients, and with American industry developed mass production during the Second World War. His laboratory at St Mary's is now the Alexander Fleming Laboratory Museum.
