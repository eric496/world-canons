# Isaac Newton

**1643 – 1727** · English  
Born: Woolsthorpe, Lincolnshire · Died: Kensington, London  
Eras & fields: [The Newtonian age](../movements/newtonian-age.md)  

> Mathematician and physicist who discovered the laws of motion and universal gravitation, invented calculus independently of Leibniz and showed that white light is a mixture of colours.

## Biography

Newton was born on Christmas Day 1642 by the old calendar, 4 January 1643 by the modern one, after his father's death, and was largely raised by his grandmother. He studied at Trinity College, Cambridge, and in 1665–66, when the university closed for the plague, made his first great discoveries in mathematics, optics and gravitation at home. He became Lucasian Professor at Cambridge in 1669, and later Warden and Master of the Royal Mint in London and President of the Royal Society. He also devoted great effort to alchemy and biblical chronology, and was knighted in 1705.

Newton's Principia of 1687 set out three laws of motion and a law of universal gravitation that explained the orbits of planets and comets, the tides and falling bodies with one set of principles. In optics he showed with prisms that white light is composed of colours and built the first reflecting telescope. His development of the calculus led to a bitter priority dispute with Leibniz. His work became the model of physical science for two centuries, until relativity and quantum theory showed its limits.

### Newton's reflecting telescope (1668)

- **Date:** 1668
- **Type:** Scientific instrument
- **Published / where:** Replica at the Royal Society, London
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Newton%27s%20reflecting%20telescope%20Isaac%20Newton)

Newton's studies of colour convinced him that lens telescopes could never be free of chromatic aberration, the coloured fringes caused by different colours bending by different amounts. In 1668 he built a small telescope that used a concave mirror of speculum metal, an alloy of copper and tin that he cast and polished himself, to gather light, with a small flat mirror at an angle to send the image to an eyepiece in the side of the tube. Only about fifteen centimetres long, it magnified about forty times, as well as a refracting telescope several times its length. A second instrument was shown to the Royal Society in 1671, which led to Newton's election as a fellow and the publication of his theory of light. The design, now called the Newtonian telescope, remains popular with amateur astronomers, and all the largest modern telescopes are reflectors.

### Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica (1687)

- **Date:** 1687
- **Type:** Treatise on mechanics and gravitation
- **Published / where:** London: Joseph Streater for the Royal Society
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Philosophi%C3%A6%20Naturalis%20Principia%20Mathematica%20Isaac%20Newton)

The Principia grew out of a visit by Edmond Halley to Cambridge in 1684 to ask Newton what orbit a planet would follow under a force decreasing with the square of distance; Newton replied that it would be an ellipse and spent the next two years writing the book, which Halley edited and paid to have printed. It opens with definitions and three laws of motion, including the law of inertia and the relation between force and change of motion. Book I develops the mathematics of motion under central forces, Book II treats motion in resisting media and refutes Descartes's vortices, and Book III, The System of the World, shows that a single universal gravitation, acting between all bodies in proportion to their masses and inversely as the square of their distance, explains Kepler's laws, the motions of the Moon and comets, the tides and the flattening of the Earth. Written largely in the language of geometry, it was hard reading, but it became the foundation of physics for two centuries. Revised editions appeared in 1713 and 1726.

### Opticks (1704)

- **Date:** 1704
- **Type:** Treatise on light and colour
- **Published / where:** London: Samuel Smith and Benjamin Walford, printers to the Royal Society
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Opticks%20Isaac%20Newton)

Opticks: or, a Treatise of the Reflexions, Refractions, Inflexions and Colours of Light was published in English the year after the death of Newton's rival Robert Hooke, though much of its content dated from experiments of the 1660s and 1670s. Using prisms, Newton showed that white sunlight is split into a spectrum of colours that cannot be further changed by a second prism, and that a second prism can recombine them into white, so that colour is a property of light, not something added by the prism. He explained the rainbow and the colours of thin films, including the rings now named after him, and described his reflecting telescope, built to avoid the colour fringes of lenses. In the Queries at the end, which grew with each edition, he speculated on the particle nature of light, the ether, chemical forces and the method of science. Written more accessibly than the Principia, the Opticks was a model of experimental science for the eighteenth century.
