# Johannes Kepler

**1571 – 1630** · German  
Born: Weil der Stadt, Württemberg · Died: Regensburg, Bavaria  
Eras & fields: [Scientific Revolution](../movements/scientific-revolution.md)  

> Astronomer who discovered that the planets move in ellipses, with speeds and periods governed by exact laws. He also founded modern optics and explained how the eye and the telescope work.

## Biography

Kepler grew up in modest and troubled circumstances in Württemberg and was educated for the Lutheran ministry at Tübingen, where his teacher Michael Maestlin introduced him to Copernicus. He became a mathematics teacher in Graz, joined Tycho Brahe in Prague in 1600 and succeeded him as imperial mathematician. His later life was disrupted by religious war and persecution: he had to defend his mother in a witchcraft trial, and he moved between Linz, Ulm and Sagan before dying at Regensburg in 1630.

Kepler was a convinced Copernican who sought the geometrical harmonies by which he believed God had ordered the cosmos. Working for years on Tycho's observations of Mars, he abandoned circles and found that the orbit is an ellipse with the Sun at one focus, and that a planet sweeps out equal areas in equal times; later he found the relation between orbital period and distance. He also explained the inverted image on the retina, the theory of the telescope and the inverse-square fall of light. His laws became the foundation on which Newton built his theory of gravity.

### Mysterium Cosmographicum (1596)

- **Date:** 1596
- **Type:** Astronomical treatise
- **Published / where:** Tübingen: Georg Gruppenbach
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Mysterium%20Cosmographicum%20Johannes%20Kepler)

The Cosmographic Mystery was Kepler's first book and the first openly Copernican treatise since De revolutionibus. Kepler believed he had discovered why there are exactly six planets and why they are spaced as they are: between the spheres of the planets, God had placed the five Platonic solids, cube, tetrahedron, dodecahedron, icosahedron and octahedron, nested within one another. A famous fold-out plate shows this model, which Kepler even hoped to have made as a silver drinking bowl for the Duke of Württemberg. The scheme was wrong, but the book was significant because Kepler sought physical causes, suggesting that a force from the Sun drives the planets and weakens with distance. It brought him to the attention of Tycho Brahe and Galileo, and he republished it with notes in 1621, saying that almost all his later work had grown from it.

### Astronomia nova (1609)

- **Date:** 1609
- **Type:** Astronomical treatise
- **Published / where:** Heidelberg: Gotthard Vögelin (printed in Prague)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Astronomia%20nova%20Johannes%20Kepler)

Astronomia nova grew out of Kepler's 'war with Mars', years of calculation using Tycho Brahe's observations of the planet whose orbit was hardest to fit. Kepler recounts his struggle step by step, including false starts: a model that fitted Tycho's positions to within eight minutes of arc was rejected because Tycho's data were better than that. He found that the planet's speed varies so that a line from the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal times, and finally that the orbit is an ellipse with the Sun at one focus, the first two of Kepler's laws. The subtitle presents it as a celestial physics based on causes, with the Sun moving the planets by a magnetic-like force. The book broke with two thousand years of uniform circular motion and was the basis of the Rudolphine Tables of 1627, which proved far more accurate than earlier tables.

### Harmonices Mundi (1619)

- **Date:** 1619
- **Type:** Treatise on cosmic harmony
- **Published / where:** Linz: Johann Planck
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Harmonices%20Mundi%20Johannes%20Kepler)

In The Harmony of the World Kepler pursued his lifelong belief that God created the cosmos according to geometrical and musical harmony. Its five books discuss regular polygons and solids, musical consonance, astrology and the motions of the planets, whose varying speeds he translated into musical intervals, giving each planet a melody and imagining the heavens singing in polyphony. Amid this, in the fifth book, he announced the discovery, made on 15 May 1618, that the squares of the planets' periods are proportional to the cubes of their mean distances from the Sun. This third law, along with the two of 1609, became the foundation of Newton's theory of gravitation. Kepler regarded the book as his greatest achievement, and its mixture of mysticism and mathematics shows how differently from modern scientists the founders of modern science could think.
