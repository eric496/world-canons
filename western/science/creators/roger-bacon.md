# Roger Bacon

**c. 1219 – c. 1292** · English  
Born: Ilchester, Somerset (probably) · Died: Oxford  
Eras & fields: [Medieval Latin scholarship](../movements/medieval-latin-science.md)  

> Franciscan friar and Oxford scholar who argued that mathematics and experience were the keys to knowledge of nature, and wrote influential work on optics.

<sub>Portrait: Statue of Roger Bacon, Oxford University Museum of Natural History · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Roger-bacon-statue.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Bacon studied and taught at Oxford and Paris, lecturing on Aristotle, and became a Franciscan friar around 1257. At the request of Pope Clement IV he composed, in great haste, a series of works setting out a reform of learning, of which the Opus Majus of 1267 is the largest. Later in life he seems to have fallen foul of his order, and a story that he was imprisoned for his teaching is uncertain.

Bacon drew heavily on Arabic sources, above all Ibn al-Haytham's optics, and argued that a 'science of experience' should confirm the conclusions of reasoning. His writings describe lenses, the rainbow and an early recipe for gunpowder. Later legend made him a wizard who built a brazen talking head; in the nineteenth century he was hailed as a lone prophet of experimental science, a view historians now see as exaggerated, but he was among the most forceful medieval advocates of mathematical natural science.

## Key works & discoveries

### Opus Majus (1267)

[![Opus Majus](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d4/Bacon_-_Opus_maius%2C_1750_-_4325246.tif/lossy-page1-960px-Bacon_-_Opus_maius%2C_1750_-_4325246.tif.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bacon_-_Opus_maius%2C_1750_-_4325246.tif)

<sub>Title page of a 1750 edition of the Opus Majus Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bacon_-_Opus_maius%2C_1750_-_4325246.tif).</sub>

- **Date:** 1267
- **Type:** Encyclopedic treatise
- **Published / where:** Paris; sent to Pope Clement IV
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Opus%20Majus%20Roger%20Bacon)

The Opus Majus, Greater Work, was written in great haste after Pope Clement IV asked Bacon in 1266 to send him his writings in secret. Its seven parts discuss the causes of error, the relation of philosophy to theology, the study of languages so that scripture and science could be read in the original, the usefulness of mathematics in every science, optics, which Bacon called perspectiva, 'experimental science', and moral philosophy. The section on optics, drawing on Ibn al-Haytham, describes vision, reflection, refraction, the rainbow and the use of lenses to magnify letters for those with weak sight. In the part on experimental science Bacon argues that arguments alone cannot give certainty without the verification of experience. The pope died in 1268 and there is no evidence that he read it. It was printed only in 1733, but it has been seen as a landmark in the medieval defence of mathematics and observation.
