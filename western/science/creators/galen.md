# Galen of Pergamon

<a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Galenus.jpg"><img align="right" width="240" src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/80/Galenus.jpg" alt="Galen of Pergamon" title="Imagined portrait of Galen"></a>

**AD 129 – c. 216** · Greek  
Born: Pergamon, Asia Minor · Died: Rome (probably)  
Eras & fields: [Roman & late-antique science](../movements/roman-science.md)  

> Physician to Roman emperors whose voluminous writings on anatomy, physiology and therapy were the foundation of medicine in Europe and the Islamic world until the seventeenth century.

<sub>Portrait: Imagined portrait of Galen · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Galenus.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Galen was the son of a wealthy architect in Pergamon, a city with a famous healing sanctuary. He studied medicine there, in Smyrna and in Alexandria, then served as physician to the gladiators of Pergamon, where he learned much about wounds. From about AD 162 he lived in Rome, winning fame through public anatomical demonstrations, and became physician to Marcus Aurelius and his successors.

Galen wrote more than any other ancient author whose works survive. He combined Hippocratic medicine and the theory of the four humours with Aristotle's philosophy and his own extensive dissections, mostly of animals such as Barbary apes and pigs, and he showed by experiment that the arteries carry blood and that the nerves control the voice. Because human dissection was then forbidden, some of his anatomy was wrong, but his authority was so great that Vesalius and Harvey had to overturn him from his own methods.

## Key works & discoveries

### On the Usefulness of the Parts of the Body (c. 165–175)

- **Date:** c. 165–175
- **Type:** Anatomical and physiological treatise
- **Published / where:** Rome; Latin translations from the 14th century
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=On%20the%20Usefulness%20of%20the%20Parts%20of%20the%20Body%20Galen%20of%20Pergamon)

De usu partium, in Greek Peri chreias morion, is Galen's great work of functional anatomy, written in Rome. Book by book he describes the hand, arm, leg, organs of nutrition and breathing, heart, brain, nerves and eyes, explaining how each is perfectly designed for its purpose, in a spirit inspired by Plato and Aristotle that made it attractive to later Christian, Jewish and Muslim readers. His anatomy was based on careful dissection, but because human bodies were not available he relied on apes and other animals; he described, for example, the rete mirabile, a network of blood vessels at the base of the brain found in ungulates but not in humans. He believed that blood is made in the liver and consumed by the body, and that some passes through tiny pores in the wall between the ventricles of the heart. Vesalius exposed many of these errors in 1543, and Harvey overturned his physiology of the blood in 1628.
