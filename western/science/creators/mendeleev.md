# Dmitri Mendeleev

**1834 – 1907** · Russian  
Born: Verkhnie Aremzyani, near Tobolsk, Siberia · Died: Saint Petersburg  
Eras & fields: [Atoms & the periodic table](../movements/chemical-atomism.md)  

> Russian chemist who arranged the elements in a periodic table by atomic weight and properties, and used its gaps to predict elements not yet discovered.

## Biography

Mendeleev was the youngest of a large family in Siberia; after his father's death his mother took him to Saint Petersburg to study. He trained as a teacher, studied in Heidelberg and attended the 1860 Karlsruhe Congress, where chemists agreed on reliable atomic weights. He became professor of chemistry at Saint Petersburg University, and later director of the Russian bureau of weights and measures. He advised the government on oil, agriculture and industry.

While writing a textbook, The Principles of Chemistry, Mendeleev looked for a natural way to order the elements and in 1869 published a table arranging them by atomic weight in which chemical properties recurred periodically. He boldly left gaps and predicted the properties of the missing elements, and the discovery of gallium, scandium and germanium within two decades made the table famous. Others, notably Lothar Meyer, made similar arrangements, but Mendeleev's predictions won him the credit. Element 101, mendelevium, is named after him.

## Key works & discoveries

### Periodic table of the elements (1869)

- **Date:** 1869
- **Type:** Classification of the elements
- **Published / where:** Saint Petersburg; published in the Zhurnal Russkogo Khimicheskogo Obshchestva and Zeitschrift für Chemie
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Periodic%20table%20of%20the%20elements%20Dmitri%20Mendeleev)

In February 1869, while writing his textbook, Mendeleev drew up a table of the 63 known elements in order of atomic weight, arranged so that elements with similar properties, such as the alkali metals or the halogens, fell into the same groups. He printed it as a single sheet titled An Attempt at a System of Elements, and presented a paper on it to the Russian Chemical Society in March. Where the order of weights clashed with chemical properties, he trusted the properties and suggested the weights were wrong, and he left gaps for missing elements. In 1871 he predicted in detail the properties of three of them, which he called eka-aluminium, eka-boron and eka-silicon. Gallium was discovered in 1875, scandium in 1879 and germanium in 1886, with properties closely matching his predictions. Later work showed that the order is set by atomic number, the charge of the nucleus, and the table's structure was explained by quantum mechanics. It is the central icon of chemistry.
