<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Blaise Pascal, c. 1690, Versailles · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Blaise_Pascal_Versailles%2C_circa_1690.JPG)</sub>

## Biography

Pascal was educated at home by his father, a tax official with a strong interest in mathematics, who took him to the scientific meetings of Marin Mersenne in Paris. At sixteen he wrote an essay on conic sections, and at nineteen he began building a mechanical calculator to help his father with tax accounts. After a religious experience in 1654 he turned increasingly to theology and the Jansenist cause, writing the Provincial Letters and the notes later published as the Pensées. He suffered poor health all his life and died at thirty-nine.

His experiments on the vacuum, including the measurement organised in 1648 on the Puy de Dôme showing that a barometer reads lower on a mountain, established that air pressure supports the column of mercury. He formulated the principle of pressure in fluids that bears his name. In correspondence with Fermat he founded the mathematical theory of probability, and his treatise on the arithmetical triangle gave a systematic account of the number pattern now called Pascal's triangle.

### Pascaline (1642–1652)

[![Pascaline](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/78/Pascaline-CnAM_823-1-IMG_1506-black.jpg/1280px-Pascaline-CnAM_823-1-IMG_1506-black.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pascaline-CnAM_823-1-IMG_1506-black.jpg)

<sub>A Pascaline, Musée des Arts et Métiers, Paris Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pascaline-CnAM_823-1-IMG_1506-black.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1642–1652
- **Type:** Mechanical calculator
- **Published / where:** Surviving examples in the Musée des Arts et Métiers, Paris, and elsewhere
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pascaline%20Blaise%20Pascal)

Pascal began designing a calculating machine in 1642, aged nineteen, to help his father, then supervising tax collection in Rouen. After some fifty prototypes he presented a finished machine in 1645 and obtained a royal privilege in 1649. It is a brass box with dials operated by a stylus; each dial turns a wheel, and an ingenious falling weight called a sautoir carries a one to the next wheel when a wheel passes nine, allowing reliable addition with carries across many digits. Subtraction was done by adding complements, and models for French currency had wheels for twenty sols and twelve deniers. About twenty machines were made, and nine survive. The Pascaline was expensive and delicate and sold poorly, but it was the first calculator to be produced in numbers and inspired Leibniz's stepped reckoner. The computer language Pascal is named in his honour.

### Traité du triangle arithmétique (1654; published 1665)

[![Traité du triangle arithmétique](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8c/FR-631136102_Em_0011_f_106_Triangulus_Arithmeticus_Definitiones.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:FR-631136102_Em_0011_f_106_Triangulus_Arithmeticus_Definitiones.png)

<sub>Page from Pascal's treatise on the arithmetical triangle Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:FR-631136102_Em_0011_f_106_Triangulus_Arithmeticus_Definitiones.png).</sub>

- **Date:** 1654; published 1665
- **Type:** Mathematical treatise
- **Published / where:** Paris: Guillaume Desprez (posthumous)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Trait%C3%A9%20du%20triangle%20arithm%C3%A9tique%20Blaise%20Pascal)

The arithmetical triangle, in which each number is the sum of the two above it, was known long before Pascal to mathematicians in India, Persia, China and Europe, where it appears for instance in the work of al-Karaji, Omar Khayyam and Yang Hui. Pascal's treatise, written in 1654 and printed after his death in 1665, gave the first systematic account of its properties and applications in Europe. He proved its properties using an early explicit statement of mathematical induction, and applied the triangle to combinations, the expansion of powers of binomials and the problem of points, how to divide the stakes fairly when a game of chance is interrupted, which he was discussing with Fermat at the same time. Because of this work the triangle is called Pascal's triangle in the West, though it is known as Yang Hui's triangle in China and Khayyam's in Iran.
