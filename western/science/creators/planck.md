# Max Planck

**1858 – 1947** · German  
Born: Kiel, Duchy of Holstein · Died: Göttingen, Germany  
Eras & fields: [Relativity & quantum mechanics](../movements/relativity-quantum.md)  

> Theoretical physicist whose hypothesis that energy is emitted in discrete quanta, made in 1900, began quantum theory.

## Biography

Planck came from a family of scholars and lawyers and studied at Munich and Berlin, where he heard Helmholtz and Kirchhoff. He was professor of theoretical physics at Berlin from 1889 to 1926 and a dominant figure in German science, presiding over the Kaiser Wilhelm Society. He won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1918. His later life was marked by tragedy: his elder son was killed at Verdun, and his younger son was executed in 1945 for involvement in the plot against Hitler.

Working on the problem of how the radiation emitted by a hot body depends on temperature and wavelength, Planck found in 1900 a formula that matched experiment, and could derive it only by assuming that energy is exchanged in packets proportional to frequency, with a new constant now called Planck's constant. He regarded this at first as a mathematical device; Einstein and Bohr showed it to be fundamental. Planck was also an early champion of Einstein's relativity.

## Key works & discoveries

### Planck's radiation law and the quantum of action (1900)

[![Planck's radiation law and the quantum of action](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Black_body.svg/1280px-Black_body.svg.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Black_body.svg)

<sub>Black-body radiation curves at different temperatures Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Black_body.svg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1900
- **Type:** Scientific papers
- **Published / where:** Verhandlungen der Deutschen Physikalischen Gesellschaft, Berlin; Annalen der Physik, 1901
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Planck%27s%20radiation%20law%20and%20the%20quantum%20of%20action%20Max%20Planck)

A black body, an ideal object that absorbs all radiation falling on it, emits light with a spectrum that depends only on its temperature, and measurements at the Physikalisch-Technische Reichsanstalt in Berlin in the 1890s gave precise data that no theory could fully explain. In October 1900 Planck found a formula that fitted the measurements perfectly at all wavelengths. To derive it, in a paper read to the German Physical Society on 14 December 1900, he had to assume that the oscillators emitting the radiation could only have energies in whole multiples of a small unit, proportional to frequency, with a constant of proportionality h, now called Planck's constant. Planck regarded this at first as a mathematical device and for years tried to reconcile it with classical physics. It was Einstein in 1905, treating light itself as made of quanta, and Bohr in 1913 who showed that quantisation was fundamental. Planck's constant is now one of the constants used to define the kilogram, since 2019.
