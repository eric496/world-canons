# Charles Baudelaire

**1821 – 1867** · French  
Born: Paris · Died: Paris  
Movements & eras: [Symbolism & Aestheticism](../movements/symbolism-aestheticism.md)  

> Poet of Les Fleurs du mal and the prose poems of Paris Spleen, art critic and translator of Poe, often called the first modern poet.

## Biography

Charles Baudelaire was born in Paris in 1821. His father died when he was six, and his mother married an army officer, General Aupick, whom Charles resented. Expelled from school and leading a bohemian life, he was sent on a voyage towards India in 1841 but turned back at Mauritius and Réunion. On coming of age he quickly ran through much of his inheritance, and his family placed his money under legal guardianship, leaving him in debt for life. He wrote art criticism, praising Delacroix and later Constantin Guys as the painter of modern life, and translated Poe's tales. Les Fleurs du mal appeared in 1857; he was prosecuted, fined and six poems were banned. A second, expanded edition came out in 1861. Ill with syphilis, he spent two unhappy years in Belgium, suffered a stroke and died in Paris in 1867.

Baudelaire combined classical verse forms with a modern subject matter of city streets, crowds, prostitutes, rag-pickers, boredom and moral decay, and found strange beauty in it. His theory of correspondences, the idea that perfumes, colours and sounds answer each other, inspired the Symbolists. His prose poems helped create a new genre. Verlaine, Rimbaud and Mallarmé followed him, and through them and T. S. Eliot his vision of the modern city entered twentieth-century poetry.

## Key works

### Les Fleurs du mal (1857; enlarged 1861)

- **Date:** 1857; enlarged 1861
- **Type:** Poetry collection
- **First published:** Paris, published by Poulet-Malassis et de Broise
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Les%20Fleurs%20du%20mal%20Charles%20Baudelaire)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Les+Fleurs+du+mal)

Les Fleurs du mal, The Flowers of Evil, was published in June 1857, and within weeks Baudelaire and his publishers were prosecuted for offending public morals. He was fined and six poems, mostly on lesbian love and erotic subjects, were banned from the book; the ban was lifted only in 1949. A second edition of 1861 added thirty-five poems, including the section Parisian Scenes, and a posthumous edition followed in 1868. The book opens with an address To the Reader, who is called hypocrite, my fellow, my brother, and is organised in sections including Spleen and Ideal, Wine, Flowers of Evil, Revolt and Death. In classical forms, especially the sonnet and the alexandrine, Baudelaire wrote about erotic love and disgust, ennui, the crowds and poor of modern Paris, intoxication and the longing for escape, in poems such as Correspondences, The Albatross, To a Passer-by and The Voyage. The collection is often seen as the beginning of modern poetry and a key source of Symbolism.
