# F. Scott Fitzgerald

**1896 – 1940** · American  
Born: St. Paul, Minnesota · Died: Hollywood, California  
Movements & eras: [Lost Generation & American modernism](../movements/lost-generation.md)  

> The chronicler of the Jazz Age, author of The Great Gatsby and Tender Is the Night.

## Biography

Francis Scott Key Fitzgerald was born in 1896 in St Paul, Minnesota, and studied at Princeton, which he left to join the army in 1917, though he was never sent overseas. While stationed in Alabama he met Zelda Sayre, who agreed to marry him after his first novel, This Side of Paradise (1920), became a bestseller. The couple became celebrities of the Jazz Age, living extravagantly in New York, on the French Riviera and in Paris, where Fitzgerald befriended Hemingway. The Great Gatsby (1925) was admired by critics but sold modestly. Zelda's mental illness, beginning in 1930, and his own alcoholism and debts darkened the following years; Tender Is the Night (1934) was a commercial failure. He worked as a Hollywood screenwriter and died of a heart attack in 1940, aged forty-four, leaving The Last Tycoon unfinished.

Fitzgerald wrote with lyrical precision about wealth, glamour, ambition and disillusion, and about the American Dream and its corruption. He supported himself mostly with stories for popular magazines, such as Babylon Revisited and The Diamond as Big as the Ritz. He died believing himself a failure, but the revival of interest after the Second World War made The Great Gatsby one of the most widely read American novels.

## Key works

### The Great Gatsby (1925)

- **Date:** 1925
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** New York, published by Charles Scribner's Sons
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Great%20Gatsby%20F.%20Scott%20Fitzgerald)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=The+Great+Gatsby)

The Great Gatsby was published in April 1925, with a famous cover by Francis Cugat showing a woman's face above the lights of an amusement park. It is narrated by Nick Carraway, a young Midwesterner who moves to West Egg on Long Island in the summer of 1922 to work in bonds. His neighbour is Jay Gatsby, a fabulously wealthy and mysterious man who throws enormous parties. Gatsby, it emerges, was born James Gatz to poor farmers and made his fortune in bootlegging, all to win back Daisy, Nick's cousin, whom he loved as a young officer and who is now married to the brutal, rich Tom Buchanan. Gatsby and Daisy resume their affair, but in a confrontation at the Plaza Hotel she chooses Tom. Driving Gatsby's car, Daisy kills Tom's mistress Myrtle, and Myrtle's husband shoots Gatsby, who dies alone. Fitzgerald's lyrical prose and his portrait of the Jazz Age and the corruption of the American Dream made the novel, which sold poorly at first, one of the most celebrated works of American literature.
