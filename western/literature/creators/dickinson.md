# Emily Dickinson

**1830 – 1886** · American  
Born: Amherst, Massachusetts · Died: Amherst, Massachusetts  
Movements & eras: [American Renaissance](../movements/american-renaissance.md)  

> The reclusive Amherst poet who wrote nearly 1,800 compressed, dazzling poems, almost none published in her lifetime.

**Links:** [Texts on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Emily+Dickinson)

## Biography

Emily Elizabeth Dickinson was born in 1830 in Amherst, Massachusetts, into a prominent family; her father was a lawyer and congressman. She studied at Amherst Academy and spent a year at Mount Holyoke Female Seminary, but declined to profess the evangelical faith expected of her. From her twenties she withdrew increasingly from society, eventually rarely leaving the family homestead, dressing in white and communicating with friends mainly by letter. Her most productive years were around the Civil War. She sent poems to the critic Thomas Wentworth Higginson, who advised against publication, and only about ten appeared in print during her life, anonymously and altered by editors. She died in Amherst in 1886. Her sister Lavinia then discovered hundreds of poems in a locked box, and the first selection was published in 1890.

Dickinson wrote mostly short poems in the hymn metres of her childhood, made strange by slant rhyme, dashes, capitalised nouns and startling compression. Her subjects are death, faith and doubt, nature, love and the inner life, approached with wit and metaphysical daring. Early editors regularised her punctuation; only Thomas Johnson's 1955 edition and R. W. Franklin's later edition presented the poems as she wrote them. She is now regarded, with Whitman, as one of the two founders of modern American poetry.

## Key works

### Poems (1890)

- **Date:** 1890
- **Type:** Poetry collection
- **First published:** Boston, published by Roberts Brothers (edited by Mabel Loomis Todd and T. W. Higginson)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Poems%20Emily%20Dickinson)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Poems+Dickinson)

Poems by Emily Dickinson was published in Boston in November 1890, four years after her death, and selected from the hundreds of manuscripts her sister Lavinia discovered. Its editors, Mabel Loomis Todd and Thomas Wentworth Higginson, arranged 115 poems under headings such as Life, Love, Nature and Time and Eternity, gave them titles and regularised much of her unconventional punctuation, rhyme and wording to suit contemporary taste. The book included poems now famous, such as Because I could not stop for Death, I never saw a moor and Success is counted sweetest. It sold well, going through eleven printings in two years, and was followed by second and third series in 1891 and 1896. Reviewers were divided, some praising her originality and others complaining of her roughness. Only with Thomas H. Johnson's complete edition of 1955 were the poems printed as she wrote them, with her characteristic dashes and capitals, revealing her as one of the most radical poets of the nineteenth century.
