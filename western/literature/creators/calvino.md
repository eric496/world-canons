# Italo Calvino

**1923 – 1985** · Italian  
Born: Santiago de las Vegas, Cuba · Died: Siena, Italy  
Movements & eras: [Postmodernism](../movements/postmodernism-literature.md)  

> Italian writer of fables, fantasies and formal experiments, including Invisible Cities and If on a winter's night a traveller.

## Biography

Italo Calvino was born in 1923 near Havana, Cuba, where his Italian parents were working as agronomists, and grew up in Sanremo on the Italian Riviera. During the Second World War he joined the communist partisans in the Ligurian mountains, the background of his first novel, The Path to the Spiders' Nests (1947). He worked for decades as an editor at the Einaudi publishing house in Turin and left the Communist Party after the Soviet invasion of Hungary in 1956. His trilogy Our Ancestors, including The Baron in the Trees, and his collection of Italian Folktales established him. Living in Paris from 1967, he joined the Oulipo group of experimental writers. Invisible Cities (1972) and If on a winter's night a traveller (1979) followed. He died in Siena in 1985 while preparing the lectures later published as Six Memos for the Next Millennium.

Calvino moved from neorealism to fantasy and to fiction built on combinatory rules and games, but his work is always light, clear and playful, informed by science, folk tale and a deep curiosity about how stories are made. He is one of the key figures of postmodern fiction and among the most translated Italian writers of the century.

## Key works

### Invisible Cities (1972)

- **Date:** 1972
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Turin, published by Einaudi
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Invisible%20Cities%20Italo%20Calvino)

Invisible Cities (Le città invisibili) was published in 1972. Its frame is a series of conversations between the Venetian traveller Marco Polo and the aged Tartar emperor Kublai Khan, who is trying to understand the vast empire he can never see for himself. Between these dialogues, Polo describes fifty-five cities, each with a woman's name, grouped under headings such as Cities and Memory, Cities and Desire, Cities and Signs, Thin Cities, Trading Cities, Cities and the Dead and Hidden Cities. There is a city suspended over an abyss on a net, a city whose inhabitants rebuild it continually, a city that exists only as a reflection, and a city made of nothing but water pipes. The book's mathematical structure reflects Calvino's links with the Oulipo, and as the dialogues proceed it emerges that every city Polo describes may be Venice. Short prose poems rather than a conventional narrative, the cities have inspired architects, urbanists and artists as well as readers of fiction.
