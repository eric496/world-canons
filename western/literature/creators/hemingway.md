# Ernest Hemingway

**1899 – 1961** · American  
Born: Oak Park, Illinois · Died: Ketchum, Idaho  
Movements & eras: [Lost Generation & American modernism](../movements/lost-generation.md)  

> The writer whose terse, understated prose transformed twentieth-century fiction, author of The Sun Also Rises and The Old Man and the Sea.

## Biography

Ernest Miller Hemingway was born in 1899 in Oak Park, Illinois, and worked as a reporter for the Kansas City Star before volunteering as an ambulance driver in Italy in the First World War, where he was badly wounded in 1918. In the 1920s he lived in Paris as a foreign correspondent, mixing with Gertrude Stein, Ezra Pound, Fitzgerald and Joyce, and published the stories of In Our Time and the novel The Sun Also Rises (1926), followed by A Farewell to Arms (1929). He later lived in Key West and Cuba, reported on the Spanish Civil War, which inspired For Whom the Bell Tolls (1940), and covered the Second World War in Europe. Married four times, he cultivated a public image of big-game hunter, fisherman and war correspondent. The Old Man and the Sea (1952) won the Pulitzer Prize, and he received the Nobel Prize in 1954. Suffering from depression and ill health, he died by suicide in Ketchum, Idaho, in 1961.

Hemingway's style, short declarative sentences, plain words, repetition and dialogue, aimed to convey emotion by leaving it unsaid, an approach he compared to an iceberg with most of its mass beneath the surface. His themes are war, love, loss, courage and stoic endurance. His influence on prose in English, from hard-boiled crime fiction to minimalism, has been enormous, while his portrayal of masculinity and of women has been much debated.

## Key works

### The Sun Also Rises (1926)

- **Date:** 1926
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** New York, published by Charles Scribner's Sons
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Sun%20Also%20Rises%20Ernest%20Hemingway)

The Sun Also Rises was published in October 1926 and based closely on a trip Hemingway and friends made to the fiesta of San Fermín in Pamplona in 1925. Its epigraphs set Gertrude Stein's remark, you are all a lost generation, against a passage from Ecclesiastes. The narrator, Jake Barnes, an American journalist in Paris, has been left impotent by a war wound and is in love with Lady Brett Ashley, a charming, promiscuous Englishwoman who loves him but will not live without sex. With Brett's fiancé Mike, the insecure Jewish writer Robert Cohn, who has had an affair with Brett, and Jake's friend Bill, they travel to Spain, fish in the Pyrenees and attend the bullfights in Pamplona, where Brett runs off with the young bullfighter Pedro Romero. The novel's stripped-down prose and understated dialogue made Hemingway's reputation and defined the disillusioned expatriate generation, though its portrayal of Cohn has been criticised as antisemitic.
