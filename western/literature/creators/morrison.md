# Toni Morrison

**1931 – 2019** · American  
Born: Lorain, Ohio · Died: The Bronx, New York  
Movements & eras: [Postmodernism](../movements/postmodernism-literature.md)  

> American novelist of Beloved and Song of Solomon, who placed African American history and experience at the centre of the novel. Nobel Prize 1993.

## Biography

Chloe Ardelia Wofford was born in 1931 in Lorain, Ohio, into a working-class family whose stories and songs she later drew on. She took the name Toni at Howard University, where she studied English, and earned a master's degree at Cornell. She taught at Howard, married and divorced, and in 1965 became an editor at Random House, where she published and championed African American writers. Her first novel, The Bluest Eye, appeared in 1970, followed by Sula, Song of Solomon (1977) and Tar Baby. Beloved (1987) won the Pulitzer Prize. In 1993 she became the first African American woman to receive the Nobel Prize in Literature. She taught at Princeton for many years and continued to publish novels, essays and criticism, including Playing in the Dark. She died in 2019.

Morrison wrote lyrical, layered novels that combine shifting points of view, myth, folklore and the supernatural with an unflinching treatment of slavery, racism, community and love. She wrote, as she said, without explaining Black life to a white gaze. Her work transformed the American canon and is widely taught around the world.

## Key works

### Beloved (1987)

- **Date:** 1987
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** New York, published by Alfred A. Knopf
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Beloved%20Toni%20Morrison)

Beloved was published in 1987 and won the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction in 1988. It was inspired by the story of Margaret Garner, an enslaved woman who escaped from Kentucky to Ohio in 1856 and killed her daughter rather than let her be recaptured. In 1873, near Cincinnati, Sethe lives with her daughter Denver at 124 Bluestone Road, a house haunted by the angry spirit of a baby. When Paul D, a man from the Kentucky plantation called Sweet Home where they were both enslaved, arrives, he drives the ghost out. Soon afterwards, a mysterious young woman who calls herself Beloved appears, and Sethe comes to believe that she is the daughter she killed eighteen years earlier. Through fragmented memories, the novel gradually reveals the horrors of slavery and what Sethe did, as Beloved's presence consumes her mother, until the community of women gathers to drive her away. Its lyrical, many-voiced prose and its confrontation with the legacy of slavery made it one of the most acclaimed American novels of its time.
