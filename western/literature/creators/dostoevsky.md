## Biography

Fyodor Mikhailovich Dostoevsky was born in Moscow in 1821, the son of a doctor at a hospital for the poor; his father was killed in 1839, possibly by his own serfs. He trained as a military engineer but resigned to write, and his first novel, Poor Folk (1846), was hailed by the critic Belinsky. In 1849 he was arrested for membership of the Petrashevsky circle of utopian socialists, sentenced to death and led out to be shot, only to have the sentence commuted at the last moment to four years of hard labour in Siberia followed by army service. He returned to Saint Petersburg in 1859 with epilepsy and a renewed Orthodox faith. Gambling, debts and the deaths of his first wife and brother marked the 1860s, but in Anna Snitkina, his stenographer and second wife, he found stability. Notes from Underground, Crime and Punishment, The Idiot, Demons and The Brothers Karamazov followed. He died in Saint Petersburg in 1881.

Dostoevsky's novels are dramas of ideas in which characters embody arguments about God, freedom, reason and morality, often in scenes of extreme tension, confession and scandal. The critic Mikhail Bakhtin described them as polyphonic: many voices, none simply the author's. He explored the psychology of guilt, pride and humiliation with an insight that impressed Nietzsche and Freud, and existentialist thinkers from Camus to Sartre saw him as a forerunner. His influence on the twentieth-century novel is immense.

- **Date:** 1864
- **Type:** Novella
- **First published:** Saint Petersburg, in the magazine Epoch (Epokha)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Notes%20from%20Underground%20Fyodor%20Dostoevsky)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Notes+from+Underground+Dostoevsky)

Notes from Underground was published in 1864 in Epoch, the journal Dostoevsky ran with his brother, in a year of personal loss. In the first part, Underground, an unnamed former official of forty, living in a squalid Saint Petersburg room, delivers a rambling, self-contradictory monologue against the utopian rationalism of writers such as Chernyshevsky, who believed humans would act in their own enlightened interest. He insists that people value their free will above happiness and will act against their interests simply to prove they are not piano keys, and he mocks the ideal of a crystal palace. In the second part, Apropos of the Wet Snow, he recalls episodes from his youth: his obsession with a soldier who brushed him aside, a disastrous dinner with old schoolmates and his cruelty to the young prostitute Liza, who offers him love. The work's unreliable, hyperconscious narrator anticipated the great novels to come and was read by Nietzsche and the existentialists as a key modern text.

### Crime and Punishment (1866)

- **Date:** 1866
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Moscow, serialised in The Russian Messenger (Russkiy Vestnik)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Crime%20and%20Punishment%20Fyodor%20Dostoevsky)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Crime+and+Punishment+Dostoevsky)

Crime and Punishment was serialised in The Russian Messenger through 1866, written under great financial pressure. Rodion Raskolnikov, a former student living in a tiny room in Saint Petersburg, has developed the theory that extraordinary men, like Napoleon, have the right to transgress moral law for a higher purpose. He murders an old pawnbroker with an axe, and is forced to kill her gentle sister too when she walks in. Falling into fever, he is drawn into a cat-and-mouse game with the shrewd investigator Porfiry Petrovich, while his mother and sister arrive and the cynical Svidrigailov pursues his sister. He finds compassion in Sonya Marmeladova, a young woman forced into prostitution to support her family, who reads him the story of Lazarus and persuades him to confess. In the epilogue he is sent to Siberia, where Sonya follows him and a spiritual renewal begins. Told mostly from inside Raskolnikov's feverish mind, the novel is a psychological thriller and a profound study of guilt, pride and redemption.

### The Brothers Karamazov (1879–1880)

[![The Brothers Karamazov](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2d/Dostoevsky-Brothers_Karamazov.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dostoevsky-Brothers_Karamazov.jpg)

<sub>Title page of the first edition, 1880 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dostoevsky-Brothers_Karamazov.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1879–1880
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Moscow, serialised in The Russian Messenger
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Brothers%20Karamazov%20Fyodor%20Dostoevsky)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=The+Brothers+Karamazov+Dostoevsky)

The Brothers Karamazov was serialised in 1879–80 and completed just months before Dostoevsky's death. In a provincial town, the buffoonish, lecherous landowner Fyodor Karamazov quarrels with his eldest son, the passionate army officer Dmitri, over money and the woman Grushenka. His second son Ivan is a brilliant sceptic, the youngest, Alyosha, a novice in the monastery of the holy elder Zosima, and the servant Smerdyakov is probably his illegitimate son. When Fyodor is murdered, Dmitri is arrested, tried and convicted, though the real killer was Smerdyakov, who claims he acted on Ivan's teaching that if there is no God everything is permitted. Ivan suffers a breakdown in which he converses with the devil. Within the novel, Ivan's prose poem The Grand Inquisitor, in which Christ returns to Seville during the Inquisition and is condemned by the church, is one of the most famous passages in literature. Freud called the novel the most magnificent ever written, and it is often regarded as Dostoevsky's masterpiece.
