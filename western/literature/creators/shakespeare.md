## Biography

William Shakespeare was baptised in Stratford-upon-Avon on 26 April 1564, the son of a glover and town official, and probably attended the local grammar school. At eighteen he married Anne Hathaway, with whom he had three children, including a son, Hamnet, who died at eleven. By 1592 he was established in London as an actor and playwright, and from 1594 he was a member and shareholder of the Lord Chamberlain's Men, later the King's Men, who built the Globe Theatre in 1599. Over about twenty-five years he wrote, alone or with collaborators, some thirty-nine plays, as well as the narrative poems Venus and Adonis and The Rape of Lucrece and a sequence of 154 sonnets. He retired to Stratford around 1613 and died there in 1616. Seven years later, two fellow actors published thirty-six of his plays in the First Folio.

Shakespeare wrote comedies, histories, tragedies and late romances, and in each he extended what the form could do. His characters, from Falstaff and Rosalind to Hamlet, Lear and Cleopatra, speak with an individual inner life that later critics saw as a new representation of human personality. His language is immensely rich, mixing high poetry and colloquial prose, and many of his phrases became part of everyday English. His reputation grew steadily, from Ben Jonson's tribute that he was not of an age but for all time to the Romantic idolatry of the nineteenth century, and his plays are now performed, translated and adapted around the world.

### Romeo and Juliet (c. 1595)

[![Romeo and Juliet](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/55/Romeo_and_juliet_brown.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Romeo_and_juliet_brown.jpg)

<sub>Ford Madox Brown, Romeo and Juliet, 1870 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Romeo_and_juliet_brown.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1595
- **Type:** Tragedy
- **First published:** London; first printed 1597 (quarto)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Romeo%20and%20Juliet%20William%20Shakespeare)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Romeo+and+Juliet+Shakespeare)

Romeo and Juliet is an early tragedy, based mainly on Arthur Brooke's poem The Tragicall Historye of Romeus and Juliet (1562), itself derived from Italian novellas. In Verona the Montagues and Capulets are locked in an ancient feud. Romeo, a Montague, gatecrashes a Capulet feast and falls in love with Juliet, the Capulet daughter; they meet at her balcony and are secretly married the next day by Friar Laurence. When Juliet's cousin Tybalt kills Romeo's friend Mercutio, Romeo kills Tybalt and is banished. To escape a forced marriage, Juliet takes a potion that simulates death, but the message explaining the plan fails to reach Romeo, who poisons himself at her tomb; Juliet wakes and stabs herself. The play's lyrical verse, including a sonnet shared by the lovers at their first meeting, and its mixture of comedy and tragedy made it one of Shakespeare's most popular works. It has inspired operas, Prokofiev's ballet, West Side Story and many films.

### A Midsummer Night's Dream (c. 1595)

[![A Midsummer Night's Dream](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/60/John_Simmons_-_Titania_sleeping_in_the_moonlight_protected_by_her_fairies.jpg/1280px-John_Simmons_-_Titania_sleeping_in_the_moonlight_protected_by_her_fairies.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:John_Simmons_-_Titania_sleeping_in_the_moonlight_protected_by_her_fairies.jpg)

<sub>John Simmons, Titania Sleeping in the Moonlight, Protected by her Fairies Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:John_Simmons_-_Titania_sleeping_in_the_moonlight_protected_by_her_fairies.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1595
- **Type:** Comedy
- **First published:** London; first printed 1600 (quarto)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Midsummer%20Night%27s%20Dream%20William%20Shakespeare)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=A+Midsummer+Night%27s+Dream+Shakespeare)

A Midsummer Night's Dream interweaves several plots around the wedding of Theseus, duke of Athens, and the Amazon queen Hippolyta. Two pairs of young lovers flee into a wood, where Oberon, king of the fairies, quarrelling with his queen Titania, orders the mischievous Puck to use a love potion. Puck's mistakes set the lovers chasing the wrong partners, while Titania, under the same spell, falls in love with the weaver Bottom, whose head Puck has turned into an ass's. Bottom and his fellow craftsmen are rehearsing a play, Pyramus and Thisbe, for the duke's wedding, and their hilariously inept performance ends the play. Shakespeare drew on Ovid, Chaucer and English folklore but invented the overall design himself. The play's blend of poetry, farce and enchantment, and its reflections on love, imagination and theatre, have made it one of his most performed comedies, inspiring Mendelssohn's incidental music and Britten's opera.

### Hamlet (c. 1600)

- **Date:** c. 1600
- **Type:** Tragedy
- **First published:** London, Globe Theatre; first printed 1603 (quarto)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Hamlet%20William%20Shakespeare)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Hamlet+Shakespeare)

Hamlet, Prince of Denmark, is Shakespeare's longest play and probably the most famous play in the world. Returning from university to the Danish court at Elsinore, Hamlet finds his father dead and his mother Gertrude remarried to his uncle Claudius, the new king. His father's ghost reveals that Claudius murdered him and demands revenge. Hamlet feigns madness, rejects Ophelia, whom he loves, stages a play to test the king's guilt, and mistakenly kills Ophelia's father Polonius, after which Ophelia goes mad and drowns. Claudius sends Hamlet to England to be killed, but he escapes and returns. In a final fencing match arranged by Claudius with Ophelia's brother Laertes, Gertrude, Laertes, Claudius and Hamlet all die. The play survives in three early versions that differ significantly. Its soliloquies, above all To be or not to be, and its hero's searching, delaying intelligence have made it a central text for ideas about consciousness and modern selfhood.

### Twelfth Night (c. 1601)

[![Twelfth Night](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/ce/Daniel_Maclise_%281806-1870%29_-_Scene_from_%27Twelfth_Night%27_%28%27Malvolio_and_the_Countess%27%29_-_N00423_-_National_Gallery.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Daniel_Maclise_%281806-1870%29_-_Scene_from_%27Twelfth_Night%27_%28%27Malvolio_and_the_Countess%27%29_-_N00423_-_National_Gallery.jpg)

<sub>Daniel Maclise, Malvolio and the Countess, 1840 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Daniel_Maclise_%281806-1870%29_-_Scene_from_%27Twelfth_Night%27_%28%27Malvolio_and_the_Countess%27%29_-_N00423_-_National_Gallery.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1601
- **Type:** Comedy
- **First published:** First recorded performance at Middle Temple Hall, London, February 1602; printed in the First Folio, 1623
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Twelfth%20Night%20William%20Shakespeare)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Twelfth+Night+Shakespeare)

Twelfth Night, or What You Will takes its title from the last night of the Christmas festivities, a time of revelry and misrule. Shipwrecked on the coast of Illyria and believing her twin brother Sebastian drowned, Viola disguises herself as a page, Cesario, and enters the service of Duke Orsino, whom she comes to love. Orsino sends her to woo the countess Olivia, who falls in love with Cesario. In a comic subplot, Olivia's riotous uncle Sir Toby Belch, the foolish Sir Andrew Aguecheek and the maid Maria trick her pompous steward Malvolio into believing that Olivia loves him, and he is locked up as mad. The arrival of Sebastian, who is mistaken for Cesario and promptly married by Olivia, resolves the confusion. The play's songs, sung by the fool Feste, and its bittersweet mixture of festivity and melancholy make it one of the finest of Shakespeare's comedies. Its play with gender and disguise has given it new life in modern productions.

### Othello (c. 1603)

[![Othello](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4b/Othello_et_Desd%C3%A9mone_%C3%A0_Venise_-_Th%C3%A9odore_Chass%C3%A9riau_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Peintures_RF_3897.jpg/960px-Othello_et_Desd%C3%A9mone_%C3%A0_Venise_-_Th%C3%A9odore_Chass%C3%A9riau_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Peintures_RF_3897.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Othello_et_Desd%C3%A9mone_%C3%A0_Venise_-_Th%C3%A9odore_Chass%C3%A9riau_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Peintures_RF_3897.jpg)

<sub>Théodore Chassériau, Othello and Desdemona in Venice, Louvre Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Othello_et_Desd%C3%A9mone_%C3%A0_Venise_-_Th%C3%A9odore_Chass%C3%A9riau_-_Mus%C3%A9e_du_Louvre_Peintures_RF_3897.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1603
- **Type:** Tragedy
- **First published:** First recorded performance at Whitehall, London, 1604; first printed 1622 (quarto)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Othello%20William%20Shakespeare)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Othello+Shakespeare)

Othello, the Moor of Venice is based on a story in Giraldi Cinthio's collection Hecatommithi (1565). Othello, a black general in the service of Venice, has secretly married Desdemona, daughter of a senator. His ensign Iago, resentful at being passed over for promotion in favour of Cassio and nursing vague suspicions, sets out to destroy him. On Cyprus, where Othello has been sent to defend the island against the Turks, Iago gets Cassio drunk and disgraced, then insinuates that Desdemona is having an affair with him, using a handkerchief she has dropped as false proof. Consumed by jealousy, Othello smothers Desdemona in her bed. When Iago's wife Emilia reveals the truth, Othello kills himself. With its tight construction and focus on a few characters, the play is the most domestic of Shakespeare's tragedies. Iago's motiveless malignity, as Coleridge called it, and the play's treatment of race have made it a subject of intense critical and theatrical attention. Verdi based his opera Otello on it.

### King Lear (c. 1605–1606)

[![King Lear](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/31/Cordelia%27s_Portion.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cordelia%27s_Portion.jpg)

<sub>Ford Madox Brown, Cordelia's Portion Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cordelia%27s_Portion.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1605–1606
- **Type:** Tragedy
- **First published:** Performed at Whitehall before James I, December 1606; first printed 1608 (quarto)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=King%20Lear%20William%20Shakespeare)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=King+Lear+Shakespeare)

King Lear draws on the legend of a pre-Christian British king recorded by Geoffrey of Monmouth and dramatised in an earlier anonymous play. The aged Lear decides to divide his kingdom among his three daughters according to how much they say they love him. Goneril and Regan flatter him extravagantly; the youngest, Cordelia, refuses to, and he disinherits her. His elder daughters then strip him of his followers and shut him out into a storm, where he goes mad on the heath, accompanied by his Fool and the disguised Earl of Kent. In a parallel plot, the Earl of Gloucester is deceived by his illegitimate son Edmund against his loyal son Edgar and is blinded. Cordelia returns with a French army but is defeated and hanged, and Lear dies with her body in his arms. The play survives in two different versions, the 1608 quarto and the 1623 Folio. Once thought too bleak to stage without a happy ending, it is now often considered Shakespeare's greatest tragedy.

### Macbeth (c. 1606)

[![Macbeth](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/First-page-first-folio-macbeth.jpg/960px-First-page-first-folio-macbeth.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:First-page-first-folio-macbeth.jpg)

<sub>First page of Macbeth in the First Folio, 1623 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:First-page-first-folio-macbeth.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1606
- **Type:** Tragedy
- **First published:** London; first printed in the First Folio, 1623
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Macbeth%20William%20Shakespeare)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Macbeth+Shakespeare)

Macbeth, Shakespeare's shortest tragedy, is based loosely on Holinshed's Chronicles and was probably written with James I, the patron of Shakespeare's company and a descendant of Banquo, in mind. Returning victorious from battle, the Scottish generals Macbeth and Banquo meet three witches, who hail Macbeth as future king and Banquo as the father of kings. Urged on by Lady Macbeth, Macbeth murders King Duncan as he sleeps in their castle and takes the throne. To secure it he has Banquo killed and slaughters the family of the nobleman Macduff. Haunted by Banquo's ghost, Macbeth becomes a bloody tyrant, while Lady Macbeth, sleepwalking, tries to wash invisible blood from her hands and dies. Trusting the witches' further riddling prophecies, Macbeth is killed by Macduff. The play's dark atmosphere, its imagery of blood, night and sleep, and its exploration of ambition and guilt make it one of the most powerful of the tragedies. Theatrical superstition holds that its name should not be spoken in a theatre.

### Sonnets (1609)

[![Sonnets](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f6/Sonnets1609titlepage.jpg/960px-Sonnets1609titlepage.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sonnets1609titlepage.jpg)

<sub>Title page of the 1609 quarto Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sonnets1609titlepage.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1609
- **Type:** Sonnet sequence
- **First published:** London, published by Thomas Thorpe
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sonnets%20William%20Shakespeare)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Sonnets+Shakespeare)

Shake-speares Sonnets was published in 1609 by Thomas Thorpe, with a mysterious dedication to Mr. W. H. as their only begetter; whether Shakespeare authorised the publication is debated. The 154 poems use the English sonnet form of three quatrains and a couplet, which became known as the Shakespearean sonnet. The first 126 are mostly addressed to a beautiful young man, urging him first to marry and have children and then promising him immortality in verse; others are addressed to a dark lady, the poet's mistress, with frank and bitter sexuality, and several concern a rival poet. Attempts to identify these figures with real people have filled many books without agreement. The sonnets explore love, desire, betrayal, ageing and time with unrivalled verbal richness, and poems such as Shall I compare thee to a summer's day? and Let me not to the marriage of true minds are among the most famous in English.

### The Tempest (c. 1610–1611)

[![The Tempest](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f9/First-page-first-folio-tempest.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:First-page-first-folio-tempest.jpg)

<sub>First page of The Tempest in the First Folio, 1623 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:First-page-first-folio-tempest.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1610–1611
- **Type:** Romance
- **First published:** Performed at Whitehall, November 1611; printed as the first play in the First Folio, 1623
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Tempest%20William%20Shakespeare)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=The+Tempest+Shakespeare)

The Tempest is probably the last play Shakespeare wrote alone. Prospero, the rightful duke of Milan, was overthrown by his brother Antonio and set adrift with his young daughter Miranda; they reached an island where Prospero, a master of magic, freed the spirit Ariel and enslaved the native Caliban. Twelve years later, a ship carrying Antonio, Alonso, king of Naples, and his son Ferdinand passes, and Prospero conjures a storm that wrecks them on the island. Through Ariel he separates the survivors, brings Miranda and Ferdinand together, foils a plot by Caliban and two drunken servants, and finally confronts his enemies, choosing forgiveness over revenge. He then breaks his staff, frees Ariel and asks the audience to set him free. Drawing on accounts of a shipwreck in Bermuda in 1609 and on Montaigne, the play has often been read as Shakespeare's farewell to the stage and, since the twentieth century, as a reflection on colonialism through the figure of Caliban.
