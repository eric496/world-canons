# Dante Alighieri

**c. 1265 – 1321** · Italian (Florentine)  
Born: Florence · Died: Ravenna  
Movements & eras: [Italian Trecento](../movements/italian-trecento.md)  

> The supreme poet of the Italian language, whose Divine Comedy, a journey through Hell, Purgatory and Paradise, is one of the summits of world literature.

## Biography

Dante Alighieri was born in Florence around 1265 into a family of minor nobility. As a boy he met Beatrice Portinari, who became the inspiration of his life's work even though he married Gemma Donati and Beatrice married another man and died young in 1290. He studied philosophy and rhetoric, wrote love poetry in the new sweet style, and gathered his early poems with prose commentary in the Vita Nuova. He entered Florentine politics on the side of the White Guelphs and served as one of the city's priors in 1300. In 1302, while away on an embassy to Rome, he was condemned and exiled by the victorious Black faction. He never returned, living at courts in Verona and elsewhere, and died in Ravenna in 1321, shortly after finishing the Comedy.

In exile Dante wrote treatises on the vernacular language and on monarchy and above all the Commedia, which later readers called divine. Its scheme is vast and precise: a hundred cantos in interlocking terza rima, a cosmos ordered by number and theology, and hundreds of historical figures judged in the afterlife. By writing it in Tuscan rather than Latin he did more than anyone to create the Italian literary language. Boccaccio lectured on him in Florence, the Romantics rediscovered him, and his visions of Hell in particular have inspired artists from Botticelli and Blake to Doré and Rodin.

### La Vita Nuova (c. 1294)

- **Date:** c. 1294
- **Type:** Prosimetrum (poems with prose commentary)
- **First published:** Florence
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=La%20Vita%20Nuova%20Dante%20Alighieri)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=La+Vita+Nuova)

La Vita Nuova, The New Life, is Dante's first book, assembled in Florence a few years after the death of Beatrice Portinari in 1290. It combines thirty-one poems, sonnets, canzoni and a ballata, with a prose narrative and commentary explaining the circumstances of each poem and analysing its structure. Dante tells how he first saw Beatrice when they were both nine, how she greeted him nine years later, how he hid his love behind screen ladies, and how her death plunged him into grief and then led him towards a spiritual understanding of love. The book ends with a vision and his resolve to write of her what had never been said of any woman, a promise fulfilled in the Divine Comedy. It is the finest work of the dolce stil novo, the sweet new style of Tuscan love poetry, and an early example of autobiographical fiction. The Pre-Raphaelite Dante Gabriel Rossetti translated it into English.

### Divine Comedy (c. 1308–1321)

- **Date:** c. 1308–1321
- **Type:** Epic poem
- **First published:** Written in exile in Verona and Ravenna; first printed in Foligno, 1472
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Divine%20Comedy%20Dante%20Alighieri)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Divine+Comedy)

Dante called his poem simply the Commedia, because it begins in misery and ends in joy; the epithet divine was added by Boccaccio and appeared on a Venetian edition of 1555. Written in exile over more than a decade, it consists of a hundred cantos in terza rima, a rhyme scheme Dante invented, divided into Inferno, Purgatorio and Paradiso. Lost in a dark wood in the Easter week of 1300, Dante is rescued by the poet Virgil, who leads him down through the nine circles of Hell, where sinners suffer punishments that mirror their sins, and up the mountain of Purgatory, where souls are purified. At its summit Beatrice, the woman he loved, takes over and guides him through the heavenly spheres to a vision of God. The poem is a vast synthesis of medieval theology, philosophy, astronomy and politics, populated with historical and contemporary figures, from Francesca da Rimini and Ulysses to popes and Florentine enemies. Written in Tuscan, it helped create the Italian language, and its images of the afterlife have shaped Western imagination ever since.
