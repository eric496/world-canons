# Émile Zola

**1840 – 1902** · French  
Born: Paris · Died: Paris  
Movements & eras: [Naturalism](../movements/naturalism-literature.md)  

> Leader of Naturalism and author of the twenty-novel Rougon-Macquart cycle, who risked everything to defend Alfred Dreyfus.

## Biography

Émile Zola was born in Paris in 1840, the son of an Italian engineer, and grew up in Aix-en-Provence, where he was a close friend of Paul Cézanne. His father died when he was seven, leaving the family poor. He failed his baccalaureate, worked in the sales department of the publisher Hachette and as a journalist and art critic, defending Manet and the Impressionists. His novel Thérèse Raquin (1867) brought him notoriety. From 1871 to 1893 he published the Rougon-Macquart, the natural and social history of a family under the Second Empire, whose seventh volume, L'Assommoir (1877), made him rich and famous. In 1898 he published J'accuse, an open letter accusing the army of covering up the wrongful conviction of the Jewish officer Alfred Dreyfus; convicted of libel, he fled to England for a year. He died in Paris in 1902, asphyxiated by a blocked chimney, and was later reinterred in the Panthéon.

Zola set out to apply the methods of science to the novel, studying heredity and environment and researching each book exhaustively, in the mines, department stores, railways and slums. His Rougon-Macquart novels, including Nana, Germinal and The Beast Within, are powerful, crowded works with an epic, often mythic sweep that goes beyond his theory. His frankness made him a scandal and a bestseller, and his naturalism influenced writers across Europe and America, from Hardy and Gissing to Dreiser.

## Key works

### Germinal (1885)

- **Date:** 1885
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Paris, serialised in Gil Blas (1884–85), then published by Charpentier
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Germinal%20%C3%89mile%20Zola)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Germinal)

Germinal is the thirteenth novel of Zola's Rougon-Macquart cycle, serialised in 1884–85 and based on his visits to the coalfields of northern France, where he went down the pits and studied the strike at Anzin. Étienne Lantier, a young machinist who has been sacked for striking an employer, arrives at the mining village of Montsou and finds work at the Le Voreux pit, lodging with the Maheu family. He witnesses the hunger, exhaustion and danger of the miners' lives, falls in love with Catherine Maheu, and, inspired by socialist ideas, leads a strike when the company cuts wages. The strike leads to starvation, riot and the shooting of strikers by soldiers, and an anarchist sabotages the mine, which floods, trapping Étienne and Catherine underground. Étienne leaves at the end, sensing a new army germinating beneath the earth. The title refers to a month of the revolutionary calendar. The novel's power made it a classic of social fiction, and at Zola's funeral crowds of miners chanted its name.
