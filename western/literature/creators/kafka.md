# Franz Kafka

**1883 – 1924** · Austrian-Czech (German-speaking)  
Born: Prague, Austria-Hungary · Died: Kierling, near Vienna, Austria  
Movements & eras: [Modernism](../movements/modernism-literature.md)  

> The Prague insurance lawyer whose stories and novels, The Metamorphosis, The Trial and The Castle, gave the world the word Kafkaesque.

**Links:** [Texts on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Franz+Kafka)

## Biography

Franz Kafka was born in Prague in 1883 into a German-speaking Jewish family; his domineering father, a self-made shopkeeper, overshadowed his life, as he described in his unsent Letter to His Father. He studied law and from 1908 worked at the Workers' Accident Insurance Institute, writing in the evenings and at night. He published only a few stories in his lifetime, including The Judgement, The Metamorphosis (1915), In the Penal Colony and A Hunger Artist. He was engaged twice to Felice Bauer and once to Julie Wohryzek, and had an intense correspondence with Milena Jesenská. Diagnosed with tuberculosis in 1917, he retired in 1922 and spent his last months in Berlin with Dora Diamant. He died at a sanatorium near Vienna in 1924. He asked his friend Max Brod to burn his manuscripts; Brod refused, and published The Trial, The Castle and Amerika.

Kafka's fiction describes nightmarish situations in calm, precise prose: a man wakes as a giant insect, another is arrested and tried for an unnamed crime, a land surveyor can never reach the castle. His works have been read as religious allegories, as critiques of bureaucracy and power, as expressions of Jewish experience or of the individual's alienation in the modern world, and they resist any single interpretation. His influence on twentieth-century literature, from Borges and Camus to García Márquez, is immense.

### The Metamorphosis (1915)

- **Date:** 1915
- **Type:** Novella
- **First published:** Die weißen Blätter (October 1915); book published by Kurt Wolff, Leipzig, 1915
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Metamorphosis%20Franz%20Kafka)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=The+Metamorphosis+Kafka)

The Metamorphosis (Die Verwandlung) was written in late 1912 and published in 1915. Its first sentence, in which Gregor Samsa wakes from uneasy dreams to find himself transformed into an enormous vermin, is among the most famous in literature. Gregor, a travelling salesman who supports his parents and sister by a job he hates, worries at first mainly about being late for work. His family are horrified; his father drives him back into his room and later wounds him with an apple that lodges in his back. His sister Grete feeds him, but over time the family grows resentful as they are forced to take jobs and take in lodgers. When Grete declares that the creature must go, Gregor, weakened, dies alone, and the relieved family take a trip to the country, noticing that Grete has become a young woman. Kafka insisted that the insect should never be drawn on the cover. The story has been read as a parable of alienation, family, illness and capitalism, and is one of the most widely read works of the century.

### The Trial (1914–1915; published 1925)

<sub>Cover of the first edition, 1925 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:TrialKafka.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1914–1915; published 1925
- **Type:** Novel (unfinished)
- **First published:** Berlin, published by Die Schmiede (edited by Max Brod)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Trial%20Franz%20Kafka)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=The+Trial+Kafka)

The Trial (Der Process) was written in 1914–15 and left unfinished, its chapters in a loose order, and was published by Kafka's friend Max Brod in 1925, against Kafka's instruction to burn his manuscripts. On the morning of his thirtieth birthday, Josef K., a bank official, is arrested in his lodgings by two warders, though he is allowed to continue working. The charge is never revealed. He attends a hearing in a tenement attic and discovers that the court has offices in attics throughout the city, staffed by corrupt and lecherous officials. A sickly lawyer, a painter who paints the judges and various women offer help that leads nowhere. In the cathedral a priest tells him the parable Before the Law, in which a man waits all his life at a door meant only for him. On the eve of his thirty-first birthday, two men take K. to a quarry and kill him like a dog, as he says. The novel's vision of opaque, inescapable authority gave the word Kafkaesque its meaning.
