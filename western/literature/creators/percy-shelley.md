## Biography

Percy Bysshe Shelley was born in 1792 into a wealthy Sussex family and educated at Eton and University College, Oxford, from which he was expelled in 1811 for co-writing a pamphlet titled The Necessity of Atheism. That year he eloped with sixteen-year-old Harriet Westbrook. In 1814 he left her for Mary Godwin, daughter of the philosopher William Godwin and Mary Wollstonecraft, and they married in 1816 after Harriet's suicide. Ostracised in England and denied custody of his children, Shelley moved to Italy in 1818, where he wrote most of his major works, including Prometheus Unbound, The Cenci, Ode to the West Wind and Adonais, an elegy for Keats. In July 1822 his boat sank in a storm in the Gulf of La Spezia, and he drowned. His body was cremated on the beach near Viareggio.

Shelley was a political radical, vegetarian and atheist who believed poetry could change the world; his Defence of Poetry ends by calling poets the unacknowledged legislators of the world. His verse is musical, rapid and abstract, full of images of wind, light, clouds and fire, and his political poems, such as The Mask of Anarchy, were later read by Chartists and campaigners for non-violent protest. Long condemned as immoral, he was championed by the Victorians as a lyric genius and remains one of the major English Romantic poets.

## Key works

### Prometheus Unbound (1820)

- **Type:** Lyrical drama
- **First published:** London, published by Charles and James Ollier
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Prometheus%20Unbound%20Percy%20Bysshe%20Shelley)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Prometheus+Unbound+Shelley)

Prometheus Unbound, a Lyrical Drama in Four Acts was written in Italy in 1818–19 and published in 1820. Aeschylus's lost sequel to Prometheus Bound apparently reconciled the Titan with Zeus; Shelley, rejecting what he called so feeble a catastrophe, made it a drama of liberation. Prometheus, chained to a precipice in the Caucasus for three thousand years, recalls and withdraws the curse he once laid on Jupiter, refusing hatred. His beloved Asia, guided by her sister Panthea, descends to the cave of Demogorgon, a mysterious primal power, and at the appointed hour Demogorgon dethrones Jupiter. Prometheus is freed by Hercules, and the last acts, in dazzling lyrical choruses, celebrate a renewed universe in which humanity is free, equal and governed by love. Shelley's preface explains his aim to familiarise readers with beautiful idealisms of moral excellence. The poem is the fullest expression of his radical political and philosophical hopes.
