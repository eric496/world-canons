# Pablo Neruda

**1904 – 1973** · Chilean  
Born: Parral, Chile · Died: Santiago, Chile  
Movements & eras: [Latin American Boom](../movements/latin-american-boom.md)  

> Chilean poet of love and politics, author of Twenty Love Poems and a Song of Despair and Canto General, and winner of the 1971 Nobel Prize.

## Biography

Ricardo Eliécer Neftalí Reyes Basoalto was born in 1904 in Parral, Chile, and grew up in Temuco in the south; he adopted the pen name Pablo Neruda as a teenager. His second book, Twenty Love Poems and a Song of Despair (1924), made him famous at twenty. He served as a consul in Burma, Ceylon, Java, Spain and Mexico, and the Spanish Civil War and the murder of his friend Lorca turned him to political poetry. He joined the Chilean Communist Party and was elected senator in 1945, but went into hiding and exile in 1948 after the party was banned. His epic Canto General (1950) traces the history of the Americas. He returned to Chile in 1952, received the Nobel Prize in 1971 and served as ambassador to France under Salvador Allende. He died in Santiago in September 1973, days after the military coup; the cause of his death has been disputed.

Neruda's poetry ranges from sensual love lyrics and the dark, surreal Residence on Earth to political epic and the simple, celebratory Elemental Odes to everyday things. Gabriel García Márquez called him the greatest poet of the twentieth century in any language. He remains one of the most widely read poets in the world.

## Key works

### Twenty Love Poems and a Song of Despair (1924)

- **Date:** 1924
- **Type:** Poetry collection
- **First published:** Santiago, published by Nascimento
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Twenty%20Love%20Poems%20and%20a%20Song%20of%20Despair%20Pablo%20Neruda)

Twenty Love Poems and a Song of Despair was published in Santiago in 1924, when Neruda was nineteen, and caused a scandal for its eroticism before becoming one of the most widely read books of poetry ever written in Spanish, with millions of copies sold. The poems are addressed to women Neruda loved as a student in Temuco and Santiago, merged into a single beloved whose body is compared to the earth, the sea, the night and the landscapes of southern Chile. They move between desire, possession, absence and loss, and include the famous Poem 20, which begins by saying the poet can write the saddest lines tonight. The final Song of Despair laments abandonment against the image of a harbour at dawn. Written in long, rhythmic free and loosely metrical lines, the poems combine late Romantic and Modernist influences with a fresh, direct sensuality. They made Neruda a celebrated poet across Latin America at the start of his career.
