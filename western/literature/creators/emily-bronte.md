# Emily Brontë

**1818 – 1848** · English  
Born: Thornton, Yorkshire · Died: Haworth, Yorkshire  
Movements & eras: [Romanticism](../movements/romanticism-literature.md)  

> The poet and novelist of Wuthering Heights, a single novel of wild passion on the Yorkshire moors. She died at thirty.

## Biography

Emily Jane Brontë was born at Thornton in Yorkshire in 1818 and grew up at Haworth parsonage, which she left only briefly, for school, a few months as a teacher near Halifax and a period studying in Brussels with Charlotte in 1842. With her younger sister Anne she created the imaginary kingdom of Gondal, the setting of many of her poems. She was reserved and fiercely independent, devoted to the moors and to her animals. Her poems appeared in the sisters' joint collection of 1846 under the name Ellis Bell, and Wuthering Heights was published in December 1847. She caught a cold at the funeral of her brother Branwell in September 1848, refused medical help and died of tuberculosis that December.

Wuthering Heights puzzled and shocked its first reviewers with its violence, its intricate nested narration and the elemental love between Catherine and Heathcliff. It is now considered one of the greatest English novels, a work that fuses Romantic intensity with a rigorous structure. Emily's poems, with their visionary independence and stoicism, are also highly regarded; Emily Dickinson admired them and asked for No coward soul is mine to be read at her funeral.

## Key works

### Wuthering Heights (1847)

- **Date:** 1847
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** London, published by Thomas Cautley Newby under the name Ellis Bell
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Wuthering%20Heights%20Emily%20Bront%C3%AB)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Wuthering+Heights)

Wuthering Heights was published in December 1847 under the pseudonym Ellis Bell, together with Anne Brontë's Agnes Grey. Its story is told mainly by the tenant Lockwood, who visits the bleak farmhouse of Wuthering Heights in 1801, and by the housekeeper Nelly Dean, who recounts its history. Mr Earnshaw brings home a dark-skinned foundling, Heathcliff, who forms an intense bond with his daughter Catherine and is persecuted by her brother Hindley. Catherine marries the refined Edgar Linton of Thrushcross Grange, though she declares that she is Heathcliff. Heathcliff leaves, returns rich and takes revenge on both families, marrying Edgar's sister Isabella and gaining control of both houses, while Catherine dies after giving birth. Only in the second generation, with Catherine's daughter and Hindley's son, is the cycle broken, and Heathcliff dies longing to rejoin Catherine's ghost. Its violence and complex structure baffled early reviewers, but it is now regarded as one of the greatest novels in English.
