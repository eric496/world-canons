# Chrétien de Troyes

**fl. c. 1160–1191** · French  
Born: Champagne, France (probably Troyes) · Died:   
Movements & eras: [Medieval epic & romance](../movements/medieval-epic-romance.md)  

> The twelfth-century French poet who created Arthurian romance, introducing Lancelot and the Holy Grail to European literature.

**Links:** [Texts on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Chr%C3%A9tien+de+Troyes)

## Biography

Almost nothing is known about Chrétien de Troyes beyond what he says in his works. He was active at the court of Marie de Champagne, daughter of Eleanor of Aquitaine, at Troyes from about 1160, and later dedicated his final romance to Philip, count of Flanders, who died in 1191. He wrote five Arthurian romances in octosyllabic rhyming couplets: Erec and Enide, Cligès, Yvain or the Knight of the Lion, Lancelot or the Knight of the Cart, and the unfinished Perceval or the Story of the Grail. He also translated Ovid, of which a version of the Philomela story probably survives.

Chrétien took the Celtic and Breton legends of King Arthur and turned them into a new kind of narrative poetry about individual knights, their adventures and their inner conflicts between love and duty. He was among the first to write about courtly love in narrative form, and he gave Lancelot his affair with Queen Guinevere and Perceval his encounter with a mysterious grail. His graceful, ironic, psychologically subtle romances were translated and continued across Europe, and they are the ultimate source of much of the Arthurian legend as later told by Malory, Tennyson and modern novelists and filmmakers.

- **Type:** Verse romance
- **First published:** Court of Marie de Champagne, Troyes
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Lancelot%2C%20the%20Knight%20of%20the%20Cart%20Chr%C3%A9tien%20de%20Troyes)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Lancelot%2C+the+Knight+of+the+Cart+Troyes)

Lancelot, or the Knight of the Cart was written by Chrétien de Troyes, by his own account on a subject and treatment supplied by his patron Marie de Champagne. When Queen Guinevere is abducted by Meleagant to the land of Gorre, from which no one returns, an unnamed knight sets out to rescue her. Having lost his horse, he hesitates for two steps before climbing into a cart used to carry criminals, a public disgrace; he is later revealed as Lancelot. He crosses a bridge made of a sword blade, frees the queen and spends a night with her, but she rebukes him for his moment of hesitation. The poem is the earliest surviving story of the love of Lancelot and Guinevere, and its picture of a knight utterly obedient to his lady became a central text of courtly love. Chrétien left it to another poet, Godefroi de Leigny, to finish. Its themes entered the great prose Lancelot cycle and, through Malory, the English Arthurian tradition.

### Perceval, the Story of the Grail (c. 1181–1190)

[![Perceval, the Story of the Grail](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/53/Perceval_%C3%A0_la_recluserie.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Perceval_%C3%A0_la_recluserie.jpg)

<sub>Perceval, medieval French manuscript illumination Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Perceval_%C3%A0_la_recluserie.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1181–1190
- **Type:** Verse romance (unfinished)
- **First published:** Written for Philip, count of Flanders
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Perceval%2C%20the%20Story%20of%20the%20Grail%20Chr%C3%A9tien%20de%20Troyes)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Perceval%2C+the+Story+of+the+Grail+Troyes)

Perceval, or the Story of the Grail was Chrétien's last romance, dedicated to Philip of Flanders and left unfinished at about 9,000 lines, perhaps because of the poet's death. Perceval, raised by his widowed mother in the forest, ignorant of chivalry, sets out for King Arthur's court and becomes a knight. At the castle of the wounded Fisher King he witnesses a strange procession, including a bleeding lance and a richly decorated grail, a serving dish, but out of misplaced politeness fails to ask whom the grail serves. He later learns that his silence has prevented the king's healing. The romance then follows Arthur's nephew Gawain's adventures before breaking off. Chrétien's grail was not yet the cup of the Last Supper, an idea added by Robert de Boron and others. Four verse continuations tried to finish the story, and it inspired Wolfram von Eschenbach's Parzival, the Holy Grail legend and, much later, Wagner's Parsifal.
