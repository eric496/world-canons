## Biography

Félix Lope de Vega y Carpio was born in Madrid in 1562 to a family of modest means and was writing verses before he could write, according to his own account. He studied at Alcalá, took part in a naval expedition to the Azores and sailed with the Armada in 1588. His life was turbulent: he was banished from Madrid for libelling a former lover's family, eloped with another woman, married twice and had many affairs and children. In 1614 he became a priest, but his romantic entanglements continued. Throughout he wrote at enormous speed, poems, novels and above all plays for Madrid's public theatres, which made him the most famous writer in Spain. He died in Madrid in 1635, and his funeral drew huge crowds.

In his verse treatise The New Art of Writing Plays (1609), Lope defended a popular theatre that mixed comedy and tragedy, ignored the classical unities and aimed above all to please the audience. His three-act comedia, written in a variety of verse forms, dealt with honour, love, history and peasant dignity, and established the model followed by Tirso de Molina, Calderón and others. About four hundred of his plays survive, among them Fuenteovejuna, Peribáñez and The Knight of Olmedo, along with a large body of lyric poetry.

## Key works

### Fuenteovejuna (c. 1612–1614; published 1619)

- **Date:** c. 1612–1614; published 1619
- **Type:** Drama (comedia)
- **First published:** Madrid, in the Dozena parte of Lope's comedias
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Fuenteovejuna%20Lope%20de%20Vega)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Fuenteovejuna+Vega)

Fuenteovejuna is based on a real event of 1476, when the inhabitants of a village in Andalusia killed the commander of the military Order of Calatrava, Fernán Gómez de Guzmán. In Lope's play the commander abuses the villagers and assaults their women, and when he abducts the young Laurencia on her wedding day, she rouses the village to revolt with a fiery speech shaming the men. The villagers storm his house and kill him. The Catholic Monarchs Ferdinand and Isabella send a judge to find the guilty, but even under torture every villager, from children to old men, answers only that Fuenteovejuna did it. Unable to punish the whole town, the king pardons them. The play combines a love story, a political plot and a vision of collective dignity and loyalty to the crown. Long neglected, it was revived in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries as a drama of popular revolt, and staged in Soviet Russia and by Lorca's touring theatre in Republican Spain.
