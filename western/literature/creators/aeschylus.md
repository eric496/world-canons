# Aeschylus

Born: Eleusis, Attica · Died: Gela, Sicily  
Movements & eras: [Greek tragedy & comedy](../movements/greek-drama.md)  

> The earliest of the three great Athenian tragedians, often called the father of tragedy. His Oresteia trilogy traces the passage from blood vengeance to civic justice.

## Biography

Aeschylus was born around 525 BC at Eleusis near Athens into a noble family. He fought against the Persians at Marathon in 490 BC, where his brother was killed, and probably also at Salamis, and his epitaph is said to have recorded his war service rather than his plays. He began competing at the Athenian festival of the Great Dionysia in the 490s and won first prize there thirteen times. He wrote perhaps seventy to ninety plays, of which seven survive under his name, including The Persians, Seven Against Thebes, The Suppliants and the Oresteia of 458 BC. He made visits to Sicily at the invitation of the tyrant Hieron and died at Gela around 456 BC.

Aristotle credits Aeschylus with adding a second actor to the single actor and chorus of early tragedy, which made real dramatic conflict possible. His language is grand, dense and rich in metaphor, and his choruses carry much of the weight of the drama. He typically worked in connected trilogies, following a curse or a moral problem across generations, and his plays wrestle with the justice of Zeus and the suffering through which, as the Oresteia says, humans learn. The authorship of Prometheus Bound, traditionally his, is now debated. His work set the scale and seriousness of tragedy for everything that followed.

### Oresteia (458 BC)

[![Oresteia](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Red-figure_krater_showing_Orestes_and_the_Furies.jpg/1280px-Red-figure_krater_showing_Orestes_and_the_Furies.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Red-figure_krater_showing_Orestes_and_the_Furies.jpg)

<sub>Orestes pursued by the Furies, red-figure krater Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Red-figure_krater_showing_Orestes_and_the_Furies.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 458 BC
- **Type:** Tragic trilogy
- **First published:** First performed at the Great Dionysia, Athens, where it won first prize
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Oresteia%20Aeschylus)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Oresteia+Aeschylus)

The Oresteia consists of three tragedies first staged together in Athens in 458 BC, two years before Aeschylus's death. In Agamemnon, the king returns victorious from Troy and is murdered in his bath by his wife Clytemnestra, in revenge for his sacrifice of their daughter Iphigenia. In The Libation Bearers, their son Orestes returns from exile and, commanded by Apollo, kills his mother and her lover Aegisthus. In The Eumenides, Orestes is pursued by the Furies, spirits of vengeance, to Athens, where the goddess Athena sets up a court, the Areopagus, to try him. The jury is evenly divided and Athena's vote acquits him; the Furies are persuaded to become benevolent guardians of the city. The trilogy has been read as a drama of the movement from private vendetta to public justice, and it raises questions about gender and authority that still provoke debate. Its massive choral odes and imagery of nets, blood and light are among the summits of Greek poetry.

### Prometheus Bound (c. 460–430 BC)

[![Prometheus Bound](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bc/Prometheus_door_Vulcanus_geketend%2C_SK-A-1606.jpg/1280px-Prometheus_door_Vulcanus_geketend%2C_SK-A-1606.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Prometheus_door_Vulcanus_geketend%2C_SK-A-1606.jpg)

<sub>Dirck van Baburen, Prometheus Being Chained by Vulcan, 1623, Rijksmuseum Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Prometheus_door_Vulcanus_geketend%2C_SK-A-1606.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 460–430 BC
- **Type:** Tragedy
- **First published:** Athens (date and circumstances of first performance unknown)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Prometheus%20Bound%20Aeschylus)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Prometheus+Bound+Aeschylus)

Prometheus Bound shows the Titan Prometheus being nailed to a crag in the Caucasus by Might, Violence and the reluctant smith-god Hephaestus, as punishment for stealing fire from the gods and giving it to humanity, along with all the arts of civilisation. Chained in place, he receives a series of visitors, among them the chorus of the daughters of Oceanus and the wandering Io, also a victim of Zeus, and reveals that he knows a secret that could overthrow the new ruler of the gods. Refusing to disclose it to Hermes, he is plunged into the abyss amid thunder and earthquake as the play ends. It was traditionally the first part of a trilogy by Aeschylus, but many scholars now doubt his authorship on grounds of style and metre. Whoever wrote it, the image of Prometheus as defiant benefactor of mankind inspired Goethe, Byron, Percy Shelley's Prometheus Unbound and Mary Shelley's Frankenstein, subtitled The Modern Prometheus.
