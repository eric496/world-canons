# Vladimir Nabokov

**1899 – 1977** · Russian-American  
Born: Saint Petersburg · Died: Montreux, Switzerland  
Movements & eras: [Postmodernism](../movements/postmodernism-literature.md)  

> The Russian-born novelist who wrote in two languages, author of Lolita and Pale Fire, and a dazzling stylist and lepidopterist.

## Biography

Vladimir Vladimirovich Nabokov was born in Saint Petersburg in 1899 into a wealthy aristocratic family; his father was a liberal politician. After the Revolution the family fled to Western Europe, and Nabokov studied at Trinity College, Cambridge. In Berlin in 1922 his father was killed shielding another man from an assassin. Under the pen name V. Sirin, Nabokov became the leading writer of the Russian émigré community, with novels such as The Defence and The Gift. In 1940 he fled with his Jewish wife Véra and their son to the United States, where he taught at Wellesley and Cornell, worked on butterflies at Harvard's Museum of Comparative Zoology and began writing in English. Lolita, rejected by American publishers, appeared in Paris in 1955 and became an international scandal and bestseller. He moved to Montreux, Switzerland, in 1961, where he wrote Pale Fire and Ada, and died in 1977.

Nabokov's fiction is full of wordplay, puzzles, unreliable narrators and parodies, written in a richly allusive, sensuous prose in his adopted English. He insisted on the primacy of style and detail and scorned novels of ideas. Lolita, narrated by a paedophile, remains controversial for its subject and admired for its artistry, while Pale Fire, a poem with a mad commentary, is a landmark of postmodern form.

## Key works

### Lolita (1955)

- **Date:** 1955
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Paris, published by Olympia Press; first US edition, G. P. Putnam's Sons, 1958
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Lolita%20Vladimir%20Nabokov)

Lolita was rejected by several American publishers and first published in Paris in 1955 by Olympia Press, which specialised in erotic and avant-garde books. Graham Greene's praise and the ensuing controversy made it notorious, and its American publication in 1958 made it a bestseller. Framed as the prison memoir of Humbert Humbert, a European émigré scholar, it tells how he marries Charlotte Haze to be near her twelve-year-old daughter Dolores, whom he calls Lolita. After Charlotte's accidental death he takes the girl on a long road trip across America, abusing her in motel after motel, until she escapes with the playwright Clare Quilty, whom Humbert eventually tracks down and murders. Nabokov's virtuoso, playful prose and Humbert's seductive, self-justifying voice force readers to see through his rhetoric to the suffering of the child he has destroyed. The novel remains controversial, but it is widely regarded as one of the masterpieces of twentieth-century fiction.
