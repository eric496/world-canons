# Rainer Maria Rilke

**1875 – 1926** · Austrian (Bohemian)  
Born: Prague, Austria-Hungary · Died: Val-Mont, near Montreux, Switzerland  
Movements & eras: [Modernism](../movements/modernism-literature.md), [Symbolism & Aestheticism](../movements/symbolism-aestheticism.md)  

> The Prague-born poet of the Duino Elegies and the Sonnets to Orpheus, one of the most intense and spiritual lyric voices in German.

## Biography

René Maria Rilke was born in Prague in 1875 to a German-speaking family; his unhappy schooling at military academies left lasting scars. He studied in Prague and Munich, where he met Lou Andreas-Salomé, who became his lover and lifelong confidante and with whom he travelled twice to Russia. He married the sculptor Clara Westhoff in 1901 but lived mostly apart from her. In Paris he worked for a time as secretary to Rodin, whose dedication to looking taught him to write his New Poems, and he wrote his only novel, The Notebooks of Malte Laurids Brigge. In 1912, at the castle of Duino near Trieste, he began the Duino Elegies, but the war interrupted him for a decade. Settling in the Swiss château of Muzot, he completed the Elegies and wrote the Sonnets to Orpheus in a burst in February 1922. He died of leukaemia in 1926.

Rilke's poetry moves from the precise, sculptural thing-poems of the Paris years to the vast meditations of the Elegies on angels, death, love and the human task of transforming the visible world into inner experience. His Letters to a Young Poet are widely read for their advice on solitude and art. He has been translated into English more often than almost any other modern poet.

## Key works

### Duino Elegies (1912–1922; published 1923)

- **Date:** 1912–1922; published 1923
- **Type:** Poetry sequence
- **First published:** Leipzig, published by Insel Verlag
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Duino%20Elegies%20Rainer%20Maria%20Rilke)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Duino+Elegies)

The Duino Elegies take their name from Duino Castle on the Adriatic near Trieste, where Rilke was a guest of Princess Marie von Thurn und Taxis in early 1912. According to the princess's account, he was walking on the cliffs in a strong wind when he heard the line that opens the first elegy, asking who, if he cried out, would hear him among the angelic orders. He wrote two elegies there, but the sequence stalled for ten years through the war and personal crisis, until, at the Château de Muzot in Switzerland in February 1922, he completed the remaining elegies in a few weeks, along with the Sonnets to Orpheus. Published in 1923, the ten elegies confront the gulf between human beings and the terrifying angels, the transience of love, the figures of lovers, heroes, children and acrobats, and death; they move from lament towards praise, suggesting that our task is to say things, house, bridge, fountain, and so transform the visible into the invisible within us. They are among the most admired poems of the twentieth century.
