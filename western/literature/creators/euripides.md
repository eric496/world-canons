# Euripides

**c. 480 BC – c. 406 BC** · Greek (Athenian)  
Born: Athens (traditionally Salamis) · Died: Macedonia  
Movements & eras: [Greek tragedy & comedy](../movements/greek-drama.md)  

> The youngest and most unsettling of the great tragedians, known for his clever women, questioning of the gods and psychological realism. More of his plays survive than of Aeschylus and Sophocles combined.

## Biography

Euripides was born around 480 BC; a later legend placed his birth on the island of Salamis on the day of the great sea battle. He first competed at the Dionysia in 455 BC but won first prize only four times in his life, a sign that his challenging plays divided Athenian audiences. Ancient biographies describe him as a bookish recluse who wrote in a cave, and he was a favourite target of the comic poet Aristophanes. Towards the end of his life he left Athens for the court of King Archelaus of Macedon, where he died around 406 BC. Eighteen or nineteen of his tragedies survive, including Medea, Hippolytus, The Trojan Women, Electra and The Bacchae, which was performed after his death.

Euripides brought the heroes of myth down to human scale and gave unprecedented voice to women, slaves and outsiders. His plays include passionate monologues, sharp debates and sudden endings in which a god appears from the machine. He questioned traditional religion and showed the horrors of war at a time when Athens was fighting Sparta. Aristotle called him the most tragic of the poets. His popularity grew after his death, and his work has been especially influential in the modern theatre, where Medea, The Trojan Women and The Bacchae are among the most staged of all Greek plays.

### Medea (431 BC)

- **Date:** 431 BC
- **Type:** Tragedy
- **First published:** First performed at the Great Dionysia, Athens, where it placed third
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Medea%20Euripides)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Medea)

Medea is set in Corinth, where Jason, leader of the Argonauts, has brought Medea, the princess from Colchis who helped him win the Golden Fleece and betrayed her own family for him. Now he has abandoned her to marry the daughter of King Creon, and Creon orders her into exile. Medea, a foreigner and a woman with no rights, pretends to accept her fate, sends the bride a poisoned robe and crown that kill her and her father, and then, after a famous monologue in which she struggles with herself, murders her two sons to destroy Jason completely. She escapes in a chariot of the sun god. The play shocked and puzzled its first audience but has become one of the most performed Greek tragedies. Its speeches on the position of women, foreignness and betrayal, and its terrifying, sympathetic heroine, have inspired versions by Seneca, Corneille, Cherubini and countless modern writers.

### The Bacchae (405 BC)

- **Date:** 405 BC
- **Type:** Tragedy
- **First published:** First performed posthumously at the Great Dionysia, Athens, where it won first prize
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Bacchae%20Euripides)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=The+Bacchae)

The Bacchae was written in Macedonia in the last years of Euripides's life and staged after his death. Dionysus, god of wine, ecstasy and theatre, arrives at Thebes disguised as a mortal stranger to establish his rites in the city of his mother Semele, whose sisters denied his divinity. He has driven the women of Thebes into a frenzy on Mount Cithaeron. The young king Pentheus tries to suppress the cult and imprisons the stranger, but the god escapes, breaks Pentheus's will and leads him, dressed as a woman, to spy on the women. There they tear him apart, led by his own mother Agave, who returns carrying his head in triumph before she comes to her senses. The play, with its magnificent choral odes, is a disturbing study of religious ecstasy, repression and the irrational. It was a central text for Nietzsche's ideas about the Dionysian and is among the most frequently staged Greek tragedies today.
