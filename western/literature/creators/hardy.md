## Biography

Thomas Hardy was born in 1840 at Higher Bockhampton near Dorchester, the son of a stonemason and builder. He was apprenticed to a local architect and worked in London and Dorset restoring churches, while teaching himself Greek and writing poetry. His first published novel, Desperate Remedies, appeared in 1871, and Far from the Madding Crowd (1874) allowed him to give up architecture. He married Emma Gifford, whom he met while restoring a church in Cornwall. Over the next twenty years he wrote novels set in his fictional Wessex, including The Return of the Native, The Mayor of Casterbridge, Tess of the d'Urbervilles (1891) and Jude the Obscure (1895). The hostile reception of Jude led him to abandon fiction for poetry, and he published eight collections and the epic drama The Dynasts. After Emma's death in 1912 he wrote moving elegies for her. He died at his Dorchester home, Max Gate, in 1928.

Hardy's novels show people struggling against chance, social convention and an indifferent universe, set against a richly observed rural world that was disappearing. His frank treatment of sexuality and his attacks on marriage and religion shocked Victorian readers. As a poet he wrote in a craggy, varied style about time, memory and loss, and he is now regarded as one of the major English poets of the twentieth century, a strong influence on Larkin and others.

## Key works

### Tess of the d'Urbervilles (1891)

- **Date:** 1891
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** London, serialised in The Graphic (with cuts) and published in three volumes by Osgood, McIlvaine
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Tess%20of%20the%20d%27Urbervilles%20Thomas%20Hardy)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Tess+of+the+d%27Urbervilles+Hardy)

Tess of the d'Urbervilles: A Pure Woman Faithfully Presented was serialised in The Graphic in 1891 in a censored version and published in full later that year. Tess Durbeyfield, the daughter of a poor carter in rural Wessex, is sent to claim kinship with a wealthy family named d'Urberville after her father learns that they descend from an ancient Norman line. Alec d'Urberville, the family's son, seduces or rapes her, a matter the novel leaves deliberately ambiguous, and her baby dies. Working as a milkmaid at a dairy farm, she falls in love with Angel Clare, a clergyman's son, but on their wedding night, when she confesses her past, he abandons her. Reduced to hard labour and desperate to support her family, she returns to Alec. When Angel comes back repentant, she kills Alec and flees with Angel, but is captured at Stonehenge and hanged. Hardy's subtitle defied Victorian morality, and the novel's attack on sexual double standards caused a storm. It is now considered one of the great tragic novels.
