## Biography

Henry James was born in New York in 1843 into a wealthy, intellectual family; his father was a theologian and his brother William became a pioneering psychologist and philosopher. He had a cosmopolitan education in Europe and America and briefly studied law at Harvard. He began publishing stories and reviews in the 1860s and in 1875 moved permanently to Europe, first Paris, where he met Flaubert and Turgenev, and from 1876 London. His novels Daisy Miller, The Portrait of a Lady (1881) and The Bostonians explored Americans abroad and in society. An attempt at a theatrical career ended in humiliation in 1895. He then wrote The Turn of the Screw and, from his house in Rye, the great late novels The Wings of the Dove, The Ambassadors and The Golden Bowl. He became a British subject in 1915, during the First World War, and died in London in 1916.

James was fascinated by the contrast between American innocence and European experience, and by the subtle workings of consciousness, perception and moral choice. He refined the use of a limited point of view and wrote influential prefaces on the art of fiction. His late style, long sentences full of qualifications and deferred meanings, is demanding but rewards close attention. He is a bridge between the nineteenth-century realist novel and Modernism.

### The Portrait of a Lady (1880–1881)

[![The Portrait of a Lady](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b4/The_Portrait_of_a_lady_cover.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Portrait_of_a_lady_cover.jpg)

- **Date:** 1880–1881
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Serialised in Macmillan's Magazine (London) and The Atlantic Monthly (Boston); book published by Macmillan, 1881
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Portrait%20of%20a%20Lady%20Henry%20James)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=The+Portrait+of+a+Lady+James)

The Portrait of a Lady was serialised in 1880–81 and published as a book in 1881; James revised it extensively for the New York Edition of 1908. Isabel Archer, a spirited young American with little money, is brought to England by her aunt and refuses two proposals, from the English lord Warburton and the American businessman Caspar Goodwood, in order to keep her freedom. Her sickly cousin Ralph Touchett, who loves her, secretly persuades his dying father to leave her a large fortune so that she may live as she wishes. In Florence, Isabel is drawn by the worldly Madame Merle to Gilbert Osmond, a cultivated American expatriate, and marries him, only to find that he is cold and controlling and that he and Madame Merle were once lovers and are the parents of his daughter Pansy. Despite everything, Isabel returns to Rome and her marriage at the novel's end. The book's subtle tracing of consciousness, especially Isabel's long midnight meditation, made it one of James's masterpieces.

### The Turn of the Screw (1898)

[![The Turn of the Screw](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/The-Turn-of-the-Screw-Collier%27s-1A.jpg/960px-The-Turn-of-the-Screw-Collier%27s-1A.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The-Turn-of-the-Screw-Collier%27s-1A.jpg)

<sub>First instalment in Collier's Weekly, 1898 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The-Turn-of-the-Screw-Collier%27s-1A.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1898
- **Type:** Novella
- **First published:** Serialised in Collier's Weekly, New York; published in The Two Magics, 1898
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Turn%20of%20the%20Screw%20Henry%20James)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=The+Turn+of+the+Screw+James)

The Turn of the Screw was serialised in Collier's Weekly in 1898 and published that year in The Two Magics. A frame narrator describes a gathering at which a man reads a manuscript left by his sister's former governess. The governess recounts how, as a young woman, she was hired by a man in London to take sole charge of his orphaned niece and nephew, Flora and Miles, at his country house of Bly, on condition that she never trouble him. She begins to see the figures of a man and a woman, whom the housekeeper identifies as the dead former valet Peter Quint and the previous governess Miss Jessel, and becomes convinced that their ghosts are corrupting the children. Her efforts to protect them lead to Flora's illness and, in the final scene, to Miles dying in her arms. Critics have debated for over a century whether the ghosts are real or the governess's delusions. The story inspired Britten's opera and many films.
