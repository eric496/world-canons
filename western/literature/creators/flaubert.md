## Biography

Gustave Flaubert was born in Rouen in 1821, the son of the chief surgeon of the city's hospital. Sent to Paris to study law, he suffered an epileptic-type seizure in 1844 and gave up his studies to live with his mother at the family house at Croisset on the Seine, where he spent most of his life writing. He travelled through Egypt and the Middle East in 1849–51 with his friend Maxime Du Camp. Madame Bovary was serialised in 1856, and he and its publishers were prosecuted for offending public morals; their acquittal in 1857 made the book a bestseller. He went on to write Salammbô, a novel of ancient Carthage, Sentimental Education (1869), The Temptation of Saint Anthony and Three Tales. He was a mentor to Maupassant and friend of George Sand and Turgenev, and died at Croisset in 1880, leaving Bouvard and Pécuchet unfinished.

Flaubert sought what he called le mot juste, the exact word, and laboured for days over single pages, reading his sentences aloud in what he called his shouting room. He believed the author should be present everywhere in the work but visible nowhere, and his impersonal, ironic narration and use of free indirect style became central techniques of the modern novel. Henry James, Joyce, Kafka and Nabokov all revered him. His letters, full of ideas about art and contempt for bourgeois stupidity, are among the great literary correspondences.

### Madame Bovary (1856–1857)

- **Date:** 1856–1857
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Paris, serialised in the Revue de Paris (1856); published by Michel Lévy (1857)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Madame%20Bovary%20Gustave%20Flaubert)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Madame+Bovary+Flaubert)

Madame Bovary: Provincial Manners was serialised in the Revue de Paris in 1856, with cuts made by the editors; Flaubert and the publishers were then prosecuted for offending public morals and religion, and their acquittal in February 1857 made the book a sensation when it appeared in volume form that April. Emma Rouault, a farmer's daughter educated in a convent and fed on romantic novels, marries Charles Bovary, a dull, devoted country doctor, and quickly finds her life in the small towns of Tostes and Yonville unbearable. She has affairs, first with the landowner Rodolphe, who abandons her, and then with the young clerk Léon, and runs up enormous debts with the scheming merchant Lheureux. Facing ruin, she swallows arsenic and dies an agonising death. Flaubert spent five years on the novel, striving for impersonal narration and perfect style. Its use of free indirect discourse and ironic detail made it a model of the modern novel, and the word bovarysme came to describe a romantic dissatisfaction with reality.

### Sentimental Education (1869)

[![Sentimental Education](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8c/Education_sentimentale_flaubert.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Education_sentimentale_flaubert.jpg)

<sub>Title page of the first edition, 1869 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Education_sentimentale_flaubert.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1869
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Paris, published by Michel Lévy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sentimental%20Education%20Gustave%20Flaubert)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Sentimental+Education+Flaubert)

Sentimental Education: The Story of a Young Man was published in 1869, though its first idea went back to Flaubert's youthful love for an older married woman, Élisa Schlésinger. In 1840 the eighteen-year-old Frédéric Moreau, on a steamboat on the Seine, sees Madame Arnoux, wife of an art dealer, and falls in love with her for life. In Paris he studies law, inherits money, frequents artists, journalists, bankers and courtesans and has affairs, while his love for Madame Arnoux remains unconsummated. The Revolution of 1848 and Louis-Napoleon's coup pass through the novel almost incidentally, seen through the eyes of characters who are distracted or self-interested. Years later Frédéric and his friend Deslauriers conclude that the best moment of their lives was a youthful failure to visit a brothel. The novel's aimless hero, its irony and its refusal of dramatic climax baffled readers but were admired by Henry James, Proust and Kafka as a new kind of fiction.
