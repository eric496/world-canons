## Biography

John Keats was born in London in 1795, the son of a livery-stable keeper; his father died when he was eight and his mother when he was fourteen. He trained as a surgeon at Guy's Hospital and qualified as an apothecary, but gave up medicine for poetry. Leigh Hunt published his early sonnets, including On First Looking into Chapman's Homer, and his first collection appeared in 1817, followed by the long poem Endymion (1818), which hostile reviewers mocked. In 1818 he nursed his brother Tom through his fatal tuberculosis and fell in love with Fanny Brawne. In an astonishing burst in 1819 he wrote The Eve of St Agnes, La Belle Dame sans Merci, the great odes and Hyperion. Already ill with tuberculosis, he travelled to Rome for his health in 1820 and died there in February 1821.

Keats's poetry is famed for its sensuous imagery, its rich music and its meditation on beauty, mortality and art. His letters, full of ideas such as negative capability, the capacity to rest in uncertainty without reaching after fact and reason, are among the finest by any English poet. His short life became a Romantic legend, fixed by Shelley's elegy Adonais, and the Victorians, the Pre-Raphaelites and later poets made him one of the best loved of all English poets.

## Key works

### Ode to a Nightingale (1819)

[![Ode to a Nightingale](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ae/W._J._Neatby_-_Keats_-_Nightingale.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:W._J._Neatby_-_Keats_-_Nightingale.jpg)

- **Date:** 1819
- **Type:** Ode
- **First published:** Annals of the Fine Arts, July 1819; collected in Lamia, Isabella, The Eve of St Agnes, and Other Poems, 1820
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ode%20to%20a%20Nightingale%20John%20Keats)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Ode+to+a+Nightingale+Keats)

Ode to a Nightingale was written in May 1819 at Wentworth Place in Hampstead, where Keats was living with his friend Charles Brown, who recalled that a nightingale had built its nest nearby and that Keats sat under a plum tree in the garden one morning and wrote the poem on scraps of paper. It is one of the group of great odes Keats composed that spring, along with those on a Grecian urn, melancholy, indolence and Psyche. In eight ten-line stanzas, the speaker, aching and numb, hears the bird's song and longs to fade away with it, leaving behind the weariness, the fever and the fret of a world where youth grows pale and dies, a reference to his brother Tom's recent death from tuberculosis. He imagines wine and then poetry carrying him to the bird, considers that it would be rich to die, and contrasts his mortality with the immortal song, before the vision fades. The ode's rich sensuous imagery and its meditation on art, beauty and death make it one of the most celebrated poems in English.
