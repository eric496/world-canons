## Biography

Giovanni Boccaccio was born in 1313, the illegitimate son of a Florentine merchant, probably in Florence or Certaldo. His father sent him to Naples to learn banking and then canon law, but he spent his time at the cultured Angevin court and began writing poems and romances in Italian. Back in Florence from about 1341, he lived through the plague of 1348, which killed perhaps half the city and provides the frame of the Decameron, completed around 1353. In 1350 he met Petrarch, who became his close friend and turned him towards classical scholarship. In his last years he gave the first public lectures on Dante in Florence. He died at Certaldo in 1375.

The Decameron established Italian prose as a literary medium and the novella as a European form. Boccaccio's tales range from bawdy farce to tragedy and show merchants, clergy, lovers and tricksters with unusual realism and humour. He also wrote vernacular poems that Chaucer used for the Knight's Tale and Troilus and Criseyde, and Latin works such as the Genealogy of the Pagan Gods and On Famous Women, which were standard reference works for centuries. Shakespeare, Chaucer, Keats and Pasolini all drew on him.

## Key works

### The Decameron (c. 1349–1353)

- **Type:** Collection of tales
- **First published:** Florence; earliest autograph manuscript in the Staatsbibliothek, Berlin
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Decameron%20Giovanni%20Boccaccio)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=The+Decameron+Boccaccio)

The Decameron, whose Greek-derived title means ten days, opens with a harrowing description of the plague that struck Florence in 1348. Seven young women and three young men meet in the church of Santa Maria Novella and decide to retreat to the countryside, where they pass the time with music, dancing and storytelling. Each day one of them is chosen as king or queen and sets a theme, and each tells one tale, so that a hundred stories are told in all. They range from bawdy tricks played on husbands and corrupt clergy to tales of wit, generosity, love and tragedy, such as the story of the patient Griselda, which ends the collection. Boccaccio's clear, flexible prose established Italian as a literary language for prose as Dante had for verse. The book was often censored, but it became one of the most influential works of European literature, mined by Chaucer, Shakespeare, Lope de Vega, Molière and Keats, and filmed by Pasolini.
