# Mary Shelley

**1797 – 1851** · English  
Born: London · Died: London  
Movements & eras: [Romanticism](../movements/romanticism-literature.md)  

> Author of Frankenstein, begun at nineteen during a ghost-story contest with Byron and Percy Shelley, and a founding work of science fiction.

## Biography

Mary Wollstonecraft Godwin was born in London in 1797, the daughter of the philosopher William Godwin and the feminist Mary Wollstonecraft, who died days after her birth. She grew up among her father's intellectual circle, and at sixteen eloped to the Continent with the married poet Percy Bysshe Shelley, whom she married in 1816. That summer, near Geneva with Byron and his physician John Polidori, she conceived the story that became Frankenstein, published anonymously in 1818. The following years in Italy were marked by the deaths of three of her four children and, in 1822, of Shelley himself. Returning to England with her surviving son, she supported herself by writing novels, including The Last Man (1826), stories, travel writing and biographies, and edited Shelley's poems. She died in London in 1851.

Frankenstein combines the Gothic novel with Romantic ideas about creation, ambition and the outcast, and with contemporary science about galvanism and the origins of life. Its framing of nested narratives and the eloquent voice of the creature give it unusual depth. The novel has had an enormous afterlife on stage and screen and is often called the first true work of science fiction. The Last Man, a story of humanity destroyed by plague, is also now regarded as a pioneering work of the genre.

## Key works

### Frankenstein (1818)

- **Date:** 1818
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** London, published anonymously by Lackington, Hughes, Harding, Mavor & Jones
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Frankenstein%20Mary%20Shelley)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Frankenstein)

Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus was published anonymously in three volumes in 1818, with a preface by Percy Shelley; a revised edition of 1831 bore Mary Shelley's name and a famous introduction describing its origin in a ghost-story contest with Byron at the Villa Diodati near Geneva in 1816. The novel is told in nested narratives. The Arctic explorer Robert Walton writes to his sister of rescuing Victor Frankenstein, a Genevan who, as a student at Ingolstadt, discovered the secret of life and assembled a creature from parts of corpses. Horrified by its appearance, he fled. The creature, abandoned and rejected by everyone he meets, educates himself by secretly watching a family and reading Paradise Lost, then, in his own eloquent narrative, demands a mate; when Victor destroys the female he was making, the creature kills his friend and his bride. Victor pursues him to the Arctic and dies. The novel fused the Gothic with questions about science, responsibility and alienation, and it is often called the first work of science fiction.
