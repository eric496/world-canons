# Mark Twain (Samuel Langhorne Clemens)

**1835 – 1910** · American  
Born: Florida, Missouri · Died: Redding, Connecticut  
Movements & eras: [Realism](../movements/realism-literature.md)  

> Humorist, lecturer and novelist of the Mississippi, author of Tom Sawyer and Adventures of Huckleberry Finn.

## Biography

Samuel Langhorne Clemens was born in 1835 in Florida, Missouri, and grew up in Hannibal on the Mississippi River, the setting of his best-known books. After his father's death he worked as a printer, then as a riverboat pilot until the Civil War closed the river. He went west to Nevada and California, prospected for silver, became a newspaper writer and adopted the pen name Mark Twain, a riverboat call for two fathoms of water. His comic story The Celebrated Jumping Frog of Calaveras County (1865) and the travel book The Innocents Abroad (1869) made him famous. He married Olivia Langdon and settled in Hartford, Connecticut, where he wrote The Adventures of Tom Sawyer, Life on the Mississippi and Adventures of Huckleberry Finn (1884–85). Failed investments bankrupted him in the 1890s, and he paid off his debts with a world lecture tour. The deaths of his wife and two daughters darkened his later writing. He died in 1910.

Twain brought the vernacular speech of ordinary Americans into literature, and his combination of humour, satire and moral seriousness made him the most famous American of his day. His later writings attacked imperialism, racism and religious hypocrisy with growing bitterness. Hemingway claimed that all modern American literature comes from Huckleberry Finn, and Twain remains a symbol of American wit and independence.

## Key works

### Adventures of Huckleberry Finn (1884–1885)

- **Date:** 1884–1885
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** London (December 1884) and New York (February 1885), published by Chatto & Windus and Charles L. Webster
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Adventures%20of%20Huckleberry%20Finn%20Mark%20Twain%20%28Samuel%20Langhorne%20Clemens%29)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Adventures+of+Huckleberry+Finn)

Adventures of Huckleberry Finn was published in Britain in December 1884 and in the United States in February 1885, with illustrations by E. W. Kemble. Told in Huck's own vernacular voice, it is set in the Mississippi valley before the Civil War. Huck, a boy who has been taken in by the Widow Douglas, is kidnapped by his drunken, violent father, fakes his own death and escapes to Jackson's Island, where he meets Jim, an enslaved man who has run away to avoid being sold. They travel down the river on a raft, encountering feuding families, a lynch mob and two con men who call themselves the King and the Duke and sell Jim. In a crisis of conscience, Huck decides that he will go to hell rather than betray Jim, and he tries to free him; Tom Sawyer reappears for a long, farcical rescue at the end. The novel's use of dialect and its satire of racism and hypocrisy made it a landmark of American literature, but its language and portrayal of Jim have made it one of the most frequently challenged books in American schools.
