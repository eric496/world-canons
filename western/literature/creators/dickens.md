# Charles Dickens

**1812 – 1870** · English  
Born: Portsmouth, Hampshire · Died: Gad's Hill Place, Kent  
Movements & eras: [Realism](../movements/realism-literature.md)  

> The most popular novelist of the Victorian age, creator of Oliver Twist, Scrooge, David Copperfield and a gallery of unforgettable characters.

## Biography

Charles John Huffam Dickens was born in Portsmouth in 1812, the son of a navy pay clerk who was imprisoned for debt in 1824; the twelve-year-old Charles was sent to work in a blacking factory, an experience he never forgot. He became a law clerk and then a parliamentary reporter, and published sketches of London life under the name Boz. The Pickwick Papers (1836–37), issued in monthly parts, made him a phenomenon at twenty-five. Over the next three decades he wrote fifteen novels, including Oliver Twist, A Christmas Carol, David Copperfield, Bleak House, Little Dorrit, A Tale of Two Cities and Great Expectations, while editing magazines and campaigning on social issues. He separated from his wife Catherine in 1858, gave hugely popular public readings in Britain and America, and died at his home near Rochester in 1870, leaving The Mystery of Edwin Drood unfinished.

Dickens's novels combine comedy, melodrama, satire and social protest, and are populated by grotesque and vivid characters with memorable names and speech. Published in instalments, they reached an enormous public, and he used them to attack the workhouse, debtors' prisons, the law's delays and the exploitation of children. His imaginative vision of London, fog, crowds and all, shaped how the city was seen. Dostoevsky, Kafka and many later writers admired him, and his stories have been adapted for stage and screen more than those of almost any other novelist.

### A Christmas Carol (1843)

- **Date:** 1843
- **Type:** Novella
- **First published:** London, published by Chapman & Hall (19 December 1843)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Christmas%20Carol%20Charles%20Dickens)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=A+Christmas+Carol)

A Christmas Carol in Prose, Being a Ghost Story of Christmas was written in about six weeks in the autumn of 1843, partly in response to a government report on child labour, and published just before Christmas with illustrations by John Leech; its first edition sold out in days. Ebenezer Scrooge, a cold, grasping London moneylender, dismisses Christmas as humbug. On Christmas Eve he is visited by the ghost of his late partner Jacob Marley, bound in chains forged by his own greed, who warns him that three spirits will come. The Ghost of Christmas Past shows Scrooge his lonely childhood and lost love; the Ghost of Christmas Present shows him the poor but loving household of his clerk Bob Cratchit, whose son Tiny Tim is ill; and the silent Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come shows him his own unmourned death. Scrooge wakes on Christmas morning a changed man. The story did much to shape the modern celebration of Christmas as a festival of family and charity, and it has been adapted countless times.

### David Copperfield (1849–1850)

- **Date:** 1849–1850
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** London, published in monthly parts by Bradbury & Evans
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=David%20Copperfield%20Charles%20Dickens)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=David+Copperfield)

The Personal History of David Copperfield was published in nineteen monthly instalments in 1849–50, illustrated by Hablot K. Browne (Phiz). Told in the first person, it follows David from his birth in Suffolk, after his father's death, through his happy early childhood with his mother and the devoted servant Peggotty, to the cruelty of his stepfather Mr Murdstone, who sends him at ten to work in a London warehouse, an episode drawn directly from Dickens's own experience. He runs away to his eccentric aunt Betsey Trotwood in Dover, is educated, works as a law clerk and becomes a writer. Along the way he meets some of Dickens's most famous characters: the debt-ridden optimist Mr Micawber, the scheming, falsely humble clerk Uriah Heep, the charming but destructive Steerforth and the steadfast Agnes Wickfield, whom he finally marries after the death of his childlike first wife Dora. Dickens called it his favourite child, and Tolstoy and Freud both admired it.

### Bleak House (1852–1853)

- **Date:** 1852–1853
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** London, published in monthly parts by Bradbury & Evans
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Bleak%20House%20Charles%20Dickens)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Bleak+House)

Bleak House was published in twenty monthly parts in 1852–53. It opens with a famous description of fog over London and the Court of Chancery, where the inheritance case of Jarndyce and Jarndyce has dragged on for generations. Half the novel is narrated in the present tense by an omniscient voice and half by Esther Summerson, an orphan who becomes housekeeper to the kindly John Jarndyce at Bleak House, with his young wards Ada and Richard, whom the case gradually destroys. The proud Lady Dedlock harbours a secret, which the sinister lawyer Tulkinghorn sets out to discover, until he is murdered and Inspector Bucket, one of the first detectives in English fiction, investigates. The novel's huge cast ranges from the crossing sweeper Jo to the philanthropist Mrs Jellyby, and its satire of the law and of social neglect is savage. When the case is finally settled, the costs have consumed the whole estate. Many critics regard it as Dickens's greatest achievement.

### Great Expectations (1860–1861)

- **Date:** 1860–1861
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** London, serialised weekly in All the Year Round
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Great%20Expectations%20Charles%20Dickens)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Great+Expectations)

Great Expectations was serialised weekly in Dickens's magazine All the Year Round from December 1860 to August 1861. Philip Pirrip, called Pip, an orphan raised by his harsh sister and her gentle husband, the blacksmith Joe Gargery, on the Kent marshes, helps an escaped convict on Christmas Eve. He is then summoned to Satis House to play with Estella, the cold ward of Miss Havisham, a rich recluse who has stopped all the clocks and still wears her wedding dress since being jilted. When a lawyer announces that Pip has great expectations from an unknown benefactor, he goes to London to become a gentleman, grows ashamed of Joe, and assumes Miss Havisham is his patron, until the convict Magwitch returns from Australia and reveals that he is the source of Pip's fortune. Narrated by the older Pip, the novel is a moving study of snobbery, guilt and loyalty. Dickens rewrote the ending at the suggestion of the novelist Bulwer-Lytton to make it less bleak, and both versions survive.
