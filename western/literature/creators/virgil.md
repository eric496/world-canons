## Biography

Publius Vergilius Maro was born in 70 BC near Mantua in northern Italy and educated in Cremona, Milan and Rome, where he studied rhetoric and Epicurean philosophy. His first collection, the Eclogues, pastoral poems written in the turmoil after Caesar's assassination, brought him to the attention of Maecenas, the cultural patron of the future emperor Augustus. He then spent seven years on the Georgics, a poem on farming, and devoted the last decade of his life to the Aeneid. In 19 BC he fell ill on a journey to Greece and died at Brundisium. He had asked for the unfinished epic to be burned, but Augustus ordered its publication.

Virgil fused Greek forms, Homer for epic, Theocritus for pastoral and Hesiod for didactic poetry, with Roman themes and an exceptional ear for the music of Latin hexameter. His poetry is marked by a deep sympathy for suffering, a sense of the cost of history and an ambiguous relation to the Augustan order it celebrates. The Aeneid became the school text of Europe for nearly two thousand years. Christians read the fourth Eclogue as a prophecy of Christ, medieval legend made Virgil a magician, and Dante chose him as his guide in the Divine Comedy. Few poets have influenced so many others.

### Georgics (29 BC)

[![Georgics](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Meister_des_Vergilius_Romanus_001.jpg/1280px-Meister_des_Vergilius_Romanus_001.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Meister_des_Vergilius_Romanus_001.jpg)

<sub>Miniature from the Vergilius Romanus, a 5th-century manuscript of Virgil, Vatican Library Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Meister_des_Vergilius_Romanus_001.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 29 BC
- **Type:** Didactic poem
- **First published:** Rome
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Georgics%20Virgil%20%28Publius%20Vergilius%20Maro%29)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Georgics+Virgil)

The Georgics is a poem of about 2,200 hexameter lines in four books, written in the years after the civil wars, when Italy's countryside lay ravaged and Octavian was establishing his rule. Nominally a farming manual in the tradition of Hesiod, it covers crops and weather, the cultivation of vines and olives, cattle and horses, and beekeeping. It is also a meditation on labour, on Italy, on the relation between humans and nature and on the violence of history; the first book ends with a vision of the omens that followed Caesar's assassination. The fourth book closes with the story of Aristaeus and the myth of Orpheus and Eurydice, one of the most celebrated passages in Latin poetry. Dryden called it the best poem of the best poet, and it has shaped writing about the countryside from Renaissance poets to Wordsworth and modern nature writing.

### Aeneid (29–19 BC)

[![Aeneid](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/f7/Aeneas%27_Flight_from_Troy_by_Federico_Barocci.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aeneas%27_Flight_from_Troy_by_Federico_Barocci.jpg)

<sub>Federico Barocci, Aeneas' Flight from Troy, 1598 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aeneas%27_Flight_from_Troy_by_Federico_Barocci.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 29–19 BC
- **Type:** Epic poem
- **First published:** Rome; published posthumously by order of Augustus
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Aeneid%20Virgil%20%28Publius%20Vergilius%20Maro%29)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Aeneid+Virgil)

The Aeneid is an epic of twelve books in Latin hexameter, left unfinished at Virgil's death and published on the orders of the emperor Augustus. Its first half recalls the Odyssey: Aeneas, escaping the fall of Troy with his father and son, wanders the Mediterranean, is shipwrecked at Carthage, tells Queen Dido the story of Troy's destruction and becomes her lover, until the gods order him to leave and Dido kills herself. After visiting the underworld, where his father shows him the future heroes of Rome, he reaches Italy, and the second half, like the Iliad, narrates his war against the Latin hero Turnus, ending abruptly when Aeneas kills him in anger. The poem celebrates Rome's destiny and Augustus's peace, yet it is shadowed throughout by loss, the cost of empire and the suffering of the defeated. It was Europe's central school text for nearly two thousand years and shaped the epic tradition of Dante, Tasso and Milton.
