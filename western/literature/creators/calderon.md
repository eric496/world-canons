# Pedro Calderón de la Barca

**1600 – 1681** · Spanish  
Born: Madrid · Died: Madrid  
Movements & eras: [Spanish Golden Age](../movements/spanish-golden-age.md)  

> The last great dramatist of the Spanish Golden Age, whose Life Is a Dream turned the comedia into philosophical theatre.

## Biography

Pedro Calderón de la Barca was born in Madrid in 1600 into a family of minor nobility and educated by the Jesuits and at the universities of Alcalá and Salamanca. His first plays were staged in the 1620s, and after Lope de Vega's death in 1635 he became the leading dramatist of Spain and the favourite playwright of Philip IV, writing lavish spectacles for the court theatre at the Buen Retiro palace. He served as a soldier in the Catalan revolt of 1640 and in 1651 was ordained a priest. In later life he wrote mainly autos sacramentales, allegorical religious plays performed at the feast of Corpus Christi. He died in Madrid in 1681.

Calderón refined Lope's popular comedia into a more tightly constructed, intellectual drama, with elaborate imagery and themes of free will, illusion, honour and divine grace. His best-known works include Life Is a Dream, The Mayor of Zalamea, The Physician of His Own Honour and the auto The Great Theatre of the World. He was rediscovered by the German Romantics, especially the Schlegel brothers and Goethe, who placed him alongside Shakespeare, and his work was translated into English by Edward FitzGerald.

## Key works

### Life Is a Dream (1635)

- **Date:** 1635
- **Type:** Drama (comedia)
- **First published:** Madrid, in the first part of Calderón's comedias, 1636
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Life%20Is%20a%20Dream%20Pedro%20Calder%C3%B3n%20de%20la%20Barca)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Life+Is+a+Dream)

Life Is a Dream (La vida es sueño) is Calderón's most celebrated play. King Basilio of Poland, an astrologer, has read in the stars that his son Segismundo will become a tyrant, and has kept him chained in a tower since birth. As an experiment, Basilio has Segismundo drugged and brought to court. Waking as a prince, Segismundo behaves violently, throwing a servant from a balcony, and is drugged again and returned to the tower, where he is told it was all a dream. Released by rebellious soldiers who want him as king, he resolves to act well whether he is dreaming or not, defeats his father and pardons him. A second plot follows Rosaura, a woman disguised as a man who seeks to restore her honour. The play's meditations on free will, fate, illusion and the nature of reality, culminating in Segismundo's famous soliloquy, made it a major work of European drama, admired by the German Romantics and constantly revived.
