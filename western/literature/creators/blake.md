## Biography

William Blake was born in London in 1757, the son of a hosier, and apprenticed at fourteen to the engraver James Basire. He studied briefly at the Royal Academy, married Catherine Boucher, who became his collaborator, and worked all his life as a commercial engraver. From childhood he reported visions of angels and spirits. In 1788 he developed a method of relief etching that let him print text and image together, which he used for Songs of Innocence (1789), Songs of Experience (1794) and a series of prophetic books including The Marriage of Heaven and Hell, Milton and Jerusalem. He spent three years in Felpham on the Sussex coast and was tried, and acquitted, for sedition in 1804. He lived and died in poverty in London in 1827, admired by a small circle of young artists.

Blake's poetry ranges from the simple-seeming lyrics of the Songs to dense mythological epics populated by his own invented beings, such as Urizen and Los. He attacked rationalism, repressive religion, slavery and the industrial and social conditions of his day, and believed in the imagination as the divine in humanity. Little known in his lifetime, he was rediscovered by the Pre-Raphaelites and Yeats, and his lyrics, such as The Tyger and the verses known as Jerusalem, are now among the best known in English.

## Key works

### Songs of Innocence and of Experience (1789–1794)

[![Songs of Innocence and of Experience](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/02/Songs_of_Innocence_and_of_Experience%2C_copy_AA%2C_object_1.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Songs_of_Innocence_and_of_Experience%2C_copy_AA%2C_object_1.jpg)

<sub>Combined title page, relief etching coloured by hand, copy AA, Fitzwilliam Museum Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Songs_of_Innocence_and_of_Experience%2C_copy_AA%2C_object_1.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1789–1794
- **Type:** Illuminated poetry
- **First published:** London; relief-etched, printed and hand-coloured by Blake and his wife
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Songs%20of%20Innocence%20and%20of%20Experience%20William%20Blake)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Songs+of+Innocence+and+of+Experience+Blake)

Blake printed Songs of Innocence in 1789 and combined it with Songs of Experience in 1794, with the subtitle Shewing the Two Contrary States of the Human Soul. Each poem was etched on copper together with its illustration and decoration using a relief technique Blake invented, then printed and coloured by hand by him and his wife Catherine, so that every copy differs; only a few dozen were made. Many poems are paired: The Lamb with The Tyger, the Innocent Chimney Sweeper with the Experienced one, Holy Thursday with its bitter counterpart. In simple forms drawn from hymns and nursery rhymes, the songs move from the joy and trust of childhood to a world of repression, poverty, child labour and organised religion, and poems such as London expose the mind-forged manacles of society. Little known in Blake's lifetime, the Songs are now among the most famous poems in English and are treasured as works of visual art.
