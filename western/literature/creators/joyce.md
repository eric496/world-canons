## Biography

James Augustine Aloysius Joyce was born in 1882 in Rathgar, Dublin, the eldest surviving child of a large family that slid into poverty through his father's improvidence. Educated by the Jesuits and at University College Dublin, he rejected Catholicism and Irish nationalism, and in 1904 left Ireland with Nora Barnacle, whom he married only in 1931. They lived in Trieste, Zurich and from 1920 Paris, where Joyce taught English and struggled with poverty and worsening eye disease. Dubliners appeared in 1914 after years of disputes with publishers, and A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man was serialised in 1914–15. Ulysses, partly serialised in an American magazine whose editors were prosecuted for obscenity, was published in Paris by Sylvia Beach in 1922. He spent seventeen years on Finnegans Wake (1939). Fleeing the German occupation of France, he returned to Zurich, where he died in 1941.

Joyce made Dublin the setting of all his fiction and from it created universal works. In Ulysses he combined an epic structure taken from the Odyssey with stream of consciousness, each chapter in a different style, and a frankness that led to bans in Britain and the United States. Finnegans Wake pushes language into multilingual puns and dream logic. He is widely regarded as the most influential novelist of the twentieth century, and Bloomsday, 16 June, the day on which Ulysses is set, is celebrated around the world.

### Dubliners (1914)

[![Dubliners](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4a/Joyce_-_Dubliners%2C_1914_-_3690390_F.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Joyce_-_Dubliners%2C_1914_-_3690390_F.jpg)

<sub>Title page of the first edition, 1914 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Joyce_-_Dubliners%2C_1914_-_3690390_F.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1914
- **Type:** Short-story collection
- **First published:** London, published by Grant Richards
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dubliners%20James%20Joyce)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Dubliners+Joyce)

Dubliners was written mainly in 1904–07, but its publication was delayed for nearly a decade as publishers and printers objected to its frankness, its use of real names and places and its references to Edward VII; one printer destroyed the sheets. It finally appeared in London in 1914. Joyce described its plan as a chapter of the moral history of his country and said he wanted to present Dublin as the centre of paralysis. The fifteen stories are arranged to move from childhood, as in The Sisters and Araby, through adolescence and maturity to public life, and are written in what he called a style of scrupulous meanness. Many end in a moment of sudden revelation, which Joyce called an epiphany. The final story, The Dead, set at a Christmas party, ends with Gabriel Conroy learning of his wife's long-dead young lover and contemplating the snow falling all over Ireland. The collection is one of the most influential in the history of the short story.

### A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man (1916)

[![A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/30/JamesJoyce_Portrait1916.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:JamesJoyce_Portrait1916.jpg)

<sub>Title page of the first edition, 1916 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:JamesJoyce_Portrait1916.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1916
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Serialised in The Egoist, London (1914–15); book published by B. W. Huebsch, New York, 1916
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Portrait%20of%20the%20Artist%20as%20a%20Young%20Man%20James%20Joyce)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=A+Portrait+of+the+Artist+as+a+Young+Man+Joyce)

A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man grew out of an earlier, abandoned novel, Stephen Hero, and was serialised in the London magazine The Egoist in 1914–15 with the help of Ezra Pound, then published as a book in New York in 1916. It follows Stephen Dedalus, Joyce's fictional double, from his earliest memories, rendered in the language of a small child, through his Jesuit schooling at Clongowes and Belvedere, a famous Christmas dinner argument over the fall of the nationalist leader Parnell, his adolescent sexual awakening, terror at a sermon on hell and a period of intense piety. A vision of a girl wading on a beach confirms his vocation as an artist, and at University College he develops a theory of aesthetics and resolves to leave Ireland, using silence, exile and cunning as his weapons. The prose style develops with Stephen's mind, an early form of stream of consciousness. The novel is a founding Modernist Künstlerroman, and Stephen reappears in Ulysses.

### Ulysses (1922)

[![Ulysses](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ab/JoyceUlysses2.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:JoyceUlysses2.jpg)

<sub>First edition, Paris, 1922 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:JoyceUlysses2.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1922
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Paris, published by Shakespeare and Company (Sylvia Beach), 2 February 1922
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ulysses%20James%20Joyce)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Ulysses+Joyce)

Ulysses was written between 1914 and 1921 in Trieste, Zurich and Paris. Parts were serialised in the American Little Review, whose editors were convicted of obscenity in 1921, and the book was published in Paris by Sylvia Beach on Joyce's fortieth birthday. It was banned in the United States until a court ruled in 1933 that it was not obscene. Its eighteen episodes, each loosely corresponding to an episode of the Odyssey, follow a single day, 16 June 1904. Leopold Bloom, a Jewish advertising canvasser, goes about his business, attends a funeral, visits a newspaper office, a pub, a maternity hospital and the brothel district, aware that his wife Molly is about to begin an affair. His wanderings cross those of the young Stephen Dedalus, and late at night Bloom takes Stephen home. The book ends with Molly's unpunctuated monologue, which closes on her word yes. Each episode uses a different style, from stream of consciousness to parody and a play script. Ulysses is widely regarded as the most important novel of the twentieth century.
