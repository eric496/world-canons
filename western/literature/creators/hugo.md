# Victor Hugo

**1802 – 1885** · French  
Born: Besançon, France · Died: Paris  
Movements & eras: [Romanticism](../movements/romanticism-literature.md)  

> The giant of French Romanticism, poet, dramatist and novelist of Notre-Dame de Paris and Les Misérables, and a republican exile under Napoleon III.

## Biography

Victor-Marie Hugo was born in Besançon in 1802, the son of an officer in Napoleon's army, and spent part of his childhood in Italy and Spain. He published his first collection of odes at twenty and soon led the young Romantics. The preface to his play Cromwell (1827) was their manifesto, and the riotous premiere of Hernani in 1830 marked their victory over classicism. Notre-Dame de Paris appeared in 1831. The drowning of his daughter Léopoldine in 1843 devastated him. Elected to the Assembly, he opposed Louis-Napoleon's coup of 1851 and went into exile for nearly twenty years, mainly on Guernsey, where he wrote Les Contemplations, The Legend of the Ages and Les Misérables. He returned to Paris in 1870 as a national symbol and died in 1885; some two million people attended his funeral and burial in the Panthéon.

Hugo's work is enormous in scale and range: lyric and epic poetry, verse drama, novels, essays, political speeches and some four thousand drawings. He championed the mixing of the sublime and the grotesque, sympathy for the poor and the outcast, and campaigns against the death penalty and for universal education. His novels have become global myths through stage musicals and films, while in France he is remembered above all as the greatest poet of the nineteenth century.

### Notre-Dame de Paris (The Hunchback of Notre-Dame) (1831)

- **Date:** 1831
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Paris, published by Gosselin
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Notre-Dame%20de%20Paris%20%28The%20Hunchback%20of%20Notre-Dame%29%20Victor%20Hugo)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Notre-Dame+de+Paris)

Notre-Dame de Paris, known in English as The Hunchback of Notre-Dame, was published in 1831 and is set in Paris in 1482. The beautiful young Roma dancer Esmeralda attracts the obsessive desire of Claude Frollo, the austere archdeacon of Notre-Dame, who orders his deaf, deformed adopted son Quasimodo, the cathedral's bell-ringer, to abduct her. She is rescued by the handsome captain Phoebus, whom she loves. When Frollo stabs Phoebus out of jealousy, Esmeralda is accused of the crime and condemned. Quasimodo, who has come to love her, carries her into the cathedral to claim sanctuary, but she is eventually captured and hanged, and Quasimodo throws Frollo from the tower; years later two skeletons are found embraced in a charnel house. The novel is also a celebration of Gothic architecture, and its success helped spark a movement to preserve Notre-Dame and other medieval buildings, leading to the cathedral's restoration by Viollet-le-Duc. It has been adapted many times for stage and screen.

### Les Misérables (1862)

- **Date:** 1862
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Brussels, published by A. Lacroix, Verboeckhoven & Cie (simultaneously in Paris)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Les%20Mis%C3%A9rables%20Victor%20Hugo)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Les+Mis%C3%A9rables)

Les Misérables was largely written in exile on Guernsey and published in 1862 in five parts, one of the great publishing events of the century. Jean Valjean, released after nineteen years in the galleys for stealing a loaf of bread and trying to escape, is transformed by the mercy of a bishop who forgives his theft of silver. Under a new name he becomes a prosperous factory owner and mayor, but he is relentlessly hunted by Inspector Javert. He rescues Cosette, the child of the abandoned working woman Fantine, from the brutal Thénardiers and raises her as his own. In Paris, Cosette falls in love with the young Marius, who joins the republican insurrection of June 1832; Valjean carries the wounded Marius through the sewers to safety, and Javert, unable to reconcile mercy with law, drowns himself. Hugo filled the novel with long digressions on Waterloo, convents, slang and the sewers of Paris. Its passion for social justice made it one of the most popular novels ever written, and the stage musical has made it known worldwide.
