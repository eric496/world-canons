# Jorge Luis Borges

**1899 – 1986** · Argentine  
Born: Buenos Aires · Died: Geneva  
Movements & eras: [Latin American Boom](../movements/latin-american-boom.md), [Postmodernism](../movements/postmodernism-literature.md)  

> The Argentine master of the short story and essay, whose labyrinths, libraries and mirrors anticipated postmodern fiction.

## Biography

Jorge Luis Borges was born in Buenos Aires in 1899 into a bilingual family and learned English before Spanish. His family lived in Geneva during the First World War and then in Spain, where he joined the avant-garde Ultraist movement. Returning to Buenos Aires in 1921, he published poetry and essays and co-founded literary magazines. A serious head injury in 1938 was followed by the stories of his greatest period, collected in Ficciones (1944) and The Aleph (1949). Under Perón he was removed from his post at a municipal library. After Perón's fall he became director of the National Library in 1955, by which time he was almost blind, and taught English literature at the University of Buenos Aires. International fame came after he shared the Formentor Prize with Samuel Beckett in 1961. He died in Geneva in 1986.

Borges's short stories are compressed philosophical fictions about infinity, time, identity and the nature of books, often disguised as essays, reviews or encyclopaedia entries. Their erudition, irony and play with invented sources made him a precursor of postmodernism and a key influence on writers such as Calvino, Eco and García Márquez. He wrote poetry throughout his life and is regarded as one of the most important writers in Spanish of the twentieth century.

## Key works

### Ficciones (1944)

- **Date:** 1944
- **Type:** Short-story collection
- **First published:** Buenos Aires, published by Sur
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ficciones%20Jorge%20Luis%20Borges)

Ficciones brings together two earlier collections, The Garden of Forking Paths (1941) and Artifices (1944), and was expanded in 1956. Its stories include Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius, in which an imaginary world described in a pirated encyclopaedia gradually takes over the real one; Pierre Menard, Author of the Quixote, a mock review of a writer who rewrites Cervantes word for word; The Library of Babel, an infinite library containing every possible book; The Garden of Forking Paths, a spy story built around a novel in which all possible outcomes occur; Funes the Memorious, about a man who forgets nothing; and The Lottery in Babylon and Death and the Compass. Combining the forms of the essay, the detective story and the philosophical puzzle, the stories explore infinity, time, identity and the nature of fiction itself. The collection made Borges internationally famous, especially after its translation in the 1960s, and was a key influence on postmodern literature and the Latin American Boom.
