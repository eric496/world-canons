# Joseph Conrad

**1857 – 1924** · Polish-British  
Born: Berdychiv, Russian Empire (now Ukraine) · Died: Bishopsbourne, Kent  
Movements & eras: [Modernism](../movements/modernism-literature.md)  

> A Polish-born sea captain who became one of the great novelists in English, author of Heart of Darkness, Lord Jim and Nostromo.

## Biography

Józef Teodor Konrad Korzeniowski was born in 1857 in Berdychiv, in Russian-ruled Ukraine, to Polish noble parents. His father, a poet and patriot, was exiled for conspiring against Russian rule, and both parents died while Conrad was a child. At sixteen he went to Marseille and joined the French merchant navy, then from 1878 the British, rising to the rank of master and becoming a British subject in 1886. In 1890 he captained a river steamer in the Congo Free State, an experience that shocked him and became the basis of Heart of Darkness. He gave up the sea in 1894 and published his first novel, Almayer's Folly, in 1895, writing in English, his third language. Lord Jim, Nostromo, The Secret Agent and Under Western Eyes followed. He lived in Kent with his wife Jessie and died in 1924.

Conrad's fiction deals with isolation, betrayal, honour and the moral darkness of colonialism and political violence. He used complex time schemes and framing narrators, such as Marlow, so that stories are filtered through memory and uncertainty, techniques that link him to Modernism. His view of imperialism has been debated, notably in Chinua Achebe's 1975 critique of Heart of Darkness as racist. His influence reaches Eliot, Faulkner, Fitzgerald, Graham Greene and many later novelists.

## Key works

### Heart of Darkness (1899)

- **Date:** 1899
- **Type:** Novella
- **First published:** Blackwood's Magazine, Edinburgh (February–April 1899); book in Youth, 1902
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Heart%20of%20Darkness%20Joseph%20Conrad)
- **Links:** [Search Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=Heart+of+Darkness)

Heart of Darkness was serialised in Blackwood's Magazine in 1899 and collected in Youth: A Narrative, and Two Other Stories in 1902. It draws on Conrad's own journey up the Congo River in 1890, in the Congo Free State controlled by King Leopold II of Belgium. On a yacht moored in the Thames at dusk, the sailor Marlow tells his companions of his experiences as a steamboat captain for a Belgian trading company. Travelling into Africa, he witnesses the brutality and absurdity of colonial exploitation and hears more and more about Kurtz, the company's most successful ivory agent, a man of great eloquence. When Marlow finally reaches the inner station, he finds Kurtz dying, surrounded by heads on stakes, having made himself a god to the local people. Kurtz's last words are The horror! The horror! Back in Europe, Marlow lies to Kurtz's fiancée about them. The novella is a central text of Modernism and of debates about colonialism, criticised by Chinua Achebe for its depiction of Africans, and it inspired the film Apocalypse Now.
