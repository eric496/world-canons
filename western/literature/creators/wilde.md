## Biography

Oscar Fingal O'Flahertie Wills Wilde was born in Dublin in 1854 to a surgeon father and a poet mother who wrote as Speranza. He studied classics at Trinity College Dublin and Magdalen College, Oxford, where he came under the influence of Walter Pater and John Ruskin and cultivated the persona of an aesthete. He lectured across America in 1882, married Constance Lloyd and edited a women's magazine. His fairy tales, his only novel The Picture of Dorian Gray (1890–91) and his society comedies, Lady Windermere's Fan, An Ideal Husband and The Importance of Being Earnest (1895), made him the toast of London. In 1895 he sued the Marquess of Queensberry, father of his lover Lord Alfred Douglas, for libel; the case collapsed, and Wilde was convicted of gross indecency and sentenced to two years' hard labour. After prison he wrote De Profundis and The Ballad of Reading Gaol, and died in exile in Paris in 1900.

Wilde championed art for art's sake and turned paradox into an art form, inverting Victorian pieties in epigrams that still circulate. Beneath his comedies of manners lies a sharp critique of hypocrisy, and Dorian Gray is both a Gothic fable and a manifesto of Aestheticism. His persecution made him a symbol of the cost of Victorian sexual morality, and in 2017 he was among the men posthumously pardoned under British law.

### The Picture of Dorian Gray (1890; revised 1891)

- **Date:** 1890; revised 1891
- **Type:** Novel
- **First published:** Lippincott's Monthly Magazine (July 1890); revised book edition, London, Ward, Lock & Co., 1891
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Picture%20of%20Dorian%20Gray%20Oscar%20Wilde)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=The+Picture+of+Dorian+Gray+Wilde)

The Picture of Dorian Gray was first published in the American Lippincott's Monthly Magazine in July 1890, where its editors cut passages they considered indecent. Reviewers attacked it as immoral, and Wilde expanded it to twenty chapters for the 1891 book edition, adding a preface of epigrams on art, including the claim that there is no such thing as a moral or an immoral book. The painter Basil Hallward completes a portrait of the beautiful young Dorian Gray, and the witty, cynical Lord Henry Wotton fills Dorian's head with a philosophy of pleasure and youth. Dorian wishes that the picture would age instead of him, and his wish is granted. He drives the actress Sibyl Vane to suicide, sinks into secret vice and murders Basil, while his face remains unspoiled and the hidden portrait grows hideous. Finally he stabs the picture and is found dead, withered and loathsome, beside the portrait restored to its original beauty. The novel was used against Wilde at his trials in 1895.

### The Importance of Being Earnest (1895)

- **Date:** 1895
- **Type:** Comedy
- **First published:** St James's Theatre, London (14 February 1895)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Importance%20of%20Being%20Earnest%20Oscar%20Wilde)
- **Links:** [Free text on Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/search/?query=The+Importance+of+Being+Earnest+Wilde)

The Importance of Being Earnest: A Trivial Comedy for Serious People premiered at the St James's Theatre in London on 14 February 1895, just weeks before Wilde's arrest; the production was closed after his conviction. Jack Worthing, a respectable guardian in the country, has invented a dissolute younger brother, Ernest, whose name he uses in town. His friend Algernon Moncrieff has invented an invalid friend, Bunbury, whose illnesses let him escape dull engagements. Jack, as Ernest, proposes to Algernon's cousin Gwendolen, but her mother Lady Bracknell objects on learning that he was found as a baby in a handbag in the cloakroom at Victoria Station. Algernon, posing as Ernest, woos Jack's ward Cecily, and both young women have set their hearts on marrying a man called Ernest. The confusions are resolved when it is revealed that Jack is Algernon's elder brother and was in fact christened Ernest. With its perfect epigrams and absurd plot, the play is regarded as one of the greatest comedies in English.
