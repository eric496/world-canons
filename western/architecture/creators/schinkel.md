# Karl Friedrich Schinkel

**1781 – 1841** · Prussian  
Born: Neuruppin, Brandenburg · Died: Berlin  
Styles: [Palladianism & Neoclassicism](../movements/neoclassicism.md), [Gothic Revival](../movements/gothic-revival.md)  

> The great architect of Prussia, who made Berlin a Neoclassical capital with the Neue Wache, the Schauspielhaus and the Altes Museum.

## Biography

Schinkel studied under Friedrich Gilly and his father David, travelled in Italy, and during the Napoleonic occupation, when little was built, worked as a painter of romantic landscapes and panoramas and as a stage designer, notably for The Magic Flute. From 1810 he served in the Prussian building administration, rising to its head. He built the Neue Wache (1816–18), the Schauspielhaus (1818–21), the Altes Museum (1823–30), the Friedrichswerder Church in Gothic, and the Bauakademie (1832–36), as well as palaces at Potsdam.

Schinkel was equally at home in Greek and Gothic and believed each age must find its own architecture. His late works, above all the red-brick Bauakademie with its expressed structural grid, pointed toward a rational, industrial architecture; he had studied English factories on a trip in 1826. Mies van der Rohe and other twentieth-century architects admired him as a model of clarity, and his buildings define the centre of Berlin.

## Key buildings

### Altes Museum (1823–1830)

<sub>Altes Museum, Museum Island, Berlin, Germany Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Altes_Museum_%28Berlin%29_%286339770591%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1823–1830
- **Type:** Museum
- **Location:** Museum Island, Berlin, Germany
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Altes%20Museum%20Karl%20Friedrich%20Schinkel)

King Frederick William III of Prussia agreed to open the royal collections to the public, and Schinkel designed a museum facing the royal palace across the Lustgarten. Its façade is a single colonnade of eighteen Ionic columns raised on a podium, recalling a Greek stoa. Behind it, a two-storey rotunda modelled on the Pantheon, lined with ancient statues, forms the heart of the building, with galleries for sculpture on the ground floor and paintings above around two courtyards. An open staircase between the columns let visitors look back over the city. It opened in 1830 and was the first building on what became Museum Island, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Damaged in the Second World War, it was restored and today shows the classical antiquities collection.
