# Giotto di Bondone

**c. 1267 – 1337** · Florentine  
Born: Vespignano, near Florence · Died: Florence  
Styles: [Gothic](../movements/gothic.md)  
Also in: [Art](../../art/creators/giotto.md)  

> The great Florentine painter, appointed in 1334 master of works of Florence Cathedral, who designed its marble-clad bell tower.

## Biography

Giotto, the most famous painter of his age, transformed Italian art with the fresco cycles of the Arena Chapel in Padua and Santa Croce in Florence. In 1334 the city of Florence appointed him master of works of the cathedral and of the city's fortifications, a sign of the prestige he had won. He laid the foundations of the bell tower beside the cathedral in July of that year and supervised the lowest storey before his death in 1337.

His design for the campanile, slender and clad in white, green and pink marble, seems to have been modified by his successors Andrea Pisano and Francesco Talenti, who completed it around 1359. A parchment drawing in Siena is often linked with Giotto's original idea, though this is debated. Giotto's appointment is a landmark in the history of the architect as an artist, a generalist of design rather than a mason trained on site.

## Key buildings

### Florence Campanile (1334–c. 1359)

[![Florence Campanile](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/69/CampanileGiotto-01.jpg/960px-CampanileGiotto-01.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:CampanileGiotto-01.jpg)

<sub>Florence Campanile, Florence, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:CampanileGiotto-01.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1334–c. 1359
- **Type:** Bell tower
- **Location:** Florence, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Florence%20Campanile%20Giotto%20di%20Bondone)

Giotto, appointed master of works of the cathedral in 1334, laid the foundations of the bell tower that year and built its lowest storey. After his death in 1337 Andrea Pisano continued it, adding the niches for statues, and Francesco Talenti completed it around 1359 with the tall windowed upper storeys, abandoning the spire Giotto may have planned. Some 85 metres tall, the square tower is clad in white Carrara, green Prato and pink Maremma marble in geometric panels matching the cathedral. Its lower storeys carry hexagonal and lozenge reliefs by Pisano, Luca della Robbia and others showing the creation of man, the arts and sciences, the planets and the sacraments, and its niches once held statues by Donatello, now in the cathedral museum. It is part of the historic centre of Florence, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
