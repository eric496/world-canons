# Alvar Aalto

**1898 – 1976** · Finnish  
Born: Kuortane, Finland · Died: Helsinki  
Styles: [Modernism & the International Style](../movements/modernism.md), [Late & Expressive Modernism](../movements/late-modernism.md)  

> Finnish architect and designer who gave modernism a humane, organic character, with the Paimio Sanatorium, Villa Mairea and bentwood furniture.

## Biography

Aalto studied at the Helsinki University of Technology and began in a Nordic classical manner before turning to modernism in the late 1920s with the Turun Sanomat newspaper building and the Paimio Sanatorium (1929–33). With his wife Aino Aalto he designed bentwood furniture and glassware, founding the company Artek in 1935. Major works include the Viipuri Library, Villa Mairea (1937–39), Baker House at MIT, Säynätsalo Town Hall, the House of Culture and Finlandia Hall in Helsinki.

Aalto softened the machine aesthetic with wood, brick and copper, undulating walls and ceilings, careful daylight and attention to the human senses. He designed buildings down to their door handles and lamps. His work became the model of a regional, humane modernism and made Finland a centre of modern design.

### Paimio Sanatorium (1929–1933)

[![Paimio Sanatorium](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/4a/Paimio_Sanatorium2.jpg/1280px-Paimio_Sanatorium2.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Paimio_Sanatorium2.jpg)

<sub>Paimio Sanatorium, Paimio, Finland Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Paimio_Sanatorium2.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1929–1933
- **Type:** Tuberculosis sanatorium
- **Location:** Paimio, Finland
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Paimio%20Sanatorium%20Alvar%20Aalto)

Alvar Aalto won a competition in 1929 for a tuberculosis sanatorium in a pine forest near Turku, at a time when fresh air, light and rest were the main treatment. The patients' wing is a long, narrow block oriented to catch the morning sun, ending in stacked open sun balconies where patients lay for hours. Aalto designed the details from the patient's position: soft-coloured ceilings for people lying in bed, indirect lighting, quiet washbasins that splash less, and the bentwood Paimio chair, angled to ease breathing. Other wings for treatment, staff and services fan out on different angles. Antibiotics later made such sanatoria obsolete, and it became a general hospital, then closed in the 2010s. It is now used for other purposes and is on Finland's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage status.

### Villa Mairea (1937–1939)

[![Villa Mairea](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d2/4Y1A7841_Alvar_Aalto%2C_Finland_%2826710745140%29.jpg/1280px-4Y1A7841_Alvar_Aalto%2C_Finland_%2826710745140%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:4Y1A7841_Alvar_Aalto%2C_Finland_%2826710745140%29.jpg)

<sub>Villa Mairea, Noormarkku, Finland Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:4Y1A7841_Alvar_Aalto%2C_Finland_%2826710745140%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1937–1939
- **Type:** Country house
- **Location:** Noormarkku, Finland
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Villa%20Mairea%20Alvar%20Aalto)

Harry and Maire Gullichsen, an industrialist couple and patrons of modern art, commissioned the Aaltos to design a house on the family estate near Pori, asking for an experiment in modern living. The L-shaped house encloses a garden with a kidney-shaped pool and a turf-roofed sauna. Inside, the large living space flows freely, its black-painted steel columns wrapped in rattan or grouped with wooden poles evoking the surrounding forest, while a stair is screened by vertical poles. White render, timber cladding, stone and a curved wooden studio mix the international and the vernacular. Villa Mairea is often described as Aalto's most personal work and an example of a softer, more humane modernism. It remains in the family and can be visited by appointment.
