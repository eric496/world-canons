# Philip Johnson

**1906 – 2005** · American  
Born: Cleveland, Ohio · Died: New Canaan, Connecticut  
Styles: [Modernism & the International Style](../movements/modernism.md), [Postmodernism](../movements/postmodernism.md)  

> American curator and architect who named the International Style in 1932, built the Glass House, and later turned to postmodernism with the AT&T Building.

## Biography

Johnson studied classics at Harvard and in 1932, with Henry-Russell Hitchcock, organised the Museum of Modern Art's exhibition Modern Architecture and the book The International Style, which introduced European modernism to America. In the 1930s he was drawn to fascist politics, which he later repudiated. He returned to Harvard to study architecture and built his Glass House at New Canaan in 1949, collaborated with Mies on the Seagram Building, and practised for decades with John Burgee, producing the AT&T Building, PPG Place and the Crystal Cathedral.

Johnson was an arbiter of taste more than an innovator, moving from Miesian modernism to historicism, postmodernism and deconstructivism, which he again helped launch with a MoMA exhibition in 1988. He was the first winner of the Pritzker Prize in 1979. His career shows how architecture's styles in the twentieth century were shaped by exhibitions, criticism and patronage as well as building.

- **Date:** 1949
- **Type:** House
- **Location:** New Canaan, Connecticut, United States
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Glass%20House%20Philip%20Johnson)

Philip Johnson built his own home on a wooded estate in New Canaan, completing it before Mies's Farnsworth House, which he had seen in design. It is a single room, about 17 by 10 metres, with walls of glass on a steel frame painted charcoal, and a brick cylinder containing the bathroom and fireplace as its only enclosed element. Low cabinets divide living, dining and sleeping areas. Opposite stands a windowless brick guest house. Over the next fifty years Johnson added an underground painting gallery, a sculpture gallery, a pavilion on the pond, a studio and other follies across the estate, a catalogue of his changing tastes. He and his partner David Whitney lived there until their deaths in 2005, leaving it to the National Trust for Historic Preservation, which opened it to the public.
