# Santiago Calatrava

**1951** · Spanish  
Born: Benimàmet, Valencia · Died: ?  
Styles: [Contemporary](../movements/contemporary-architecture.md)  

> Spanish architect and engineer of bridges, stations and the City of Arts and Sciences in Valencia, whose white structures recall skeletons and wings.

## Biography

Calatrava studied architecture in Valencia and civil engineering at the ETH in Zurich, where he wrote a doctoral thesis on folding structures and opened his practice in 1981. He designed the Stadelhofen station in Zurich, the Alamillo Bridge in Seville, the Lyon-Saint Exupéry airport station, the Milwaukee Art Museum extension, the City of Arts and Sciences in his native Valencia, the Turning Torso in Malmö and the World Trade Center Transportation Hub in New York.

Calatrava's work merges sculpture, architecture and engineering in white steel and concrete, with forms derived from his studies of the human body and animals. His buildings are admired for their drama and criticised for cost overruns and practical problems.

## Key buildings

### City of Arts and Sciences (1991–2009)

- **Date:** 1991–2009
- **Type:** Cultural complex
- **Location:** Valencia, Spain
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=City%20of%20Arts%20and%20Sciences%20Santiago%20Calatrava)

After a catastrophic flood in 1957, Valencia diverted the river Turia, and its old bed became a long park. At its south-eastern end the regional government built the City of Arts and Sciences, mostly designed by Santiago Calatrava, a native of the city. Its buildings include the Hemisfèric (1998), a planetarium and IMAX cinema like a great eye with a moving lid, the Science Museum, with its rib-like structure, the Umbracle, a landscaped walkway, the Ágora, and the Palau de les Arts Reina Sofía (2005), an opera house under a huge feather-like crest, set among shallow pools. The oceanographic park was designed by Félix Candela. The complex became a symbol of Valencia and a tourist attraction, but its cost overruns and maintenance problems, such as the loss of mosaic tiles from the opera house, have been widely criticised.
