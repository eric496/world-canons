# Rem Koolhaas

**1944** · Dutch  
Born: Rotterdam · Died: ?  
Styles: [Deconstructivism & Digital Form](../movements/deconstructivism.md), [Contemporary](../movements/contemporary-architecture.md)  

> Dutch architect and writer, founder of OMA and author of Delirious New York, whose buildings include the Seattle Central Library and the CCTV Headquarters in Beijing.

## Biography

Koolhaas worked as a journalist and screenwriter before studying at the Architectural Association in London and at Cornell. In 1975 he founded the Office for Metropolitan Architecture with Elia and Zoe Zenghelis and Madelon Vriesendorp, and published Delirious New York (1978), a 'retroactive manifesto' for Manhattan. OMA's works include the Kunsthal in Rotterdam, the Euralille master plan, the Maison à Bordeaux, the Casa da Música in Porto, the Seattle Central Library (2004) and the CCTV Headquarters (2012). He taught at Harvard, wrote S,M,L,XL with Bruce Mau, and in 2014 directed the Venice Architecture Biennale.

Koolhaas treats architecture as a response to the forces of the modern metropolis, programme and global capitalism rather than as an exercise in style. His research studios at Harvard examined shopping, the Pearl River Delta and Lagos, and many leading architects trained at OMA. He received the Pritzker Prize in 2000.

### Seattle Central Library (1999–2004)

- **Date:** 1999–2004
- **Type:** Public library
- **Location:** Seattle, Washington, United States
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Seattle%20Central%20Library%20Rem%20Koolhaas)

OMA, led by Rem Koolhaas with Joshua Prince-Ramus, designed Seattle's new central library by analysing the library's functions and grouping them into five stable 'platforms' stacked and shifted to fit the site, with more fluid public spaces in between. A diamond-patterned steel and glass skin stretches over the whole, creating a striking faceted form. The 'Books Spiral', a continuous ramp of shelving running through four floors, keeps the non-fiction collection in unbroken Dewey order, and a great top-lit reading room crowns the building. A bright red floor of meeting rooms and a 'living room' open to the city are among its features. It opened in 2004 and has been heavily used.

### CCTV Headquarters (2004–2012)

- **Date:** 2004–2012
- **Type:** Media headquarters
- **Location:** Beijing, China
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=CCTV%20Headquarters%20Rem%20Koolhaas)

China Central Television commissioned OMA, led by Rem Koolhaas and Ole Scheeren, with the engineers Arup, for its headquarters in Beijing's new business district. Instead of a conventional skyscraper, two leaning towers rise from a common base and join at the top in a cantilevered L-shaped bridge, forming a continuous loop of spaces for the broadcaster's production, offices and services. The irregular diagonal grid on its glass skin expresses the forces in the structure, denser where stresses are greater. Its shape earned it popular nicknames, and a fire in an adjacent building in 2009 delayed its completion. The building was seen as a symbol of China's ambitions before the 2008 Olympics, and its commission for a state broadcaster provoked debate in the West.
