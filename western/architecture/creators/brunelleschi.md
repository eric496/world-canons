# Filippo Brunelleschi

**1377 – 1446** · Florentine  
Born: Florence · Died: Florence  
Styles: [Renaissance](../movements/renaissance-architecture.md)  
Also in: [Art](../../art/creators/brunelleschi.md)  

> Goldsmith turned engineer and architect who built the dome of Florence Cathedral and, with buildings such as the Foundling Hospital and San Lorenzo, founded Renaissance architecture.

## Biography

Brunelleschi, son of a Florentine notary, trained as a goldsmith and in 1401 competed, against Lorenzo Ghiberti, for the bronze doors of the Baptistery. After losing, he turned to architecture and, according to his early biographers, studied the ruins of ancient Rome. Around 1415 he demonstrated linear perspective with painted panels of the Baptistery. From 1418 to his death he directed the construction of the cathedral dome, and in the 1420s and 1430s he designed the Ospedale degli Innocenti, the Old Sacristy and church of San Lorenzo, the Pazzi Chapel and Santo Spirito.

Brunelleschi combined two things: brilliant engineering, including the hoisting machines and herringbone brickwork that allowed the dome to be built without centring, and a new architectural language of Corinthian columns, round arches and grey stone articulating white walls, all governed by simple ratios. His buildings made antiquity a living model rather than a source of fragments, and every Renaissance architect after him built on his example.

### Ospedale degli Innocenti (1419–1445)

- **Date:** 1419–1445
- **Type:** Foundling hospital
- **Location:** Piazza della Santissima Annunziata, Florence, Italy

The silk guild of Florence commissioned Brunelleschi in 1419 to design a hospital for foundlings, children abandoned by their parents. Its loggia on the square, with nine round arches on slender Corinthian columns of grey pietra serena against white walls, is governed by a simple module: the spacing of the columns equals their height and the depth of the loggia, so that each bay is a cube under a domical vault. The clarity and classical detail made it the manifesto of a new architecture. Between the arches are glazed terracotta roundels of swaddled infants added by Andrea della Robbia in the 1480s. The institution cared for children for centuries; a revolving wheel, the ruota, allowed babies to be left anonymously until 1875. It still houses child welfare organisations and a museum.

### Dome of Florence Cathedral (1420–1436)

[![Dome of Florence Cathedral](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Cattedrale_di_Santa_Maria_del_Fiore_%E2%80%93_Il_Duomo_di_Firenze.jpg/1280px-Cattedrale_di_Santa_Maria_del_Fiore_%E2%80%93_Il_Duomo_di_Firenze.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cattedrale_di_Santa_Maria_del_Fiore_%E2%80%93_Il_Duomo_di_Firenze.jpg)

<sub>Dome of Florence Cathedral, Santa Maria del Fiore, Florence, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cattedrale_di_Santa_Maria_del_Fiore_%E2%80%93_Il_Duomo_di_Firenze.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1420–1436
- **Type:** Cathedral dome
- **Location:** Santa Maria del Fiore, Florence, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Dome%20of%20Florence%20Cathedral%20Filippo%20Brunelleschi)

By 1418 the cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore had an octagonal crossing some 42 metres across, but no one knew how to roof it: there was not enough timber for the usual centring, and the design forbade external buttresses. Brunelleschi won the commission and built the dome between 1420 and 1436, the largest masonry dome ever built. It has two shells, an inner and an outer, linked by ribs and horizontal rings of stone and timber chains, and its bricks are laid in a herringbone pattern that held each course in place while the mortar set, so that the dome supported itself as it rose. He also invented hoisting machines, including a reversible ox-driven hoist, to raise some 37,000 tonnes of material. The marble lantern was completed after his death to his design. The dome dominates Florence and inspired every great dome that followed, including Michelangelo's at St Peter's.

### Pazzi Chapel (c. 1430–1460s)

[![Pazzi Chapel](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ac/Florence%2C_Santa_Croce%2C_Cappella_dei_Pazzi%2C_1440s-70s.jpg/1280px-Florence%2C_Santa_Croce%2C_Cappella_dei_Pazzi%2C_1440s-70s.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Florence%2C_Santa_Croce%2C_Cappella_dei_Pazzi%2C_1440s-70s.jpg)

<sub>Pazzi Chapel, Santa Croce, Florence, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Florence%2C_Santa_Croce%2C_Cappella_dei_Pazzi%2C_1440s-70s.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1430–1460s
- **Type:** Chapter house
- **Location:** Santa Croce, Florence, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pazzi%20Chapel%20Filippo%20Brunelleschi)

The Pazzi family commissioned a chapter house for the friars of Santa Croce, set at the end of the first cloister. It is traditionally attributed to Brunelleschi, though it was completed long after his death and some scholars argue that others, perhaps Michelozzo or Giuliano da Maiano, designed part of it, especially the porch. The interior is a rectangle covered by a ribbed dome on pendentives, with a small square altar space under its own dome, and every surface is articulated by grey stone pilasters, arches and circles against white plaster. Glazed terracotta roundels of the apostles by Luca della Robbia and the evangelists add colour. The chapel is a three-dimensional diagram of the proportional thinking that defined Renaissance architecture.
