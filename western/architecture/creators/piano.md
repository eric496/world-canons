# Renzo Piano

**1937** · Italian  
Born: Genoa · Died: ?  
Styles: [High-tech](../movements/high-tech.md), [Contemporary](../movements/contemporary-architecture.md)  

> Italian architect, co-designer of the Centre Pompidou, whose later museums and towers, among them the Shard in London, combine lightness, craft and engineering.

## Biography

Piano grew up in a family of builders in Genoa and studied at the Milan Polytechnic. After the Centre Pompidou with Richard Rogers, he founded the Renzo Piano Building Workshop, with offices in Genoa and Paris. Its works include the Menil Collection in Houston, Kansai International Airport, the Jean-Marie Tjibaou Cultural Centre in New Caledonia, the Potsdamer Platz redevelopment in Berlin, the California Academy of Sciences, the Shard in London and the Whitney Museum in New York.

Piano's architecture is detailed with craftsmanlike care, uses light roofs and filtered daylight, and seeks to integrate engineering and context rather than impose a single style. He received the Pritzker Prize in 1998 and was named a senator for life in Italy in 2013.

## Key buildings

### The Shard (2009–2012)

<sub>The Shard, Southwark, London, England Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Shard_at_sunset_2017_%28cropped%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 2009–2012
- **Type:** Mixed-use skyscraper
- **Location:** Southwark, London, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Shard%20Renzo%20Piano)

The developer Irvine Sellar commissioned Renzo Piano to design a tower beside London Bridge station, and Piano is said to have sketched it on a napkin. The building tapers to 310 metres, its faceted glass planes splitting apart at the top, where they appear unfinished, so that the tower seems to dissolve into the sky. It contains offices, restaurants, a hotel, apartments and a public viewing gallery near the summit. Its slim, pointed form was intended to sit lightly among London's church spires, though conservation bodies objected to its effect on views of St Paul's and the Tower. On completion in 2012 it was the tallest building in the European Union.
