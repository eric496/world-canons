# Charles Barry

> Victorian architect who won the competition to rebuild the Palace of Westminster after the fire of 1834, working with Augustus Pugin.

<sub>Portrait: John Prescott Knight, portrait of Charles Barry · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sir_Charles_Barry_by_John_Prescott_Knight.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Barry was the son of a stationer and spent three years travelling in France, Italy, Greece, Turkey, Egypt and Syria (1817–20), drawing ancient and Renaissance buildings. Back in London he built Gothic churches and then the Travellers Club (1829–32) and Reform Club (1837–41) in the manner of Italian Renaissance palaces, a style he made fashionable for clubs and banks. In 1836 his design won the competition for the new Houses of Parliament, which occupied him until his death; he was knighted in 1852.

Barry was a master of planning and of the picturesque skyline. At Westminster he organised a vast and complicated building around a clear, symmetrical plan, while Pugin supplied the Gothic detail and furnishings. How credit should be divided between them was argued by their sons and is still discussed. Barry's sons Edward Middleton Barry and Charles Barry Jr continued his practice.

## Key buildings

### Palace of Westminster (1840–1870)

[![Palace of Westminster](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/Palace_of_Westminster_as_seen_from_Westminster_Bridge.jpg/1280px-Palace_of_Westminster_as_seen_from_Westminster_Bridge.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Palace_of_Westminster_as_seen_from_Westminster_Bridge.jpg)

<sub>Palace of Westminster, Westminster, London, England Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Palace_of_Westminster_as_seen_from_Westminster_Bridge.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1840–1870
- **Type:** Parliament building
- **Location:** Westminster, London, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Palace%20of%20Westminster%20Charles%20Barry)

Most of the old Palace of Westminster burned in October 1834; only Westminster Hall, the crypt and the Jewel Tower survived. A competition required a design in Gothic or Elizabethan style, and Barry's won in 1836. He organised the new palace, about 300 metres along the Thames, around a central lobby between the House of Lords and the House of Commons, with long corridors and courtyards, while Augustus Pugin designed the Perpendicular Gothic detail, the tiles, wallpapers, furniture, stained glass and metalwork. The House of Lords opened in 1847 and the Commons in 1852; work continued until 1870. The Victoria Tower and the clock tower, now the Elizabeth Tower, frame the skyline. Bombed in 1941, the Commons chamber was rebuilt by Giles Gilbert Scott. With Westminster Abbey it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and a major restoration is planned.
