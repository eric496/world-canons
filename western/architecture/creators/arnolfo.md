# Arnolfo di Cambio

**c. 1240 – c. 1300–1310** · Florentine  
Born: Colle di Val d'Elsa, Tuscany · Died: ?  
Styles: [Gothic](../movements/gothic.md)  

> Sculptor and architect who designed the original plan of Florence Cathedral and is traditionally credited with the Palazzo Vecchio and the church of Santa Croce.

## Biography

Arnolfo trained as a sculptor in the workshop of Nicola Pisano, working on the pulpit of Siena Cathedral, and then served Charles of Anjou in Rome, where he created tombs and the elaborate Gothic canopies over the altars of San Paolo fuori le Mura and Santa Cecilia. By the 1290s he was in Florence, where he was given charge of the new cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore, begun in 1296, and carved sculpture for its façade.

Arnolfo's cathedral plan, with its wide nave and vast octagonal crossing, set the challenge that Brunelleschi's dome would answer more than a century later. The sixteenth-century writer Vasari also credited him with the Palazzo Vecchio and Santa Croce, attributions that are plausible but undocumented. His work combines the Gothic of France with a Tuscan love of broad spaces and clear volumes.

- **Type:** Franciscan church

Santa Croce was begun in 1294 for the Franciscan friars, who needed a vast hall for preaching to the crowds of Florence; Vasari attributed the design to Arnolfo di Cambio. Its wide nave, lined by octagonal piers and covered with an open timber roof, gives a broad, airy space quite different from the vaulted cathedrals of France. Rich Florentine families endowed its chapels, which Giotto and his followers decorated with frescoes, among them Giotto's scenes of Saint Francis in the Bardi Chapel. Brunelleschi's Pazzi Chapel stands in the cloister. Santa Croce holds the tombs or memorials of Michelangelo, Galileo, Machiavelli, Rossini and Dante (whose body remains in Ravenna), and its neo-Gothic marble façade was added only in 1857–63. The flood of 1966 caused grave damage, including to Cimabue's crucifix.

### Palazzo Vecchio (1299–1314)

[![Palazzo Vecchio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b3/Piazza_della_signoria%2C_palazzo_vecchio%2C_veduta_01.jpg/1280px-Piazza_della_signoria%2C_palazzo_vecchio%2C_veduta_01.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Piazza_della_signoria%2C_palazzo_vecchio%2C_veduta_01.jpg)

<sub>Palazzo Vecchio, Piazza della Signoria, Florence, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Piazza_della_signoria%2C_palazzo_vecchio%2C_veduta_01.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1299–1314
- **Type:** Town hall
- **Location:** Piazza della Signoria, Florence, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Palazzo%20Vecchio%20Arnolfo%20di%20Cambio)

The Palazzo dei Priori, later called the Palazzo Vecchio, was built from 1299 to house the government of the Florentine republic, the Signoria. Vasari attributed it to Arnolfo di Cambio, a plausible but undocumented attribution. It is a massive block of rusticated stone, crowned by projecting battlements on corbels and by a slender tower 94 metres tall, built over an older tower and set off-centre. The Medici, who became dukes, lived here in the sixteenth century and had Vasari remodel the interiors, including the vast Hall of the Five Hundred; Leonardo and Michelangelo had been commissioned to paint battle scenes for it, which were never completed. It remains Florence's town hall and a museum, and Michelangelo's David stood before it until 1873, where a copy now stands.
