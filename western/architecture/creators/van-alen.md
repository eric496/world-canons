# William Van Alen

**1883 – 1954** · American  
Born: Brooklyn, New York · Died: New York City  
Styles: [Art Deco](../movements/art-deco.md)  

> New York architect of the Chrysler Building, the Art Deco skyscraper that was briefly the tallest building in the world.

## Biography

Van Alen studied at the Pratt Institute and in the ateliers of New York, and won a scholarship to the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris. Back in New York he practised with H. Craig Severance, then alone. The Chrysler Building, commissioned by the automobile maker Walter Chrysler, was completed in 1930 and, with its secretly assembled stainless-steel spire, overtook its rival 40 Wall Street, designed by Severance, as the world's tallest building. A dispute over his fee ended his career as a major architect, and the Depression left him few commissions.

Van Alen rejected historical styles for an architecture of modern materials and machine-age imagery. The Chrysler Building's terraced crown of stainless-steel sunbursts, its gargoyles in the form of radiator caps and eagles, and its lavish lobby are among the most admired designs of Art Deco.

## Key buildings

### Chrysler Building (1928–1930)

- **Date:** 1928–1930
- **Type:** Skyscraper
- **Location:** Manhattan, New York City, United States
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Chrysler%20Building%20William%20Van%20Alen)

Built for the automobile magnate Walter P. Chrysler, the building rose during the race for the tallest skyscraper in New York. To beat 40 Wall Street, Van Alen had a 38-metre spire assembled secretly inside the crown and raised into place in about ninety minutes in October 1929, bringing the height to 319 metres. It was the tallest building in the world for eleven months, until the Empire State Building. Its terraced crown of stainless-steel arches with triangular windows, its eagle-head gargoyles and its brickwork frieze of hubcaps celebrate the car and the machine age. The triangular lobby, with marble, chrome and a ceiling mural on the theme of transport, is one of the great Art Deco interiors.
