# Donato Bramante

<a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Donato_Bramante.jpg"><img align="right" width="240" src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/35/Donato_Bramante.jpg" alt="Donato Bramante" title="Portrait of Donato Bramante"></a>

**1444 – 1514** · Italian  
Born: Fermignano, near Urbino · Died: Rome  
Styles: [Renaissance](../movements/renaissance-architecture.md)  

> Painter and architect who brought the High Renaissance to Rome with the Tempietto and the first design for the new St Peter's Basilica.

<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Donato Bramante · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Donato_Bramante.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Bramante trained as a painter in the circle of Piero della Francesca and Urbino's humanist court, then spent some twenty years in Milan under Ludovico Sforza, where he knew Leonardo da Vinci and built Santa Maria presso San Satiro, with its illusionistic painted choir, and the tribune of Santa Maria delle Grazie. After the French conquest of Milan in 1499 he moved to Rome and studied its ruins. Pope Julius II made him his architect, entrusting him with the Belvedere Court of the Vatican and, from 1506, the rebuilding of St Peter's.

Bramante's Roman works, such as the cloister of Santa Maria della Pace and the Tempietto, have a gravity and mass new to the Renaissance, modelled closely on ancient buildings. His plan for St Peter's, a Greek cross crowned by a huge dome, was altered by his successors but set the project's scale. Raphael, Peruzzi and Antonio da Sangallo worked under him, and his circle created the High Renaissance language of Roman architecture.

## Key buildings

### Tempietto of San Pietro in Montorio (c. 1502–1510)

[![Tempietto of San Pietro in Montorio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Tempietto_di_San_Pietro_in_Montorio.jpg/1280px-Tempietto_di_San_Pietro_in_Montorio.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tempietto_di_San_Pietro_in_Montorio.jpg)

<sub>Bramante's Tempietto in the courtyard of San Pietro in Montorio, Rome Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tempietto_di_San_Pietro_in_Montorio.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1502–1510
- **Type:** Commemorative shrine
- **Location:** Rome, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Tempietto%20of%20San%20Pietro%20in%20Montorio%20Donato%20Bramante)

The Tempietto, 'little temple', stands in the cloister of San Pietro in Montorio on the Janiculum, on the spot where tradition held that Saint Peter was crucified. It was commissioned by Ferdinand and Isabella of Spain; its date is debated, though an inscription gives 1502. Bramante based it on round Roman temples such as that at Tivoli: a cylindrical cella ringed by sixteen Tuscan Doric columns, a balustrade, a drum and a hemispherical dome, with a crypt below. Only about four and a half metres across inside, it has a monumental gravity. Bramante planned a circular colonnaded courtyard around it, which was not built but is known from Serlio's treatise. Serlio published it, and Palladio included it in his Four Books as the only modern building among the ancient temples; it inspired domes such as that of St Paul's.
