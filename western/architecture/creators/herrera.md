# Juan de Herrera

<a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Juan_de_Herrera.jpg"><img align="right" width="240" src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/65/Juan_de_Herrera.jpg" alt="Juan de Herrera" title="Portrait of Juan de Herrera"></a>

**c. 1530 – 1597** · Spanish  
Born: Roiz, Cantabria · Died: Madrid  
Styles: [Renaissance](../movements/renaissance-architecture.md)  

> Architect, mathematician and courtier of Philip II of Spain who completed El Escorial and created the severe Herrerian style.

<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Juan de Herrera · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Juan_de_Herrera.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Herrera served as a soldier in Italy and Flanders in the retinue of Prince Philip, and studied mathematics, astronomy and mechanics. In 1563 he joined the works at El Escorial under its first architect, Juan Bautista de Toledo, and after Toledo's death in 1567 he took charge, redesigning the basilica and completing the vast monastery-palace by 1584. Philip II also employed him on the palace at Aranjuez, the cathedral of Valladolid and the Archive of the Indies in Seville, and Herrera founded an academy of mathematics in Madrid.

The Herrerian style, called estilo desornamentado, the unornamented style, strips classical architecture to granite planes, simple orders and pyramidal pinnacles. It reflected Philip II's piety and the sober spirit of the Counter-Reformation. Herrera's austere monumentality shaped Spanish architecture for decades and remained a reference point for official building in Spain.

## Key buildings

### El Escorial (1563–1584)

[![El Escorial](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/59/Monasterio_de_El_Escorial_en_Madrid.jpg/1280px-Monasterio_de_El_Escorial_en_Madrid.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Monasterio_de_El_Escorial_en_Madrid.jpg)

<sub>El Escorial, San Lorenzo de El Escorial, near Madrid, Spain Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Monasterio_de_El_Escorial_en_Madrid.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1563–1584
- **Type:** Monastery, palace and royal mausoleum
- **Location:** San Lorenzo de El Escorial, near Madrid, Spain
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=El%20Escorial%20Juan%20de%20Herrera)

Philip II founded the monastery of San Lorenzo de El Escorial to commemorate the Spanish victory over France at Saint-Quentin in 1557 and to serve as a burial place for his father Charles V and his dynasty. Juan Bautista de Toledo drew up the plan, a vast rectangle of courtyards, and after his death Juan de Herrera completed the work. Built of grey granite with little ornament, it combines a monastery, a basilica with a dome, a royal palace, a library, a college and a pantheon of kings, where most Spanish monarchs since Charles V are buried. Philip governed his empire from a modest suite beside the basilica's high altar. The library has frescoed vaults and important manuscripts. El Escorial expressed the Catholic piety and imperial power of Habsburg Spain and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
