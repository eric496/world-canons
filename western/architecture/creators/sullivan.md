# Louis Sullivan

**1856 – 1924** · American  
Born: Boston, Massachusetts · Died: Chicago  
Styles: [Chicago School](../movements/chicago-school.md)  

> Chicago architect who gave the steel-framed skyscraper its first convincing form and coined the phrase 'form follows function'.

## Biography

Sullivan studied briefly at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and the École des Beaux-Arts and worked for Frank Furness in Philadelphia and William Le Baron Jenney in Chicago. In partnership with the engineer Dankmar Adler from 1883, he designed the Auditorium Building (1886–89), the Wainwright Building in St Louis (1890–91), the Guaranty Building in Buffalo (1894–96) and the Schlesinger and Mayer store (now the Sullivan Center) in Chicago. After the partnership ended in 1895 his commissions dwindled, and his late works were mainly small banks in the Midwest. He died poor.

In his essay The Tall Office Building Artistically Considered (1896), Sullivan argued that a skyscraper should be proud and soaring and that form ever follows function. His buildings articulate base, vertical shaft and cornice, and are covered with intricate organic terracotta and iron ornament of his own invention. Frank Lloyd Wright, who worked for him, called him his Lieber Meister.

### Wainwright Building (1890–1891)

- **Date:** 1890–1891
- **Type:** Office building
- **Location:** St Louis, Missouri, United States
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Wainwright%20Building%20Louis%20Sullivan)

The brewer Ellis Wainwright commissioned Adler and Sullivan to design a ten-storey office building in St Louis on a steel frame. Sullivan treated the tall building as a whole: a two-storey base for shops, an attic crowning the top with a deep frieze of terracotta foliage and a cornice, and between them a shaft of continuous vertical piers rising uninterrupted, with recessed spandrels between the windows, so that the building appears to soar. Since not every pier contains a column, the design emphasises an image of verticality rather than a literal statement of structure. Frank Lloyd Wright called it the moment when the tall building became architecture. It was saved from demolition in the 1970s and restored as state government offices.

### Carson, Pirie, Scott and Company Building (Sullivan Center) (1899–1904)

[![Carson, Pirie, Scott and Company Building (Sullivan Center)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Carson%2C_Pirie%2C_Scott_and_Company_Building_%28Sullivan_Center%29%2C_Chicago%2C_Illinois_%289179422705%29.jpg/1280px-Carson%2C_Pirie%2C_Scott_and_Company_Building_%28Sullivan_Center%29%2C_Chicago%2C_Illinois_%289179422705%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Carson%2C_Pirie%2C_Scott_and_Company_Building_%28Sullivan_Center%29%2C_Chicago%2C_Illinois_%289179422705%29.jpg)

<sub>Carson, Pirie, Scott and Company Building (Sullivan Center), Chicago, Illinois, United States Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Carson%2C_Pirie%2C_Scott_and_Company_Building_%28Sullivan_Center%29%2C_Chicago%2C_Illinois_%289179422705%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1899–1904
- **Type:** Department store
- **Location:** Chicago, Illinois, United States
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Carson%2C%20Pirie%2C%20Scott%20and%20Company%20Building%20%28Sullivan%20Center%29%20Louis%20Sullivan)

Built for the Schlesinger and Mayer department store and soon taken over by Carson, Pirie, Scott and Company, the building on State Street was Sullivan's last large commission. The upper storeys express the steel frame directly as a grid of broad horizontal 'Chicago windows', each with a large fixed pane between narrow opening sashes, clad in white terracotta. At street level, where shoppers passed, the display windows are framed by intricate cast-iron ornament of foliage and geometric forms in Sullivan's personal style, swelling over the rounded corner entrance. The store was later extended by Daniel Burnham's firm and others. Carson's closed in 2007, and the building was restored and renamed the Sullivan Center, with offices and shops.
