# Jules Hardouin-Mansart

**1646 – 1708** · French  
Born: Paris · Died: Marly-le-Roi  
Styles: [Baroque](../movements/baroque.md)  

> First Architect to Louis XIV, who enlarged Versailles, created its Hall of Mirrors and built the domed church of Les Invalides.

## Biography

Jules Hardouin trained with his great-uncle François Mansart, the leading French classical architect of the previous generation, whose name he added to his own. He won the favour of Louis XIV with the château of Clagny for Madame de Montespan, and from 1678 directed the enlargement of Versailles: the Hall of Mirrors, the north and south wings, the Orangery, the Grand Trianon and the Royal Chapel. He also designed the Place des Victoires and Place Vendôme in Paris and the Dôme des Invalides, and in 1699 became superintendent of the king's buildings.

Running a large office of draughtsmen and designers, Hardouin-Mansart produced architecture on an industrial scale for the most powerful monarchy in Europe. His style is sober classicism handled with Baroque grandeur, crowned by the gilded dome of the Invalides. The steep roof named after his great-uncle, the mansard, became a staple of French building.

### Les Invalides (Dôme des Invalides) (1677–1706)

[![Les Invalides (Dôme des Invalides)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/H%C3%B4tel_des_Invalides_from_the_Eiffel_Tower%2C_23_July_2009.jpg/1280px-H%C3%B4tel_des_Invalides_from_the_Eiffel_Tower%2C_23_July_2009.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:H%C3%B4tel_des_Invalides_from_the_Eiffel_Tower%2C_23_July_2009.jpg)

- **Date:** 1677–1706
- **Type:** Royal chapel, later mausoleum
- **Location:** Paris, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Les%20Invalides%20%28D%C3%B4me%20des%20Invalides%29%20Jules%20Hardouin-Mansart)

Louis XIV founded the Hôtel des Invalides in 1670 to house disabled and aged soldiers, and Libéral Bruant built its courtyards and soldiers' church. In 1677 Hardouin-Mansart was commissioned to add a royal chapel joined to the soldiers' church, completed in 1706. It is a centrally planned church under a dome, with a two-storey columned façade, and the dome has three shells: an inner one open at the top, through which one sees a painted second dome lit by hidden windows, and an outer timber and lead dome rising to a gilded lantern 107 metres high. Admired as the finest French classical dome, it was regilded in 1989. In 1840 Napoleon's remains were returned from Saint Helena, and Louis Visconti designed his red quartzite tomb in a crypt under the dome, completed in 1861.

### Hall of Mirrors (1678–1684)

[![Hall of Mirrors](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f1/Chateau_Versailles_Galerie_des_Glaces.jpg/1280px-Chateau_Versailles_Galerie_des_Glaces.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chateau_Versailles_Galerie_des_Glaces.jpg)

<sub>Hall of Mirrors, Palace of Versailles, France Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chateau_Versailles_Galerie_des_Glaces.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1678–1684
- **Type:** Palace gallery
- **Location:** Palace of Versailles, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Hall%20of%20Mirrors%20Jules%20Hardouin-Mansart)

Hardouin-Mansart replaced Le Vau's garden terrace with a gallery 73 metres long, completed in 1684. Seventeen arched windows overlooking the gardens are matched on the opposite wall by seventeen arches filled with 357 mirrors, a luxury made in France to compete with Venetian glass. Charles Le Brun painted the vaulted ceiling with thirty scenes glorifying Louis XIV's reign and victories, and the gallery originally held solid silver furniture, melted down in 1689 to pay for war. It was used for daily passage, court festivities and receptions of ambassadors. The German Empire was proclaimed there in 1871, and the Treaty of Versailles was signed there in 1919. A major restoration was completed in 2007.
