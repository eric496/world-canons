# Master builders of Chartres

**active c. 1194–1260** · French  
Styles: [Gothic](../movements/gothic.md)  

> The unknown master masons who rebuilt Chartres Cathedral after the fire of 1194, creating the classic model of High Gothic.

## Biography

When fire destroyed most of the Romanesque cathedral of Chartres in 1194, sparing only the west towers, the crypt and the Royal Portal, the chapter and townspeople rebuilt the church at great speed, with the main structure largely complete by about 1220 and consecration in 1260. No names of the designers are recorded. Scholars have analysed the masonry stone by stone and proposed several successive teams of builders, but how many masters there were and who led the work remain debated.

The Chartres builders defined High Gothic: a three-storey elevation of arcade, triforium and tall clerestory, quadripartite rib vaults, and flying buttresses that allowed the upper walls to become great windows. They also oversaw one of the largest and best-preserved ensembles of medieval stained glass and sculpture. Chartres became the reference point for Reims, Amiens and the cathedrals that followed.

## Key buildings

### Chartres Cathedral (1194–1260)

[![Chartres Cathedral](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/28/Notre_Dame_de_Chartres.jpg/1280px-Notre_Dame_de_Chartres.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Notre_Dame_de_Chartres.jpg)

<sub>Chartres Cathedral, Chartres, France Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Notre_Dame_de_Chartres.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1194–1260
- **Type:** Gothic cathedral
- **Location:** Chartres, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Chartres%20Cathedral%20Master%20builders%20of%20Chartres)

Chartres was a great centre of devotion to the Virgin, whose tunic was its chief relic. After a fire in 1194 destroyed most of the earlier church, the cathedral was rebuilt in about twenty-five years around the surviving west façade with its twelfth-century Royal Portal. The new building set the pattern of High Gothic: a nave elevation of arcade, narrow triforium and a clerestory as tall as the arcade, quadripartite vaults and flying buttresses. Some 170 windows preserve most of their original thirteenth-century glass, including the Notre-Dame de la Belle Verrière and three great roses, and thousands of sculpted figures fill the transept portals. The two west spires differ: the south is Romanesque, the north flamboyant Gothic of the early sixteenth century. A UNESCO World Heritage Site, it was cleaned and restored in the 2000s and 2010s.
