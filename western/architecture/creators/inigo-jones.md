# Inigo Jones

**1573 – 1652** · English

Styles: [Palladianism & Neoclassicism](../movements/neoclassicism.md)  

> The first English architect to master Italian classicism, designer of the Queen's House at Greenwich and the Banqueting House in Whitehall.

<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Inigo Jones · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:PortraitInigoJones.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

The son of a London clothworker, Jones first made his name as a designer of masques, the lavish court entertainments of James I, for which he created costumes, scenery and stage machinery with Ben Jonson. He travelled in Italy, and on a long tour in 1613–14 with the collector the Earl of Arundel he studied ancient ruins and the buildings of Palladio, annotating his own copy of the Four Books. Appointed Surveyor of the King's Works in 1615, he designed the Queen's House, the Banqueting House, the Queen's Chapel at St James's and the piazza of Covent Garden. The Civil War ended his career.

Jones introduced to England, still building in a Tudor and Jacobean manner, a pure classical architecture based on Palladio and on ancient Rome. His buildings seemed revolutionary in their restraint and proportion. A century later Lord Burlington and his circle revered him as the father of English Palladianism, published his designs and made his style the foundation of Georgian architecture.

### Queen's House (1616–1635)

[![Queen's House](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/Queens_House.jpg/1280px-Queens_House.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Queens_House.jpg)

<sub>Queen's House, Greenwich, London, England Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Queens_House.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1616–1635
- **Type:** Royal villa
- **Location:** Greenwich, London, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Queen%27s%20House%20Inigo%20Jones)

Anne of Denmark, wife of James I, commissioned Inigo Jones in 1616 to build a house at Greenwich, straddling the road from London to Dover between the palace gardens and the park. Work stopped at her death in 1619 and was completed in 1635 for Queen Henrietta Maria, wife of Charles I. The white, cubic house, with a loggia facing the park, a terrace and simple proportioned windows, must have appeared astonishing beside the red-brick Tudor palace. Its central hall is a perfect cube of 40 feet, and the Tulip Stairs form the first geometrical, self-supporting spiral staircase in Britain. The house was later the centrepiece of Wren's Royal Hospital, whose layout kept the view to it open. It is part of the National Maritime Museum.

### Banqueting House, Whitehall (1619–1622)

[![Banqueting House, Whitehall](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/14/Banqueting_House_London.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Banqueting_House_London.jpg)

<sub>Banqueting House, Whitehall, Whitehall, London, England Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Banqueting_House_London.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1619–1622
- **Type:** Royal hall
- **Location:** Whitehall, London, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Banqueting%20House%2C%20Whitehall%20Inigo%20Jones)

James I commissioned Inigo Jones to build a new Banqueting House in the Palace of Whitehall, for court ceremonies and the masques Jones himself designed. Its façade, with Ionic and Corinthian orders in Portland stone, is modelled on Palladian palaces. Inside, a double cube hall, 110 by 55 feet, has a gallery and a flat ceiling divided into panels, which Charles I commissioned Peter Paul Rubens to paint with an allegory of the wise rule of his father, installed in 1636. Masques were stopped to protect the paintings from candle smoke. On 30 January 1649 Charles I walked through the hall to a scaffold outside a window, where he was executed. The rest of the palace burned in 1698, and the Banqueting House alone survives.
