## Biography

The son of a sculptor, Bernini was a prodigy whose marble groups for Cardinal Scipione Borghese made him famous in his early twenties. Pope Urban VIII made him the leading artist of Rome: in 1624 he began the bronze baldachin over the high altar of St Peter's, and from 1629 he was architect of the basilica. Under Alexander VII he designed the colonnades of St Peter's Square (1656–67) and the Scala Regia, as well as the church of Sant'Andrea al Quirinale. In 1665 he travelled to Paris at the invitation of Louis XIV to design the east front of the Louvre, but his plans were rejected.

Bernini thought of architecture as theatre, uniting building, sculpture, painting and hidden light to overwhelm the viewer, as in the Cornaro Chapel with its Ecstasy of Saint Teresa. His square at St Peter's embraces the crowd like arms, and his small oval Sant'Andrea concentrates the effect of a great church. His rivalry with Borromini defined Roman Baroque, and his manner of designing whole ensembles spread across Catholic Europe.

### St Peter's Square (1656–1667)

[![St Peter's Square](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/St_Peter%27s_Square%2C_Vatican_City_-_April_2007.jpg/1280px-St_Peter%27s_Square%2C_Vatican_City_-_April_2007.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St_Peter%27s_Square%2C_Vatican_City_-_April_2007.jpg)

<sub>St Peter's Square, Vatican City Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St_Peter%27s_Square%2C_Vatican_City_-_April_2007.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1656–1667
- **Type:** Piazza and colonnade
- **Location:** Vatican City
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=St%20Peter%27s%20Square%20Gian%20Lorenzo%20Bernini)

Pope Alexander VII commissioned Bernini to create a square in front of St Peter's large enough for the crowds receiving the papal blessing. Bernini designed a trapezoidal space opening onto a vast oval, framed by two curved colonnades four columns deep, with 284 Doric columns and 140 statues of saints along the top. He compared the colonnades to the motherly arms of the Church welcoming believers and heretics alike. The Egyptian obelisk raised by Domenico Fontana in 1586 stands at the centre between two fountains, and discs set in the pavement mark points from which the rows of columns line up as one. A third arm closing the oval was never built, and Bernini's intended effect of surprise was altered when Mussolini's Via della Conciliazione cleared the old neighbourhood in front in the 1930s.

### Sant'Andrea al Quirinale (1658–1670)

[![Sant'Andrea al Quirinale](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/15/Sant%27Andrea_al_Quirinale.jpg/960px-Sant%27Andrea_al_Quirinale.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sant%27Andrea_al_Quirinale.jpg)

<sub>Sant'Andrea al Quirinale, Rome, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sant%27Andrea_al_Quirinale.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1658–1670
- **Type:** Jesuit church
- **Location:** Rome, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sant%27Andrea%20al%20Quirinale%20Gian%20Lorenzo%20Bernini)

Cardinal Camillo Pamphilj paid for this church for the Jesuit novitiate on the Quirinal Hill, opposite the papal palace. Bernini set an oval plan across the short axis, so the visitor enters on the long side and faces the altar directly across a short space, with the side chapels arranged around. The interior is clad in dark, richly coloured marble below and white and gold above, where a coffered dome lit from hidden windows rises. Above the altar, a painting of Saint Andrew's martyrdom, lit from a hidden source, is crowned by a sculpture of the saint rising through a broken pediment toward heaven, uniting painting, sculpture and architecture in a single drama. Bernini's son reported that his father sat there in old age, taking pleasure in the work.
