# Antoni Gaudí

**1852 – 1926** · Spanish (Catalan)  
Born: Reus, Catalonia · Died: Barcelona  
Styles: [Art Nouveau & Modernisme](../movements/art-nouveau.md)  

> Catalan architect of the Sagrada Família, Casa Batlló, Casa Milà and Park Güell, whose structural and natural forms are unique in modern architecture.

## Biography

Gaudí, the son of a coppersmith, studied at the Barcelona School of Architecture, graduating in 1878. He found his great patron in the industrialist Eusebi Güell, for whom he built a palace, a crypt for a workers' colony and Park Güell. In 1883 he took over the expiatory church of the Sagrada Família, begun the year before in Gothic Revival style. His Barcelona houses include Casa Vicens, Casa Batlló (1904–06) and Casa Milà (1906–12). From 1914 he worked only on the Sagrada Família, living in its workshop. He died after being struck by a tram.

Gaudí drew on Gothic, Mudéjar and Catalan traditions and on the forms of nature, and he designed with hanging chain models whose curves, inverted, give arches that carry their loads in pure compression. His buildings use catenary arches, branching columns, hyperboloids and broken-tile mosaic, called trencadís. Seven of his works are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the Sagrada Família, still under construction, has become a symbol of Barcelona.

### Sagrada Família (1882–present)

[![Sagrada Família](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ef/SF_maig_2_cropped.jpg/960px-SF_maig_2_cropped.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SF_maig_2_cropped.jpg)

<sub>Sagrada Família, Barcelona, Spain Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SF_maig_2_cropped.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1882–present
- **Type:** Basilica
- **Location:** Barcelona, Spain
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sagrada%20Fam%C3%ADlia%20Antoni%20Gaud%C3%AD)

The Basílica de la Sagrada Família was begun in 1882 by a devotional association as an expiatory church funded by donations, to a Gothic Revival design by Francisco de Paula del Villar. Gaudí took over in 1883 and transformed it into a vast symbolic project: three façades representing the Nativity, the Passion and the Glory, eighteen spires for the apostles, the evangelists, the Virgin and Christ, and an interior forest of branching, tree-like columns. He worked on it for forty-three years, completing only the crypt, the apse walls and part of the Nativity façade. His workshop was burned and his models smashed in the Spanish Civil War, and their fragments were later pieced together. Construction resumed and, with computer design, has accelerated; the church was consecrated as a basilica in 2010 and the central tower of Jesus Christ, which makes it the tallest church in the world, was scheduled to be completed in 2026, the centenary of Gaudí's death. It is partly a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Park Güell (1900–1914)

- **Date:** 1900–1914
- **Type:** Park, originally a housing estate
- **Location:** Barcelona, Spain
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Park%20G%C3%BCell%20Antoni%20Gaud%C3%AD)

Eusebi Güell bought a hillside above Barcelona to create a garden city of houses for the wealthy, on the English model, and commissioned Gaudí to design it. Only two houses were built and sold, one bought by Gaudí himself, and the project was abandoned; the city acquired the land as a park in 1926. Gaudí's infrastructure remains: gatehouses with mushroom-like roofs, a monumental stair with a mosaic salamander, a hall of eighty-six Doric columns intended as a market, supporting a great terrace above, edged by a serpentine bench covered in broken-tile mosaic made largely by Josep Maria Jujol, and paths carried on rough stone viaducts that merge with the hillside. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Casa Batlló (1904–1906)

[![Casa Batlló](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/bf/Casa_Batllo_Overview_Barcelona_Spain_cut.jpg/1280px-Casa_Batllo_Overview_Barcelona_Spain_cut.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Casa_Batllo_Overview_Barcelona_Spain_cut.jpg)

<sub>Casa Batlló, Passeig de Gràcia, Barcelona, Spain Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Casa_Batllo_Overview_Barcelona_Spain_cut.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1904–1906
- **Type:** Apartment building remodelling
- **Location:** Passeig de Gràcia, Barcelona, Spain
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Casa%20Batll%C3%B3%20Antoni%20Gaud%C3%AD)

The textile industrialist Josep Batlló commissioned Gaudí to remodel his family's apartment building on Barcelona's fashionable Passeig de Gràcia. Gaudí added a new façade covered in broken ceramic and coloured glass, with balconies shaped like masks or skulls and bone-like columns at the lower floors, giving it the nickname of the house of bones. The roof's ridge of scaly glazed tiles has been read as the back of a dragon pierced by the cross-topped tower, an allusion to Saint George, the patron saint of Catalonia, though Gaudí left no explanation. Inside, the stair well is tiled in blues that grow paler toward the bottom to spread light evenly, and walls and ceilings curve without corners. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a museum.

### Casa Milà (La Pedrera) (1906–1912)

[![Casa Milà (La Pedrera)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/de/Casa_Mil%C3%A0%2C_general_view.jpg/1280px-Casa_Mil%C3%A0%2C_general_view.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Casa_Mil%C3%A0%2C_general_view.jpg)

<sub>Casa Milà (La Pedrera), Passeig de Gràcia, Barcelona, Spain Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Casa_Mil%C3%A0%2C_general_view.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1906–1912
- **Type:** Apartment building
- **Location:** Passeig de Gràcia, Barcelona, Spain
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Casa%20Mil%C3%A0%20%28La%20Pedrera%29%20Antoni%20Gaud%C3%AD)

Pere Milà and his wife Roser Segimon commissioned Gaudí to build an apartment building on a corner of the Passeig de Gràcia. Its undulating limestone façade, with wrought-iron balconies like seaweed, earned it the mocking name La Pedrera, 'the stone quarry'. Inside, it has no load-bearing walls: a structure of stone and brick columns and steel beams allows the apartments to be divided freely, around two courtyards that bring in light. The attic is roofed by catenary brick arches, and on the roof terrace chimneys and ventilation towers stand like helmeted warriors. Gaudí left before completion after disputes with the owners. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1984, it was restored by a savings bank foundation and is open to visitors.
