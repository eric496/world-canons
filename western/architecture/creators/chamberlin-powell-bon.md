# Chamberlin, Powell and Bon

**founded 1952** · British  
Styles: [Brutalism](../movements/brutalism.md)  

> London practice of Peter Chamberlin, Geoffry Powell and Christoph Bon, designers of the Golden Lane Estate and the Barbican Estate in the City of London.

## Biography

The three partners taught together at Kingston School of Art. In 1952 Powell won the competition for the Golden Lane Estate in the City of London, and they formed a partnership on the understanding that whoever won would share the work. From 1955 they planned the redevelopment of the bomb-damaged Barbican area as a residential neighbourhood, built from 1965 to 1976, with the Barbican Arts Centre opening in 1982. They also designed buildings for Leeds University and New Hall, Cambridge.

The Barbican combines three residential towers, long terrace blocks raised on columns, lakes and gardens, a school, a church and an arts centre in a single walkable complex with pedestrian walkways above the streets. Its bush-hammered concrete, vaulted roofs and romantic medieval echoes make it one of the most complete achievements of British Brutalism.

### Barbican Estate (1965–1976; arts centre 1982)

- **Date:** 1965–1976; arts centre 1982
- **Type:** Housing estate and arts centre
- **Location:** City of London, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Barbican%20Estate%20Chamberlin%2C%20Powell%20and%20Bon)

The Cripplegate area of the City of London was destroyed by bombing in 1940, and the City Corporation decided to rebuild it as a residential district for about 4,000 people. Chamberlin, Powell and Bon designed some 2,000 flats in three triangular towers, among the tallest residential buildings in London when completed, and long terrace blocks raised on columns around lakes, gardens and the surviving medieval church of St Giles Cripplegate, with the City of London School for Girls and the Guildhall School of Music and Drama. Pedestrian walkways separate people from traffic. The arts centre, opened by the Queen in 1982, contains concert hall, theatres, cinemas, a library and a conservatory. Its bush-hammered concrete was much disliked, but the estate was listed Grade II in 2001 and is now among the most sought-after addresses in London.
