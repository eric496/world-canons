# Richard Rogers

**1933 – 2021** · British (Italian-born)  
Born: Florence · Died: London  
Styles: [High-tech](../movements/high-tech.md)  

> British architect who with Renzo Piano designed the Centre Pompidou in Paris and later the Lloyd's building, leading figures of high-tech architecture.

## Biography

Rogers was born in Florence to an Anglo-Italian family that moved to England in 1939. He studied at the Architectural Association and at Yale, where he met Norman Foster, with whom he formed Team 4 in 1963. With Renzo Piano he won the competition for the Centre Pompidou in 1971. His later firm, now Rogers Stirk Harbour and Partners, designed the Lloyd's building (1986), the Millennium Dome, Terminal 4 of Madrid Barajas Airport and the Leadenhall Building. He sat in the House of Lords and championed compact, sustainable cities.

Rogers turned buildings inside out, placing structure, lifts, stairs and service ducts on the exterior to free flexible open floors and to give the city a lively, legible machine. He received the Pritzker Prize in 2007.

### Centre Pompidou (1971–1977)

- **Date:** 1971–1977
- **Type:** Arts centre
- **Location:** Beaubourg, Paris, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Centre%20Pompidou%20Richard%20Rogers)

President Georges Pompidou wanted a centre for modern art, books, music and design in the Beaubourg district of Paris, and Renzo Piano, Richard Rogers and Gianfranco Franchini won the international competition of 1971, with engineers from Ove Arup and Partners. They designed a flexible container of six column-free floors, 166 metres long, whose steel structure, cast steel brackets called gerberettes, bracing and services are placed outside: blue for air, green for water, yellow for electricity, red for circulation, with a glass-tube escalator climbing the façade over a sloping public square. Opened in 1977, it was likened to an oil refinery, but became one of the most visited cultural buildings in France and the founding work of high-tech architecture. It closed in 2025 for a renovation planned to last about five years.

### Lloyd's Building (1978–1986)

- **Date:** 1978–1986
- **Type:** Insurance market
- **Location:** City of London, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Lloyd%27s%20Building%20Richard%20Rogers)

The insurance market Lloyd's of London needed a building that could expand and adapt, and Richard Rogers designed one around a central atrium about 60 metres high under a barrel-vaulted glass roof, with the underwriting floors arranged as galleries connected by escalators. Lifts, staircases, toilets and service ducts are placed in six stainless-steel towers outside the main block, so that they can be replaced without disturbing the core, and the clear glass lift cars run on the exterior. The Adam Room, an eighteenth-century dining room, was reassembled inside. Completed in 1986, it became in 2011 the youngest building in England listed Grade I.
