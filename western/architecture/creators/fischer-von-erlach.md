## Biography

Fischer was the son of a sculptor in Graz and spent some sixteen years in Rome and Naples, where he knew Bernini's circle and studied ancient and modern buildings. Returning to Austria in the 1680s, he became architect to the Habsburg court, tutor in architecture to the future Emperor Joseph I, and was ennobled as von Erlach. He designed churches in Salzburg, including the Kollegienkirche, palaces in Vienna and Prague, the first plans for Schönbrunn, the Court Library in the Hofburg and, from 1716, the Karlskirche.

Fischer created an imperial Baroque that combined Roman grandeur with symbols of Habsburg power. In 1721 he published the Entwurff einer historischen Architectur, a survey in engraved plates of buildings from the Temple of Solomon and the wonders of the world to Egypt, China and the Islamic world, ending with his own works. It was the first attempt at a universal history of architecture and helped widen European taste.

## Key buildings

### Karlskirche (1716–1737)

[![Karlskirche](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/39/Karlskirche_Abendsonne_1.jpg/1280px-Karlskirche_Abendsonne_1.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Karlskirche_Abendsonne_1.jpg)

<sub>Karlskirche, Vienna, Austria Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Karlskirche_Abendsonne_1.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1716–1737
- **Type:** Votive church
- **Location:** Vienna, Austria
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Karlskirche%20Johann%20Bernhard%20Fischer%20von%20Erlach)

During the plague of 1713, Emperor Charles VI vowed a church to his patron saint, Charles Borromeo, and Fischer von Erlach won the competition to design it. After his death in 1723 his son Joseph Emanuel completed it, modifying the design. The façade combines a classical temple portico, an oval dome on a high drum and two giant columns modelled on Trajan's Column, wound with reliefs of the saint's life, flanked by low pavilions. The columns also recalled the Pillars of Hercules on the Habsburg emblem and the Temple of Solomon, so that the church brought together Rome, Jerusalem and imperial power. Inside, the oval dome is painted with frescoes by Johann Michael Rottmayr. The church, which faces Karlsplatz, is a symbol of Vienna.
