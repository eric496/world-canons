# Denys Lasdun

**1914 – 2001** · English  
Born: London · Died: London  
Styles: [Brutalism](../movements/brutalism.md)  

> English architect of the Royal National Theatre on London's South Bank and the University of East Anglia, leading works of British Brutalism.

## Biography

Lasdun studied at the Architectural Association, worked with Wells Coates and joined Berthold Lubetkin's Tecton group in the 1930s. After war service he built housing in Bethnal Green, the Royal College of Physicians in Regent's Park (1964), the University of East Anglia's ziggurat residences and teaching wall, and buildings for the University of London. The National Theatre, designed from 1963 and opened in 1976, was his most important work. He was knighted in 1976.

Lasdun thought of buildings as urban landscapes, with layered horizontal terraces and strata that make public space continuous with the city. The National Theatre's board-marked concrete divided opinion, famously drawing Prince Charles's criticism, but it was listed Grade II* in 1994 and is now widely admired.

## Key buildings

### Royal National Theatre (1967–1976)

- **Date:** 1967–1976
- **Type:** Theatre
- **Location:** South Bank, London, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Royal%20National%20Theatre%20Denys%20Lasdun)

The idea of a National Theatre had been discussed for a century before Lasdun was appointed in 1963, working with a committee including Laurence Olivier and Peter Brook. The building beside Waterloo Bridge contains three theatres: the large open-stage Olivier, inspired by the theatre of Epidaurus, the proscenium Lyttelton and the flexible Cottesloe, now the Dorfman. Outside, horizontal concrete terraces project at many levels like geological strata, extending the foyers into public balconies overlooking the Thames, while fly towers rise above. The concrete is board-marked, recording the grain of the timber formwork. Opened in stages in 1976–77, the building was at first much criticised; it was listed Grade II* in 1994 and has since been renovated.
