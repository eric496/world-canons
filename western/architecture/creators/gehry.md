# Frank Gehry

**1929 – 2025** · Canadian-American  
Born: Toronto · Died: Santa Monica, California  
Styles: [Deconstructivism & Digital Form](../movements/deconstructivism.md)  

> Canadian-American architect of the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao and the Walt Disney Concert Hall, whose sculptural, computer-designed forms changed the image of architecture.

## Biography

Frank Owen Goldberg moved from Toronto to Los Angeles in 1947, studied architecture at the University of Southern California and city planning at Harvard, and opened his own office in 1962. His renovation of his own house in Santa Monica (1978), wrapped in chain-link fencing and corrugated metal, made his name. He then designed the Vitra Design Museum in Germany, the Dancing House in Prague, the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao (1997), the Walt Disney Concert Hall (2003), the Fondation Louis Vuitton in Paris and many other buildings worldwide.

Gehry worked from crumpled paper and hand-made models, and his office adapted software from the aerospace industry to design and fabricate complex curved surfaces, a revolution in how buildings are made. The success of Bilbao in reviving its city gave rise to the term 'Bilbao effect' for iconic cultural buildings. He received the Pritzker Prize in 1989.

### Guggenheim Museum Bilbao (1993–1997)

[![Guggenheim Museum Bilbao](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Museo_Guggenheim%2C_Bilbao_%2831273245344%29.jpg/1280px-Museo_Guggenheim%2C_Bilbao_%2831273245344%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Museo_Guggenheim%2C_Bilbao_%2831273245344%29.jpg)

<sub>Guggenheim Museum Bilbao, Bilbao, Spain Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Museo_Guggenheim%2C_Bilbao_%2831273245344%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1993–1997
- **Type:** Art museum
- **Location:** Bilbao, Spain
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Guggenheim%20Museum%20Bilbao%20Frank%20Gehry)

The Basque government and the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation agreed in 1991 to build a museum of modern and contemporary art as part of the regeneration of Bilbao, a declining industrial port. Frank Gehry's design, on a former dockland site by the river and under a road bridge, is a cluster of curved volumes clad in some 33,000 thin titanium panels, with walls of limestone and glass, around a tall atrium. Its complex forms were designed and fabricated using CATIA, software developed for aircraft design. The galleries range from classical rooms to a huge boat-shaped hall for large-scale works such as Richard Serra's The Matter of Time. After it opened in 1997 visitors flooded into Bilbao, and the term 'Bilbao effect' came to describe the hope that a spectacular building can regenerate a city.

### Walt Disney Concert Hall (1999–2003)

[![Walt Disney Concert Hall](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8c/Walt_Disney_Concert_Hall%2C_LA%2C_CA%2C_jjron_22.03.2012.jpg/1280px-Walt_Disney_Concert_Hall%2C_LA%2C_CA%2C_jjron_22.03.2012.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Walt_Disney_Concert_Hall%2C_LA%2C_CA%2C_jjron_22.03.2012.jpg)

<sub>Walt Disney Concert Hall, Los Angeles, California, United States Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Walt_Disney_Concert_Hall%2C_LA%2C_CA%2C_jjron_22.03.2012.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1999–2003
- **Type:** Concert hall
- **Location:** Los Angeles, California, United States
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Walt%20Disney%20Concert%20Hall%20Frank%20Gehry)

Lillian Disney, widow of Walt Disney, gave an initial gift of 50 million dollars in 1987 for a concert hall in downtown Los Angeles, and Gehry won the competition in 1988. The project stalled for years over funding, and the design of the exterior changed from stone to stainless steel after Bilbao. Its curving, sail-like panels enclose foyers and gardens around a vineyard-style auditorium in which the audience surrounds the orchestra, lined with Douglas fir and designed with the acoustician Yasuhisa Toyota, with an organ of curved pipes. It opened in 2003 as the home of the Los Angeles Philharmonic. Glare from polished panels heated neighbouring buildings and they were dulled. It became a symbol of downtown Los Angeles.
