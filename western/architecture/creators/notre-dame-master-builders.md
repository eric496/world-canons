# Master builders of Notre-Dame

**active 1163–c. 1345** · French  
Styles: [Gothic](../movements/gothic.md)  

> The successive masters who built Notre-Dame de Paris over nearly two centuries, among them Jean de Chelles and Pierre de Montreuil, who designed the transept façades.

## Biography

Bishop Maurice de Sully began the new cathedral of Paris in 1163, and its choir, nave and west front rose in stages over the following eighty years under masters whose names are unrecorded. From the mid-thirteenth century, named architects appear: Jean de Chelles, whose name is carved on the south transept, began the transept façades in the 1250s, and Pierre de Montreuil continued them. Later masters including Jean Ravy completed the choir chapels and screen in the fourteenth century.

Notre-Dame began as an early Gothic building with sexpartite vaults and galleries, and was modernised as it rose, gaining huge rose windows and flying buttresses among the earliest of their kind on a great scale. Its history is a reminder that Gothic cathedrals were collective, generational works. Nineteenth-century restoration by Viollet-le-Duc and the rebuilding after the fire of 2019 continue that long history.

### Notre-Dame de Paris (1163–c. 1345)

- **Date:** 1163–c. 1345
- **Type:** Gothic cathedral
- **Location:** Île de la Cité, Paris, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Notre-Dame%20de%20Paris%20Master%20builders%20of%20Notre-Dame)

Bishop Maurice de Sully began Notre-Dame in 1163, and the choir, nave and west façade were built over the next eighty years. The early design, with galleries over the aisles and six-part vaults, was enlarged in the thirteenth century with taller clerestory windows, flying buttresses among the earliest of their kind, and the great rose windows of the transepts by Jean de Chelles and Pierre de Montreuil. The west front, with its three sculpted portals, gallery of kings and twin towers, is a model of balance. Damaged in the Revolution and neglected, the cathedral was made famous anew by Victor Hugo's novel of 1831 and restored by Lassus and Viollet-le-Duc, who added the spire. On 15 April 2019 a fire destroyed the roof and spire; after five years of reconstruction, the cathedral reopened in December 2024.
