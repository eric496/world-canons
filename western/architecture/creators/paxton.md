# Joseph Paxton

**1803 – 1865** · English  
Born: Milton Bryan, Bedfordshire · Died: Sydenham, London  
Styles: [Iron, Glass & Engineering](../movements/iron-and-glass.md)  

> Gardener and glasshouse designer who created the Crystal Palace for the Great Exhibition of 1851, a landmark of prefabricated iron and glass building.

## Biography

The son of a farmer, Paxton became head gardener to the Duke of Devonshire at Chatsworth in 1826, aged twenty-three. There he designed glasshouses of increasing ambition, including the Great Conservatory and the lily house built to make the giant Amazonian water lily Victoria regia flower, whose ribbed leaf he said inspired its structure. In 1850, when the committee for the Great Exhibition could not agree on a building, he sketched a vast glasshouse on blotting paper, and it was built in Hyde Park in about nine months. Knighted, he became a railway director, a member of Parliament and a designer of parks and houses.

The Crystal Palace was assembled from standardised iron columns, girders and panes of glass manufactured in quantity and fixed on site, a radical system that made speed and scale possible. It was moved to Sydenham after the exhibition and burned in 1936. Paxton's achievement showed that a building could be a kind of machine, a lesson for engineers and, later, for modernist and high-tech architects.

## Key buildings

### The Crystal Palace (1850–1851)

[![The Crystal Palace](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d8/Crystal_Palace_General_view_from_Water_Temple.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Crystal_Palace_General_view_from_Water_Temple.jpg)

<sub>The Crystal Palace, Hyde Park, London; moved to Sydenham, destroyed 1936 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Crystal_Palace_General_view_from_Water_Temple.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1850–1851
- **Type:** Exhibition hall (destroyed)
- **Location:** Hyde Park, London; moved to Sydenham, destroyed 1936
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Crystal%20Palace%20Joseph%20Paxton)

The Great Exhibition of the Works of Industry of All Nations, promoted by Prince Albert, needed a building that could be erected quickly and removed afterwards. Joseph Paxton's design, based on his glasshouses, was accepted in July 1850, and the contractors Fox, Henderson and Co. built it in Hyde Park in about nine months. It was about 563 metres long, made of standardised cast-iron columns and girders, timber and some 300,000 panes of glass, assembled from parts mass-produced off site, with a vaulted transept tall enough to enclose existing elm trees. Six million visitors came to see over 100,000 exhibits. The magazine Punch called it the Crystal Palace. It was rebuilt in enlarged form at Sydenham Hill in south London, where it served as a place of entertainment until it burned down in 1936.
