# Andrea Palladio

**1508 – 1580** · Venetian  
Born: Padua · Died: Maser or Vicenza, Republic of Venice  
Styles: [Renaissance](../movements/renaissance-architecture.md), [Palladianism & Neoclassicism](../movements/neoclassicism.md)  

> Architect of villas, palaces and churches in the Veneto whose Four Books of Architecture made him the most imitated architect in Western history.

## Biography

Andrea di Pietro della Gondola was the son of a miller and trained as a stonemason. In Vicenza the humanist Gian Giorgio Trissino took him up, educated him, took him to Rome to study ancient buildings, and gave him the classical name Palladio. From the 1540s he designed palaces in Vicenza and the Basilica's new loggias, and villas for Venetian landowners who were turning to agriculture on the mainland. In his later years he built the churches of San Giorgio Maggiore and Il Redentore in Venice and began the Teatro Olimpico in Vicenza, completed after his death.

Palladio combined careful study of Roman temples, baths and houses with practical sense and clear proportional systems. His villas, with temple-fronted porticoes on working farmhouses, his church façades interlocking two temple fronts, and his rooms sized by harmonic ratios became a universal model. I quattro libri dell'architettura (1570), illustrated with woodcuts of his own designs, carried his ideas to Britain, Ireland, North America and Russia, where Palladianism shaped country houses and public buildings for two centuries.

### Basilica Palladiana (1549–1614)

[![Basilica Palladiana](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Basilica_Palladiana_%28Vicenza%29_-_facade_on_Piazza_dei_signori.jpg/1280px-Basilica_Palladiana_%28Vicenza%29_-_facade_on_Piazza_dei_signori.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Basilica_Palladiana_%28Vicenza%29_-_facade_on_Piazza_dei_signori.jpg)

<sub>Basilica Palladiana, Piazza dei Signori, Vicenza, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Basilica_Palladiana_%28Vicenza%29_-_facade_on_Piazza_dei_signori.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1549–1614
- **Type:** Civic hall loggias
- **Location:** Piazza dei Signori, Vicenza, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Basilica%20Palladiana%20Andrea%20Palladio)

The Palazzo della Ragione, Vicenza's fifteenth-century town hall, had a vast hall on the upper floor covered by a keel-shaped roof, but its surrounding loggias collapsed in 1496. After several architects had been consulted, the young Palladio won the commission in 1549 to rebuild them. He wrapped the irregular medieval building in two storeys of arcades, Doric below and Ionic above, using the motif of an arch flanked by two narrow openings under a lintel, ever since called the Palladian or Serlian window. By varying the width of the small side openings, he absorbed the irregular spacing of the old building while keeping the arches uniform. Palladio called the building a basilica after the Roman halls of justice. It was completed after his death and is the centrepiece of Vicenza.

### Villa La Rotonda (1566–c. 1591)

[![Villa La Rotonda](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d8/Larotonda2009.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Larotonda2009.JPG)

<sub>Villa La Rotonda, Vicenza, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Larotonda2009.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1566–c. 1591
- **Type:** Villa
- **Location:** Vicenza, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Villa%20La%20Rotonda%20Andrea%20Palladio)

Paolo Almerico, a retired cleric of the papal court, asked Palladio for a residence on a hilltop just outside Vicenza. Palladio designed a perfectly symmetrical square block with an identical Ionic temple portico on each side, each with a broad flight of steps, around a central round hall under a dome, so that every side commands a view of the surrounding countryside. He described it in his Four Books not as a farm villa but as a suburban house for pleasure, set like a theatre in the landscape. After Palladio's death Vincenzo Scamozzi completed it with a lower dome than first planned. The Rotonda was copied at Chiswick House and Mereworth Castle in England and at Monticello and many other houses in America. It is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site of Vicenza and the Palladian villas.

### Il Redentore (1577–1592)

[![Il Redentore](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ae/Chiesa_del_Santissimo_Redentore%2C_Venice%2C_Italy.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chiesa_del_Santissimo_Redentore%2C_Venice%2C_Italy.jpg)

<sub>Il Redentore, Giudecca, Venice, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chiesa_del_Santissimo_Redentore%2C_Venice%2C_Italy.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1577–1592
- **Type:** Votive church
- **Location:** Giudecca, Venice, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Il%20Redentore%20Andrea%20Palladio)

During a plague that killed perhaps a third of Venice's population, the Senate vowed in 1576 to build a church dedicated to Christ the Redeemer, and Palladio was chosen to design it on the island of Giudecca, run by Capuchin friars. Its white façade, facing the city across the Giudecca canal, interlocks a tall temple front with a lower, wider one, a solution for adapting a temple front to a church with a high nave and lower side chapels. Inside, a single nave with side chapels leads to a domed crossing and a choir screened by a curved row of columns, with the friars' choir beyond, reached through clear spatial stages. Every July, during the Festa del Redentore, a pontoon bridge is built across the canal so that Venetians can walk to the church, as the doge once did.
