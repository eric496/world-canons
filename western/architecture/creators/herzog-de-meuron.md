# Herzog & de Meuron

**founded 1978** · Swiss  
Born: Basel · Died: ?  
Styles: [Contemporary](../movements/contemporary-architecture.md)  

> Basel practice of Jacques Herzog and Pierre de Meuron, known for Tate Modern, the Beijing National Stadium and the Elbphilharmonie in Hamburg.

## Biography

Jacques Herzog and Pierre de Meuron, both born in Basel in 1950, studied together at the ETH in Zurich under Aldo Rossi among others and founded their practice in Basel in 1978. Early works such as the Signal Box in Basel and the Ricola storage building attracted attention for their surfaces. The conversion of Bankside Power Station into Tate Modern (2000) brought international fame, followed by the de Young Museum in San Francisco, the Allianz Arena in Munich, the Beijing National Stadium with Ai Weiwei (2008), the Elbphilharmonie (2017) and M+ in Hong Kong.

Herzog & de Meuron have no signature style; each project begins with experiments in material, from printed glass and copper bands to concrete and stone, and they often collaborate with artists. They received the Pritzker Prize in 2001.

### Tate Modern (1995–2000; extension 2016)

[![Tate Modern](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/Tate_Modern_-_Bankside_Power_Station.jpg/1280px-Tate_Modern_-_Bankside_Power_Station.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tate_Modern_-_Bankside_Power_Station.jpg)

<sub>Tate Modern, Bankside, London, England Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tate_Modern_-_Bankside_Power_Station.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1995–2000; extension 2016
- **Type:** Art museum in a former power station
- **Location:** Bankside, London, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Tate%20Modern%20Herzog%20%26%20de%20Meuron)

Bankside Power Station, designed by Giles Gilbert Scott and built in stages from 1947 to 1963, closed in 1981. The Tate chose it for a new museum of modern and contemporary art, and Herzog & de Meuron won the competition in 1995 with a scheme that preserved the brick building and its tall chimney. They turned the former Turbine Hall into a vast covered street and space for commissioned installations, and added a two-storey light beam of glass along the roof. Tate Modern opened in 2000, received many more visitors than expected, and helped transform the South Bank. The architects added the Blavatnik Building, a twisted pyramid of perforated brick, in 2016. It is among the most visited museums of modern art in the world.

### Elbphilharmonie (2007–2016; opened 2017)

[![Elbphilharmonie](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Elbphilharmonie_2025.jpg/1280px-Elbphilharmonie_2025.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Elbphilharmonie_2025.jpg)

<sub>Elbphilharmonie, HafenCity, Hamburg, Germany Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Elbphilharmonie_2025.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 2007–2016; opened 2017
- **Type:** Concert hall
- **Location:** HafenCity, Hamburg, Germany
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Elbphilharmonie%20Herzog%20%26%20de%20Meuron)

The Elbphilharmonie stands on the tip of a peninsula in Hamburg's harbour, on top of the Kaispeicher A, a brick warehouse of the 1960s for cocoa, tea and tobacco. Herzog & de Meuron raised a glass structure on it whose roof rises and falls like waves, made of curved panes partly printed with dots. Between the old warehouse and the new building, a public plaza 37 metres above the ground gives views over the city and harbour. Inside are a hotel, apartments and three concert halls; the great hall, with seats rising in vineyard terraces around the stage, is lined with 10,000 gypsum panels individually milled to scatter sound, designed with the acoustician Yasuhisa Toyota. Costs rose from an estimated 77 million to about 789 million euros and the opening was delayed by years, but since 2017 it has become a symbol of Hamburg.
