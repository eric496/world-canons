# Claude-Nicolas Ledoux

**1736 – 1806** · French  
Born: Dormans, Champagne · Died: Paris  
Styles: [Palladianism & Neoclassicism](../movements/neoclassicism.md)  

> Visionary French Neoclassical architect of the Royal Saltworks at Arc-et-Senans and the Paris toll gates, whose ideal city anticipated modern planning.

## Biography

Ledoux trained in Paris under Jacques-François Blondel and built fashionable town houses and the pavilion of Louveciennes for Madame du Barry. As inspector of saltworks he designed the Royal Saltworks at Arc-et-Senans (1775–79), and he built a theatre at Besançon. In the 1780s he designed some fifty toll houses for the hated customs wall around Paris, of which four survive, including the Rotonde de la Villette. His royal connections led to his imprisonment during the Terror, and he spent his last years preparing his book L'Architecture considérée sous le rapport de l'art, des moeurs et de la législation (1804).

Ledoux reduced architecture to elemental geometry, cubes, cylinders, pyramids and heavy rusticated columns, and believed buildings should express their purpose and shape society. His book expanded the saltworks into an ideal city, Chaux, with houses for workers and symbolic public buildings. Long dismissed as a megalomaniac, he was rediscovered in the twentieth century as a precursor of modernism.

## Key buildings

### Royal Saltworks at Arc-et-Senans (1775–1779)

[![Royal Saltworks at Arc-et-Senans](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/27/France_arc_et_senas_saline_royal_main_building_1.jpg/1280px-France_arc_et_senas_saline_royal_main_building_1.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:France_arc_et_senas_saline_royal_main_building_1.jpg)

<sub>Royal Saltworks at Arc-et-Senans, Arc-et-Senans, Doubs, France Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:France_arc_et_senas_saline_royal_main_building_1.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1775–1779
- **Type:** Industrial complex
- **Location:** Arc-et-Senans, Doubs, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Royal%20Saltworks%20at%20Arc-et-Senans%20Claude-Nicolas%20Ledoux)

Salt was a royal monopoly and a valuable source of tax. Ledoux, as inspector of saltworks in Franche-Comté, designed a new works near the forest of Chaux, which supplied wood to fire the brine evaporation pans; the brine was piped from Salins. Buildings are arranged on a semicircle: the director's house at the centre, with a temple portico of columns banded with stone drums and a chapel, flanked by the two salt-boiling halls, and around the arc the houses of workers, coopers and blacksmiths, with gardens behind. The gatehouse is a grotto-like portico of heavy Doric columns, with carved urns spilling petrified salt water. The works operated until 1895. Ledoux later imagined it as the centre of an ideal city. Restored after the 1920s, it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
