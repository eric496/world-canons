# Zaha Hadid

**1950 – 2016** · Iraqi-British  
Born: Baghdad · Died: Miami, Florida  
Styles: [Deconstructivism & Digital Form](../movements/deconstructivism.md), [Contemporary](../movements/contemporary-architecture.md)  

> Iraqi-British architect, the first woman to win the Pritzker Prize, whose dynamic, flowing buildings range from the Vitra Fire Station to the Heydar Aliyev Center.

## Biography

Hadid studied mathematics at the American University of Beirut and architecture at the Architectural Association in London, where she worked with Rem Koolhaas. For years her explosive, painted competition designs, such as The Peak in Hong Kong (1983), were celebrated but unbuilt. Her first major building was the Vitra Fire Station (1993), followed by the Rosenthal Center in Cincinnati, the Phaeno Science Centre, MAXXI in Rome, the London Aquatics Centre for the 2012 Olympics, the Guangzhou Opera House and the Heydar Aliyev Center in Baku. She won the Pritzker Prize in 2004 and died suddenly at sixty-five.

Influenced by the Russian avant-garde, especially Malevich, Hadid began with fragmented, angular forms and moved, with her partner Patrik Schumacher and digital tools, toward continuous, fluid surfaces. Her success as a woman in a male-dominated profession made her a role model, while some commissions for authoritarian states drew criticism.

### Vitra Fire Station (1990–1993)

- **Date:** 1990–1993
- **Type:** Fire station, later exhibition space
- **Location:** Vitra Campus, Weil am Rhein, Germany
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Vitra%20Fire%20Station%20Zaha%20Hadid)

After a fire in 1981 destroyed much of the Vitra furniture company's factory at Weil am Rhein, near Basel, its chairman Rolf Fehlbaum began commissioning buildings from leading architects for the site. Zaha Hadid, then known mainly for her paintings and unbuilt projects, was asked to design a small fire station for the company's own fire brigade. She created a long, narrow building of exposed concrete planes that slant, tilt and slice past each other, like a frozen explosion of movement, with a roof canopy projecting sharply over the entrance. When local fire brigades took over, the building became a space for displaying Vitra's chair collection and events. It established Hadid's reputation as a builder.

### Heydar Aliyev Center (2007–2012)

- **Date:** 2007–2012
- **Type:** Cultural centre
- **Location:** Baku, Azerbaijan
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Heydar%20Aliyev%20Center%20Zaha%20Hadid)

Named after the former president of Azerbaijan and father of his successor, the centre was commissioned by the government as a symbol of the modernising, oil-rich state. Zaha Hadid Architects designed a building in which the landscaped plaza seems to rise, fold and curve into a continuous white surface that becomes walls and roof, without a visible break, over a conference hall, a museum and galleries. A space frame of steel supports the cladding of glass-fibre reinforced panels, whose forms were developed digitally. The building won the Design Museum's Design of the Year award in 2014. It also drew criticism over the commissioning government's record on human rights and the evictions associated with the site.
