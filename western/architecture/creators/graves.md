# Michael Graves

**1934 – 2015** · American  
Born: Indianapolis, Indiana · Died: Princeton, New Jersey  
Styles: [Postmodernism](../movements/postmodernism.md)  

> American architect and designer whose Portland Building of 1982 became the emblem of postmodern architecture.

## Biography

Graves studied at the University of Cincinnati and Harvard, won the Rome Prize and taught at Princeton for nearly forty years. He first gained notice as one of the 'New York Five', architects of white neo-Corbusian houses, before turning to colourful, abstracted classicism with the Portland Building (1982) and the Humana Building in Louisville. He designed hotels and offices for Disney and, through Alessi and Target, kettles and household products that brought design to a mass market. Paralysed by an illness in 2003, he devoted his late work to healthcare design.

Graves's architecture uses simplified columns, keystones and garlands in pastel colours, treating the façade as a symbolic picture. His work was both popular and derided, and the Portland Building became a test case for whether postmodern buildings should be preserved.

## Key buildings

### Portland Building (1980–1982)

[![Portland Building](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/76/Portland_Building_1982.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Portland_Building_1982.jpg)

<sub>Portland Building, Portland, Oregon, United States Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Portland_Building_1982.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1980–1982
- **Type:** Municipal office building
- **Location:** Portland, Oregon, United States
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Portland%20Building%20Michael%20Graves)

The city of Portland held a design competition for a fifteen-storey municipal office building on a modest budget, judged with Philip Johnson as adviser, and Michael Graves won. On a square block he painted and applied a giant classical vocabulary: huge tapering pilasters on the façades, a stylised keystone, garlands, and colours of terracotta, blue and cream, with small square windows. Above the entrance stands Portlandia, a large copper statue by Raymond Kaskey. The building was celebrated as a turning point away from modernism and criticised for its cramped, dark interiors and leaking walls. It was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2011 and was extensively rebuilt between 2017 and 2020, preserving its appearance.
