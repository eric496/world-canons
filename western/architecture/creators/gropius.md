# Walter Gropius

**1883 – 1969** · German-American  
Born: Berlin · Died: Boston, Massachusetts  
Styles: [Modernism & the International Style](../movements/modernism.md)  

> Founder of the Bauhaus school and designer of the Fagus Factory and the Bauhaus building at Dessau, central figures of modern architecture and design.

## Biography

Gropius worked for Peter Behrens in Berlin and in 1911, with Adolf Meyer, designed the Fagus shoe-last factory at Alfeld with glass walls wrapped around the corners. After serving in the First World War, he founded the Bauhaus at Weimar in 1919, bringing together art, craft and later industrial design, and moved it to Dessau in 1925 into buildings of his own design. He left the school in 1928, emigrated to England in 1934 and in 1937 to the United States, where he headed the architecture department at Harvard and founded The Architects Collaborative.

Gropius saw architecture as a collective, rational discipline uniting the arts with industrial production and social housing. The Bauhaus, with teachers such as Kandinsky, Klee, Moholy-Nagy and Breuer, became the most influential design school of the century, and its building at Dessau a manifesto of the new architecture. At Harvard he trained a generation of American architects in modernist principles.

### Fagus Factory (1911–1925)

- **Date:** 1911–1925
- **Type:** Factory
- **Location:** Alfeld, Lower Saxony, Germany
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Fagus%20Factory%20Walter%20Gropius)

Carl Benscheidt, a manufacturer of shoe lasts, commissioned Walter Gropius and Adolf Meyer in 1911 to redesign the façade of his new factory, whose plan had already been drawn by another architect. Gropius and Meyer gave the main building walls of glass and steel set in front of the brick piers, and wrapped the glass around the corners without corner supports, so that the walls seem weightless. The building expressed a modern, light and healthy workplace, and its image was meant to advertise the company. Further buildings were added until 1925, including interiors in the early Bauhaus style. The factory is still in operation, making shoe lasts, and was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2011.

### Bauhaus Building, Dessau (1925–1926)

- **Date:** 1925–1926
- **Type:** Art and design school
- **Location:** Dessau, Saxony-Anhalt, Germany
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Bauhaus%20Building%2C%20Dessau%20Walter%20Gropius)

When the Bauhaus left Weimar under political pressure in 1925, the industrial city of Dessau offered it a new home, and Gropius designed its building. It is a pinwheel of separate wings for separate functions: a workshop block with a continuous curtain wall of glass, a teaching wing, a bridge over the road housing the administration, an assembly hall and canteen, and a five-storey block of studio flats for students with small balconies. Its asymmetrical composition can only be grasped by walking around it, and the school's workshops made its lighting, furniture and lettering. The Nazis closed the Dessau Bauhaus in 1932. Damaged in the war and altered, the building was restored and is used again by the Bauhaus Dessau Foundation. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
