# Oscar Niemeyer

**1907 – 2012** · Brazilian  
Born: Rio de Janeiro · Died: Rio de Janeiro  
Styles: [Late & Expressive Modernism](../movements/late-modernism.md)  

> Brazilian architect whose sensuous concrete curves, from Pampulha to the public buildings of Brasília, gave modernism a new freedom.

## Biography

Niemeyer studied at the National School of Fine Arts in Rio and worked under Lúcio Costa on the Ministry of Education and Health building (1936–43), for which Le Corbusier was consultant. For Juscelino Kubitschek, then mayor of Belo Horizonte, he designed the Pampulha complex (1940–43), including the church of São Francisco de Assis. When Kubitschek became president, Niemeyer designed the principal buildings of the new capital Brasília, laid out by Costa and inaugurated in 1960. A lifelong communist, he worked in exile in France and Algeria during the military dictatorship, and kept designing until his death aged 104.

Niemeyer rejected the right angle in favour of free curves inspired, he said, by the mountains, rivers and bodies of Brazil. He exploited reinforced concrete for thin shells, domes and slender columns that make heavy buildings appear weightless. He shared the Pritzker Prize in 1988 with Gordon Bunshaft.

### Cathedral of Brasília (1958–1970)

[![Cathedral of Brasília](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/Catedral_Metropolitana_de_Brasilia.jpg/1280px-Catedral_Metropolitana_de_Brasilia.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Catedral_Metropolitana_de_Brasilia.jpg)

<sub>Cathedral of Brasília, Brasília, Brazil Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Catedral_Metropolitana_de_Brasilia.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1958–1970
- **Type:** Cathedral
- **Location:** Brasília, Brazil
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Cathedral%20of%20Bras%C3%ADlia%20Oscar%20Niemeyer)

The Metropolitan Cathedral was one of the first buildings of Brazil's new capital, laid out by Lúcio Costa, and Niemeyer designed it as a hyperboloid of sixteen curving concrete columns, each weighing many tonnes, which meet at a ring and open upward like hands raised to heaven or a crown of thorns. The spaces between them are glazed, and since 1987–90 the glass has been decorated with blue, green and white stained glass by Marianne Peretti. Visitors enter through a dark passage that descends below ground and emerge into the bright circular nave, a contrast intended to lift the spirit. Four bronze evangelists by Alfredo Ceschiatti stand outside, and angels hang from steel cables inside. Its structure was completed in 1960 and it was dedicated in 1970. Brasília is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### National Congress of Brazil (1958–1960)

[![National Congress of Brazil](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/93/National_Congress_of_Brazil.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:National_Congress_of_Brazil.jpg)

<sub>National Congress of Brazil, Brasília, Brazil Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:National_Congress_of_Brazil.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1958–1960
- **Type:** Parliament building
- **Location:** Brasília, Brazil
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=National%20Congress%20of%20Brazil%20Oscar%20Niemeyer)

At the end of the Monumental Axis of Brasília, in the Square of the Three Powers, Niemeyer designed the National Congress as a horizontal platform carrying two contrasting bowls: an upturned dome over the Senate and an open, upturned saucer over the larger Chamber of Deputies, expressing the more reflective and the more open houses of parliament. Between them rise twin 28-storey office towers joined by a bridge, forming an H that has been read as standing for humanity. The building was inaugurated with the city on 21 April 1960. Its clean geometry against the sky became the symbol of Brazilian modernism and of Kubitschek's developmental optimism, and its ramps and roof have been the scene of political demonstrations, including the storming of 8 January 2023.
