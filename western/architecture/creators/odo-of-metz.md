# Odo of Metz

**active c. 790–805** · Frankish  
Styles: [Carolingian & Romanesque](../movements/romanesque.md)  

> Frankish architect traditionally credited with Charlemagne's Palatine Chapel at Aachen, one of the earliest surviving monuments of medieval northern Europe.

## Biography

Odo of Metz is known only from a single line of verse, a dedicatory inscription recorded in the Middle Ages, which names him as the builder of the chapel of Charlemagne's palace at Aachen. Nothing else is known of his life, and some historians are cautious about the attribution. He presumably worked within the circle of scholars and craftsmen gathered at Charlemagne's court around 790 to 805.

The chapel attributed to him is an octagon surrounded by a sixteen-sided ambulatory and gallery, built of stone and covered with a domed vault. It draws on San Vitale in Ravenna and on Roman imperial buildings, and Charlemagne brought columns and marbles from Rome and Ravenna for it. Odo's achievement was to revive large-scale vaulted masonry in the north, giving visible form to the Carolingian idea of a renewed Roman Empire.

## Key buildings

### Palatine Chapel, Aachen (c. 796–805)

[![Palatine Chapel, Aachen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e2/Aachener_Dom_Oktogon.jpg/960px-Aachener_Dom_Oktogon.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aachener_Dom_Oktogon.jpg)

<sub>Palatine Chapel, Aachen, Aachen, Germany Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aachener_Dom_Oktogon.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 796–805
- **Type:** Palace chapel
- **Location:** Aachen, Germany
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Palatine%20Chapel%2C%20Aachen%20Odo%20of%20Metz)

Charlemagne built his palace at Aachen in the 790s, and its chapel, consecrated by Pope Leo III according to tradition in 805, survives as the core of Aachen Cathedral. Attributed to Odo of Metz, it is an octagon of stone covered by a domed vault, surrounded by a sixteen-sided ambulatory and a gallery where the emperor's throne still stands. The design draws on San Vitale in Ravenna but is heavier and more geometric, and Charlemagne brought ancient columns and marble from Rome and Ravenna. Charlemagne was buried there in 814, and for six centuries German kings were crowned on his throne. A Gothic glass choir was added in the fourteenth century. The cathedral was among the first sites inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1978.
