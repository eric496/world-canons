# Norman Foster

**1935** · British  
Born: Reddish, Stockport · Died: ?  
Styles: [High-tech](../movements/high-tech.md), [Contemporary](../movements/contemporary-architecture.md)  

> British architect whose firm designed the HSBC headquarters in Hong Kong, the glass dome of the Reichstag and 30 St Mary Axe, leading high-tech and sustainable design.

## Biography

Foster grew up in a working-class family near Manchester, studied at the University of Manchester and at Yale, and founded Team 4 with Richard Rogers before setting up Foster Associates in 1967. Early works such as the Willis Faber and Dumas building in Ipswich and the Sainsbury Centre for Visual Arts were followed by the Hongkong and Shanghai Bank headquarters (1986), Stansted Airport, the Reichstag dome in Berlin (1999), the Great Court of the British Museum, the Millau Viaduct with the engineer Michel Virlogeux, and 30 St Mary Axe in London (2004). Foster and Partners became one of the largest architecture firms in the world.

Foster's buildings emphasise lightweight structure, prefabrication, daylight and natural ventilation, and his practice has made energy performance a central design concern. He received the Pritzker Prize in 1999 and a life peerage.

### HSBC Main Building, Hong Kong (1979–1986)

[![HSBC Main Building, Hong Kong](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/38/HK_HSBC_Main_Building_2008.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:HK_HSBC_Main_Building_2008.jpg)

<sub>HSBC Main Building, Hong Kong, Central, Hong Kong Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:HK_HSBC_Main_Building_2008.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1979–1986
- **Type:** Bank headquarters
- **Location:** Central, Hong Kong
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=HSBC%20Main%20Building%2C%20Hong%20Kong%20Norman%20Foster)

The Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation commissioned Norman Foster to rebuild its headquarters on Statue Square, and it was among the most expensive buildings in the world when completed. Instead of a central core, the floors hang from five sets of steel suspension trusses carried by eight masts, so that the ground floor is an open public plaza through which pedestrians pass. Escalators rise from the plaza into a tall atrium, lit by a 'sunscoop' of mirrors that reflects daylight down into it. Prefabricated parts were made around the world, and the services are in modules placed along the sides. Feng shui advisers were consulted on the design.

### Reichstag Dome (1992–1999)

[![Reichstag Dome](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Reichstag_dome_interior_%288423297236%29.jpg/1280px-Reichstag_dome_interior_%288423297236%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Reichstag_dome_interior_%288423297236%29.jpg)

<sub>Inside the Reichstag dome, with its spiral ramps and mirrored cone Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Reichstag_dome_interior_%288423297236%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1992–1999
- **Type:** Parliament building renovation
- **Location:** Berlin, Germany
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Reichstag%20Dome%20Norman%20Foster)

After German reunification, the Bundestag decided to move back to the Reichstag building, completed in 1894 to a design by Paul Wallot, burned in 1933 and badly damaged in 1945. Foster and Partners won the competition to rebuild it, and the scheme was preceded by Christo and Jeanne-Claude's wrapping of the building in 1995. Inside the gutted shell, Foster created a new plenary chamber and offices, and crowned it with a glass dome, with two spiral ramps on which visitors can walk above the legislators, a symbol of transparency and democratic accountability. A mirrored cone at the centre reflects daylight into the chamber and draws out exhaust air. Graffiti by Soviet soldiers from 1945 were preserved in the interiors. It opened in 1999.

### 30 St Mary Axe (The Gherkin) (2001–2004)

[![30 St Mary Axe (The Gherkin)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8e/30_St_Mary_Axe%2C_%27Gherkin%27.JPG/960px-30_St_Mary_Axe%2C_%27Gherkin%27.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:30_St_Mary_Axe%2C_%27Gherkin%27.JPG)

<sub>30 St Mary Axe (The Gherkin), City of London, England Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:30_St_Mary_Axe%2C_%27Gherkin%27.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 2001–2004
- **Type:** Office tower
- **Location:** City of London, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=30%20St%20Mary%20Axe%20%28The%20Gherkin%29%20Norman%20Foster)

The tower stands on the site of the Baltic Exchange, damaged by an IRA bomb in 1992. Commissioned by the reinsurance company Swiss Re, Foster and Partners designed a 41-storey tower, 180 metres high, whose circular plan swells in the middle and narrows to the top. Its diagonally braced steel structure, the diagrid, sits on the perimeter, freeing the floors of columns, and spiral light wells formed by rotating each floor plan draw air and daylight through the building, reducing energy use. Only one piece of curved glass, the lens at the summit, is used; the rest of the curve is made of flat panes. It won the RIBA Stirling Prize in 2004 and became a symbol of the City of London.
