## Biography

Peter Parler came from a family of masons; his father Heinrich worked on the Church of the Holy Cross at Schwäbisch Gmünd, one of the first great German hall churches. In 1356, aged about twenty-three, Peter was summoned to Prague by Emperor Charles IV to continue the new cathedral of St Vitus after the death of its first architect, Matthias of Arras. He also built the Charles Bridge across the Vltava and its Old Town tower, and the Parler workshop produced sculpture and buildings throughout Bohemia.

Parler was among the most inventive architects of the late Middle Ages. His net vaults with their parallel ribs, his hanging bosses and his playful tracery anticipated the virtuoso late Gothic of Central Europe. The portrait busts of the triforium at St Vitus, which include one of Parler himself, are among the earliest realistic portraits of the period. The Parler family's work spread through relatives and pupils to Vienna, Ulm, Freiburg and beyond.

## Key buildings

### St Vitus Cathedral (1344–1929)

[![St Vitus Cathedral](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/PragueCathedral03.jpg/1280px-PragueCathedral03.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:PragueCathedral03.jpg)

<sub>St Vitus Cathedral, Prague Castle, Prague, Czech Republic Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:PragueCathedral03.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1344–1929
- **Type:** Gothic cathedral
- **Location:** Prague Castle, Prague, Czech Republic
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=St%20Vitus%20Cathedral%20Peter%20Parler)

When Prague became an archbishopric in 1344, the future Emperor Charles IV began a new cathedral within Prague Castle, first under Matthias of Arras, who built a French-style choir, and from 1356 under Peter Parler. Parler and his sons built the upper choir with its innovative net vault, the chapel of Saint Wenceslas lined with semi-precious stones and wall paintings, the Golden Gate with its mosaic of the Last Judgement, and the base of the great tower. The triforium contains busts of the royal family, archbishops and the architects themselves. The Hussite wars stopped work, and the nave and west front were completed only between 1873 and 1929 in a Gothic Revival style. It holds the tombs of Bohemian kings and the Bohemian crown jewels.
