# Jørn Utzon

**1918 – 2008** · Danish  
Born: Copenhagen · Died: Copenhagen  
Styles: [Late & Expressive Modernism](../movements/late-modernism.md)  

> Danish architect whose winning design of 1957 for the Sydney Opera House produced one of the most recognised buildings of the twentieth century.

## Biography

Utzon, son of a naval architect, studied at the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts, worked briefly with Alvar Aalto, and travelled in Mexico, the United States, China and Japan. In 1957, still little known, he won the international competition for an opera house on Bennelong Point in Sydney. Years of work on the structure of its shells followed, with the engineers Ove Arup and Partners, but after disputes with the New South Wales government over costs and design he resigned in 1966 and never returned to see the building finished. He later built the Bagsværd Church near Copenhagen and the National Assembly of Kuwait.

Utzon's architecture draws on platforms and roofs, on natural growth and on the additive repetition of parts. He received the Pritzker Prize in 2003, and the Sydney Opera House became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2007, one of the few sites so listed in its architect's lifetime. Late in life he was reconciled with Sydney and drew up guidelines for the building's future.

## Key buildings

### Sydney Opera House (1959–1973)

[![Sydney Opera House](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e1/Sydney_Opera_House_2026.jpg/1280px-Sydney_Opera_House_2026.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sydney_Opera_House_2026.jpg)

<sub>Sydney Opera House, Bennelong Point, Sydney, Australia Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sydney_Opera_House_2026.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1959–1973
- **Type:** Performing arts centre
- **Location:** Bennelong Point, Sydney, Australia
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sydney%20Opera%20House%20J%C3%B8rn%20Utzon)

Utzon won the international competition of 1957 with sketches for a performing arts centre on a granite platform jutting into Sydney Harbour, crowned by soaring shells. The jury, including Eero Saarinen, recognised its originality, but nobody knew how to build the shells. After years of work with the engineers Ove Arup and Partners, Utzon found in 1961 a solution by deriving all their curves from the surface of a single sphere, so that they could be assembled from prefabricated ribs, and they were covered with over a million glazed white and cream tiles from Sweden. A change of state government, rising costs and disputes led to Utzon's resignation in 1966, and the interiors were completed by Peter Hall and others. Queen Elizabeth II opened it in 1973. In 2007 it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site; Utzon's son Jan has since worked on revisions toward his father's intentions.
