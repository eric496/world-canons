## Biography

Mackintosh trained at the Glasgow School of Art in evening classes while apprenticed to an architect, and joined the firm of Honeyman and Keppie. With his future wife Margaret Macdonald, her sister Frances and Herbert MacNair he formed 'The Four', whose graphic work and furniture were admired in Vienna, where they exhibited at the Secession in 1900. He designed the Glasgow School of Art (1897–1909), the Willow Tea Rooms, Hill House at Helensburgh and Queen's Cross Church. Commissions dried up after 1914, and he later painted watercolours in France.

Mackintosh combined the massing of Scottish baronial castles with elongated, geometric Art Nouveau ornament, white interiors and dark, tall-backed furniture. His work was better appreciated on the Continent than in Britain during his lifetime. The School of Art's Mackintosh Building was gutted by fires in 2014 and 2018.

## Key buildings

### Glasgow School of Art (Mackintosh Building) (1897–1909)

[![Glasgow School of Art (Mackintosh Building)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/12/Schoolofart1.jpg/1280px-Schoolofart1.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Schoolofart1.jpg)

<sub>Glasgow School of Art (Mackintosh Building), Glasgow, Scotland Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Schoolofart1.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1897–1909
- **Type:** Art school
- **Location:** Glasgow, Scotland
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Glasgow%20School%20of%20Art%20%28Mackintosh%20Building%29%20Charles%20Rennie%20Mackintosh)

Mackintosh won the competition for a new building for the Glasgow School of Art in 1896, and it was built in two phases, the east half in 1897–99 and the west in 1907–09, on a steep sloping site. The north front has huge studio windows for steady light, framed by stone and decorated with wrought-iron brackets and finials. The west wing is more severe and vertical, with tall oriel windows. Its library, of dark wood with suspended lamps and slender posts, was regarded as one of the finest interiors of its era. Mackintosh designed the furniture and fittings. A fire in 2014 destroyed the library, and as restoration neared completion a second fire in 2018 gutted the building. The school has committed to rebuilding it.
