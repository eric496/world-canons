<sub>Portrait: Paul Baudry, portrait of Charles Garnier, 1868 · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Charles_Garnier_1868_by_Paul_Baudry_1828_1886_edit.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Garnier, son of a wheelwright, studied at the École des Beaux-Arts and won the Prix de Rome in 1848, spending five years in Italy and visiting Greece. Little known, he won the competition of 1861 for a new opera house in Paris, which was built between 1861 and 1875, interrupted by the Franco-Prussian War and the Commune. He later designed the casino and opera house at Monte Carlo and villas at Bordighera on the Riviera.

Asked by the Empress Eugénie what style his design was, Garnier is said to have answered that it was Napoleon III style. The Opéra combines a rigorous plan, expressing each function in its own volume, with a sumptuous synthesis of Renaissance and Baroque forms, coloured marbles, gilding and sculpture. Its grand staircase is designed as a stage on which the audience itself performs, and the building became the model for opera houses and theatres across the world.

## Key buildings

### Palais Garnier (Paris Opéra) (1861–1875)

[![Palais Garnier (Paris Opéra)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Paris_Opera_full_frontal_architecture%2C_May_2009.jpg/1280px-Paris_Opera_full_frontal_architecture%2C_May_2009.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Paris_Opera_full_frontal_architecture%2C_May_2009.jpg)

<sub>Palais Garnier (Paris Opéra), Paris, France Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Paris_Opera_full_frontal_architecture%2C_May_2009.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1861–1875
- **Type:** Opera house
- **Location:** Paris, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Palais%20Garnier%20%28Paris%20Op%C3%A9ra%29%20Charles%20Garnier)

The opera house was a centrepiece of Baron Haussmann's rebuilding of Paris under Napoleon III. Charles Garnier, aged thirty-five, won the competition of 1861 from over 170 entrants. Construction was slowed by an underground water table, which was contained by a vast concrete tank, later the inspiration for the lake in Gaston Leroux's The Phantom of the Opera, and by the war of 1870–71 and the Commune, and the house opened in 1875. Each function has its own volume: the façade and loggia, the grand staircase and foyers, the horseshoe auditorium under a dome, and the huge stage house. Inside, coloured marbles, onyx, gilding, mosaics, mirrors and sculpture create a theatre of society in which the audience is on display. Marc Chagall painted a new auditorium ceiling in 1964. It remains a home of the Paris Opera Ballet.
