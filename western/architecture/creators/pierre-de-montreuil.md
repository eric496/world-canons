# Pierre de Montreuil

**c. 1200 – 1267** · French  
Born: ? · Died: Paris  
Styles: [Gothic](../movements/gothic.md)  

> Leading Parisian architect of the mid-thirteenth century, master of works at Saint-Denis and Notre-Dame, traditionally credited with the Sainte-Chapelle.

## Biography

Pierre de Montreuil is documented in Paris from the 1240s. He worked at the abbey of Saint-Germain-des-Prés, where he built a refectory and a Lady Chapel, both now lost, and he is associated with the rebuilding of Saint-Denis. By 1265 he was master of works at Notre-Dame, where he continued the south transept façade begun by Jean de Chelles. His tombstone at Saint-Germain-des-Prés called him a doctor of stones, a sign of the high status a master mason could reach.

Since the nineteenth century he has often been named as the architect of Louis IX's Sainte-Chapelle, consecrated in 1248, but there is no document to prove it, and many historians now treat the attribution as uncertain. What is clear is that he was a master of the Rayonnant style, in which walls are reduced to thin tracery frames and glass. His career marks the moment when the names of Gothic architects begin to be recorded and celebrated.

## Key buildings

### Sainte-Chapelle (1242–1248)

[![Sainte-Chapelle](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Sainte_Chapelle_-_Upper_level_1.jpg/960px-Sainte_Chapelle_-_Upper_level_1.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sainte_Chapelle_-_Upper_level_1.jpg)

<sub>Sainte-Chapelle, the upper chapel with its stained glass Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sainte_Chapelle_-_Upper_level_1.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1242–1248
- **Type:** Palace chapel
- **Location:** Île de la Cité, Paris, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sainte-Chapelle%20Pierre%20de%20Montreuil)

King Louis IX built the Sainte-Chapelle within the royal palace in Paris to house the Crown of Thorns and other Passion relics he had bought from the Latin emperor of Constantinople. It was consecrated in 1248. A lower chapel served the palace staff; the upper chapel, for the king, is a single vessel in which the walls are reduced to slender piers between fifteen windows about fifteen metres tall, showing over a thousand scenes from the Bible culminating in the story of the relics. Iron bars and chains concealed in the masonry help stabilise the structure. The chapel is traditionally, but not certainly, attributed to Pierre de Montreuil. The relics were dispersed in the Revolution, and the chapel was extensively restored in the nineteenth century; about two thirds of the glass is original.
