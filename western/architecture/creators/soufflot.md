# Jacques-Germain Soufflot

**1713 – 1780** · French  
Born: Irancy, Burgundy · Died: Paris  
Styles: [Palladianism & Neoclassicism](../movements/neoclassicism.md)  

> French architect of the church of Sainte-Geneviève in Paris, now the Panthéon, a founding monument of Neoclassicism.

## Biography

Soufflot studied in Rome in the 1730s and made his name in Lyon with the Hôtel-Dieu hospital and a theatre. In 1749–51 he accompanied the future Marquis de Marigny, brother of Madame de Pompadour and director of the king's buildings, on a tour of Italy that included the Greek temples at Paestum. Through Marigny he was appointed in 1755 to design the new church of Sainte-Geneviève in Paris, the project that occupied the rest of his life. He also served as controller of royal buildings in Paris.

Soufflot aimed to combine the lightness of Gothic structure with the purity of Greek columns, using freestanding columns inside rather than heavy piers and reinforcing the masonry with iron. Critics doubted the piers could carry the dome, and cracks led to controversy that continued after his death; the building was completed in 1790 by his pupil Jean-Baptiste Rondelet. Converted into the Panthéon during the Revolution, it became a model for domed buildings across Europe and America.

## Key buildings

### Panthéon (Church of Sainte-Geneviève) (1757–1790)

<sub>Panthéon (Church of Sainte-Geneviève), Paris, France Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pantheon_of_Paris_007.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1757–1790
- **Type:** Church, later secular mausoleum
- **Location:** Paris, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Panth%C3%A9on%20%28Church%20of%20Sainte-Genevi%C3%A8ve%29%20Jacques-Germain%20Soufflot)

Louis XV vowed during an illness in 1744 to rebuild the abbey church of Sainte-Geneviève, the patron saint of Paris, and Soufflot's design was chosen in 1755. It is a Greek cross with a dome over the crossing on a high colonnaded drum, approached through a giant Corinthian portico modelled on the Pantheon in Rome. Inside, Soufflot used freestanding columns carrying straight entablatures and light vaults, seeking to combine the lightness of Gothic and the purity of Greek architecture. Doubts about whether the piers could carry the dome led to controversy, and the piers were later reinforced. Finished in 1790, it was converted by the revolutionary government into a mausoleum for great Frenchmen, and windows were blocked to make it more sombre. It changed status several times in the nineteenth century; Voltaire, Rousseau, Victor Hugo, Marie Curie and others are buried there. Foucault demonstrated the earth's rotation with his pendulum there in 1851.
