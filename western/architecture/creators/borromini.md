# Francesco Borromini

<a href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Borromini.jpg"><img align="right" width="240" src="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cc/Borromini.jpg" alt="Francesco Borromini" title="Portrait of Francesco Borromini"></a>

**1599 – 1667** · Italian (Ticinese)  
Born: Bissone, Ticino · Died: Rome  
Styles: [Baroque](../movements/baroque.md)  

> Brilliant and difficult Baroque architect whose churches of San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane and Sant'Ivo alla Sapienza fold walls and domes into complex geometry.

<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Francesco Borromini · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Borromini.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Francesco Castelli, later called Borromini, came from a family of stonemasons on Lake Lugano and arrived in Rome around 1619 to work under his relative Carlo Maderno at St Peter's. After Maderno's death he served for a time under Bernini on the baldachin and Palazzo Barberini, an uneasy collaboration that became a lifelong rivalry. His independent career began in 1634 with the monastery and church of San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane, followed by the Oratory of the Filippini, Sant'Ivo alla Sapienza, the remodelling of San Giovanni in Laterano and Sant'Agnese in Agone. Melancholy and isolated in his last years, he took his own life in 1667.

Borromini built mostly in brick and stucco, on cramped sites and modest budgets, but his plans are generated from interlocking triangles, circles and ovals that make walls undulate and domes seem to expand. He invented new ornament and treated the classical orders with great freedom, which his critics called extravagant. His influence was strongest in northern Italy and Central Europe, in Guarini's Turin and the churches of Bohemia and Bavaria, and twentieth-century architects rediscovered him as a master of space.

### San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane (1634–1646; façade 1665–1667)

[![San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/San_Carlo_alle_Quattro_Fontane_-_Front.jpg/960px-San_Carlo_alle_Quattro_Fontane_-_Front.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:San_Carlo_alle_Quattro_Fontane_-_Front.jpg)

<sub>San Carlo alle Quattro Fontane, Rome, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:San_Carlo_alle_Quattro_Fontane_-_Front.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1634–1646; façade 1665–1667
- **Type:** Church
- **Location:** Rome, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=San%20Carlo%20alle%20Quattro%20Fontane%20Francesco%20Borromini)

The Spanish Trinitarian order commissioned Borromini to build a monastery and church on a cramped corner site at the Quattro Fontane crossroads. The whole church could fit inside one of the piers of St Peter's dome. Its plan is generated from two equilateral triangles and an oval, producing walls that ripple in and out, with columns that are set close and continuous. Above, pendentives carry an oval dome whose coffers of crosses, octagons and hexagons diminish in size toward the lantern, so that the dome seems higher than it is, all in white stucco. The façade, his last work, completed after his death, undulates concave-convex-concave below and concave above. The church made Borromini famous; visitors from across Europe asked for its plans.

### Sant'Ivo alla Sapienza (1642–1660)

[![Sant'Ivo alla Sapienza](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Sant%27Ivo_alla_Sapienza_-Rome.jpg/1280px-Sant%27Ivo_alla_Sapienza_-Rome.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sant%27Ivo_alla_Sapienza_-Rome.jpg)

<sub>Sant'Ivo alla Sapienza, Rome, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sant%27Ivo_alla_Sapienza_-Rome.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1642–1660
- **Type:** University church
- **Location:** Rome, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sant%27Ivo%20alla%20Sapienza%20Francesco%20Borromini)

Borromini was given the church at the end of the courtyard of the Sapienza, the university of Rome, whose existing two-storey arcades he had to respect. His plan is based on a six-pointed star formed by two triangles, with alternating concave and convex-ended apses, an emblem associated with wisdom and with the bee of Pope Urban VIII's family, the Barberini. From the floor, the pattern rises unbroken into a white dome, with no drum, to the lantern. Outside, the concave façade continues the courtyard, and over it rise a stepped drum and a lantern that twists in a spiral like a corkscrew, topped by a flame and a crown. It is one of the most original buildings in Rome and has been compared to the spiralling towers of ancient legend.
