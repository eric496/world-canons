## Biography

Rastrelli was the son of the Florentine sculptor Carlo Bartolomeo Rastrelli, who moved with his family to St Petersburg in 1716 to work for Peter the Great. The young Rastrelli trained in Russia and probably abroad, and under the empresses Anna and especially Elizabeth he became chief architect of the imperial court. He built the Smolny Convent, the Catherine Palace at Tsarskoye Selo, the Great Palace at Peterhof, the Stroganov Palace and, from 1754, the Winter Palace. Catherine the Great preferred Neoclassicism, and he left court service in 1763.

Rastrelli's buildings have long, rhythmic façades of coloured stucco with white columns and gilded ornament, and interiors of mirrors, carving and gold. He combined Italian and French Baroque and Rococo with Russian traditions such as onion domes and five-domed churches. His style, called Elizabethan Baroque, gave St Petersburg its most festive buildings.

## Key buildings

### Winter Palace (1754–1762)

[![Winter Palace](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/74/RUS-2016-Aerial-SPB-Winter_Palace_%28crop%29.jpg/1280px-RUS-2016-Aerial-SPB-Winter_Palace_%28crop%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:RUS-2016-Aerial-SPB-Winter_Palace_%28crop%29.jpg)

<sub>Winter Palace, Palace Square, St Petersburg, Russia Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:RUS-2016-Aerial-SPB-Winter_Palace_%28crop%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1754–1762
- **Type:** Imperial palace
- **Location:** Palace Square, St Petersburg, Russia
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Winter%20Palace%20Francesco%20Bartolomeo%20Rastrelli)

Empress Elizabeth commissioned Rastrelli to build a new Winter Palace on the Neva, the fourth on the site. Completed in 1762, just after her death, it is a four-sided block around a courtyard, with over a thousand rooms and façades on the river and Palace Square, punctuated by columns, window surrounds and statues on the roofline. Rastrelli's Jordan Staircase survived a fire in 1837, after which much of the interior was rebuilt by Vasily Stasov and Alexander Bryullov. The palace was the main residence of the tsars until 1904, the scene of the massacre of Bloody Sunday in 1905, and in October 1917 the seat of the Provisional Government stormed by the Bolsheviks. It is now part of the State Hermitage Museum, founded on Catherine the Great's collection.
