# Callicrates

**active mid-5th century BC** · Greek (Athenian)  
Styles: [Ancient Greek](../movements/ancient-greek.md)  

> Athenian architect named with Ictinus as a builder of the Parthenon and credited with the small Ionic temple of Athena Nike on the Acropolis.

## Biography

Callicrates appears in ancient sources and inscriptions as one of the leading architects of Periclean Athens. Plutarch names him with Ictinus as a builder of the Parthenon, and an Athenian decree assigns him the design of the temple of Athena Nike, apparently approved around 449 BC but built only in the 420s. He is also associated with work on the Long Walls connecting Athens to its port at Piraeus.

How the work on the Parthenon was divided between Ictinus and Callicrates is not known, and scholars have suggested that Callicrates may have been more the practical builder. The temple of Athena Nike, a jewel-like Ionic building perched on the bastion beside the Propylaea, shows a gift for elegance on a small scale. Together with the Erechtheion it made the Ionic order a full partner of the Doric in the Athenian Acropolis.

- **Date:** c. 427–424 BC
- **Type:** Ionic temple
- **Location:** Acropolis, Athens, Greece
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Temple%20of%20Athena%20Nike%20Callicrates)

The temple of Athena Nike, Athena as goddess of victory, stands on a projecting bastion beside the Propylaea, the gateway to the Acropolis. A decree of the mid-fifth century entrusted its design to Callicrates, but it was built only in the 420s, during the Peloponnesian War. It is an amphiprostyle temple, with four slender Ionic columns at front and back and none along the sides, and a sculpted frieze showing gods and battles, including Greeks fighting Persians. A parapet around the bastion carried famous reliefs of winged Victories, among them one adjusting her sandal, now in the Acropolis Museum. The Ottomans dismantled the temple in 1686 to build a gun battery; it was reassembled from its original blocks in the 1830s and again in recent decades.
