# John Soane

> Highly original English Neoclassical architect of the Bank of England and of his own house-museum in Lincoln's Inn Fields.

<sub>Portrait: Thomas Lawrence, portrait of John Soane · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Thomas_Lawrence_John_Soane.JPG)</sub>

## Biography

Soane was the son of a bricklayer and trained with George Dance the Younger and Henry Holland. A Royal Academy scholarship sent him to Italy in 1778–80. In 1788 he became architect to the Bank of England, which he rebuilt over forty-five years as a complex of top-lit banking halls behind a windowless wall; it was largely demolished in the 1920s. He became professor of architecture at the Royal Academy, designed Dulwich Picture Gallery (1811–17), the first purpose-built public art gallery in Britain, and remodelled three houses in Lincoln's Inn Fields as his home, office and museum.

Soane stripped classicism down to incised lines, shallow domes and arches, and used hidden sources of light to create mysterious, layered interiors. His collection of antiquities, casts, drawings and paintings, arranged as a teaching museum, was preserved by an Act of Parliament in 1833 to remain as he left it. His idiosyncratic style has been admired by modern and postmodern architects alike.

## Key buildings

### Sir John Soane's Museum (1792–1824)

[![Sir John Soane's Museum](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6f/Sir_John_Soane%27s_Museum%2C_13_Lincoln%27s_Inn_Fields%2C_London_WC2_-_geograph.org.uk_-_399109.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sir_John_Soane%27s_Museum%2C_13_Lincoln%27s_Inn_Fields%2C_London_WC2_-_geograph.org.uk_-_399109.jpg)

- **Date:** 1792–1824
- **Type:** House and museum
- **Location:** Lincoln's Inn Fields, London, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Sir%20John%20Soane%27s%20Museum%20John%20Soane)

Soane bought and rebuilt three adjoining houses on Lincoln's Inn Fields between 1792 and 1824, turning them into a home, an office for his practice and a museum for his students. Behind a façade with a projecting loggia of Portland stone, small rooms open into each other through arches, shafts and domes, lit from above by coloured glass and multiplied by convex mirrors. Casts, fragments, models, drawings and urns cover the walls, around a domed space and a crypt containing the sarcophagus of the Egyptian pharaoh Seti I, bought in 1824. A picture room with hinged panels holds Hogarth's series A Rake's Progress and An Election, as well as paintings by Canaletto and Turner. An Act of Parliament of 1833 preserved the house as Soane left it, free to visitors, and so it remains.
