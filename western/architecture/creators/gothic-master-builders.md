# Gothic master builders

**active 12th–16th century** · European  
Styles: [Gothic](../movements/gothic.md)  

> The master masons of the great Gothic cathedrals and chapels of France, England, Germany and Italy, some known by name, many not.

## Biography

From the thirteenth century, master masons were increasingly recognised as professionals. At Amiens a labyrinth set into the floor named the successive masters Robert de Luzarches and Thomas and Renaud de Cormont; at Reims a similar labyrinth, now lost, recorded Jean d'Orbais and his successors. At Salisbury the canon Elias of Dereham and the mason Nicholas of Ely are connected with the work, while Cologne Cathedral was begun under Master Gerhard. Masters travelled, kept drawings, trained apprentices in lodges and were sometimes buried within their churches.

These builders spread the Gothic of the Île-de-France across Europe and adapted it to local traditions: the soaring French cathedral, the long, low English cathedral with its single spire, the hall churches of Germany, and late variants such as English Perpendicular and fan vaulting. Their methods relied on geometry, templates and experience rather than calculation, yet produced some of the tallest and boldest masonry structures ever built.

### Reims Cathedral (1211–c. 1345)

[![Reims Cathedral](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Exterior_view_of_the_west_facade_of_Notre-Dame_Cathedral_in_Reims.jpg/960px-Exterior_view_of_the_west_facade_of_Notre-Dame_Cathedral_in_Reims.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Exterior_view_of_the_west_facade_of_Notre-Dame_Cathedral_in_Reims.jpg)

<sub>Reims Cathedral, Reims, France Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Exterior_view_of_the_west_facade_of_Notre-Dame_Cathedral_in_Reims.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1211–c. 1345
- **Type:** Gothic cathedral
- **Location:** Reims, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Reims%20Cathedral%20Gothic%20master%20builders)

Reims Cathedral stands where Clovis, king of the Franks, was baptised around 500, and almost all French kings were crowned there. After a fire in 1210 the cathedral was rebuilt from 1211; a lost labyrinth named Jean d'Orbais, Jean le Loup, Gaucher de Reims and Bernard de Soissons as its masters. The building introduced bar tracery, in which windows are divided by thin stone bars, a decisive step for later Gothic. Its west front, with rose window and portals glazed rather than filled with sculpted tympana, carries some 2,300 statues, including the smiling angel. German shelling in the First World War set the cathedral on fire and badly damaged it, an event that became a symbol of wartime destruction; restoration, aided by the Rockefeller family, allowed reopening in 1938. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Amiens Cathedral (1220–c. 1270)

[![Amiens Cathedral](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2c/0_Amiens_-_Cath%C3%A9drale_Notre-Dame_%281%29.JPG/960px-0_Amiens_-_Cath%C3%A9drale_Notre-Dame_%281%29.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:0_Amiens_-_Cath%C3%A9drale_Notre-Dame_%281%29.JPG)

<sub>Amiens Cathedral, Amiens, France Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:0_Amiens_-_Cath%C3%A9drale_Notre-Dame_%281%29.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1220–c. 1270
- **Type:** Gothic cathedral
- **Location:** Amiens, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Amiens%20Cathedral%20Gothic%20master%20builders)

Amiens Cathedral was begun in 1220 to house the relic of the head of John the Baptist. A labyrinth in the nave floor names its designer, Robert de Luzarches, and his successors Thomas and Renaud de Cormont. Built rapidly to a unified design, it has an interior volume of about 200,000 cubic metres and a nave vault 42 metres high. Its tall arcade, glazed triforium in the choir and huge clerestory give an effect of soaring lightness. The west front's three portals, with the figure of Christ known as the Beau Dieu, form one of the great sculptural programmes of the Middle Ages, and laser cleaning revealed traces of its original paint. John Ruskin wrote a guide to it, The Bible of Amiens. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Salisbury Cathedral (1220–1258; spire c. 1320)

[![Salisbury Cathedral](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7f/SalisburyCathedral-wyrdlight-EastExt.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SalisburyCathedral-wyrdlight-EastExt.jpg)

<sub>Salisbury Cathedral, Salisbury, England Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:SalisburyCathedral-wyrdlight-EastExt.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1220–1258; spire c. 1320
- **Type:** Gothic cathedral
- **Location:** Salisbury, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Salisbury%20Cathedral%20Gothic%20master%20builders)

In 1220 the bishop and canons left their windswept hilltop cathedral at Old Sarum and began a new one in the meadows below, and the main building was completed in only thirty-eight years, giving it unusual unity. It is the classic example of the Early English Gothic style: long and low, with double transepts, lancet windows, dark Purbeck marble shafts, and a vast walled close of lawns. The tower and spire, added around 1320, reach about 123 metres, the tallest in Britain, and their weight has visibly bent the crossing piers. The chapter house holds one of the four surviving original copies of Magna Carta of 1215. John Constable's paintings made the cathedral seen across the water meadows one of the best-known images of England.

### Cologne Cathedral (1248–1880)

[![Cologne Cathedral](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/04/K%C3%B6lner_Dom_-_Westfassade_2022_ohne_Ger%C3%BCst-0968_b.jpg/960px-K%C3%B6lner_Dom_-_Westfassade_2022_ohne_Ger%C3%BCst-0968_b.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:K%C3%B6lner_Dom_-_Westfassade_2022_ohne_Ger%C3%BCst-0968_b.jpg)

<sub>Cologne Cathedral, Cologne, Germany Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:K%C3%B6lner_Dom_-_Westfassade_2022_ohne_Ger%C3%BCst-0968_b.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1248–1880
- **Type:** Gothic cathedral
- **Location:** Cologne, Germany
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Cologne%20Cathedral%20Gothic%20master%20builders)

Cologne Cathedral was begun in 1248 under Master Gerhard to house the relics of the Three Kings, brought from Milan in 1164. Its choir, modelled on Amiens and Sainte-Chapelle, was consecrated in 1322, but work stopped around 1473, leaving a medieval crane standing on the unfinished south tower for four centuries. The rediscovery of medieval drawings of the façade in the early nineteenth century and Romantic and nationalist enthusiasm led to completion between 1842 and 1880, with funding from the Prussian state and a public building society; it was briefly the tallest building in the world. It survived Allied bombing in the Second World War though hit many times. Its five-aisled nave, treasury and stained glass, including a window by Gerhard Richter of 2007, attract millions of visitors. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### King's College Chapel (1446–1515)

[![King's College Chapel](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/f/fd/KingsCollegeChapel.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:KingsCollegeChapel.jpg)

<sub>King's College Chapel, Cambridge, England Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:KingsCollegeChapel.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1446–1515
- **Type:** College chapel
- **Location:** Cambridge, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=King%27s%20College%20Chapel%20Gothic%20master%20builders)

Henry VI founded King's College in 1441 and laid the foundation stone of its chapel in 1446, but the Wars of the Roses interrupted building, and it was completed under Henry VII and Henry VIII. The chapel is a single tall space, nearly 90 metres long, in the Perpendicular Gothic style, with walls almost entirely of glass framed by buttresses. The master mason John Wastell built its fan vault in 1508–15, a stone ceiling of cone-shaped fans with fine tracery, the largest of its kind. The windows, made mostly in the 1510s–40s by Flemish and English glaziers, form the most complete set of Renaissance-era glass in England, and Henry VIII's oak screen bears the initials of Anne Boleyn. The college choir's Christmas Eve Festival of Nine Lessons and Carols, broadcast since 1928, has made the interior famous worldwide.
