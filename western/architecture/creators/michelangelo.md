> Sculptor, painter and poet who became, in later life, the most influential architect of the sixteenth century, designing the Laurentian Library, the Campidoglio and the dome of St Peter's.

<sub>Portrait: Daniele da Volterra, portrait of Michelangelo (detail) · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Michelangelo_Daniele_da_Volterra_%28dettaglio%29.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Michelangelo came to architecture through sculpture. His first major architectural commissions, in the 1510s and 1520s, were for the Medici in Florence: a façade for San Lorenzo that was never built, the New Sacristy that houses the Medici tombs, and the Laurentian Library. After settling in Rome in 1534 he redesigned the Capitoline Hill, completed the Palazzo Farnese, and in 1546, aged seventy-one, became architect of St Peter's, which he directed until his death, refusing payment. He also designed the Porta Pia and converted the Baths of Diocletian into the church of Santa Maria degli Angeli.

Michelangelo treated architecture as a body, with walls that swell and tighten like muscle, and he broke the classical rules with conscious freedom: columns recessed into walls, brackets that support nothing, staircases that pour like lava. This licence made him a founder of Mannerism and an inspiration for the Baroque. His plan for St Peter's simplified Bramante's, and its ribbed dome, completed by Giacomo della Porta after his death, became the model for domes from London to Washington.

### St Peter's Basilica (1506–1626; dome 1547–1590)

[![St Peter's Basilica](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Basilica_di_San_Pietro_in_Vaticano_September_2015-1a.jpg/1280px-Basilica_di_San_Pietro_in_Vaticano_September_2015-1a.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Basilica_di_San_Pietro_in_Vaticano_September_2015-1a.jpg)

<sub>St Peter's Basilica, Vatican City Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Basilica_di_San_Pietro_in_Vaticano_September_2015-1a.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1506–1626; dome 1547–1590
- **Type:** Papal basilica
- **Location:** Vatican City
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=St%20Peter%27s%20Basilica%20Michelangelo%20Buonarroti)

In 1506 Pope Julius II laid the foundation stone of a new St Peter's to replace the fourth-century basilica built by Constantine over the apostle's tomb. Bramante's plan was a Greek cross under a great dome. After his death in 1514, Raphael, Peruzzi and Antonio da Sangallo the Younger changed it repeatedly, until Michelangelo, appointed in 1546, simplified and unified the design, built most of the drum, and designed the ribbed double-shelled dome, which Giacomo della Porta and Domenico Fontana completed in 1588–90. Under Paul V, Carlo Maderno extended the nave into a Latin cross and added the façade, and the church was consecrated in 1626. Bernini then designed the baldachin, the Cathedra Petri and the square. The largest church in the world when built, it was financed partly by the sale of indulgences, which helped provoke the Reformation.

### Laurentian Library (1524–1571)

[![Laurentian Library](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/Medicibib.jpg/1280px-Medicibib.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Medicibib.jpg)

<sub>Laurentian Library, San Lorenzo, Florence, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Medicibib.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1524–1571
- **Type:** Library
- **Location:** San Lorenzo, Florence, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Laurentian%20Library%20Michelangelo%20Buonarroti)

Pope Clement VII, a Medici, commissioned Michelangelo in 1524 to design a library for the family's manuscripts above the cloister of San Lorenzo. The long reading room, with its carved wooden desks and ceiling, is calm and orderly. The vestibule, or ricetto, is the opposite: a tall, cramped space where paired columns are sunk into the walls, consoles hang below them supporting nothing, and blind windows frame no view. From the floor a staircase seems to flow down like lava, its central flight of curved steps flanked by two straight ones. Michelangelo left Florence in 1534, and Vasari and Ammannati completed the staircase from his model, in grey pietra serena. The vestibule's deliberate breaking of classical rules made it one of the most influential rooms of the Renaissance.

### Piazza del Campidoglio (1536–c. 1655)

[![Piazza del Campidoglio](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/36/Piazza_del_Campidoglio_2015.jpg/1280px-Piazza_del_Campidoglio_2015.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Piazza_del_Campidoglio_2015.jpg)

<sub>Piazza del Campidoglio, Capitoline Hill, Rome, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Piazza_del_Campidoglio_2015.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1536–c. 1655
- **Type:** Civic square
- **Location:** Capitoline Hill, Rome, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Piazza%20del%20Campidoglio%20Michelangelo%20Buonarroti)

In 1538 Pope Paul III had the ancient bronze equestrian statue of Marcus Aurelius moved to the Capitoline Hill, the ancient centre of Rome and seat of the city government, and Michelangelo designed a new square around it. He set the statue on a pedestal at the centre of a trapezoidal piazza paved with an oval star pattern, rising slightly in the middle. He gave new façades to the medieval Palazzo dei Conservatori and Palazzo Senatorio and designed a matching Palazzo Nuovo opposite, all using a 'giant order' of pilasters rising through two storeys, and a ramped stair, the Cordonata, leading up from the city. Most of the work was carried out after his death, and the pavement was laid only in 1940. The palaces house the Capitoline Museums, the oldest public museum in the world, and the original statue is now indoors.
