# Vitruvius

**c. 80–70 BC – after c. 15 BC** · Roman  
Styles: [Ancient Roman](../movements/ancient-roman.md)  

> Roman architect and military engineer whose ten books On Architecture are the only complete treatise on the subject to survive from antiquity. Rediscovered in the Renaissance, they became the foundation of Western architectural theory.

## Biography

Marcus Vitruvius Pollio served as a military engineer under Julius Caesar, building siege machines and artillery, and later received a pension through the favour of Octavian, the future Augustus, to whom he dedicated his treatise. The only building he says he designed is a basilica at Fanum Fortunae (modern Fano), which has not survived. Even his full name is partly uncertain, and his dates are inferred from the text.

De architectura covers far more than buildings: town planning, materials, temples and the orders, public buildings, houses, water supply, sundials and machines. Its insistence that architecture must have firmitas, utilitas and venustas (strength, usefulness and beauty) became a lasting formula, and its passage relating the proportions of temples to those of the human body inspired Leonardo's Vitruvian Man. Copied through the Middle Ages, it was studied intensely from the fifteenth century and printed, translated and illustrated across Europe.

## Key buildings

### De architectura (Ten Books on Architecture) (c. 30–15 BC)

[![De architectura (Ten Books on Architecture)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/89/De_architectura.jpg/1280px-De_architectura.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:De_architectura.jpg)

<sub>De architectura, early printed edition Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:De_architectura.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 30–15 BC
- **Type:** Treatise
- **Location:** Rome; earliest manuscripts c. 800, first printed c. 1486
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=De%20architectura%20%28Ten%20Books%20on%20Architecture%29%20Vitruvius)

Vitruvius dedicated his ten books on architecture to Augustus. They cover the education of the architect, building materials, temples and the orders, public buildings such as theatres and baths, private houses, wall painting and stucco, water and aqueducts, astronomy and sundials, and machines for war and peace. The work defines architecture by the triad of firmness, commodity and delight, and relates the proportions of buildings to the well-built human body. It survived in medieval manuscripts, was studied by humanists such as Poggio Bracciolini and Alberti, and was first printed in Rome around 1486. Illustrated editions, such as Fra Giocondo's of 1511 and Daniele Barbaro's with drawings by Palladio, made it the bible of Renaissance and Neoclassical architects, even though its diagrams were lost and its meaning often obscure.
