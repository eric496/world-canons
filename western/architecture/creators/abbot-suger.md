# Abbot Suger

**c. 1081 – 1151** · French  
Born: ? · Died: Saint-Denis  
Styles: [Gothic](../movements/gothic.md)  

> Abbot of Saint-Denis and adviser to two French kings, whose rebuilding of his abbey church in the 1130s and 1140s is regarded as the birth of Gothic architecture.

## Biography

Suger was of modest birth and was given as a boy to the royal abbey of Saint-Denis north of Paris, where he was educated alongside the future King Louis VI. He became abbot in 1122 and served as a leading counsellor to Louis VI and Louis VII, acting as regent of France while Louis VII was on the Second Crusade. He reformed the abbey's finances and administration, and from about 1135 rebuilt its church, the burial place of French kings.

Suger was a patron rather than a mason, and the name of his master builder is unknown. But his writings on the consecration and administration of the abbey explain his aims: to fill the church with light, which he understood, following the theology of light attributed to Dionysius, as an image of the divine. The new west front and especially the choir of 1140–44, with its ring of stained-glass windows and slender columns, launched the Gothic style that spread across France and Europe.

- **Date:** c. 1135–1144
- **Type:** Abbey church
- **Location:** Saint-Denis, near Paris, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Basilica%20of%20Saint-Denis%20Abbot%20Suger)

The royal abbey of Saint-Denis held the relics of the patron saint of France and the tombs of its kings. Abbot Suger rebuilt the west end around 1135–40 with a façade of three portals and twin towers and a rose window, and then a new choir, consecrated in 1144 before the king and many bishops. In the choir, an anonymous master combined pointed arches, rib vaults and slender columns to create a double ambulatory with radiating chapels whose walls are almost entirely stained glass, so that the whole east end shines with what Suger called uninterrupted light. The nave and transept were rebuilt in the Rayonnant style from 1231. The tombs were desecrated during the Revolution and later restored. Saint-Denis became a cathedral in 1966.
