# Le Corbusier

**1887 – 1965** · Swiss-French  
Born: La Chaux-de-Fonds, Switzerland · Died: Roquebrune-Cap-Martin, France  
Styles: [Modernism & the International Style](../movements/modernism.md), [Brutalism](../movements/brutalism.md)  

> Swiss-French architect, planner and polemicist, designer of the Villa Savoye, the Unité d'Habitation and the chapel at Ronchamp, the most influential architect of the twentieth century.

## Biography

Charles-Édouard Jeanneret trained as an engraver in his Swiss watchmaking town, travelled in Europe and the Mediterranean, and worked briefly for Auguste Perret and Peter Behrens before settling in Paris in 1917. With Amédée Ozenfant he founded Purism and the magazine L'Esprit Nouveau, adopting the pseudonym Le Corbusier, and his book Vers une architecture (1923) proclaimed the house a machine for living in. With his cousin Pierre Jeanneret he designed white villas culminating in the Villa Savoye, and urban schemes for towers in parks. After the Second World War he built the Unité d'Habitation in Marseille, the chapel of Notre-Dame du Haut at Ronchamp, the monastery of La Tourette and the capital of Chandigarh in India. Seventeen of his works are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

His five points of a new architecture (pilotis, roof garden, free plan, ribbon windows, free façade) and his proportional system, the Modulor, codified modernism, while his late raw-concrete buildings launched Brutalism. His urban ideas, applied crudely by others, are blamed for many failings of postwar planning, and his dealings with the Vichy regime have drawn growing criticism. His influence on architects worldwide remains unmatched.

### Villa Savoye (1928–1931)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Villa Savoye (Centre des monuments nationaux) →](https://www.villa-savoye.fr/)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1928–1931
- **Type:** Country house
- **Location:** Poissy, near Paris, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Villa%20Savoye%20Le%20Corbusier)

Pierre and Eugénie Savoye commissioned a weekend house on a meadow at Poissy, and Le Corbusier and Pierre Jeanneret designed it as a demonstration of the five points of a new architecture: pilotis lifting the house above the ground, a free plan, a free façade, ribbon windows and a roof garden. The ground floor, curved to follow the turning circle of a car, holds the entrance and service rooms; a ramp leads up to the main living floor around an open terrace and on to a sunroof sheltered by curving screens, an architectural promenade. The family suffered from leaks and found the house impractical, and it was nearly demolished after the war before André Malraux had it protected. Restored, it is open as a museum and is part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of Le Corbusier's works.

### Unité d'Habitation, Marseille (1947–1952)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Fondation Le Corbusier →](https://www.fondationlecorbusier.fr/)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1947–1952
- **Type:** Housing block
- **Location:** Marseille, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Unit%C3%A9%20d%27Habitation%2C%20Marseille%20Le%20Corbusier)

After the Second World War the French reconstruction ministry commissioned Le Corbusier to build a prototype of mass housing in Marseille. The Unité d'Habitation, also called the Cité Radieuse, is a slab of eighteen storeys on massive tapering pilotis, containing 337 apartments of twenty-three types for up to about 1,600 people. Most flats are two-storey units interlocking around internal 'streets' every third floor, with a double-height living room and a balcony on each side, shaded by deep sun-breakers. Halfway up, a street of shops and a hotel, and on the roof a nursery school, a running track, a paddling pool and sculptural ventilation stacks, make it a vertical village. Its rough board-marked concrete, béton brut, gave Brutalism its name, and its proportions follow Le Corbusier's Modulor. Variants were built in Nantes, Berlin, Briey and Firminy.

### Notre-Dame du Haut, Ronchamp (1950–1955)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Colline Notre-Dame du Haut →](https://www.collinenotredameduhaut.com/)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1950–1955
- **Type:** Pilgrimage chapel
- **Location:** Ronchamp, Haute-Saône, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Notre-Dame%20du%20Haut%2C%20Ronchamp%20Le%20Corbusier)

A pilgrimage chapel on the hill of Bourlémont, near Belfort, was destroyed in the fighting of 1944, and the diocese, encouraged by reformers of religious art, commissioned Le Corbusier to rebuild it. Surprising those who knew his machine-age villas, he created a sculptural building of thick, curved white walls, reusing the rubble of the old chapel, under a dark concrete roof shaped like a hull or a crab shell, raised slightly on hidden posts so a thin band of light separates it from the walls. The south wall is pierced by deep, irregular windows with coloured glass. Three towers bring light down onto side altars, and an outdoor altar and pulpit serve large open-air congregations. Ronchamp's free forms divided modernists and opened the way for expressive architecture. It is part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing of Le Corbusier's works.
