# Early Christian & Byzantine builders

**active 4th–11th century** · Roman / Byzantine  
Styles: [Early Christian & Byzantine](../movements/early-christian-byzantine.md)  

> The largely anonymous architects and mosaicists of the late Roman and Byzantine world who created the Christian basilica and the domed, mosaic-covered church.

## Biography

When Christianity became a favoured and then an official religion in the fourth century, builders adapted the Roman basilica, a long hall with aisles and an apse, for congregational worship, and the Roman mausoleum for shrines and baptisteries. The bishops, emperors and patrons who paid for these churches are often recorded; the architects rarely are. In the Eastern Empire centred on Constantinople, and in its Italian outposts at Ravenna and Venice, Byzantine builders developed domes, vaults and lavish marble and mosaic interiors.

Early Christian and Byzantine architecture turned attention inward. Plain brick exteriors hide interiors in which light, colour and gold create an image of heaven. The basilica plan shaped the Western church for a millennium, while the domed centralised church became the norm in the Orthodox world from Greece and the Balkans to Russia. Their mosaics are among the greatest survivals of late antique art.

### Santa Sabina (c. 422–432)

[![Santa Sabina](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/80/Santa_Sabina_%28Rome%29_-_Esterno.jpg/1280px-Santa_Sabina_%28Rome%29_-_Esterno.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Santa_Sabina_%28Rome%29_-_Esterno.jpg)

<sub>Santa Sabina, Aventine Hill, Rome, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Santa_Sabina_%28Rome%29_-_Esterno.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 422–432
- **Type:** Early Christian basilica
- **Location:** Aventine Hill, Rome, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Santa%20Sabina%20Early%20Christian%20%26%20Byzantine%20builders)

Santa Sabina was built by Peter of Illyria, a priest, in the time of Pope Celestine I, as recorded in a mosaic inscription over the entrance. It is the classic early Christian basilica: a tall nave lined with twenty-four reused fluted Corinthian columns carrying arcades, high clerestory windows, aisles, and an apse at the end, with an emphasis on light and calm proportion. Much of its marble inlay has been lost, but its carved cypress doors, with panels of biblical scenes, include one of the earliest surviving depictions of the crucifixion. Given to Saint Dominic in 1222, it is still the headquarters of the Dominican order. Restorations in the early twentieth century removed later alterations to recover its original appearance.

### Basilica of San Vitale (526–547)

[![Basilica of San Vitale](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/51/Basilica_of_San_Vitale.jpg/1280px-Basilica_of_San_Vitale.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Basilica_of_San_Vitale.jpg)

<sub>Basilica of San Vitale, Ravenna, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Basilica_of_San_Vitale.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 526–547
- **Type:** Byzantine church
- **Location:** Ravenna, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Basilica%20of%20San%20Vitale%20Early%20Christian%20%26%20Byzantine%20builders)

San Vitale was begun under Bishop Ecclesius when Ravenna was ruled by the Ostrogoths, funded by the banker Julianus Argentarius, and consecrated in 547 after the city had been retaken by Justinian's Byzantine Empire. Its plan is an octagon whose central domed space is surrounded by an ambulatory and gallery, opening through curved columned niches so that space seems to flow outward. The plain brick exterior contrasts with an interior of marble and mosaic, above all the apse panels in which the emperor Justinian and empress Theodora, with their courts, bring offerings to the altar. San Vitale was a model for Charlemagne's chapel at Aachen. With other Ravenna monuments it forms a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### St Mark's Basilica (1063–1094)

[![St Mark's Basilica](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Venezia_Basilica_di_San_Marco_Fassade_2.jpg/1280px-Venezia_Basilica_di_San_Marco_Fassade_2.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Venezia_Basilica_di_San_Marco_Fassade_2.jpg)

<sub>St Mark's Basilica, Venice, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Venezia_Basilica_di_San_Marco_Fassade_2.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1063–1094
- **Type:** Byzantine church
- **Location:** Venice, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=St%20Mark%27s%20Basilica%20Early%20Christian%20%26%20Byzantine%20builders)

St Mark's was built as the chapel of the Doge of Venice to house the relics of Saint Mark, stolen from Alexandria in 828. The present church, begun in 1063 and consecrated in 1094, follows the plan of the lost Church of the Holy Apostles in Constantinople: a Greek cross with a dome over the crossing and each arm. Over the following centuries the Venetians covered it with more than 8,000 square metres of gold-ground mosaic, raised taller outer domes, and encrusted the façade with marble, sculptures and spoils, including the bronze horses taken from Constantinople in the Fourth Crusade of 1204. Its mixture of Byzantine, Romanesque, Gothic and Islamic elements reflects Venice's position between East and West. It became the cathedral of Venice in 1807.
