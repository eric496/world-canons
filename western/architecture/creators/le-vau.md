# Louis Le Vau

**1612 – 1670** · French  
Born: Paris · Died: Paris  
Styles: [Baroque](../movements/baroque.md)  

> French Baroque architect of Vaux-le-Vicomte and First Architect to Louis XIV, who began the transformation of Versailles into a palace.

## Biography

Le Vau was the son of a master mason and built town houses for wealthy Parisians on the Île Saint-Louis, including the Hôtel Lambert. His breakthrough was the château of Vaux-le-Vicomte (1656–61) for the finance minister Nicolas Fouquet, created with the painter Charles Le Brun and the garden designer André Le Nôtre. After Fouquet's fall, Louis XIV took the same team into royal service. Le Vau became First Architect of the King, worked at the Louvre and the Tuileries, designed the Collège des Quatre-Nations (now the Institut de France), and from 1668 wrapped the old hunting lodge at Versailles in a new stone envelope.

Le Vau's gift was for grand, unified ensembles in which architecture, interior decoration and landscape work together, an approach that became the model of French royal magnificence. His buildings combine classical restraint with Baroque movement, such as the oval domed salon at Vaux. Versailles, completed by Hardouin-Mansart, was imitated by princes across Europe for a century.

### Vaux-le-Vicomte (1656–1661)

- **Date:** 1656–1661
- **Type:** Château
- **Location:** Maincy, Seine-et-Marne, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Vaux-le-Vicomte%20Louis%20Le%20Vau)

Nicolas Fouquet, Louis XIV's superintendent of finances, built Vaux-le-Vicomte south-east of Paris with Le Vau as architect, Charles Le Brun as painter and decorator, and André Le Nôtre as garden designer. Surrounded by a moat, the château centres on an oval domed salon facing the garden, and Le Nôtre's formal gardens, with parterres, terraces, canals and statues, stretch along an axis as far as the eye can see, using optical effects to hide changes in level. On 17 August 1661 Fouquet held a lavish fête for the young king. Three weeks later he was arrested for embezzlement and spent the rest of his life in prison, and Louis employed the same team at Versailles. The château remains in private hands and is open to visitors.

### Palace of Versailles (1661–1710)

[![Palace of Versailles](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Versailles_Cour_de_Marbre_20170617_facade.jpg/1280px-Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Versailles_Cour_de_Marbre_20170617_facade.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Versailles_Cour_de_Marbre_20170617_facade.jpg)

<sub>Palace of Versailles, the Marble Court Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Versailles_Cour_de_Marbre_20170617_facade.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1661–1710
- **Type:** Royal palace and gardens
- **Location:** Versailles, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Palace%20of%20Versailles%20Louis%20Le%20Vau)

Louis XIII had a hunting lodge at Versailles, and from 1661 his son Louis XIV began transforming it. Le Vau wrapped the old brick château in a stone envelope from 1668, with a terrace facing the gardens. From 1678 Jules Hardouin-Mansart enlarged the palace, filling the terrace with the Hall of Mirrors and adding long north and south wings, and in 1682 the king moved the court and government there. Le Brun directed the decoration and Le Nôtre laid out the vast gardens with their parterres, fountains and Grand Canal. The palace became an instrument of rule, where nobles were kept close to the king in an elaborate etiquette. The Revolution forced Louis XVI to leave in 1789; Louis-Philippe made it a museum of French history in 1837, and the treaty ending the First World War was signed there in 1919.
