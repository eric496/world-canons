# Giacomo Barozzi da Vignola

**1507 – 1573** · Italian  
Born: Vignola, near Modena · Died: Rome  
Styles: [Mannerism](../movements/mannerism.md)  

> Architect of the Farnese and the Jesuits, designer of the Gesù in Rome and author of the most widely used handbook of the classical orders.

<sub>Portrait: Engraved portrait of Vignola from his Regola delli cinque ordini, 1562 · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vignola_Regola_1562.JPG)</sub>

## Biography

Vignola trained as a painter in Bologna, moved to Rome around 1530 and worked measuring ancient monuments for a Vitruvian academy, then spent time at the French court at Fontainebleau. Back in Rome he worked for Pope Julius III on the Villa Giulia and became architect to the powerful Farnese family, for whom he transformed a pentagonal fortress at Caprarola into a palace. In 1568 Cardinal Alessandro Farnese commissioned him to design the Gesù, the mother church of the Jesuit order, and he succeeded Michelangelo as architect of St Peter's.

His Regola delli cinque ordini d'architettura (1562) reduced the five orders to simple modular rules on clearly engraved plates, and it remained a standard textbook for architects and builders into the twentieth century. The Gesù, with its broad nave for preaching, side chapels and dome, became the model for Counter-Reformation churches across Catholic Europe and Latin America.

## Key buildings

### Church of the Gesù (1568–1584)

[![Church of the Gesù](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a6/Jesus_Church.jpg/1280px-Jesus_Church.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jesus_Church.jpg)

<sub>Church of the Gesù, Rome, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Jesus_Church.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1568–1584
- **Type:** Jesuit church
- **Location:** Rome, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Church%20of%20the%20Ges%C3%B9%20Giacomo%20Barozzi%20da%20Vignola)

Cardinal Alessandro Farnese funded the main church of the Society of Jesus, founded by Ignatius of Loyola, and Vignola designed it in 1568. The Jesuits required a church in which large congregations could see the altar and hear sermons. Vignola gave it a single wide barrel-vaulted nave with side chapels instead of aisles, a short transept and a dome over the crossing that lights the altar. After Vignola's death in 1573 Giacomo della Porta completed it with a different façade, two storeys linked by scrolls, which was widely imitated. In the late seventeenth century Giovanni Battista Gaulli painted the ceiling with the Triumph of the Name of Jesus, figures and clouds appearing to burst into the church, and Andrea Pozzo designed the altar of Saint Ignatius. The Gesù became the pattern for Jesuit churches across Europe, Latin America and Asia.
