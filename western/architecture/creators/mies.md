# Ludwig Mies van der Rohe

**1886 – 1969** · German-American  
Born: Aachen · Died: Chicago  
Styles: [Modernism & the International Style](../movements/modernism.md), [Late & Expressive Modernism](../movements/late-modernism.md)  

> German-American architect of the Barcelona Pavilion, the Farnsworth House and the Seagram Building, who refined modern architecture to steel, glass and space.

## Biography

Mies, the son of a stonemason, had no formal architectural education and worked for Peter Behrens in Berlin. In the early 1920s he made visionary projects for glass skyscrapers, directed the Weissenhof housing exhibition at Stuttgart in 1927, and designed the German Pavilion at the Barcelona International Exposition of 1929 and the Villa Tugendhat in Brno. He was the last director of the Bauhaus, closed under Nazi pressure in 1933, and emigrated to Chicago in 1938 to head the architecture school of what became the Illinois Institute of Technology, whose campus he designed. His American works include the Farnsworth House, the Lake Shore Drive apartments, the Seagram Building and the Neue Nationalgalerie in Berlin.

Mies sought a universal architecture of the industrial age: a precise steel or concrete frame, walls of glass, and open space that flows freely. His aphorisms 'less is more' and 'God is in the details' express his concern with proportion, fine materials and refined joints. His glass towers became the model of corporate architecture worldwide, much imitated and often cheapened.

### Barcelona Pavilion (1929; reconstructed 1983–1986)

- **Date:** 1929; reconstructed 1983–1986
- **Type:** Exhibition pavilion
- **Location:** Montjuïc, Barcelona, Spain
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Barcelona%20Pavilion%20Ludwig%20Mies%20van%20der%20Rohe)

Mies van der Rohe and Lilly Reich designed the German Pavilion at the 1929 Barcelona International Exposition as a representational building with no exhibits, used for the opening ceremony with the Spanish king. A flat roof rests on eight cruciform chrome-clad steel columns, freeing walls of onyx, green marble and glass to stand as independent planes that guide visitors through a continuous space, with two shallow pools, one holding a bronze statue by Georg Kolbe. The Barcelona Chair was designed for the pavilion. It was dismantled in 1930, but its photographs made it one of the most influential buildings of the century. A permanent reconstruction was built on the original site in 1983–86.

### Villa Tugendhat (1928–1930)

- **Date:** 1928–1930
- **Type:** House
- **Location:** Brno, Czech Republic
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Villa%20Tugendhat%20Ludwig%20Mies%20van%20der%20Rohe)

Grete and Fritz Tugendhat, from wealthy industrial families, commissioned Mies to build a house on a sloping site overlooking Brno. From the street, it appears as a low single storey; on the garden side, the main floor opens as a single living space about 280 square metres in area, divided only by a wall of golden onyx and a curved wall of Macassar ebony, with chrome cruciform columns. Two huge glass panes could be lowered electrically into the floor. Mies and Lilly Reich designed the furniture, including the Tugendhat and Brno chairs. The family, who were Jewish, fled in 1938; the house was confiscated, damaged in the war, and used for other purposes. Restored and opened as a museum, it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Farnsworth House (1945–1951)

- **Date:** 1945–1951
- **Type:** Weekend house
- **Location:** Plano, Illinois, United States
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Farnsworth%20House%20Ludwig%20Mies%20van%20der%20Rohe)

Edith Farnsworth, a Chicago physician, commissioned Mies to design a weekend retreat on a wooded site beside the Fox River. The house is a single glass-walled room between a floor slab and a roof slab, held about 1.6 metres above the floodplain by eight white-painted steel columns welded to the edges, with a terrace and floating steps. A wooden core contains the kitchen, bathrooms and fireplace. The house became the subject of a lawsuit between architect and client over costs, and Farnsworth complained about living in a glass box. It has been flooded several times. Philip Johnson's Glass House was inspired by it. It is now a museum owned by the National Trust for Historic Preservation.

### Seagram Building (1954–1958)

- **Date:** 1954–1958
- **Type:** Office tower
- **Location:** Park Avenue, New York City, United States
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Seagram%20Building%20Ludwig%20Mies%20van%20der%20Rohe)

Samuel Bronfman, head of the distillers Seagram, planned a headquarters on Park Avenue; his daughter Phyllis Lambert persuaded him to hire Mies van der Rohe, with Philip Johnson as collaborator. Mies set the 38-storey tower back from the street behind an open granite plaza with two pools, an unusual gift of space in Manhattan that influenced New York's later zoning rules. The structure is concrete-encased steel, but its façade is clad in bronze mullions and amber-tinted glass, with non-structural bronze I-beams applied to express the frame, an expensive refinement. Johnson designed the interiors, including the Four Seasons restaurant. Lavishly built and precisely detailed, the Seagram was the model for countless glass office towers worldwide.
