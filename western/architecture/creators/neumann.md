<sub>Portrait: Portrait of Balthasar Neumann, Museum für Franken, Würzburg · [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Balthasar_Neumann_W%C3%BCrzburg_Mainfr%C3%A4nkisches_Museum_Marienberg.jpg)</sub>

## Biography

Neumann trained as a bell- and gun-founder, came to Würzburg in 1711 and rose through the artillery of the prince-bishop's army, studying engineering and architecture and visiting Vienna, northern Italy and Paris. From 1720 he directed the building of the Würzburg Residence for the Schönborn prince-bishops, consulting French and Viennese architects but giving the palace its final form. He went on to design churches, palaces, bridges and town plans across Franconia and the Rhineland, including the staircase at Brühl, the abbey church at Neresheim and the pilgrimage church of Vierzehnheiligen.

Neumann's genius lay in space and structure. His vaults, like the unsupported ceiling over the Würzburg staircase that Tiepolo painted, were daring feats of engineering, and his church plans of interlocking ovals create interiors that seem to float and move. Working with stuccoists and painters, he brought the fusion of the arts in German Rococo to its height.

### Würzburg Residence (1720–1744)

[![Würzburg Residence](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Residencia%2C_Wurzburgo%2C_Alemania%2C_2025-10-03%2C_DD_104.jpg/1280px-Residencia%2C_Wurzburgo%2C_Alemania%2C_2025-10-03%2C_DD_104.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Residencia%2C_Wurzburgo%2C_Alemania%2C_2025-10-03%2C_DD_104.jpg)

<sub>Würzburg Residence, Würzburg, Germany Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Residencia%2C_Wurzburgo%2C_Alemania%2C_2025-10-03%2C_DD_104.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1720–1744
- **Type:** Prince-bishop's palace
- **Location:** Würzburg, Germany
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=W%C3%BCrzburg%20Residence%20Balthasar%20Neumann)

The Schönborn prince-bishops of Würzburg, rich and ambitious rulers in Franconia, built the Residence from 1720 with Balthasar Neumann in charge, consulting leading architects from Paris and Vienna such as Robert de Cotte, Germain Boffrand and Johann Lukas von Hildebrandt. Around a forecourt are long wings with five courtyards, and the interiors include the Imperial Hall, the court chapel and the Mirror Cabinet. The highlight is the staircase, roofed by a single vault some 18 by 30 metres without supports, a daring structure that critics predicted would collapse. In 1752–53 Giovanni Battista Tiepolo painted it with an allegory of Apollo and the four continents, among the largest frescoes ever made. The vault survived Allied bombing in 1945, which destroyed much of the palace and city; the palace was restored and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Basilica of the Fourteen Holy Helpers (Vierzehnheiligen) (1743–1772)

[![Basilica of the Fourteen Holy Helpers (Vierzehnheiligen)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Vierzehnheiligen_Fassade.JPG/960px-Vierzehnheiligen_Fassade.JPG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vierzehnheiligen_Fassade.JPG)

<sub>Basilica of the Fourteen Holy Helpers (Vierzehnheiligen), Bad Staffelstein, Bavaria, Germany Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vierzehnheiligen_Fassade.JPG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1743–1772
- **Type:** Pilgrimage church
- **Location:** Bad Staffelstein, Bavaria, Germany
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Basilica%20of%20the%20Fourteen%20Holy%20Helpers%20%28Vierzehnheiligen%29%20Balthasar%20Neumann)

Vierzehnheiligen, on a hillside above the Main valley, marks the place where a shepherd reported visions of the Christ Child and the Fourteen Holy Helpers, saints invoked against disease and misfortune, in 1445–46. The Cistercian abbey of Langheim commissioned a new pilgrimage church, and after another architect had begun building in the wrong place, Neumann took over in 1743. He had to keep the altar of the apparition in the middle of the nave, and designed a plan of interlocking ovals that places it at the heart of the church. The interior, decorated by the Feichtmayr and Übelherr workshops with stucco, and with frescoes by Giuseppe Appiani, is white, pink and gold, with abundant windows. The free-standing altar of the Fourteen Holy Helpers, shaped like a Rococo carriage or coral reef, is the focus of pilgrimage to this day.
