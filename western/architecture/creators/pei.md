# I. M. Pei

**1917 – 2019** · Chinese-American  
Born: Guangzhou, China · Died: New York City  
Styles: [Late & Expressive Modernism](../movements/late-modernism.md)  

> Chinese-American architect of geometric modern buildings, among them the East Building of the National Gallery in Washington and the glass pyramid of the Louvre.

## Biography

Ieoh Ming Pei went to the United States in 1935 to study architecture at the University of Pennsylvania and MIT, and later at Harvard under Gropius. He worked for the developer William Zeckendorf before founding his own firm, later Pei Cobb Freed. His works include the National Center for Atmospheric Research in Colorado, the John F. Kennedy Library, the East Building of the National Gallery of Art (1978), the Grand Louvre project with its pyramid (1989), the Bank of China Tower in Hong Kong (1990), the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame and the Museum of Islamic Art in Doha.

Pei worked in a sharply geometric modernism of triangles, prisms and precise stone and glass, combining it with careful response to historic settings. The Louvre pyramid, attacked at first in France, became a beloved landmark. He received the Pritzker Prize in 1983.

### Louvre Pyramid (1984–1989)

> 🖼️ **[View it at Musée du Louvre →](https://www.louvre.fr/)**  
> <sub>Still under copyright, so it is not reproduced here.</sub>

- **Date:** 1984–1989
- **Type:** Museum entrance
- **Location:** Cour Napoléon, Louvre, Paris, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Louvre%20Pyramid%20I.%20M.%20Pei)

President François Mitterrand's Grand Louvre project moved the finance ministry out of the palace and reorganised the museum, and he chose I. M. Pei in 1983 without a competition. Pei proposed a vast underground reception hall under the Cour Napoléon, lit and marked by a glass pyramid about 21 metres high, flanked by smaller pyramids and fountains. Its steel framework and specially made clear glass were designed for maximum transparency, so that the historic façades are seen through it. The project was fiercely attacked in France as an alien intrusion, but the pyramid, opened in 1989, became one of the most recognisable images of Paris. An inverted pyramid was added in the underground shopping arcade in 1993.

### Bank of China Tower (1985–1990)

[![Bank of China Tower](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/25/HK_Bank_of_China_Tower_2008_%282%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:HK_Bank_of_China_Tower_2008_%282%29.jpg)

<sub>Bank of China Tower, Central, Hong Kong Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:HK_Bank_of_China_Tower_2008_%282%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1985–1990
- **Type:** Office skyscraper
- **Location:** Central, Hong Kong
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Bank%20of%20China%20Tower%20I.%20M.%20Pei)

The Bank of China commissioned Pei, whose father had been a senior figure in the bank, to design its Hong Kong headquarters, to be finished before the handover of the colony to China in 1997. The 367-metre tower, the tallest in Asia when completed in 1990, rises from a square divided diagonally into four triangles, which terminate at different heights like the shoots of bamboo, an image of growth. Its structure is a space frame of diagonal braces expressed on the façade in triangles, which carries wind loads efficiently with much less steel than a conventional tower. Its sharp angles drew criticism from practitioners of feng shui.
