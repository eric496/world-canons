# Apollodorus of Damascus

**c. AD 60 – c. 130** · Roman (Syrian Greek)  
Born: Damascus, Roman Syria · Died: ?  
Styles: [Ancient Roman](../movements/ancient-roman.md)  

> Engineer and architect to the emperor Trajan, builder of the bridge over the Danube and of Trajan's Forum in Rome. He is traditionally credited with Trajan's Column and Trajan's Market.

## Biography

Apollodorus came from Damascus in the Roman province of Syria and became Trajan's chief engineer. During the Dacian Wars he built a great bridge across the Danube at Drobeta, around 105, famous in antiquity and shown on Trajan's Column. In Rome he designed the Forum of Trajan, the largest and most splendid of the imperial forums, with its Basilica Ulpia, libraries and column, completed around 112. The adjoining market complex and the Baths of Trajan are often attributed to him as well.

According to the historian Cassius Dio, Apollodorus mocked the young Hadrian's architectural ambitions and was later banished and put to death by him, a story many historians treat with caution. Whatever the truth, his work shows the confidence of Roman engineering at the height of the empire: vast vaulted halls, terraced concrete markets and monumental planning that integrated architecture, sculpture and urban space. A treatise on siege engines under his name also survives.

## Key buildings

### Trajan's Column (AD 107–113)

[![Trajan's Column](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/65/Trajan%27s_Column_HD.jpg/960px-Trajan%27s_Column_HD.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Trajan%27s_Column_HD.jpg)

<sub>Trajan's Column, Trajan's Forum, Rome, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Trajan%27s_Column_HD.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** AD 107–113
- **Type:** Triumphal column
- **Location:** Trajan's Forum, Rome, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Trajan%27s%20Column%20Apollodorus%20of%20Damascus)

Trajan's Column stood in a courtyard between the libraries of Trajan's Forum, a complex usually credited to Apollodorus of Damascus, and was dedicated in 113. About thirty metres tall, it is built from nineteen drums of Carrara marble, hollowed out to contain a spiral staircase lit by small windows. A continuous relief band some 200 metres long spirals twenty-three times around the shaft, depicting Trajan's two campaigns against the Dacians with over 2,500 figures: marching, building, fighting and negotiating. The emperor's ashes were placed in its base, and a statue of Trajan crowned it until it was replaced in 1587 by Saint Peter. Its relief is a key source for the Roman army, and the column inspired imitations from the column of Marcus Aurelius to the Vendôme Column in Paris.
