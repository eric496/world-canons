# Romanesque master builders

**active 11th–12th century** · European  
Styles: [Carolingian & Romanesque](../movements/romanesque.md)  

> The masons and master builders, mostly anonymous, who raised the great abbeys, pilgrimage churches and cathedrals of eleventh- and twelfth-century Europe.

## Biography

Around the year 1000 a wave of church building spread across Western Europe, driven by monastic reform, pilgrimage, growing towns and newly powerful rulers such as the German emperors and the Norman dukes. Master masons travelled between building sites, and a few names survive in inscriptions, such as Buscheto at Pisa or Gislebertus, the sculptor of Autun. Most of the men who designed and built these churches, however, are unrecorded.

Romanesque buildings share thick walls, round arches, massive piers and an increasing ambition to vault naves in stone, partly for fireproofing and partly for grandeur and acoustics. Regional schools varied widely, from the striped marble of Tuscany to the pilgrimage churches of southern France and the Norman cathedrals of England. Their structural experiments, especially Durham's rib vaults, led directly to the Gothic.

### Speyer Cathedral (1030–1106)

[![Speyer Cathedral](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Speyer_dom_11.jpg/1280px-Speyer_dom_11.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Speyer_dom_11.jpg)

<sub>Speyer Cathedral, Speyer, Germany Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Speyer_dom_11.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1030–1106
- **Type:** Romanesque cathedral
- **Location:** Speyer, Germany
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Speyer%20Cathedral%20Romanesque%20master%20builders)

The emperor Conrad II began the cathedral at Speyer around 1030 as a dynastic burial church, and his grandson Henry IV rebuilt it on an even grander scale from about 1080, vaulting the nave in stone. With its long nave, towers at each end and domes over the crossing and westwork, it was meant to rival the great churches of antiquity and to proclaim imperial power at the height of the Investiture Contest with the papacy. The nave's groin vaults, among the largest of their time, rest on alternating piers with half-columns rising the full height of the walls. Eight emperors and kings are buried in its crypt. Damaged by fire in 1689, it was rebuilt and restored in the eighteenth to twentieth centuries and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Pisa Cathedral and Leaning Tower (1063–1372)

[![Pisa Cathedral and Leaning Tower](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c6/View_of_the_Cathedral_facade_from_the_Baptistry_%28Pisa%29.jpg/1280px-View_of_the_Cathedral_facade_from_the_Baptistry_%28Pisa%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:View_of_the_Cathedral_facade_from_the_Baptistry_%28Pisa%29.jpg)

<sub>Pisa Cathedral and Leaning Tower, Piazza dei Miracoli, Pisa, Italy Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:View_of_the_Cathedral_facade_from_the_Baptistry_%28Pisa%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1063–1372
- **Type:** Romanesque cathedral and bell tower
- **Location:** Piazza dei Miracoli, Pisa, Italy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pisa%20Cathedral%20and%20Leaning%20Tower%20Romanesque%20master%20builders)

Pisa, then a rich maritime republic, began its cathedral in 1063, paid for with spoils from a raid on Palermo. Its architect Buscheto is buried in the façade. Built of white and grey marble, it combines a basilica with a dome over the crossing, and its façade of stacked open arcades became the hallmark of Pisan Romanesque. The circular baptistery was begun in 1152 and the bell tower in 1173; the tower began to lean during construction because of soft ground, and its builders, resuming work after long pauses, curved the upper storeys to compensate. It was completed in 1372 and by the late twentieth century leaned about 5.5 degrees; engineering work in 1990–2001 reduced the tilt and stabilised it. With the cemetery, the Camposanto, the ensemble forms the Piazza dei Miracoli, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

### Basilica of Saint-Sernin (c. 1080–1120)

[![Basilica of Saint-Sernin](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7f/Basilique_Saint-Sernin_de_Toulouse_-_exposition_ouest-1-.jpg/1280px-Basilique_Saint-Sernin_de_Toulouse_-_exposition_ouest-1-.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Basilique_Saint-Sernin_de_Toulouse_-_exposition_ouest-1-.jpg)

<sub>Basilica of Saint-Sernin, Toulouse, France Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Basilique_Saint-Sernin_de_Toulouse_-_exposition_ouest-1-.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1080–1120
- **Type:** Pilgrimage church
- **Location:** Toulouse, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Basilica%20of%20Saint-Sernin%20Romanesque%20master%20builders)

Saint-Sernin was built over the tomb of Saint Saturninus, first bishop of Toulouse, to receive pilgrims on the way to Santiago de Compostela. Its altar was consecrated in 1096. It is a model pilgrimage church: a long barrel-vaulted nave with double aisles, a transept with aisles, and an ambulatory with radiating chapels that allowed crowds to circulate past relics without disturbing services. The building is mostly in the red brick of Toulouse with stone details, and its octagonal crossing tower rises in tiers. Sculpture of the late eleventh century, in the Porte Miègeville and the marble reliefs in the ambulatory, is among the earliest monumental Romanesque carving. Viollet-le-Duc's nineteenth-century restoration was partly reversed in the 1990s.

### Durham Cathedral (1093–1133)

[![Durham Cathedral](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d6/Durham_MMB_02_Cathedral.jpg/1280px-Durham_MMB_02_Cathedral.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Durham_MMB_02_Cathedral.jpg)

<sub>Durham Cathedral, Durham, England Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Durham_MMB_02_Cathedral.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1093–1133
- **Type:** Norman cathedral
- **Location:** Durham, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Durham%20Cathedral%20Romanesque%20master%20builders)

Durham Cathedral was begun in 1093 by Bishop William of St Calais, one of the Norman prelates installed after the Conquest, to house the shrine of Saint Cuthbert and the remains of Bede. Set high above a loop of the River Wear beside the castle, it was built largely in forty years. Its nave alternates huge compound piers with cylindrical columns carved with chevrons, spirals and flutes, and its stone ribbed vaults, with pointed transverse arches in the nave, are among the earliest in Europe, anticipating the structural system of Gothic. The Galilee Chapel and the Chapel of the Nine Altars were added in the twelfth and thirteenth centuries. Together with the castle it forms a UNESCO World Heritage Site and is often called the finest Norman building in England.
