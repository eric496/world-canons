# Eero Saarinen

**1910 – 1961** · Finnish-American  
Born: Kirkkonummi, Finland · Died: Ann Arbor, Michigan  
Styles: [Late & Expressive Modernism](../movements/late-modernism.md)  

> Finnish-American architect and designer of the Gateway Arch, the TWA Flight Center and the Tulip chair, who sought a distinct form for every building.

## Biography

Eero was the son of the Finnish architect Eliel Saarinen, who emigrated to the United States in 1923 and headed the Cranbrook Academy of Art in Michigan. Eero studied sculpture in Paris and architecture at Yale, worked with his father, and with Charles Eames won prizes for furniture design. His breakthrough came with the General Motors Technical Center (1948–56) and the competition for the Jefferson National Expansion Memorial in St Louis, won in 1948 and built as the Gateway Arch (1963–65). He went on to design the Kresge Auditorium at MIT, the TWA Flight Center, Dulles Airport and the CBS Building, but died of a brain tumour at fifty-one, leaving many works to be completed by his office.

Saarinen believed each building should have a style suited to its purpose, which critics took as inconsistency. His expressive concrete shells and swooping curves captured the optimism of postwar America, and his furniture for Knoll, including the Tulip and Womb chairs, remains in production.

### TWA Flight Center (1959–1962)

- **Date:** 1959–1962
- **Type:** Airport terminal
- **Location:** John F. Kennedy International Airport, New York City, United States
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=TWA%20Flight%20Center%20Eero%20Saarinen)

Trans World Airlines commissioned Eero Saarinen to design its terminal at what was then Idlewild Airport, and he sought to capture the excitement of flight. Four thin concrete shells, curved like wings and meeting at a central skylight, are supported on Y-shaped piers; under them, the interior flows without straight lines, with curving stairs, balconies, a sunken lounge with views of the runways, and tubular corridors to the gates, all in white tiles and red carpet. Saarinen died before it opened in 1962, and his office completed it. The terminal could not adapt to jumbo jets and security screening, and closed in 2001. Designated a New York City landmark in 1994, it reopened in 2019 as the lobby of the TWA Hotel.

### Gateway Arch (1963–1965)

[![Gateway Arch](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/St_Louis_night_expblend_cropped.jpg/1280px-St_Louis_night_expblend_cropped.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St_Louis_night_expblend_cropped.jpg)

<sub>Gateway Arch, St Louis, Missouri, United States Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St_Louis_night_expblend_cropped.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1963–1965
- **Type:** Monument
- **Location:** St Louis, Missouri, United States
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Gateway%20Arch%20Eero%20Saarinen)

In 1947–48 Eero Saarinen won a competition for the Jefferson National Expansion Memorial in St Louis, commemorating the westward expansion of the United States from the city once called the Gateway to the West. His design, a weighted catenary arch, is as wide at its base as it is tall, 192 metres, and is built of triangular sections with stainless-steel outer skins and carbon-steel inner skins, filled with concrete at the lower levels. Construction began in 1963 and the last section was set in 1965, after Saarinen's death. Small capsule trams inside each leg carry visitors to an observation room at the top. The area around it became the Gateway Arch National Park in 2018.
