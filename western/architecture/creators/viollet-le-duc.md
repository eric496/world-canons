# Eugène Viollet-le-Duc

**1814 – 1879** · French  
Born: Paris · Died: Lausanne  
Styles: [Gothic Revival](../movements/gothic-revival.md)  

> French architect, restorer and theorist who restored Notre-Dame de Paris, Carcassonne and Pierrefonds and taught that Gothic was a rational structural system.

## Biography

Viollet-le-Duc refused to attend the École des Beaux-Arts and taught himself by travelling and drawing medieval buildings. Prosper Mérimée, inspector of historic monuments, gave him the restoration of the abbey of Vézelay in 1840, and in 1844 he and Jean-Baptiste Lassus won the commission to restore Notre-Dame de Paris, for which he designed the new spire and many of the gargoyles and chimeras. He went on to restore Saint-Denis, Amiens, the walled city of Carcassonne and the castle of Pierrefonds for Napoleon III. His ten-volume Dictionnaire raisonné de l'architecture française appeared between 1854 and 1868.

He defined restoration as reinstating a building in a complete state that may never have existed at any time, an approach now widely criticised. Yet his analysis of Gothic as a rational system of forces, and his call in the Entretiens sur l'architecture for a modern architecture of iron and honest construction, inspired Art Nouveau architects such as Horta and Gaudí and early modernists. His drawings remain unrivalled records of medieval building.

## Key buildings

### Château de Pierrefonds (1857–1885)

[![Château de Pierrefonds](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d1/Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Pierrefonds_vu_depuis_le_Parc.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Pierrefonds_vu_depuis_le_Parc.jpg)

<sub>Château de Pierrefonds, Pierrefonds, Oise, France Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ch%C3%A2teau_de_Pierrefonds_vu_depuis_le_Parc.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1857–1885
- **Type:** Castle reconstruction
- **Location:** Pierrefonds, Oise, France
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ch%C3%A2teau%20de%20Pierrefonds%20Eug%C3%A8ne%20Viollet-le-Duc)

Pierrefonds was built around 1400 by Louis of Orléans, brother of Charles VI, and dismantled on the orders of Cardinal Richelieu in 1617. Napoleon III, who visited the ruins, commissioned Viollet-le-Duc in 1857 to restore part of it as a residence, and the project expanded into a full reconstruction. Viollet-le-Duc rebuilt walls, towers, machicolations and battlements according to his understanding of medieval military architecture, but the interiors, with painted decoration and carvings, are a nineteenth-century fantasy. Work stopped after the fall of the Second Empire in 1870 and finished after his death. Pierrefonds is a textbook case of the restoration philosophy of its architect, admired for its imagination and criticised for inventing rather than preserving.
