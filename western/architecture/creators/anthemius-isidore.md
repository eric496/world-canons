# Anthemius of Tralles & Isidore of Miletus

**active 6th century** · Byzantine Greek  
Styles: [Early Christian & Byzantine](../movements/early-christian-byzantine.md)  

> Two Greek mathematicians and engineers from Asia Minor whom the emperor Justinian commissioned to design Hagia Sophia in Constantinople, built in under six years from 532.

## Biography

Anthemius came from Tralles in western Asia Minor, from a family of doctors and scholars; he wrote on burning mirrors and conic sections and was famous for mechanical ingenuity. Isidore of Miletus was a geometer who taught in Constantinople and edited the works of Archimedes. After the Nika riots of 532 destroyed the earlier church of Hagia Sophia, Justinian chose these two theorists rather than conventional builders, and the new church was dedicated in 537. Anthemius seems to have died soon after.

Their solution, a vast dome on pendentives over a square bay, buttressed by half-domes to east and west, created the largest enclosed space of its age and appeared, as the court historian Procopius wrote, to hang from heaven. When the first dome collapsed after earthquakes in 558, Isidore's nephew, Isidore the Younger, rebuilt it higher and more stable. The pair stand at the head of a tradition of architects as mathematicians, and their building set the pattern for Byzantine and Ottoman domed architecture.

## Key buildings

### Hagia Sophia (532–537)

- **Date:** 532–537
- **Type:** Church, later mosque
- **Location:** Istanbul, Turkey
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Hagia%20Sophia%20Anthemius%20of%20Tralles%20%26%20Isidore%20of%20Miletus)

After the Nika riots burned the previous church in 532, the emperor Justinian had Hagia Sophia, the church of Holy Wisdom, rebuilt in less than six years under the mathematicians Anthemius of Tralles and Isidore of Miletus. They combined a basilica with a centralised plan: a dome about 31 metres across rises on four pendentives, curved triangles that turn a square into a circle, and is extended east and west by half-domes so that the nave forms one enormous space. A ring of forty windows at the base makes the dome seem to float on light, and the interior was clad in coloured marble and gold mosaic. The first dome fell in 558 and was rebuilt higher. For nearly a thousand years the cathedral of Constantinople, it became a mosque after the Ottoman conquest in 1453, with minarets added, a museum in 1934 and a mosque again in 2020. It inspired Ottoman architects such as Sinan.
