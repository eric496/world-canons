# Christopher Wren

> Astronomer turned architect who rebuilt London after the Great Fire of 1666, designing St Paul's Cathedral and over fifty City churches.

## Biography

Wren was a mathematician and astronomer, a professor at Gresham College and then Savilian Professor of Astronomy at Oxford, and a founder of the Royal Society. He turned to architecture in the 1660s with the Sheldonian Theatre at Oxford and Pembroke College Chapel at Cambridge, and visited Paris in 1665. After the Great Fire of 1666 he proposed a new plan for London, which was not adopted, and as Surveyor of the King's Works from 1669 he rebuilt St Paul's Cathedral (1675–1710) and fifty-one parish churches. He also designed the Royal Hospitals at Chelsea and Greenwich, parts of Hampton Court Palace and Trinity College Library, Cambridge.

Wren brought a scientist's mind to architecture, solving structural problems such as the triple dome of St Paul's, whose inner dome, brick cone and outer timber shell each do different work. His style draws on French and Italian Baroque and on Inigo Jones, but his steeples, rising in stacked stages, were an English invention imitated across the colonies. His epitaph in St Paul's tells the reader, in Latin, to look around for his monument.

### St Paul's Cathedral (1675–1710)

[![St Paul's Cathedral](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cb/St_Pauls_aerial_%28cropped%29.jpg/1280px-St_Pauls_aerial_%28cropped%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St_Pauls_aerial_%28cropped%29.jpg)

<sub>St Paul's Cathedral, City of London, England Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St_Pauls_aerial_%28cropped%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1675–1710
- **Type:** Cathedral
- **Location:** City of London, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=St%20Paul%27s%20Cathedral%20Christopher%20Wren)

Old St Paul's, a huge Gothic cathedral, was gutted in the Great Fire of 1666. Wren's first designs, including a Greek-cross 'Great Model', were rejected by the clergy, and his approved design of 1675 was a Latin-cross plan, which he altered as he built. The cathedral was completed in 1710, the first in England built by a single architect in his lifetime. Its dome, inspired by St Peter's and Bramante's Tempietto, consists of three parts: a low inner dome seen from inside, a brick cone that carries the stone lantern, and an outer timber dome covered with lead. Wren used screen walls along the upper aisles to hide the flying buttresses. The cathedral survived the Blitz in 1940, an image of defiance, and has been the scene of state funerals, jubilees and royal weddings.

### Old Royal Naval College (Royal Hospital for Seamen) (1696–1712)

[![Old Royal Naval College (Royal Hospital for Seamen)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Royal_Naval_College_Greenwich_view_from_the_Thames.jpg/1280px-Royal_Naval_College_Greenwich_view_from_the_Thames.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Royal_Naval_College_Greenwich_view_from_the_Thames.jpg)

<sub>Old Royal Naval College (Royal Hospital for Seamen), Greenwich, London, England Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Royal_Naval_College_Greenwich_view_from_the_Thames.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1696–1712
- **Type:** Hospital
- **Location:** Greenwich, London, England
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Old%20Royal%20Naval%20College%20%28Royal%20Hospital%20for%20Seamen%29%20Christopher%20Wren)

In 1694 Queen Mary II founded a hospital for disabled and retired seamen on the site of the old Tudor palace at Greenwich, and Wren gave his services free. Because the queen insisted on keeping the view from the river to Inigo Jones's Queen's House, Wren designed two pairs of courts separated by a broad avenue, with twin domes and colonnades over the Painted Hall and the chapel. Nicholas Hawksmoor and John Vanbrugh continued the work. James Thornhill painted the Painted Hall from 1707 to 1726, a vast celebration of Protestant monarchy and naval power. The hospital closed in 1869, and the buildings became the Royal Naval College in 1873; since 1998 they have housed the University of Greenwich and Trinity Laban. With the park and observatory they form the Maritime Greenwich World Heritage Site.
