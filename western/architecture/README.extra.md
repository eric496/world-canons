## About this section

**How the buildings were chosen.** The list follows the buildings that recur in standard histories of Western architecture and in architecture school surveys: works that founded or summed up a style, changed how buildings were made, or became symbols of their cities. Each architect is represented by one to five buildings. Breadth across centuries and countries was preferred to completeness for any one period, so many fine buildings are missing. Buildings by Western architects outside Europe and North America (Brasília, Sydney, Dhaka, Hong Kong, Beijing, Baku) are included where they are central to the architect's work.

**Anonymous builders.** Before the Renaissance most designers are unrecorded. Ancient, Byzantine, Romanesque and Gothic buildings whose architects are unknown are grouped under collective entries such as *Roman imperial builders* or *Master builders of Chartres*; where a name is known only by tradition, the description says so.

**Dates.** Dates are those of design and construction; long projects show their main campaign, and later additions are mentioned in the description.

**Images.** All photographs and pictures are hosted on Wikimedia Commons and link to their file pages, where the photographer, licence and attribution are given. Photographs of modern buildings are shown as Commons hosts them under the freedom-of-panorama rules of each country. Portraits of architects are paintings, drawings, engravings or sculptures in the public domain only; architects known only from photographs have no portrait.
