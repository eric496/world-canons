# Maurice Ravel

**1875 – 1937** · French  
Born: Ciboure, Basses-Pyrénées · Died: Paris  
Eras & schools: [Impressionism & French modernism](../movements/impressionism-music.md), [Neoclassicism & mid-century tonal modernism](../movements/neoclassicism-music.md)  

> A master of orchestration and precise craftsmanship, Ravel wrote Boléro, Daphnis et Chloé and some of the finest piano music of the century.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Ravel,_Maurice)

## Biography

Maurice Ravel was born in 1875 in Ciboure in the French Basque country, to a Swiss engineer father and a Basque mother, and grew up in Paris. He studied at the Conservatoire, where Gabriel Fauré taught him composition. His repeated failures to win the Prix de Rome culminated in a scandal in 1905 that led to the director's resignation. Works such as Jeux d'eau, the String Quartet and Miroirs established him alongside Debussy. For Diaghilev's Ballets Russes he wrote Daphnis et Chloé (1912). He served as a driver at the front in the First World War, and Le tombeau de Couperin commemorates friends killed in it. He toured North America in 1928, and Boléro appeared that year. A neurological illness ended his composing in the early 1930s, and he died in Paris in 1937 after brain surgery.

Ravel admired clarity, craftsmanship and objectivity, describing himself as artificial by nature. He was drawn to Classical forms, dance, Spanish music, fairy tales and jazz, as in his two piano concertos and Violin Sonata. His orchestration, including his arrangement of Mussorgsky's Pictures at an Exhibition, is a model of brilliance and precision. He is often grouped with Debussy as an Impressionist, but his music is more sharply drawn and Classical in its proportions.

### Daphnis et Chloé (1909–12)

[![Daphnis et Chloé](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/6f/Harvard_Theatre_Collection_-_Bakst%2C_MS_Thr_414.4_%289%29.jpg/1280px-Harvard_Theatre_Collection_-_Bakst%2C_MS_Thr_414.4_%289%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Harvard_Theatre_Collection_-_Bakst%2C_MS_Thr_414.4_%289%29.jpg)

<sub>Léon Bakst, design for Daphnis et Chloé (Harvard Theatre Collection) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Harvard_Theatre_Collection_-_Bakst%2C_MS_Thr_414.4_%289%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1909–12
- **Type:** Ballet (choreographic symphony), one act in three parts
- **Premiere / first published:** Théâtre du Châtelet, Paris, 8 June 1912, Ballets Russes, choreographed by Michel Fokine
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Daphnis%20et%20Chlo%C3%A9%20Maurice%20Ravel)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Daphnis+et+Chlo%C3%A9+Ravel)

Ravel wrote Daphnis et Chloé for Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes, based on the ancient Greek novel by Longus about the love of a shepherd and shepherdess on the island of Lesbos. Chloé is abducted by pirates, and the god Pan comes to her rescue. Ravel called it a choreographic symphony and built it from a few recurring themes. The orchestra is enormous and includes a wordless chorus. The ballet's premiere, with Vaslav Nijinsky and Tamara Karsavina, was overshadowed by Nijinsky's scandalous L'après-midi d'un faune in the same season. The music survives mainly in the two concert suites, especially the second, which opens with the sunrise, a radiant passage of murmuring strings and birdsong.

### Boléro (1928)

- **Date:** 1928
- **Type:** Ballet music for orchestra
- **Premiere / first published:** Paris Opéra, 22 November 1928, danced by Ida Rubinstein
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Bol%C3%A9ro%20Maurice%20Ravel)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Bol%C3%A9ro+Ravel)

The dancer Ida Rubinstein commissioned Boléro from Ravel for her company. Ravel described it as an experiment: a single melody in two parts, repeated eighteen times over an insistent snare-drum rhythm, with no development, only an orchestral crescendo, as each repetition is given to different instruments and combinations. In the last minutes the music modulates abruptly and collapses. Ravel was surprised by its popularity, and once said it contained no music. The original choreography by Bronislava Nijinska was set in a Spanish tavern. It became one of the most performed orchestral works of the twentieth century, and its copyright long earned huge royalties.
