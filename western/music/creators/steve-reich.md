# Steve Reich

**1936** · American  
Born: New York · Died: ?  
Eras & schools: [Minimalism & new simplicity](../movements/minimalism-music.md)  

> A founder of minimalism, Reich builds music from repeating patterns that gradually shift out of phase, culminating in Music for 18 Musicians.

## Biography

Steve Reich was born in New York in 1936, studied philosophy at Cornell University and composition at the Juilliard School and Mills College, where he studied with Luciano Berio. In San Francisco in the mid-1960s he discovered phasing with tape loops in It's Gonna Rain, then transferred it to live performers in Piano Phase. In 1966 he founded his own ensemble, and in 1970 he studied drumming in Ghana. Music for 18 Musicians (1976) brought him a wide audience. Later works such as Different Trains (1988), about wartime train journeys and the Holocaust, use recorded speech; Double Sextet won the Pulitzer Prize in 2009.

Reich's music emphasises audible processes: listeners can hear how the music is built as it unfolds. He draws on African drumming, Balinese gamelan, Hebrew cantillation, jazz and the Western canon from Pérotin to Bach. He has influenced composers, rock and electronic musicians alike.

## Key works

### Music for 18 Musicians (1974–76)

- **Date:** 1974–76
- **Type:** Ensemble work
- **Premiere / first published:** The Town Hall, New York, 24 April 1976, Steve Reich and Musicians
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Music%20for%2018%20Musicians%20Steve%20Reich)

Reich wrote Music for 18 Musicians for his own ensemble, which consists of violin, cello, two clarinets doubling bass clarinet, four women's voices, four pianos, three marimbas, two xylophones and a metallophone. The work begins and ends with a cycle of eleven chords, and each chord becomes the basis of a short section, lasting about five minutes. There is no conductor: the metallophone gives cues for changes, as in a Balinese gamelan or West African drumming, and the length of some passages depends on the breath of the singers and clarinettists. The 1978 ECM recording sold more than 100,000 copies, which was unusual for new music. It is often considered Reich's masterpiece and one of the defining works of minimalism.
