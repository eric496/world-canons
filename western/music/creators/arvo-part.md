# Arvo Pärt

**1935** · Estonian  
Born: Paide, Estonia · Died: ?  
Eras & schools: [Minimalism & new simplicity](../movements/minimalism-music.md)  

> Pärt's spare, bell-like tintinnabuli style, steeped in chant and Orthodox spirituality, made him one of the most performed living composers.

## Biography

Arvo Pärt was born in 1935 in Paide, Estonia, and studied at the Tallinn Conservatory, working as a sound engineer for Estonian radio. His early works used serial techniques and collage, which brought him into conflict with Soviet authorities, especially Credo (1968) with its religious text. After years of near silence spent studying Gregorian chant and Renaissance polyphony, he emerged in 1976 with Für Alina and the tintinnabuli style, followed by Fratres, Tabula rasa and Spiegel im Spiegel (all 1977). He emigrated with his family in 1980, settling in Berlin, and returned to Estonia in the 2000s.

Tintinnabuli combines a melodic voice moving stepwise with a voice outlining the notes of a triad, like bells. The music is slow, quiet and often sacred, drawing on Orthodox Christianity, which Pärt adopted. Recordings on the ECM label from 1984 brought him international fame.

### Tabula rasa (1977)

- **Date:** 1977
- **Type:** Double concerto for two violins, string orchestra and prepared piano
- **Premiere / first published:** Tallinn, 30 September 1977, Gidon Kremer and Tatiana Grindenko
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Tabula%20rasa%20Arvo%20P%C3%A4rt)

Tabula rasa was written for the violinists Gidon Kremer and Tatiana Grindenko and the conductor Eri Klas. It is in two movements: Ludus (Game), in which the soloists and orchestra repeat and expand phrases separated by silences, building to a climax, and Silentium, a slow, multi-layered canon in which the voices move at different speeds and gradually sink into silence. It uses the tintinnabuli technique that Pärt had developed the year before, in which one voice outlines a triad while another moves stepwise. Its 1984 recording on the ECM label introduced Pärt to Western audiences.

### Spiegel im Spiegel (1978)

- **Date:** 1978
- **Type:** Duo for violin (or cello) and piano
- **Premiere / first published:** Written for the violinist Vladimir Spivakov, 1978
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Spiegel%20im%20Spiegel%20Arvo%20P%C3%A4rt)

Spiegel im Spiegel (Mirror in the Mirror) was one of Pärt's last works written before he left the Soviet Union. A violin plays slow, simple scale phrases that grow by one note each time, moving alternately up and down from the note A, over a piano accompaniment of rising triads in F major, with deep bass notes and high bell-like notes. The title refers to the infinity of images produced by parallel mirrors. Its calm simplicity has made it one of Pärt's most popular works, used in many films and dance pieces.
