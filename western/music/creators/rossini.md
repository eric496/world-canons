## Biography

Gioachino Rossini was born in Pesaro in 1792 to musician parents and studied in Bologna. His first opera was staged in Venice in 1810, and within a few years he was the leading composer in Italy, writing up to four operas a year. Tancredi and L'italiana in Algeri (both 1813) and Il barbiere di Siviglia (1816) made him famous across Europe, and he spent several years writing for Naples. In 1824 he moved to Paris, where his last opera, Guillaume Tell, appeared in 1829. He then wrote no more operas, for reasons of health and perhaps changing taste, though he later wrote a Stabat Mater, the Petite messe solennelle and many piano and vocal pieces. He died near Paris in 1868.

Rossini's comic operas are celebrated for their sparkle and energy, especially the famous Rossini crescendo, in which a phrase repeats with ever-growing orchestration. His serious operas developed the conventions of Italian romantic opera that Bellini, Donizetti and the young Verdi inherited. Guillaume Tell helped create French grand opera. His wit and gastronomy became legendary, and Barber has never left the repertoire.

## Key works

### The Barber of Seville (Il barbiere di Siviglia) (1816)

[![The Barber of Seville (Il barbiere di Siviglia)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/55/Alexandre_Fragonard_-_Sc%C3%A8ne_de_L%27orage_%28Barbier_de_S%C3%A9ville%29.jpg/1280px-Alexandre_Fragonard_-_Sc%C3%A8ne_de_L%27orage_%28Barbier_de_S%C3%A9ville%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Alexandre_Fragonard_-_Sc%C3%A8ne_de_L%27orage_%28Barbier_de_S%C3%A9ville%29.jpg)

- **Date:** 1816
- **Type:** Opera buffa, two acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Teatro Argentina, Rome, 20 February 1816
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Barber%20of%20Seville%20%28Il%20barbiere%20di%20Siviglia%29%20Gioachino%20Rossini)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Il+barbiere+di+Siviglia+Rossini)

Rossini wrote The Barber of Seville in a few weeks, to a libretto by Cesare Sterbini based on Beaumarchais's comedy, the prequel to The Marriage of Figaro. Count Almaviva, with the help of the resourceful barber Figaro, wins the ward Rosina from her guardian, Dr Bartolo, who wants to marry her himself. The premiere was a fiasco, disrupted by supporters of the composer Giovanni Paisiello, whose earlier opera on the same story was popular, and by stage accidents, but the second night was a triumph. The overture had been used in two earlier operas. With its patter songs, comic ensembles and crescendos, and arias such as Figaro's Largo al factotum and Rosina's Una voce poco fa, it has never left the stage.
