# Philip Glass

**1937** · American  
Born: Baltimore, Maryland · Died: ?  
Eras & schools: [Minimalism & new simplicity](../movements/minimalism-music.md)  

> Glass's repetitive, additive music reached mass audiences through operas such as Einstein on the Beach and his film scores.

## Biography

Philip Glass was born in Baltimore in 1937 and studied at the University of Chicago and the Juilliard School, then with Nadia Boulanger in Paris, where he also worked with the Indian musician Ravi Shankar. Back in New York he formed the Philip Glass Ensemble in 1968 and, for years, supported himself as a plumber and taxi driver. Einstein on the Beach, created with the director Robert Wilson, premiered in 1976. Satyagraha (1980), about Gandhi, and Akhnaten (1984) completed a trilogy of 'portrait operas'. His film scores include Koyaanisqatsi (1982) and The Hours (2002).

Glass prefers to speak of 'music with repetitive structures' rather than minimalism. His music develops through additive and subtractive rhythmic processes over simple harmonies, with the amplified sound of his ensemble. His later works, including fourteen symphonies and many string quartets, are more conventional in form, and his collaborations span rock, dance and theatre.

## Key works

### Einstein on the Beach (1975–76)

- **Date:** 1975–76
- **Type:** Opera in four acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Festival d'Avignon, 25 July 1976; Metropolitan Opera House, New York, November 1976
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Einstein%20on%20the%20Beach%20Philip%20Glass)

Glass and the director Robert Wilson created Einstein on the Beach, a work without a conventional plot about the figure of Albert Einstein, organised around images such as a train, a trial and a spaceship, connected by short interludes called knee plays. The texts include numbers, solfège syllables and spoken texts by Christopher Knowles, Lucinda Childs and Samuel M. Johnson. The music, for the amplified Philip Glass Ensemble, chorus and solo violin, is built on repeated patterns, with additive rhythmic processes. It lasts around five hours without an interval, and the audience is free to come and go. The two sold-out performances at the Metropolitan Opera in 1976 left Glass in debt. It is regarded as a landmark of contemporary opera.
