# Modest Mussorgsky

**1839 – 1881** · Russian  
Born: Karevo, Pskov Governorate, Russia · Died: Saint Petersburg  
Eras & schools: [Nationalist schools](../movements/nationalist-schools.md)  

> The most original of the Russian 'Mighty Handful', Mussorgsky wrote the opera Boris Godunov and Pictures at an Exhibition in a raw, deeply Russian idiom.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Mussorgsky,_Modest)

## Biography

Modest Mussorgsky was born in 1839 on his family's estate at Karevo in the Pskov region and trained for the army, serving as an officer in the Preobrazhensky Guards. In the late 1850s he joined the circle of Mily Balakirev, later called the Mighty Handful, which also included Borodin, Cui and Rimsky-Korsakov and aimed to create a national Russian music. After the emancipation of the serfs ruined his family's estate, he worked as a civil servant. Boris Godunov, rejected in its first version, was staged at the Mariinsky Theatre in 1874. Alcoholism wrecked his health, and he died in a military hospital in Saint Petersburg in 1881, leaving several works unfinished.

Mussorgsky sought to capture the rhythms of Russian speech and the life of the people with brutal honesty, using modal harmonies, folk-like melody and unconventional forms that his peers considered crude. After his death Rimsky-Korsakov revised and reorchestrated Boris and other works, which helped them win fame but smoothed away their roughness; today the original versions are preferred. Debussy, Ravel and later Shostakovich admired him greatly, and Ravel's orchestration of Pictures at an Exhibition is famous.

### Boris Godunov (1868–69; revised 1871–72)

- **Date:** 1868–69; revised 1871–72
- **Type:** Opera, prologue and four acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Mariinsky Theatre, Saint Petersburg, 8 February 1874 (revised version)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Boris%20Godunov%20Modest%20Mussorgsky)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Boris+Godunov+Mussorgsky)

Mussorgsky based his opera on Pushkin's drama about Boris Godunov, who became Tsar in 1598 after the death of the young heir Dmitry, whom he is suspected of having had murdered, and on the historian Karamzin. The first version, completed in 1869, was rejected by the Imperial Theatres, partly because it lacked a major female role. Mussorgsky revised it, adding a Polish act with a love story, and it was staged in 1874. The crowd scenes, in which the Russian people are central, the coronation scene with its pealing bells, and Boris's hallucinations of guilt are powerful. After Mussorgsky's death, Rimsky-Korsakov prepared a polished version that made the opera famous; since the mid-twentieth century the composer's own versions have increasingly been preferred.

### Pictures at an Exhibition (1874)

- **Date:** 1874
- **Type:** Piano suite (orchestrated by Ravel, 1922)
- **Premiere / first published:** Composed June 1874; published 1886; Ravel's orchestration premiered in Paris, 19 October 1922
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pictures%20at%20an%20Exhibition%20Modest%20Mussorgsky)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Pictures+at+an+Exhibition+Mussorgsky)

Mussorgsky wrote this piano suite in a few weeks in 1874 after a memorial exhibition of drawings and watercolours by his friend the artist and architect Viktor Hartmann, who had died the previous year. Ten pieces depict individual pictures, such as a gnome, an old castle, a Polish ox-cart, unhatched chicks in their shells, the witch Baba Yaga's hut on hen's legs and Hartmann's design for a great gate in Kiev. They are linked by a recurring Promenade, representing the visitor walking around the gallery. Only a few of Hartmann's pictures survive. The suite was published only after Mussorgsky's death. It became famous in Ravel's brilliant orchestration of 1922, commissioned by the conductor Serge Koussevitzky.
