## Biography

Igor Stravinsky was born in 1882 at Oranienbaum near Saint Petersburg, the son of a leading bass at the Mariinsky Theatre. He studied law but took private lessons in composition with Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov. Sergei Diaghilev commissioned The Firebird for his Ballets Russes in Paris in 1910, followed by Petrushka (1911) and The Rite of Spring (1913), whose premiere caused one of the most famous scandals in music history. Cut off from Russia by war and revolution, he lived in Switzerland and then France, becoming a French citizen in 1934. In 1939 he moved to the United States, settling in Hollywood, and became an American citizen in 1945. From the late 1940s his assistant Robert Craft helped turn him towards serial music. He died in New York in 1971 and was buried in Venice near Diaghilev.

Stravinsky's Russian ballets use folk-derived melodies, layered ostinatos, clashing harmonies and violently shifting irregular metres. From Pulcinella (1920), based on eighteenth-century music, he led the neoclassical movement, borrowing older forms and styles with ironic detachment, as in the Symphony of Psalms and the opera The Rake's Progress. His late serial works, such as Agon and Threni, completed a career of constant reinvention. Few composers had a wider influence on the music of the twentieth century.

### The Firebird (1909–10)

[![The Firebird](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/L%C3%A9on_Bakst_001.jpg/960px-L%C3%A9on_Bakst_001.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:L%C3%A9on_Bakst_001.jpg)

<sub>Léon Bakst, costume design for the Firebird, 1910 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:L%C3%A9on_Bakst_001.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1909–10
- **Type:** Ballet, two scenes
- **Premiere / first published:** Palais Garnier, Paris, 25 June 1910, Ballets Russes, choreographed by Michel Fokine
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Firebird%20Igor%20Stravinsky)

Diaghilev commissioned The Firebird after Anatoly Lyadov failed to deliver the score. The story combines Russian folk tales: Prince Ivan captures the magical Firebird, who gives him a feather in exchange for her freedom; he later uses it to overcome the immortal sorcerer Kashchei and free thirteen princesses. Stravinsky, then twenty-eight, wrote a brilliant score in the style of his teacher Rimsky-Korsakov, using chromatic harmonies for the magic world and Russian folk melodies for the human characters. The premiere was a triumph. Stravinsky later made three concert suites, and the Infernal Dance of Kashchei, the Berceuse and the Finale became popular.

### Petrushka (1910–11)

- **Date:** 1910–11
- **Type:** Ballet, four scenes
- **Premiere / first published:** Théâtre du Châtelet, Paris, 13 June 1911, Ballets Russes, with Nijinsky as Petrushka
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Petrushka%20Igor%20Stravinsky)

Petrushka began as a concert piece for piano and orchestra, which Diaghilev persuaded Stravinsky to turn into a ballet. With the designer Alexandre Benois, he set it at a Shrovetide fair in Saint Petersburg in the 1830s, where a magician brings three puppets to life: the sad Petrushka, the Ballerina and the brutal Moor. Petrushka, jealous of the Moor, is killed by him, and his ghost appears above the theatre. The 'Petrushka chord', combining C major and F-sharp major triads, symbolises the puppet's double nature. Stravinsky used street songs and folk tunes, and created layered textures of the fair's crowds. Vaslav Nijinsky danced the title role at the premiere, choreographed by Fokine.

### The Rite of Spring (Le sacre du printemps) (1911–13)

[![The Rite of Spring (Le sacre du printemps)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/62/Roerich_Rite_of_Spring.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Roerich_Rite_of_Spring.jpg)

<sub>Nicholas Roerich, design for The Rite of Spring, 1913 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Roerich_Rite_of_Spring.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1911–13
- **Type:** Ballet, two parts
- **Premiere / first published:** Théâtre des Champs-Élysées, Paris, 29 May 1913, Ballets Russes, choreographed by Vaslav Nijinsky
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Rite%20of%20Spring%20%28Le%20sacre%20du%20printemps%29%20Igor%20Stravinsky)

The Rite of Spring was conceived by Stravinsky and the painter and archaeologist Nicholas Roerich, who designed the sets and costumes, as a series of scenes of prehistoric Russian rituals celebrating the coming of spring, culminating in a young girl dancing herself to death. It opens with a high bassoon solo based on a Lithuanian folk melody and moves through violent, irregular rhythms, polytonal harmonies and hammering ostinatos. At the premiere, Nijinsky's choreography, with its turned-in feet and heavy stamping, and the music provoked an uproar in the audience, with shouting and fights that, according to some accounts, drowned out the orchestra; the extent of the riot has probably been exaggerated in later accounts. A year later, the concert performance in Paris was a triumph. It is considered one of the most influential works of the twentieth century.

### Symphony of Psalms (1930)

- **Date:** 1930
- **Type:** Choral symphony
- **Premiere / first published:** Palais des Beaux-Arts, Brussels, 13 December 1930; Boston premiere 19 December 1930
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Symphony%20of%20Psalms%20Igor%20Stravinsky)

The conductor Serge Koussevitzky commissioned this symphony for the fiftieth anniversary of the Boston Symphony Orchestra, but its premiere took place in Brussels a few days earlier. Stravinsky, who had returned to the Russian Orthodox Church in 1926, set Latin texts from the Psalms in the Vulgate translation. He omitted violins, violas and clarinets, giving prominence to woodwinds, brass, two pianos and a harp. The first movement is a prayer, the second a double fugue, and the third, the longest, ends with a slow 'Laudate Dominum' over a repeated figure in the bass, like bells. It is one of the masterpieces of Stravinsky's neoclassical period.
