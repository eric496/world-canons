# Benjamin Britten

**1913 – 1976** · British  
Born: Lowestoft, Suffolk · Died: Aldeburgh, Suffolk  
Eras & schools: [Neoclassicism & mid-century tonal modernism](../movements/neoclassicism-music.md)  

> The leading British composer of the twentieth century, Britten revived English opera with Peter Grimes and wrote the War Requiem.

## Biography

Benjamin Britten was born in Lowestoft in 1913, the son of a dentist, and studied privately with Frank Bridge and then at the Royal College of Music. In the 1930s he wrote music for documentary films and collaborated with W. H. Auden. A pacifist, he went to America with his partner, the tenor Peter Pears, in 1939, returning in 1942 as a conscientious objector. Peter Grimes, premiered at Sadler's Wells in June 1945, launched a series of operas including Billy Budd, The Turn of the Screw and Death in Venice. In 1948 he and Pears founded the Aldeburgh Festival in Suffolk. The War Requiem was written for the consecration of the new Coventry Cathedral in 1962. He was made a life peer shortly before his death in Aldeburgh in 1976.

Britten's music is tonal but distinctively modern, with brilliant writing for voice, a keen sense of drama and often a theme of innocence destroyed or the outsider persecuted by society. He drew on Purcell, Mahler and Balinese gamelan, and wrote widely for amateurs and children, as in Noye's Fludde and The Young Person's Guide to the Orchestra.

### Peter Grimes (1944–45)

- **Date:** 1944–45
- **Type:** Opera, prologue and three acts, Op. 33
- **Premiere / first published:** Sadler's Wells Theatre, London, 7 June 1945
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Peter%20Grimes%20Benjamin%20Britten)

Britten read an article by E. M. Forster about the Suffolk poet George Crabbe while in California in 1941, and decided to write an opera on Crabbe's poem The Borough. The libretto, by Montagu Slater, portrays Peter Grimes, a rough, isolated fisherman suspected of having caused the death of his young apprentices, who is driven by the hostility of the townspeople to sail out to sea and sink his boat. The Four Sea Interludes evoke the North Sea coast in different moods. The opera, premiered a month after the end of the war in Europe, with Peter Pears in the title role, was a triumph and established Britten as a leading opera composer. Its theme of the outsider persecuted by the crowd has often been linked to his position as a gay man and pacifist.

### War Requiem (1961–62)

- **Date:** 1961–62
- **Type:** Choral and orchestral work, Op. 66
- **Premiere / first published:** St Michael's Cathedral, Coventry, 30 May 1962
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=War%20Requiem%20Benjamin%20Britten)

Britten wrote the War Requiem for the consecration of the new Coventry Cathedral, built next to the ruins of the medieval cathedral destroyed by German bombing in 1940. He interwove the Latin requiem mass, sung by soprano, chorus and orchestra, with nine poems by Wilfred Owen, killed in the last week of the First World War, sung by tenor and baritone with a chamber orchestra, and a boys' choir with organ placed in the distance. He intended the three soloists to be from Britain, Germany and the Soviet Union, though the Soviet soprano Galina Vishnevskaya was not allowed to travel for the premiere. The work ends with Owen's Strange Meeting, in which two dead soldiers from opposing sides meet. The Decca recording of 1963 sold hundreds of thousands of copies.
