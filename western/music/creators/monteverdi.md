# Claudio Monteverdi

**1567 – 1643** · Italian  
Born: Cremona, Duchy of Milan · Died: Venice  
Eras & schools: [Late Renaissance & Venetian](../movements/late-renaissance-music.md), [Early Baroque & opera](../movements/early-baroque-music.md)  

> Monteverdi carried music from the Renaissance madrigal into the Baroque, and his Orfeo of 1607 is the earliest opera still regularly staged.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Monteverdi,_Claudio)

## Biography

Claudio Monteverdi was born in Cremona in 1567, the son of a barber-surgeon, and studied with the cathedral choirmaster Marc'Antonio Ingegneri, publishing his first music at fifteen. Around 1590 he joined the court of the Gonzaga in Mantua, becoming its maestro di cappella in 1601. There he wrote L'Orfeo (1607), and in 1610 he published the Vespers of the Blessed Virgin. In 1613 he became maestro di cappella at St Mark's in Venice, the most prestigious church post in Italy, which he held for thirty years. He took holy orders after his wife's death and in old age wrote operas for Venice's new public opera houses, including L'incoronazione di Poppea. He died in Venice in 1643.

Monteverdi published nine books of madrigals that trace the shift from the old style to the new. When the theorist Giovanni Artusi attacked his harsh dissonances, Monteverdi replied that in his second practice the words should be mistress of the music, not its servant. He embraced the basso continuo, the expressive solo voice and the concerted style with instruments, and invented an 'agitated' style (stile concitato) of rapid repeated notes for battle and anger. His operas show a deep understanding of human character, and since their twentieth-century revival they have been central to the opera repertory.

### L'Orfeo (1607)

- **Date:** 1607
- **Type:** Opera (favola in musica), prologue and five acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Ducal palace, Mantua, 24 February 1607
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=L%27Orfeo%20Claudio%20Monteverdi)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=L%27Orfeo+Monteverdi)

L'Orfeo, with a libretto by Alessandro Striggio, was commissioned for the Accademia degli Invaghiti in Mantua during the carnival of 1607, a few years after the first Florentine operas. It tells the myth of the singer Orpheus, who descends into the underworld to reclaim his dead wife Eurydice, but loses her again when he looks back. Monteverdi combined the new recitative with songs, choruses, dances and instrumental interludes, and specified an unusually rich orchestra, including strings, brass, recorders, organ, harpsichords and plucked instruments, to create different colours for the pastoral world and Hades. The centrepiece is Orpheus's florid plea to the ferryman Charon, Possente spirto. The score was printed in 1609 and 1615, which helped it survive. Revived in the twentieth century, it is the first opera to remain in the repertoire.

### Vespers of 1610 (Vespro della Beata Vergine) (1610)

- **Date:** 1610
- **Type:** Sacred collection for voices and instruments
- **Premiere / first published:** Published in Venice, 1610, dedicated to Pope Paul V
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Vespers%20of%201610%20%28Vespro%20della%20Beata%20Vergine%29%20Claudio%20Monteverdi)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Vespro+della+Beata+Vergine+Monteverdi)

Monteverdi published this collection in Venice in 1610 together with a conservative mass, dedicating them to Pope Paul V, perhaps hoping for a post in Rome. It includes music for Vespers on feasts of the Virgin: psalms, a hymn and two Magnificats, along with motets for solo voices, a sonata in which a soprano repeatedly sings Sancta Maria over instruments, and the echo piece Audi coelum. Plainchant runs through many movements as a structural thread, while around it Monteverdi deploys every modern device, from virtuoso solo singing and dance rhythms to grand choral and instrumental writing. Whether it was meant to be performed as a whole is debated. Since its revival in the twentieth century it has become one of the most popular works of the period.

### L'incoronazione di Poppea (1643)

- **Date:** 1643
- **Type:** Opera, prologue and three acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Teatro Santi Giovanni e Paolo, Venice, carnival 1643
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=L%27incoronazione%20di%20Poppea%20Claudio%20Monteverdi)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=L%27incoronazione+di+Poppea+Monteverdi)

Written for the public opera houses of Venice at the end of Monteverdi's life, to a libretto by Giovanni Francesco Busenello, Poppea tells how the Roman emperor Nero sets aside his wife Ottavia and the counsel of the philosopher Seneca to crown his mistress Poppea empress. It is one of the first operas on a historical subject, and it ends with the lovers' rapturous duet Pur ti miro, while the noble characters are exiled or dead. The music mixes recitative, arioso and songs to portray a wide range of characters, comic and tragic. The two surviving scores, from Venice and Naples, differ, and parts of the music, including probably the final duet, are by younger composers, a question of authorship that remains debated. Poppea is now regularly staged.
