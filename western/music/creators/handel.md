# George Frideric Handel

**1685 – 1759** · German-British  
Born: Halle, Duchy of Magdeburg · Died: London  
Eras & schools: [High Baroque](../movements/high-baroque.md)  

> German-born and London-based, Handel was the leading composer of Italian opera in England and created the English oratorio, above all Messiah.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Handel,_George_Frideric)

## Biography

Georg Friedrich Händel was born in Halle in 1685, the same year as Bach. His father wanted him to study law, but he became a violinist and harpsichordist at the Hamburg opera, and from 1706 spent four years in Italy, where he met Corelli and Scarlatti and had operas and oratorios performed with success. After a brief post as Kapellmeister to the Elector of Hanover, he settled in London, where his opera Rinaldo triumphed in 1711. When the Elector became George I of Britain, Handel remained in favour, writing the Water Music and later the coronation anthems, including Zadok the Priest. He ran opera companies for two decades and, when the fashion for Italian opera faded in the 1730s, turned to English oratorio. He became a British subject in 1727, went blind in his last years and was buried in Westminster Abbey in 1759.

Handel combined German counterpoint, Italian melody and English choral tradition. His operas, such as Giulio Cesare, are full of brilliant and moving arias, and his oratorios, from Saul and Israel in Egypt to Messiah and Jephtha, use the chorus with unprecedented grandeur. Unlike Bach, he never fell out of fashion: Messiah has been performed every year since his death, and the 1784 Handel commemoration in Westminster Abbey began the tradition of mass choral festivals. Haydn and Beethoven revered him.

### Water Music (1717)

- **Date:** 1717
- **Type:** Three orchestral suites, HWV 348–350
- **Premiere / first published:** On barges on the River Thames, London, 17 July 1717
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Water%20Music%20George%20Frideric%20Handel)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Water+Music+Handel)

On 17 July 1717 King George I and his courtiers travelled up the Thames from Whitehall to Chelsea on a barge, while some fifty musicians played Handel's music from another barge nearby. A newspaper reported that the king liked it so much that he had it repeated three times. The music, later grouped into three suites in F, D and G, consists of overtures, airs, minuets, hornpipes and bourrées. Horns and trumpets, which carried well outdoors, give the F and D major suites their brilliance. The story that the music reconciled Handel with the king, who was annoyed that he had abandoned his post in Hanover, is a later legend. Published in parts from 1733, the Water Music has remained one of Handel's most popular works.

### Giulio Cesare in Egitto (1724)

- **Date:** 1724
- **Type:** Opera seria, three acts, HWV 17
- **Premiere / first published:** King's Theatre, Haymarket, London, 20 February 1724
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Giulio%20Cesare%20in%20Egitto%20George%20Frideric%20Handel)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Giulio+Cesare+Handel)

Written for the Royal Academy of Music, the London opera company Handel directed, Giulio Cesare tells of Julius Caesar's arrival in Egypt after his victory over Pompey and his affair with Cleopatra, who schemes against her brother Ptolemy. The libretto by Nicola Haym is full of intrigue and contrasting emotions, giving rise to a series of magnificent arias. The title role was written for the celebrated castrato Senesino and Cleopatra for the soprano Francesca Cuzzoni. Cleopatra's arias, including V'adoro, pupille, accompanied by an on-stage band, and the lament Piangerò la sorte mia, are among Handel's finest. The opera was a great success in its time. After two centuries of neglect, it led the revival of Handel's operas from the 1920s onwards.

### Messiah (1741)

- **Date:** 1741
- **Type:** English oratorio, HWV 56
- **Premiere / first published:** Great Music Hall, Fishamble Street, Dublin, 13 April 1742
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Messiah%20George%20Frideric%20Handel)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Messiah+Handel)

Handel composed Messiah in about three and a half weeks in August and September 1741, to a libretto compiled from the King James Bible and the Book of Common Prayer by Charles Jennens. It was first performed in Dublin on 13 April 1742 as a charity concert, to an audience so eager that ladies were asked not to wear hoops in their dresses. Unlike Handel's other oratorios, it has no dramatic characters; its three parts reflect on the prophecy and birth of Christ, his passion and resurrection, and the redemption of the world. Choruses such as 'For unto us a child is born' and 'Hallelujah' alternate with arias like 'I know that my Redeemer liveth'. London received it coolly at first, but annual charity performances for the Foundling Hospital from 1750 made it popular. The custom of standing for the Hallelujah chorus is traditionally linked to George II, though there is no contemporary evidence for this. Messiah has never left the repertoire.

### Music for the Royal Fireworks (1749)

- **Date:** 1749
- **Type:** Orchestral suite, HWV 351
- **Premiere / first published:** Green Park, London, 27 April 1749 (public rehearsal at Vauxhall Gardens, 21 April)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Music%20for%20the%20Royal%20Fireworks%20George%20Frideric%20Handel)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Music+for+the+Royal+Fireworks+Handel)

George II commissioned this music to accompany a fireworks display in Green Park celebrating the Peace of Aix-la-Chapelle, which ended the War of the Austrian Succession. The king wanted only military instruments, so Handel scored it for a large band of 24 oboes, 12 bassoons, 9 trumpets, 9 horns and timpani, adding strings later for concert performance. A public rehearsal at Vauxhall Gardens on 21 April reportedly drew 12,000 people and caused a traffic jam on London Bridge. At the display itself, part of the elaborate wooden structure caught fire. The suite consists of an overture, a bourrée, La paix, La réjouissance and two minuets.
