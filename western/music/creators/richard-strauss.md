## Biography

Richard Strauss was born in Munich in 1864, the son of Franz Strauss, principal horn player of the court orchestra. He was a prolific prodigy, and the conductor Hans von Bülow gave him his first posts. Tone poems such as Don Juan (1889), Till Eulenspiegels lustige Streiche and Also sprach Zarathustra made him the leading German composer after Wagner, while he also built a major career as a conductor in Weimar, Munich, Berlin and Vienna. With the poet Hugo von Hofmannsthal he wrote Elektra, Der Rosenkavalier and Ariadne auf Naxos. In 1933 he accepted the presidency of the Nazi Reich Music Chamber, resigning in 1935 after a letter to his Jewish librettist Stefan Zweig was intercepted; his conduct under the regime is still debated. He died in Garmisch in 1949, a year after writing the Four Last Songs.

Strauss's tone poems display virtuoso orchestration and vivid, sometimes humorous storytelling. Salome and Elektra pushed harmony towards dissonance and expressionism, but with Der Rosenkavalier he turned towards a lush, Mozartian lyricism and waltz-filled nostalgia, which he kept for the rest of his life. His writing for the soprano voice, from the Marschallin to the Four Last Songs, is especially cherished.

### Also sprach Zarathustra (1896)

- **Date:** 1896
- **Type:** Tone poem, Op. 30
- **Premiere / first published:** Frankfurt, 27 November 1896, conducted by Strauss
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Also%20sprach%20Zarathustra%20Richard%20Strauss)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Also+sprach+Zarathustra+Strauss)

Strauss wrote this tone poem 'freely after Friedrich Nietzsche', inspired by Nietzsche's philosophical novel Thus Spoke Zarathustra. Its nine sections take titles from chapters of the book, such as Of the Backworldsmen, Of Joys and Passions, Of Science and the Dance Song, and trace humanity's development towards the idea of the Übermensch. The opening, a sunrise built on a low C followed by a rising trumpet fanfare of C, G and C and a blazing orchestral chord, became world-famous after Stanley Kubrick used it in 2001: A Space Odyssey in 1968. The work ends ambiguously, with B major and C major chords alternating. Strauss said he did not want to write philosophical music, but to convey in music an idea of the evolution of the human race.

### Salome (1903–05)

- **Date:** 1903–05
- **Type:** Opera, one act, Op. 54
- **Premiere / first published:** Königliches Opernhaus, Dresden, 9 December 1905
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Salome%20Richard%20Strauss)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Salome+Strauss)

Strauss set Hedwig Lachmann's German translation of Oscar Wilde's play Salome, itself scandalous. The princess Salome is fascinated by the prophet Jochanaan (John the Baptist), imprisoned by her stepfather Herod, who rejects her. Herod lusts after Salome and promises her anything if she dances for him; after the Dance of the Seven Veils she demands Jochanaan's head on a silver platter, and in a long final scene she kisses the dead lips, before Herod orders her killed. The dissonant, richly orchestrated score caused a sensation; the opera was banned in London and Vienna and withdrawn from the Metropolitan Opera after one performance in 1907. Its success enabled Strauss to build a villa in Garmisch.

### Der Rosenkavalier (1909–10)

[![Der Rosenkavalier](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/4a/Robert_Sterl_Schuch_dirigiert_Rosenkavalier.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Robert_Sterl_Schuch_dirigiert_Rosenkavalier.jpg)

<sub>Robert Sterl, Ernst von Schuch conducting Der Rosenkavalier Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Robert_Sterl_Schuch_dirigiert_Rosenkavalier.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1909–10
- **Type:** Comic opera, three acts, Op. 59
- **Premiere / first published:** Königliches Opernhaus, Dresden, 26 January 1911
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Der%20Rosenkavalier%20Richard%20Strauss)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Der+Rosenkavalier+Strauss)

Der Rosenkavalier (The Knight of the Rose), with a libretto by Hugo von Hofmannsthal, is set in Maria Theresa's Vienna. The Marschallin, a princess in her thirties, has a young lover, Octavian. Octavian is sent to present a silver rose to Sophie on behalf of the boorish Baron Ochs, but falls in love with her. The Marschallin, reflecting on time and age, gives him up, and the opera ends with a trio for three sopranos (Octavian is a trouser role) and a duet. Strauss filled the score with anachronistic waltzes and lush harmony, a deliberate turn from the modernism of Elektra. The premiere was such a success that special trains ran from Berlin to Dresden. It is one of the most beloved operas of the twentieth century.
