## Biography

Hector Berlioz was born in 1803 in La Côte-Saint-André near Grenoble, the son of a doctor, and was sent to Paris to study medicine. Overwhelmed by the opera, he switched to music and entered the Conservatoire, eventually winning the Prix de Rome in 1830. That year the Symphonie fantastique, inspired by his obsession with the Irish actress Harriet Smithson, whom he later married, announced his radical originality. He wrote music criticism to make a living, conducted his works across Europe, especially in Germany and Russia, and published a treatise on orchestration. His opera Les Troyens was never staged complete in his lifetime. He died in Paris in 1869.

Berlioz drew inspiration from literature, from Shakespeare, Byron, Goethe and Virgil, and often gave his works explicit narratives, as in Harold en Italie and Roméo et Juliette. He thought in terms of orchestral colour, used huge forces when he wanted them, as in the Grande messe des morts, and also wrote music of great delicacy. His Grand traité d'instrumentation became a standard text, and his ideas influenced Liszt, Wagner and Russian composers. His reputation in France grew slowly, and his full stature was recognised only in the twentieth century.

## Key works

### Symphonie fantastique (1830)

- **Date:** 1830
- **Type:** Programme symphony, Op. 14
- **Premiere / first published:** Paris Conservatoire, 5 December 1830
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Symphonie%20fantastique%20Hector%20Berlioz)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Symphonie+fantastique+Berlioz)

Berlioz wrote the Symphonie fantastique, subtitled 'Episode in the life of an artist', in 1830, inspired by his unrequited passion for the Irish Shakespearean actress Harriet Smithson. A printed programme tells how a young musician, in despair over love, poisons himself with opium and dreams. His beloved is represented by a melody, the idée fixe, which recurs in each movement: at a ball, in a pastoral scene with shepherds' pipes and distant thunder, at his own execution in a march to the scaffold, and finally, grotesquely distorted, at a witches' sabbath that quotes the Dies irae chant. Berlioz's orchestra included cor anglais, E-flat clarinet, harps, bells and four timpani for the thunder. Smithson attended a performance in 1832, and the two married the following year. The work is a founding example of the Romantic programme symphony.
