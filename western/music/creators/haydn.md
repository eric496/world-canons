## Biography

Franz Joseph Haydn was born in 1732 in the village of Rohrau, the son of a wheelwright. He sang as a choirboy at St Stephen's Cathedral in Vienna until his voice broke, then scraped a living as a teacher and freelance musician, learning composition largely on his own. In 1761 he entered the service of the Esterházy princes, and from 1766 to 1790 he was their Kapellmeister, mostly at the remote palace of Eszterháza, writing symphonies, operas, baryton trios and church music. As he said, isolation forced him to become original. His fame spread through publication, and after Prince Nikolaus died in 1790 he made two triumphant visits to London, writing his last twelve symphonies for the impresario Johann Peter Salomon. Back in Vienna he composed The Creation and The Seasons. He died in 1809, as Napoleon's armies occupied the city.

Haydn wrote over a hundred symphonies, sixty-eight string quartets, many piano sonatas and trios, operas and masses. He did more than anyone to shape sonata form and the genres of the Classical style, developing whole movements from small motifs and treating the four players of the quartet as equal partners in conversation. His music is full of wit and surprise, but also of searching seriousness. He was a friend of Mozart, who dedicated six quartets to him, and briefly a teacher of Beethoven.

### Symphony No. 94 in G major, 'Surprise' (1791)

- **Date:** 1791
- **Type:** Symphony, Hob. I:94
- **Premiere / first published:** Hanover Square Rooms, London, 23 March 1792
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Symphony%20No.%2094%20in%20G%20major%2C%20%27Surprise%27%20Joseph%20Haydn)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Symphony+No.94+Haydn)

Haydn wrote this symphony for the second of his London seasons organised by the violinist and impresario Johann Peter Salomon. Its nickname comes from the slow movement: a simple, quiet theme is repeated even more softly and then interrupted by a loud chord with a stroke of the timpani. The story that this was meant to wake dozing audiences was popularised later; Haydn said he wanted to make a sensation with something new. The movement continues as a set of variations. The first movement begins with a slow introduction and has a lively, dance-like main section, and the minuet is a brisk peasant dance. The symphony was an immediate success and remains among his most popular.

### String Quartets, Op. 76 (1797)

- **Date:** 1797
- **Type:** Six string quartets
- **Premiere / first published:** Published in Vienna and London, 1799; dedicated to Count Joseph Erdődy
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=String%20Quartets%2C%20Op.%2076%20Joseph%20Haydn)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=String+Quartets+Op.76+Haydn)

Haydn wrote these six quartets in 1796–97 for Count Joseph Erdődy, after his return from London; they were the last complete set he finished. They show his mastery of the genre he did more than anyone to create: dialogue between four equal instruments, motivic development and surprising harmonic turns. No. 2 is called the 'Fifths' for its opening intervals, No. 4 the 'Sunrise' for its rising first theme, and No. 3 the 'Emperor', because its slow movement is a set of variations on the hymn Gott erhalte Franz den Kaiser, which Haydn had written in 1797 as an anthem for Emperor Francis II. That melody later became the tune of the German national anthem. The set has been central to the quartet repertoire ever since.

### The Creation (Die Schöpfung) (1796–98)

- **Date:** 1796–98
- **Type:** Oratorio, Hob. XXI:2
- **Premiere / first published:** Schwarzenberg Palace, Vienna, 29–30 April 1798 (private); Burgtheater, 19 March 1799 (public)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Creation%20%28Die%20Sch%C3%B6pfung%29%20Joseph%20Haydn)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Die+Sch%C3%B6pfung+Haydn)

Inspired by the Handel oratorios he heard in London, Haydn composed The Creation to a German text adapted by Baron Gottfried van Swieten from an English libretto based on Genesis and Milton's Paradise Lost. It was published in 1800 with German and English texts. The work opens with an orchestral portrait of chaos, with hesitant, unresolved harmonies, before the chorus sings 'And there was light' on a blazing C major chord. The three archangels then narrate the six days of creation, with vivid musical pictures of storms, whales, lions and birds, and the final part celebrates Adam and Eve in Eden. A performance in Vienna in 1808, the composer's last public appearance, was a great tribute to the old Haydn.
