## Biography

Gustav Mahler was born in 1860 in Kaliště in Bohemia into a German-speaking Jewish family and grew up in Jihlava. He studied at the Vienna Conservatory and rose through opera houses from Ljubljana and Olomouc to Kassel, Prague, Leipzig, Budapest and Hamburg. In 1897, having converted to Catholicism, partly because Jews were barred from the post, he became director of the Vienna Court Opera, which he transformed through ten demanding years. He composed mainly in summer holidays in his lakeside composing huts. In 1902 he married Alma Schindler. In 1907 his elder daughter died and he was diagnosed with a heart condition, and he left Vienna to conduct at the Metropolitan Opera and the New York Philharmonic. He died in Vienna in 1911, leaving his Tenth Symphony unfinished.

Mahler's symphonies combine the sublime and the banal: folk songs, marches, Ländler, birdcalls and hymns sit beside vast climaxes and music of profound spiritual searching. Several include voices, and his song cycles, such as Kindertotenlieder and Das Lied von der Erde, are closely tied to them. He expanded the orchestra and the length of the symphony. Long neglected after his death and banned by the Nazis, his music was championed by Bruno Walter and Leonard Bernstein and became central to the repertoire from the 1960s.

### Symphony No. 2 in C minor, 'Resurrection' (1888–94)

- **Date:** 1888–94
- **Type:** Symphony with soprano, alto and chorus
- **Premiere / first published:** Berlin, 13 December 1895, Berlin Philharmonic conducted by Mahler
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Symphony%20No.%202%20in%20C%20minor%2C%20%27Resurrection%27%20Gustav%20Mahler)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Symphony+No.2+Mahler)

Mahler worked on his Second Symphony for six years. The first movement, originally a tone poem called Totenfeier (Funeral Rites), asks the meaning of life and death; the next three movements look back at memories of happiness, the irony of life, in an orchestral version of his song about St Anthony preaching to the fish, and simple faith, in the song Urlicht. Mahler had difficulty finding a finale until he heard Klopstock's hymn Die Auferstehung (Resurrection) at the funeral of the conductor Hans von Bülow in 1894. The finale, lasting more than half an hour, includes an offstage band, a 'great summons' of horns and birdsong, before the chorus enters almost inaudibly and builds to an overwhelming climax with organ and bells. Its premiere was Mahler's first major success as a composer.

### Das Lied von der Erde (1908–09)

- **Date:** 1908–09
- **Type:** Song-symphony for tenor, alto (or baritone) and orchestra
- **Premiere / first published:** Munich, 20 November 1911, conducted by Bruno Walter (posthumous)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Das%20Lied%20von%20der%20Erde%20Gustav%20Mahler)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Das+Lied+von+der+Erde+Mahler)

Mahler wrote The Song of the Earth in 1908–09, after the death of his daughter and the diagnosis of his heart disease. It sets six poems from Hans Bethge's Die chinesische Flöte, free German adaptations of Tang-dynasty Chinese poems, including several attributed to Li Bai. Mahler, reportedly superstitious about writing a ninth symphony after Beethoven, Schubert and Bruckner had died after theirs, did not number it. The songs alternate between tenor and alto, from the drinking song of the earth's misery to youth, beauty and the drunkard in spring. The final movement, Der Abschied (The Farewell), lasts about half an hour and ends with the word 'ewig' (forever) repeated over a sustained chord. It was premiered after Mahler's death by his assistant Bruno Walter.

### Symphony No. 9 (1909–10)

- **Date:** 1909–10
- **Type:** Symphony
- **Premiere / first published:** Vienna, 26 June 1912, Vienna Philharmonic conducted by Bruno Walter (posthumous)
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Symphony%20No.%209%20Gustav%20Mahler)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Symphony+No.9+Mahler)

Mahler wrote his Ninth Symphony in 1909–10 in his summer composing hut in Toblach, South Tyrol, and between his conducting seasons in New York. Like the Pathétique of Tchaikovsky, it ends with a slow movement. The first movement, with its hesitant rhythm, which some have linked to Mahler's irregular heartbeat, builds to catastrophic climaxes; the second is a grotesque series of Ländler; the third a savage Rondo-Burleske. The final Adagio fades away into near silence over its last pages, marked ersterbend (dying away). Alban Berg described the first movement as the most heavenly thing Mahler wrote. Bruno Walter conducted the premiere after Mahler's death.
