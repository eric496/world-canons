# Terry Riley

**1935** · American  
Born: Colfax, California · Died: ?  
Eras & schools: [Minimalism & new simplicity](../movements/minimalism-music.md)  

> Riley's In C (1964), built from 53 short repeated patterns, is often regarded as the first major minimalist composition.

## Biography

Terry Riley was born in 1935 in Colfax, California, and studied at San Francisco State University and the University of California, Berkeley. Associated with La Monte Young and the San Francisco Tape Music Center, he experimented with tape loops. In C was first performed in San Francisco in November 1964 with Steve Reich among the players. Riley's later works include A Rainbow in Curved Air (1969) and many works for the Kronos Quartet. From 1970 he studied North Indian raga singing with Pandit Pran Nath.

Riley's music uses repetition, gradual change, modal harmony and improvisation, drawing on jazz and Indian music. His use of keyboards and tape delay influenced rock musicians including The Who and Soft Machine.

## Key works

### In C (1964)

- **Date:** 1964
- **Type:** Work for any number of performers
- **Premiere / first published:** San Francisco Tape Music Center, 4 November 1964
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=In%20C%20Terry%20Riley)

In C consists of a single page of 53 short musical phrases. Any number of performers, on any instruments, play through them in order, each deciding how many times to repeat each phrase before moving on, while a steady pulse on the note C, played on piano or mallet instruments, keeps the ensemble together. The result is a constantly shifting web of patterns, different in each performance, usually lasting between forty-five and ninety minutes. The idea of the pulse was suggested by Steve Reich, who played at the premiere. The 1968 recording on Columbia Records brought the work to a wide audience. It is often considered the first major minimalist composition.
