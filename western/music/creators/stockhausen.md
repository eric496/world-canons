# Karlheinz Stockhausen

**1928 – 2007** · German  
Born: Mödrath, near Cologne · Died: Kürten, near Cologne  
Eras & schools: [Post-war avant-garde](../movements/postwar-avant-garde.md)  

> A pioneer of electronic music and serialism, Stockhausen led the post-war avant-garde with works such as Gesang der Jünglinge.

## Biography

Karlheinz Stockhausen was born in 1928 at Mödrath near Cologne. His mother was killed under the Nazi euthanasia programme and his father died in the war. He studied in Cologne, then with Messiaen in Paris, and from 1953 worked at the electronic music studio of West German Radio in Cologne, which he later directed. He taught at the Darmstadt summer courses, which became a centre of the avant-garde. From 1977 to 2003 he composed Licht, a cycle of seven operas named for the days of the week. He died at Kürten in 2007.

Stockhausen applied serial organisation to all aspects of sound, combined electronic and acoustic sources, explored the spatial placement of sound and wrote 'intuitive music' guided by texts. Works such as Gruppen for three orchestras and Kontakte influenced not only composers but also rock and electronic musicians, and his face appears on the cover of the Beatles' Sgt. Pepper album.

## Key works

### Gesang der Jünglinge (1955–56)

- **Date:** 1955–56
- **Type:** Electronic music
- **Premiere / first published:** WDR Cologne, broadcast concert, 30 May 1956
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Gesang%20der%20J%C3%BCnglinge%20Karlheinz%20Stockhausen)

Stockhausen produced Gesang der Jünglinge (Song of the Youths) at the electronic music studio of West German Radio in Cologne. It is based on the Song of the Three Holy Children from the Book of Daniel, who praise God in the fiery furnace. Stockhausen recorded a boy singing the text and combined it with electronically generated sounds, from pure sine tones to noise, aiming for a continuum between vocal and electronic sound, with the words sometimes intelligible and sometimes broken into sounds. It was originally composed for five groups of loudspeakers surrounding the audience, an early experiment in spatial sound. It is regarded as one of the first masterpieces of electronic music.
