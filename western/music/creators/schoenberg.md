# Arnold Schoenberg

**1874 – 1951** · Austrian-American  
Born: Vienna · Died: Los Angeles  
Eras & schools: [Second Viennese School & modernism](../movements/second-viennese-school.md)  

> Schoenberg led music beyond tonality and created the twelve-tone method, one of the most influential developments of twentieth-century music.

## Biography

Arnold Schoenberg was born in Vienna in 1874 into a Jewish family and was largely self-taught, apart from lessons with Alexander Zemlinsky, whose sister he married. His early works, such as the string sextet Verklärte Nacht (1899) and Gurre-Lieder, extend Wagner and Brahms. From around 1908 he abandoned key altogether in works such as the Five Orchestral Pieces, Erwartung and Pierrot lunaire. He was also an Expressionist painter linked with Kandinsky's Blue Rider group. His teaching drew pupils including Berg and Webern. In the early 1920s he formulated the method of composing with twelve tones. He taught at the Prussian Academy of Arts in Berlin until the Nazis dismissed him in 1933; he formally returned to Judaism in Paris and emigrated to the United States, teaching at the University of California, Los Angeles. He died in Los Angeles in 1951, leaving his opera Moses und Aron unfinished.

Schoenberg saw his work as the logical continuation of the German tradition, from Bach and Brahms to Wagner and Mahler. He called his abandonment of tonality the emancipation of dissonance. The twelve-tone method arranges the twelve notes into a fixed series, which can be inverted, reversed and transposed to generate a whole work. Few composers have been as controversial: his music has never been popular, but its ideas dominated composition for decades after the Second World War, and his textbooks on harmony and composition are still widely read.

### Verklärte Nacht (1899)

- **Date:** 1899
- **Type:** String sextet, Op. 4 (later string orchestra)
- **Premiere / first published:** Vienna, 18 March 1902, Rosé Quartet with additional players
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Verkl%C3%A4rte%20Nacht%20Arnold%20Schoenberg)

Verklärte Nacht (Transfigured Night) is a one-movement string sextet, written in three weeks in 1899, based on a poem by Richard Dehmel. A woman walking with her lover through a moonlit forest confesses that she is pregnant by another man; he replies that their love will transfigure the child as their own. The music follows the poem's five stanzas, moving from D minor anguish to a radiant D major. Its harmonies, influenced by Wagner and Brahms, were too daring for the Vienna Tonkünstlerverein, which rejected it because of one supposedly non-existent chord. Schoenberg later arranged it for string orchestra, and it is his most popular work.

### Pierrot lunaire (1912)

- **Date:** 1912
- **Type:** Melodrama for reciter and five players, Op. 21
- **Premiere / first published:** Choralion-Saal, Berlin, 16 October 1912, with Albertine Zehme
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Pierrot%20lunaire%20Arnold%20Schoenberg)

The actress Albertine Zehme commissioned Pierrot lunaire, setting twenty-one poems by the Belgian Albert Giraud in Otto Erich Hartleben's German translation, about the commedia dell'arte clown Pierrot, intoxicated by moonlight, in visions of violence, religion and nostalgia. Schoenberg wrote it for a reciter and five players on eight instruments. The voice uses Sprechstimme, a half-spoken, half-sung delivery whose exact performance is still debated. The music is atonal, but within it Schoenberg uses old forms such as canons, a passacaglia and a fugue. Stravinsky, who attended a performance in 1912, called it the solar plexus of early twentieth-century music. Its instrumental combination, the Pierrot ensemble, became a standard for later composers.
