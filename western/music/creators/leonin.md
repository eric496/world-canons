# Léonin

**fl. c. 1150 – c. 1201** · French  
Born: Paris (probably) · Died: Paris  
Eras & schools: [Ars antiqua (Notre-Dame school)](../movements/ars-antiqua.md)  

> The first named composer of polyphony, Léonin is credited with the Magnus liber organi, the great book of two-voice organum for the cathedral of Notre-Dame in Paris.

## Biography

Almost all we know about Léonin comes from an English student in Paris in the late thirteenth century, known as Anonymous IV, who called him the best composer of organum and said he made the Magnus liber organi for the services of Notre-Dame. Scholars have identified him, with some probability, with a Leonius who was a canon of Notre-Dame and a poet, active from the 1150s until about 1201. He would thus have worked while the new Gothic cathedral was being built, a setting that mirrors the new soaring music.

Léonin's organum takes a chant and slows it down so that each note is held as a long drone, while a second voice sings flowing, melismatic lines above. In other sections, called discant, both voices move in measured rhythm. The Magnus liber covered the main feasts of the church year and was revised and extended by later musicians, Pérotin above all, so it survives only in later, altered copies. The idea that a composer might be named and remembered, and the system of rhythmic modes the book helped establish, mark the beginning of the written polyphonic tradition of Western music.

## Key works

### Magnus liber organi (c. 1160–1200)

[![Magnus liber organi](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/74/Magnus_Liber_OrganiFC.png)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Magnus_Liber_OrganiFC.png)

<sub>Opening page of the Florence manuscript of the Magnus liber organi Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Magnus_Liber_OrganiFC.png).</sub>

- **Date:** c. 1160–1200
- **Type:** Collection of two-voice organum
- **Premiere / first published:** Notre-Dame Cathedral, Paris; survives in manuscripts now in Florence, Wolfenbüttel and Madrid
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Magnus%20liber%20organi%20L%C3%A9onin)

The Magnus liber organi, the Great Book of Organum, was the collection of polyphonic settings of chant for the main feasts of the church year used at Notre-Dame in Paris. The theorist known as Anonymous IV credited its first form to Léonin and its revision to Pérotin. It contained organa for two voices in which a sustained chant underpins a florid upper voice, along with sections in measured discant. The book itself does not survive; its contents are known from three large later manuscripts, the most important now in the Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana in Florence, each preserving a different stage of revision. The book's rhythmic notation, based on patterns of long and short notes, was one of the first ways of writing rhythm precisely. Together these collections are the starting point of the history of composed polyphony in the West.
