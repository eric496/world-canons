# Anton Bruckner

**1824 – 1896** · Austrian  
Born: Ansfelden, Upper Austria · Died: Vienna  
Eras & schools: [Late Romantic & post-Wagnerian](../movements/late-romantic.md)  

> An organist and devout Catholic, Bruckner wrote vast, cathedral-like symphonies that fuse Wagnerian harmony with the tradition of Beethoven and Schubert.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Bruckner,_Anton)

## Biography

Anton Bruckner was born in 1824 in Ansfelden in Upper Austria, the son of a village schoolmaster. He became a choirboy and later organist at the monastery of St Florian, and in 1855 cathedral organist in Linz, while studying counterpoint by correspondence with the theorist Simon Sechter well into his late thirties. A performance of Tannhäuser in 1863 overwhelmed him, and Wagner became his idol. From 1868 he lived in Vienna, teaching at the conservatory and university and famous as an organ improviser. His symphonies met hostility from critics such as Hanslick, who saw him as a Wagnerian, but the Seventh Symphony (1884) brought success. He died in Vienna in 1896, leaving his Ninth Symphony unfinished, and is buried beneath the organ at St Florian.

Bruckner's eleven symphonies, including two early ones, unfold on a huge scale through blocks of sound, sudden silences, long crescendos and brass chorales that recall the organ and church music. He revised them repeatedly, often at the urging of friends, so that several survive in multiple versions, a problem that still occupies editors and conductors. His masses and motets are also central to Catholic choral music.

## Key works

### Symphony No. 7 in E major (1881–83)

- **Date:** 1881–83
- **Type:** Symphony, WAB 107
- **Premiere / first published:** Leipzig, 30 December 1884, conducted by Arthur Nikisch
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Symphony%20No.%207%20in%20E%20major%20Anton%20Bruckner)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Symphony+No.7+Bruckner)

Bruckner began his Seventh Symphony in 1881, opening it with a long, rising cello melody that he said came to him in a dream. While writing the slow movement he heard of Wagner's illness and death in Venice in February 1883, and he ended the Adagio with a funeral lament for four Wagner tubas, the instruments Wagner had designed for the Ring. The premiere in Leipzig under Arthur Nikisch and a performance in Munich under Hermann Levi brought Bruckner his first wide recognition at the age of sixty. The work is dedicated to King Ludwig II of Bavaria. Unlike several of his symphonies, it survives essentially in one version, although the cymbal clash at the climax of the Adagio is disputed.
