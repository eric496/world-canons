## Biography

Erik Satie was born in Honfleur in 1866 and studied without distinction at the Paris Conservatoire. In Montmartre he played piano at the cabaret Le Chat Noir, joined a Rosicrucian sect and founded his own church with himself as its only member. The Gymnopédies (1888) and Gnossiennes belong to these years. In 1898 he moved to the suburb of Arcueil, walking to Paris each day. At nearly forty he enrolled at the Schola Cantorum to study counterpoint. Championed by Ravel and Cocteau, he wrote the ballet Parade (1917) with Picasso's designs for the Ballets Russes, and became a guiding spirit of the younger composers known as Les Six. He died in Paris in 1925.

Satie rejected the grandeur of Wagner and the refinement of Debussy alike, favouring bare textures, repetition and static harmony, often with absurd titles and performance instructions. His idea of furniture music, meant to be heard but not listened to, anticipated ambient music. John Cage championed him, staging his Vexations, a short piece marked to be repeated 840 times.

## Key works

### Gymnopédies (1888)

[![Gymnopédies](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cc/Gymnop%C3%A9die_1_d%27Erik_Satie%2C_autographe.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gymnop%C3%A9die_1_d%27Erik_Satie%2C_autographe.jpg)

<sub>Satie's autograph of the first Gymnopédie Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gymnop%C3%A9die_1_d%27Erik_Satie%2C_autographe.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1888
- **Type:** Three piano pieces
- **Premiere / first published:** Published in Paris, 1888 (No. 1) and 1895
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Gymnop%C3%A9dies%20Erik%20Satie)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Gymnop%C3%A9dies+Satie)

Satie wrote the three Gymnopédies in 1888 while working as a pianist in Montmartre. The title probably refers to an ancient Spartan festival at which naked young men danced, perhaps taken from a poem by his friend J. P. Contamine de Latour. Each piece has a slow, simple melody over a gently rocking accompaniment of alternating bass notes and chords, with unusual, modal harmonies. Their restraint and repetitiveness were the opposite of the expansive Romantic style. Debussy orchestrated the first and third in 1897. The pieces became widely known in the twentieth century, used in films and advertising, and are often cited as precursors of ambient music.
