# György Ligeti

**1923 – 2006** · Hungarian-Austrian  
Born: Dicsőszentmárton, Romania · Died: Vienna  
Eras & schools: [Post-war avant-garde](../movements/postwar-avant-garde.md)  

> Ligeti invented a music of dense, shimmering sound clouds in Atmosphères and Lux Aeterna, later heard in Kubrick's 2001.

## Biography

György Ligeti was born in 1923 into a Hungarian Jewish family in Transylvania, Romania. His father and brother died in Nazi concentration camps, and he survived forced labour. He studied and taught at the Budapest Academy, but his modernist leanings were stifled by the Communist regime. After the Soviet suppression of the Hungarian uprising in 1956 he fled to Vienna and then Cologne, where he worked in the electronic studio and encountered the avant-garde. Atmosphères (1961) made his name. He taught for many years in Hamburg and later wrote the opera Le Grand Macabre, the Piano Études and concertos. He died in Vienna in 2006.

Ligeti's micropolyphony weaves many lines into a dense texture in which individual voices are lost but the mass of sound slowly changes. Later he drew on African polyrhythms, mechanical patterns and fractal geometry. Stanley Kubrick used several of his works in 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968), without permission at first, bringing his music to a huge audience.

### Atmosphères (1961)

- **Date:** 1961
- **Type:** Orchestral work
- **Premiere / first published:** Donaueschingen Festival, 22 October 1961, SWF Symphony Orchestra conducted by Hans Rosbaud
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Atmosph%C3%A8res%20Gy%C3%B6rgy%20Ligeti)

Ligeti wrote Atmosphères for the Donaueschingen Festival of contemporary music, and its success made him one of the leading figures of the avant-garde. It opens with a huge chord containing all the notes of the chromatic scale over five octaves, played softly, and develops through dense clusters in which each instrumentalist plays a separate part. Changes of colour and texture take the place of melody and rhythm. Ligeti called the technique micropolyphony. Stanley Kubrick used it in 2001: A Space Odyssey, without the composer's consent, during the star gate sequence, which made it widely known.

### Lux Aeterna (1966)

- **Date:** 1966
- **Type:** Work for 16-part unaccompanied chorus
- **Premiere / first published:** Stuttgart, 2 November 1966, Schola Cantorum Stuttgart conducted by Clytus Gottwald
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Lux%20Aeterna%20Gy%C3%B6rgy%20Ligeti)

Ligeti set the text of the communion from the Latin requiem mass, Lux aeterna luceat eis, Domine (May eternal light shine upon them, Lord), for sixteen solo voices. Each voice moves in strict canon at slightly different rhythms, so the individual lines blur into a slowly changing cloud of sound in which the words dissolve. The music moves from high women's voices down to low basses and back, giving the impression of light. Like Atmosphères, it was used in 2001: A Space Odyssey, in the scene on the Moon. It is one of the best-known choral works of the late twentieth century.
