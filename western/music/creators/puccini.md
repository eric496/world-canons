## Biography

Giacomo Puccini was born in Lucca in 1858 into a family that had provided the town's church musicians for four generations. A performance of Verdi's Aida in Pisa decided him on opera, and he studied at the Milan Conservatory with Amilcare Ponchielli. The publisher Giulio Ricordi supported him from his first opera, and Manon Lescaut (1893) made his name. La bohème (1896), Tosca (1900) and Madama Butterfly (1904) followed, the last a failure at its premiere before revision made it a triumph. He lived mostly at Torre del Lago, by a lake in Tuscany, where he loved hunting. La fanciulla del West premiered at the Metropolitan Opera in New York in 1910. He died in Brussels in 1924 during treatment for throat cancer, leaving Turandot unfinished; Franco Alfano completed it.

Puccini had an unmatched gift for soaring, emotional melody and a sure sense of theatre, building scenes with cinematic pacing and orchestral detail. Associated with the verismo movement's realism, he favoured intimate dramas of love and suffering, especially of young women. He absorbed contemporary harmony, including Debussy's, and used exotic colour in Butterfly and Turandot. Critics long patronised his popularity, but his craft is now widely admired.

### La bohème (1893–95)

[![La bohème](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/70/La_Boheme_poster_by_Hohenstein.PNG)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_Boheme_poster_by_Hohenstein.PNG)

<sub>Adolfo Hohenstein, poster for La bohème, 1896 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:La_Boheme_poster_by_Hohenstein.PNG).</sub>

- **Date:** 1893–95
- **Type:** Opera, four acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Teatro Regio, Turin, 1 February 1896, conducted by Arturo Toscanini
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=La%20boh%C3%A8me%20Giacomo%20Puccini)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=La+boh%C3%A8me+Puccini)

La bohème, with a libretto by Luigi Illica and Giuseppe Giacosa, is based on Henri Murger's stories of bohemian life in 1840s Paris. The poet Rodolfo falls in love with his neighbour, the seamstress Mimì, when she knocks on his door for a light; their friends, the painter Marcello and the fickle Musetta, quarrel and make up. The lovers separate because Rodolfo cannot bear to watch Mimì's illness worsen in their cold garret, and she returns to die among her friends. The arias 'Che gelida manina' and 'Sì, mi chiamano Mimì', Musetta's waltz and the Café Momus scene show Puccini's gift for melody and theatrical timing. Critics were cool at the premiere, but the public loved it, and it is among the most performed operas in the world. It inspired the musical Rent.

### Tosca (1896–99)

[![Tosca](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/40/Tosca_%281899%29.jpg/960px-Tosca_%281899%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tosca_%281899%29.jpg)

<sub>Poster for Tosca, 1899 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tosca_%281899%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1896–99
- **Type:** Opera, three acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Teatro Costanzi, Rome, 14 January 1900
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Tosca%20Giacomo%20Puccini)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Tosca+Puccini)

Tosca is based on Victorien Sardou's play, which Puccini had seen with Sarah Bernhardt in the title role. Its action takes place over less than a day in Rome in June 1800, during Napoleon's campaign in Italy, in real places: the church of Sant'Andrea della Valle, the Palazzo Farnese and the Castel Sant'Angelo. The singer Floria Tosca tries to save her lover, the painter Cavaradossi, from the brutal police chief Scarpia, who wants her for himself. She stabs Scarpia but is deceived: Cavaradossi's mock execution turns out to be real, and she throws herself from the castle battlements. The arias 'Vissi d'arte', 'Recondita armonia' and 'E lucevan le stelle' are famous. The musicologist Joseph Kerman dismissed it as a 'shabby little shocker', but it is one of the most popular operas.

### Madama Butterfly (1901–04)

[![Madama Butterfly](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b4/Hohenstein_Madama_Butterfly.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hohenstein_Madama_Butterfly.jpg)

<sub>Adolfo Hohenstein, poster for Madama Butterfly, 1904 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hohenstein_Madama_Butterfly.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1901–04
- **Type:** Opera, two acts (originally), revised in three acts
- **Premiere / first published:** La Scala, Milan, 17 February 1904; revised version Teatro Grande, Brescia, 28 May 1904
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Madama%20Butterfly%20Giacomo%20Puccini)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Madama+Butterfly+Puccini)

Madama Butterfly is based on David Belasco's play, itself drawn from a short story by John Luther Long. The American naval lieutenant Pinkerton marries the fifteen-year-old geisha Cio-Cio-San in Nagasaki, a marriage he does not take seriously. She waits faithfully for years with their son, singing 'Un bel dì'; when he returns with an American wife to take the boy, she kills herself. Puccini studied Japanese melodies, which he wove into the score, together with a quotation from The Star-Spangled Banner. The premiere at La Scala was a fiasco; after revisions it succeeded in Brescia three months later. Its depiction of Japan and its exoticism have been reconsidered in recent decades, but it remains one of the most performed operas.
