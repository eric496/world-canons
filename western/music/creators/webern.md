# Anton Webern

**1883 – 1945** · Austrian  
Born: Vienna · Died: Mittersill, Salzburg  
Eras & schools: [Second Viennese School & modernism](../movements/second-viennese-school.md)  

> Webern's brief, crystalline works pushed Schoenberg's methods to radical concentration and became a model for the post-war avant-garde.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Webern,_Anton)

## Biography

Anton Webern was born in Vienna in 1883 into an aristocratic family, which dropped the 'von' after titles were abolished in 1918. He studied musicology at the University of Vienna, writing a doctorate on the Renaissance composer Heinrich Isaac, and from 1904 studied composition with Schoenberg. He worked as a theatre conductor and later as conductor of the Vienna Workers' Symphony Concerts, but the Nazis banned his music, and in the war years he lived mainly by proof-reading and teaching. In September 1945, shortly after the war ended, he stepped outside a house in Mittersill to smoke a cigar during a curfew and was shot dead by an American army cook.

Webern's complete numbered works can be heard in a few hours. His pieces are tiny in scale, with single notes passed between instruments in a technique called pointillism, transparent textures and extreme dynamics, and after 1924 they use twelve-tone rows of great symmetry and canons inspired by his Renaissance studies. After the war Boulez, Stockhausen and others made him their model, and even Stravinsky praised him late in life.

## Key works

### Symphony, Op. 21 (1927–28)

- **Date:** 1927–28
- **Type:** Symphony for chamber orchestra
- **Premiere / first published:** League of Composers, New York, 18 December 1929
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Symphony%2C%20Op.%2021%20Anton%20Webern)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Symphony+Op.21+Webern)

Webern's Symphony is scored for a small ensemble of clarinet, bass clarinet, two horns, harp and strings, and lasts only about ten minutes. Its twelve-tone row is a palindrome: its second half is a reverse of the first. The first movement consists of double canons in mirror form, and the second is a theme with seven variations that is itself symmetrical around its centre. The notes are scattered between instruments in sparse points of sound, a style sometimes called pointillism. When performed in New York in 1929, it baffled the audience. After 1945, young composers such as Boulez and Stockhausen took it as a model for total serialism.
