## Biography

Franz Schubert was born in 1797 in a suburb of Vienna, the son of a schoolmaster. He sang in the imperial court chapel choir and studied at the city's seminary, where Antonio Salieri taught him composition. He worked for a time as a teacher in his father's school but from about 1818 lived precariously as a composer, supported by a circle of devoted friends who held musical evenings, the Schubertiades, devoted to his music. Only a small part of his output was published or performed publicly in his lifetime. In late 1822 he contracted syphilis, and he died in 1828, possibly of typhoid, a year after Beethoven, beside whom he was later reburied.

Schubert's songs, including Erlkönig, Gretchen am Spinnrade and the cycles Die schöne Müllerin and Winterreise, give the piano a role equal to the voice, painting scenes and states of mind. His instrumental music, such as the Unfinished Symphony, the Great C major Symphony, the String Quintet and the last piano sonatas, combines Classical forms with expansive lyricism and bold harmonic shifts. Much of it was discovered after his death: Schumann found the Great C major Symphony and Mendelssohn conducted its premiere in 1839.

### Erlkönig (1815)

[![Erlkönig](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/99/Erlkonig1sted.jpg/1280px-Erlkonig1sted.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Erlkonig1sted.jpg)

<sub>First edition, Vienna, 1821 Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Erlkonig1sted.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1815
- **Type:** Lied, D. 328
- **Premiere / first published:** Published as Op. 1, Vienna, 1821; first public performance 7 March 1821
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Erlk%C3%B6nig%20Franz%20Schubert)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Erlk%C3%B6nig+Schubert)

Schubert was seventeen when he set Goethe's ballad of a father riding through the night with his sick child, who is lured and finally killed by the supernatural Erl-King. According to his friend Joseph von Spaun, he wrote it in one afternoon. The piano part hammers out the galloping hooves in relentless repeated octaves, and the single singer has to portray four characters, the narrator, the frightened child, the reassuring father and the seductive Erl-King, in different registers and styles. The child's cries rise in pitch each time, and at the end the music stops for the stark final line. Goethe was reportedly indifferent to it, but it became Schubert's first published work and made his name.

### Symphony No. 8 in B minor, 'Unfinished' (1822)

[![Symphony No. 8 in B minor, 'Unfinished'](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/30/Symphony_No._8_in_B_minor.jpg/1280px-Symphony_No._8_in_B_minor.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Symphony_No._8_in_B_minor.jpg)

<sub>Schubert's autograph score Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Symphony_No._8_in_B_minor.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1822
- **Type:** Symphony (two movements), D. 759
- **Premiere / first published:** Vienna, 17 December 1865, conducted by Johann von Herbeck
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Symphony%20No.%208%20in%20B%20minor%2C%20%27Unfinished%27%20Franz%20Schubert)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Symphony+No.8+Schubert)

Schubert wrote two movements of this symphony in 1822 and sketched a third, a scherzo, in piano score, but never finished it. Why he abandoned it is unknown; theories include his illness, painful associations or the feeling that the two movements were complete. He gave the manuscript to his friend Anselm Hüttenbrenner, who kept it until 1865, when the conductor Johann von Herbeck persuaded him to release it for the premiere. The opening, with a hushed theme in the cellos and basses and a lyrical second theme, and the slow movement in E major are full of poignant contrasts. Some musicologists believe the entr'acte from Rosamunde was originally the finale. It is one of the most popular symphonies.

### Winterreise (1827)

- **Date:** 1827
- **Type:** Song cycle of 24 songs, D. 911
- **Premiere / first published:** Published by Haslinger, Vienna, 1828
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Winterreise%20Franz%20Schubert)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Winterreise+Schubert)

Schubert set twenty-four poems by Wilhelm Müller in Winterreise (Winter Journey), composed in two parts in 1827, during his last illness. A young man, rejected in love, leaves town at night and wanders through a frozen landscape, encountering a weathervane, a frozen river, a crow, a signpost and a graveyard. The cycle ends with an old hurdy-gurdy player, ignored by all, whose droning tune suggests a bleak future. Schubert's friends were dismayed by the sombreness of the songs when he first sang them, but he said he liked them more than all others. The piano evokes each scene with economy: the turning weathervane, the trudging steps, the hurdy-gurdy's drone. Winterreise is a summit of the song repertoire for singers.

### String Quintet in C major (1828)

- **Date:** 1828
- **Type:** String quintet, D. 956
- **Premiere / first published:** Vienna, 17 November 1850 (first public performance); published 1853
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=String%20Quintet%20in%20C%20major%20Franz%20Schubert)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=String+Quintet+D.956+Schubert)

Schubert completed the String Quintet in the late summer of 1828, two months before his death, and it was neither performed nor published in his lifetime. Unlike Mozart's quintets, which add a second viola, it adds a second cello, giving the sound richness and depth. The first movement is vast and lyrical; the Adagio, in which the inner voices sing a slow hymn over pizzicato bass and violin figures, is surrounded by a violent central section; the scherzo is energetic and the finale has a Hungarian character. Many musicians consider it the greatest work of chamber music, and the Adagio is often requested at funerals and played at moments of mourning.
