# Antonio Vivaldi

**1678 – 1741** · Italian  
Born: Venice · Died: Vienna  
Eras & schools: [High Baroque](../movements/high-baroque.md)  

> The 'Red Priest' of Venice wrote hundreds of concertos, including The Four Seasons, which shaped the Baroque concerto across Europe.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Vivaldi,_Antonio)

## Biography

Antonio Vivaldi was born in Venice in 1678, the son of a violinist at St Mark's. He was ordained a priest in 1703, and his red hair earned him the nickname il prete rosso, but ill health, probably asthma, soon excused him from saying mass. The same year he became violin master at the Ospedale della Pietà, a Venetian home for foundling girls famous for its female orchestra and choir, with which he was associated on and off for most of his career. He also wrote and produced dozens of operas. His printed concertos, beginning with L'estro armonico in 1711, made him famous across Europe. He died poor in Vienna in 1741, having gone there probably in search of imperial patronage.

Vivaldi wrote about five hundred concertos, many for violin but also for bassoon, cello, flute, mandolin and other instruments. He established the three-movement fast-slow-fast pattern and ritornello form, in which a returning orchestral refrain alternates with solo episodes, and gave the soloist brilliant, idiomatic music. Bach transcribed several of his concertos for keyboard. Vivaldi was largely forgotten after his death, and much of his music was rediscovered only in the twentieth century, when manuscripts were found in Turin; The Four Seasons became one of the most recorded works of all.

## Key works

### The Four Seasons (Le quattro stagioni) (c. 1718–20; pub. 1725)

- **Date:** c. 1718–20; pub. 1725
- **Type:** Four violin concertos
- **Premiere / first published:** Published in Il cimento dell'armonia e dell'inventione, Op. 8, Amsterdam, 1725
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=The%20Four%20Seasons%20%28Le%20quattro%20stagioni%29%20Antonio%20Vivaldi)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Four+Seasons+Vivaldi)

The Four Seasons are the first four concertos of Vivaldi's Op. 8, published in Amsterdam in 1725 under the title The Contest between Harmony and Invention, but probably written several years earlier, perhaps in Mantua. Each concerto in three movements is accompanied by a sonnet, possibly by Vivaldi himself, and letters in the score link lines of poetry to musical details: birdsong in Spring, a barking dog, a summer storm, drunken peasants and hunting in Autumn, chattering teeth and slipping on ice in Winter. The vivid pictorialism fits within ritornello form, and the solo violin part is brilliant. The works were popular in Vivaldi's time but forgotten later; after a recording revival from the 1940s they became among the most recorded pieces of classical music.
