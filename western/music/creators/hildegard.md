# Hildegard of Bingen

**1098 – 1179** · German  
Born: Bermersheim vor der Höhe, Rhineland · Died: Rupertsberg, near Bingen

## Biography

Hildegard was born in 1098 into a noble family in the Rhineland and, as a child of about eight, was placed with the anchoress Jutta at the monastery of Disibodenberg. She took vows there and in 1136 succeeded Jutta as head of the community of nuns. From her early forties she recorded the visions she had experienced since childhood, beginning with Scivias, and with papal approval became famous across Europe. Around 1150 she founded her own convent at Rupertsberg near Bingen, and later a second across the Rhine at Eibingen. She corresponded with popes, emperors and Bernard of Clairvaux, wrote on medicine and natural history, and undertook preaching tours, which was extraordinary for a woman of her time. She died in 1179 and was formally canonised and named a Doctor of the Church in 2012.

About seventy of Hildegard's chants survive, gathered under the title Symphonia armonie celestium revelationum, together with the sung drama Ordo Virtutum. Her melodies sound unlike most plainchant: they leap widely, span unusually large ranges and spin long, ecstatic phrases out of a few recurring motifs, setting her own vivid Latin poetry rich in images of greenness, light and the Virgin. She thought of music as an echo of the harmony of heaven lost at the Fall. Largely forgotten for centuries, her music was rediscovered in the late twentieth century through recordings, and she is now among the best-known composers of the Middle Ages.

## Key works

### Ordo Virtutum (c. 1151)

- **Date:** c. 1151
- **Type:** Sung morality play
- **Premiere / first published:** Probably first performed at Rupertsberg convent, near Bingen
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Ordo%20Virtutum%20Hildegard%20of%20Bingen)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Ordo+Virtutum+Hildegard+Bingen)

Hildegard wrote the Ordo Virtutum (Play of the Virtues) around 1151, probably for the nuns of her new convent at Rupertsberg, and a short version of it closes her visionary book Scivias. It dramatises the struggle for a human soul, Anima, between sixteen Virtues and the Devil. Anima at first rejoices, then grows weary of the Virtues' discipline and is lured away by the Devil, before returning penitent and wounded to be welcomed back, while the Virtues bind the Devil. All the characters sing in Hildegard's soaring, wide-ranging chant except the Devil, who can only speak or shout, a striking image of evil as the absence of harmony. It is the oldest known morality play by several centuries and survives with its music in the Riesenkodex at Wiesbaden. Rediscovered in modern times, it has been staged and recorded many times since the 1980s.
