## Biography

Giovanni Battista Lulli was born in Florence in 1632, the son of a miller, and was taken to France at thirteen as an Italian tutor for a royal cousin. A gifted dancer and violinist, he caught the young Louis XIV's eye, danced alongside him in court ballets and in 1653 was made royal composer of instrumental music. He collaborated with Molière on comedy-ballets such as Le bourgeois gentilhomme, became a naturalised Frenchman, and in 1672 acquired a royal monopoly over opera in France. He died in 1687 from gangrene after striking his foot with the long staff he used to beat time.

With the librettist Philippe Quinault, Lully created the tragédie en musique, a five-act opera with prologue in praise of the king, closely fitted to French verse, with dances, choruses and spectacular machinery. Works such as Atys and Armide fixed this form for a century. He drilled the king's string orchestra into famous discipline and established the French overture, a slow dotted opening followed by a fast fugal section, imitated by Bach, Handel and many others.

## Key works

### Armide (1686)

[![Armide](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/71/Scene_from_Armide_by_Lully_-_drawing_by_Saint-Aubin_-_Boston_MFA.jpg/1280px-Scene_from_Armide_by_Lully_-_drawing_by_Saint-Aubin_-_Boston_MFA.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Scene_from_Armide_by_Lully_-_drawing_by_Saint-Aubin_-_Boston_MFA.jpg)

<sub>Gabriel de Saint-Aubin, drawing of a scene from Lully's Armide (Museum of Fine Arts, Boston) Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Scene_from_Armide_by_Lully_-_drawing_by_Saint-Aubin_-_Boston_MFA.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1686
- **Type:** Tragédie en musique, prologue and five acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Paris Opéra (Théâtre du Palais-Royal), 15 February 1686
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Armide%20Jean-Baptiste%20Lully)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Armide+Lully)

Armide is based on Tasso's epic Jerusalem Delivered and tells of the sorceress Armide, who captures the crusader knight Renaud intending to kill him but falls in love with him instead. When he is rescued and leaves her, she destroys her enchanted palace in despair. Its libretto is by Philippe Quinault, and it was the last of their tragédies en musique. Armide's monologue Enfin il est en ma puissance, in which she hesitates over the sleeping Renaud, was admired by Rameau and Rousseau for the way recitative follows French speech. The work includes celebrated dances, such as a passacaille. Gluck later set the same libretto in 1777, a sign of its prestige.
