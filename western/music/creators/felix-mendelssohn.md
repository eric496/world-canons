# Felix Mendelssohn

**1809 – 1847** · German  
Born: Hamburg · Died: Leipzig  
Eras & schools: [Early Romantic](../movements/early-romantic.md)  

> Composer, pianist and conductor, Mendelssohn wrote polished Romantic works such as A Midsummer Night's Dream and led the revival of Bach.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Mendelssohn,_Felix)

## Biography

Jakob Ludwig Felix Mendelssohn Bartholdy was born in Hamburg in 1809, the grandson of the philosopher Moses Mendelssohn, into a wealthy family that had converted from Judaism to Lutheranism. A prodigy comparable to Mozart, he wrote the String Octet at sixteen and the overture to A Midsummer Night's Dream at seventeen. In 1829 he conducted the St Matthew Passion in Berlin, its first performance since Bach's death. He travelled widely, especially in Britain, where he was a favourite of Queen Victoria and premiered Elijah in Birmingham in 1846. As conductor of the Leipzig Gewandhaus Orchestra from 1835, he built one of Europe's finest orchestras, and in 1843 he founded the Leipzig Conservatory. Exhausted by overwork and grief over the death of his sister Fanny, herself a gifted composer, he died in 1847 at thirty-eight.

Mendelssohn combined Romantic imagination with Classical poise and craftsmanship. His Hebrides overture and Scottish and Italian symphonies evoke landscape, his Violin Concerto in E minor remains one of the most loved, and his Songs without Words were played in homes everywhere. He did much to create the modern concert repertoire of past masterpieces. After his death his reputation suffered, partly from antisemitic attacks, most notoriously Wagner's essay of 1850, and the Nazis banned his music.

### A Midsummer Night's Dream (Overture 1826; incidental music 1843)

- **Date:** Overture 1826; incidental music 1843
- **Type:** Concert overture, Op. 21, and incidental music, Op. 61
- **Premiere / first published:** Overture first performed Stettin, 20 February 1827; incidental music Neues Palais, Potsdam, 14 October 1843
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=A%20Midsummer%20Night%27s%20Dream%20Felix%20Mendelssohn)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Midsummer+Night%27s+Dream+Mendelssohn)

Mendelssohn wrote the concert overture to Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream at seventeen, after reading the play in German translation with his sister Fanny. Four wind chords open the door to fairyland, and rapid, light string music evokes the fairies, while later themes portray the lovers, the court, and Bottom's braying as a donkey. In 1843, at the request of King Frederick William IV of Prussia, he wrote incidental music for a staging of the play in Potsdam, including the Scherzo, Nocturne and Wedding March, and reused the overture. The Wedding March became a staple of weddings after it was played at the marriage of Queen Victoria's daughter Victoria in 1858.

### Violin Concerto in E minor (1844)

- **Date:** 1844
- **Type:** Violin concerto, Op. 64
- **Premiere / first published:** Leipzig Gewandhaus, 13 March 1845, with Ferdinand David as soloist
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Violin%20Concerto%20in%20E%20minor%20Felix%20Mendelssohn)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Violin+Concerto+Op.64+Mendelssohn)

Mendelssohn promised a concerto to his friend Ferdinand David, leader of the Gewandhaus Orchestra, in 1838 and completed it in 1844 after long consultation with him. Its innovations include the soloist's immediate entry without an orchestral introduction, the placing of the cadenza before the recapitulation rather than near the end of the first movement, and the linking of all three movements without pauses, with a single bassoon note leading to the slow movement. The finale is a sparkling, elfin rondo. The concerto quickly became a cornerstone of the violin repertoire.
