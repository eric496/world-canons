# Bedřich Smetana

**1824 – 1884** · Czech  
Born: Litomyšl, Bohemia · Died: Prague  
Eras & schools: [Nationalist schools](../movements/nationalist-schools.md)  

> The founder of Czech national music, Smetana wrote the opera The Bartered Bride and the cycle of symphonic poems Má vlast.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Smetana,_Bedřich)

## Biography

Bedřich Smetana was born in Litomyšl in eastern Bohemia in 1824, the son of a brewer, and grew up speaking German, as did most educated Czechs then. He studied in Prague, opened a music school there with Liszt's encouragement, and took part in the revolution of 1848. From 1856 to 1861 he worked in Gothenburg in Sweden. Returning to Prague as Czech national feeling rose, he wrote operas for the new Provisional Theatre, including The Bartered Bride (1866), and became its principal conductor. In 1874 he went completely deaf, probably as a result of syphilis, but went on to compose Má vlast and his autobiographical string quartet From My Life. He died in a Prague asylum in 1884.

Smetana drew on Czech dances such as the polka and furiant, on Czech history and legend and on the Bohemian landscape, while using the progressive techniques of Liszt and Wagner. His eight operas created a Czech national repertoire, and Má vlast, especially Vltava, which follows the river from its sources through Prague, became a symbol of the nation. The Prague Spring festival opens each year on the anniversary of his death with Má vlast.

## Key works

### Má vlast (1874–79)

- **Date:** 1874–79
- **Type:** Cycle of six symphonic poems
- **Premiere / first published:** Complete cycle first performed in Prague, 5 November 1882
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=M%C3%A1%20vlast%20Bed%C5%99ich%20Smetana)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=M%C3%A1+vlast+Smetana)

Smetana composed Má vlast (My Homeland) between 1874 and 1879, mostly after he had gone completely deaf. The six symphonic poems evoke Czech land and myth: Vyšehrad, the castle of the ancient kings in Prague; Vltava, which follows the river from two springs through forests, a peasant wedding and moonlit nymphs to Prague; Šárka, the legend of a warrior maiden; From Bohemia's Woods and Fields; and Tábor and Blaník, about the Hussite warriors of the fifteenth century and their legendary return. The Hussite chorale 'Ye who are warriors of God' unites the last two. Performed complete for the first time in 1882, it became a symbol of Czech identity and opens the Prague Spring festival each year.
