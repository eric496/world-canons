# Richard Wagner

**1813 – 1883** · German  
Born: Leipzig · Died: Venice  
Eras & schools: [High Romantic & opera](../movements/high-romantic.md)  

> Wagner remade opera as music drama, writing his own librettos and creating the Ring cycle, Tristan und Isolde and a theatre at Bayreuth for his works.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Wagner,_Richard)

## Biography

Richard Wagner was born in Leipzig in 1813 and grew up in a theatrical family in Dresden and Leipzig. After years of struggle as a conductor in provincial theatres and a period of poverty in Paris, he had his first great success in Dresden with Rienzi in 1842 and became Kapellmeister there, following it with Der fliegende Holländer, Tannhäuser and Lohengrin. His part in the Dresden uprising of 1849 forced him into exile in Switzerland, where he wrote the theoretical writings that shaped his later work and began Der Ring des Nibelungen. In 1864 the young King Ludwig II of Bavaria rescued him from debt and became his patron. He married Cosima von Bülow, Liszt's daughter, in 1870, and built the Festspielhaus in Bayreuth, where the Ring was first performed complete in 1876. His last work, Parsifal, appeared there in 1882. He died in Venice in 1883.

Wagner's music dramas abandon the division into arias and recitative for a continuous flow of music, woven from leitmotifs: short themes associated with characters, objects and ideas, transformed as the drama unfolds. His chromatic harmony, especially in Tristan und Isolde, pushed tonality to new limits, and his huge orchestra created new sonorities. Few artists have been as influential or as divisive. His writings include virulent antisemitism, notably Das Judenthum in der Musik, and his works were later exploited by the Nazis, which continues to shadow his legacy.

### Tristan und Isolde (1857–59)

- **Date:** 1857–59
- **Type:** Music drama, three acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Königliches Hof- und Nationaltheater, Munich, 10 June 1865, conducted by Hans von Bülow
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Tristan%20und%20Isolde%20Richard%20Wagner)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Tristan+und+Isolde+Wagner)

Wagner interrupted his work on the Ring to write Tristan und Isolde, inspired by the medieval romance of Gottfried von Strassburg, Schopenhauer's philosophy and his own passion for Mathilde Wesendonck, the wife of a patron. Isolde, an Irish princess being brought by the knight Tristan to marry King Mark of Cornwall, intends to poison herself and Tristan, but her maid substitutes a love potion. Their love can only be fulfilled in night and death. The Prelude opens with the 'Tristan chord', an ambiguous dissonance that resolves only into further dissonance, and the music yearns towards resolutions that are postponed until Isolde's final Liebestod. Tristan was considered unperformable in Vienna after dozens of rehearsals, and was premiered in Munich with Ludwig II's support. Its harmony is often seen as the beginning of the end of traditional tonality.

### Die Meistersinger von Nürnberg (1861–67)

- **Date:** 1861–67
- **Type:** Music drama, three acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Königliches Hof- und Nationaltheater, Munich, 21 June 1868
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Die%20Meistersinger%20von%20N%C3%BCrnberg%20Richard%20Wagner)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Die+Meistersinger+von+N%C3%BCrnberg+Wagner)

Die Meistersinger is set in sixteenth-century Nuremberg, where the shoemaker-poet Hans Sachs, a historical figure, helps the young knight Walther win a singing contest and the hand of Eva, against the pedantic town clerk Beckmesser. The work celebrates German art and the balance between tradition and innovation, and contains Wagner's most diatonic and contrapuntal music, including a famous overture that combines several themes at once. Many have seen Beckmesser as an antisemitic caricature and as a portrait of the critic Hanslick, though the extent of such meanings is debated. The Nazis made the opera a showpiece at Bayreuth, a history that later productions often confront.

### Der Ring des Nibelungen (1848–1874)

- **Date:** 1848–1874
- **Type:** Cycle of four music dramas
- **Premiere / first published:** Complete cycle first performed at the Bayreuth Festspielhaus, 13–17 August 1876
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Der%20Ring%20des%20Nibelungen%20Richard%20Wagner)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Der+Ring+des+Nibelungen+Wagner)

Wagner worked on the Ring for more than a quarter of a century. He wrote the texts in reverse order, beginning with Siegfried's death, and then composed the music in order: Das Rheingold, Die Walküre, Siegfried, with a long interruption, and Götterdämmerung. The story, drawn from the Nibelungenlied and Icelandic sagas, follows a magic ring forged from stolen Rhine gold, the curse it bears, and the downfall of the gods, through the god Wotan, his daughter the Valkyrie Brünnhilde and the hero Siegfried. It was conceived as a critique of power and greed. The music is woven from a network of leitmotifs for characters, objects and ideas, such as the Ride of the Valkyries. Wagner built the Festspielhaus in Bayreuth, with its hidden orchestra pit, to stage it, and the first complete cycle in 1876 was attended by the German emperor, Tchaikovsky, Liszt and Bruckner. It is considered the most ambitious work in the history of opera.

### Parsifal (1877–82)

- **Date:** 1877–82
- **Type:** Bühnenweihfestspiel (stage consecration festival play), three acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Bayreuth Festspielhaus, 26 July 1882
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Parsifal%20Richard%20Wagner)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Parsifal+Wagner)

Parsifal, based on Wolfram von Eschenbach's medieval epic, tells of a guileless youth who becomes the redeemer of the Grail knights, whose king Amfortas suffers from a wound that will not heal. Through compassion and resistance to the temptation of Kundry, Parsifal recovers the sacred spear and heals the wound. Wagner called it a festival play for the consecration of the stage and wanted it performed only at Bayreuth; this exclusivity lasted until the Metropolitan Opera staged it in New York in 1903 and other houses after copyright expired in 1913. The music is slow-moving, luminous and harmonically rich. Its religious themes, its mixture of Christian and Buddhist ideas and its possible racial undertones have long been debated.
