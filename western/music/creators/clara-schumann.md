# Clara Schumann

**1819 – 1896** · German  
Born: Leipzig · Died: Frankfurt  
Eras & schools: [Early Romantic](../movements/early-romantic.md)  

> One of the great pianists of the nineteenth century, Clara Schumann was also a gifted composer and the leading interpreter of her husband's and Brahms's music.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Schumann,_Clara)

## Biography

Clara Wieck was born in Leipzig in 1819 and trained by her father, Friedrich Wieck, as a piano prodigy. She toured Europe from childhood, winning the admiration of Goethe, Paganini and Liszt, and was named a royal and imperial chamber virtuoso in Vienna at eighteen. In 1840 she married Robert Schumann against her father's opposition. She bore eight children, managed the household and kept performing, and after Robert's breakdown and death she supported the family through concert tours across Europe, including many visits to Britain. She was a close friend of Johannes Brahms for over forty years. From 1878 she taught at the Hoch Conservatory in Frankfurt, where she died in 1896.

Clara Schumann composed mostly before 1854, including a Piano Concerto begun at fourteen, a Piano Trio in G minor, songs and piano pieces such as the Variations on a Theme by Robert Schumann. She doubted herself as a composer, once writing that a woman must not desire to compose. As a performer she helped change concert life, playing from memory and favouring serious works by Beethoven, Chopin, Robert Schumann and Brahms over showpieces. Her music has been revived and recorded widely since the 1970s.

## Key works

### Piano Concerto in A minor (1833–35)

- **Date:** 1833–35
- **Type:** Piano concerto, Op. 7
- **Premiere / first published:** Leipzig Gewandhaus, 9 November 1835, with the composer as soloist and Mendelssohn conducting
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Piano%20Concerto%20in%20A%20minor%20Clara%20Schumann)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Piano+Concerto+Op.7+Clara+Schumann)

Clara Wieck began this concerto when she was about thirteen, writing what became its finale first as a one-movement piece, possibly with help from Robert Schumann on the orchestration. She completed it in 1835 and played it at the Leipzig Gewandhaus with Mendelssohn conducting. Its three movements are linked without pauses. The slow movement is a remarkable Romanze in which the orchestra falls silent, leaving the piano in duet with a solo cello, an idea that Brahms later echoed in his Second Piano Concerto. The work shows the influence of Chopin and of the virtuoso concertos of the day. After decades of neglect, it has been recorded and performed frequently since the late twentieth century.
