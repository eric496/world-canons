# Arcangelo Corelli

**1653 – 1713** · Italian  
Born: Fusignano, Papal States · Died: Rome  
Eras & schools: [High Baroque](../movements/high-baroque.md)  

> Violinist and composer, Corelli published only six collections, but they fixed the forms of the sonata and the concerto grosso for the whole Baroque.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Corelli,_Arcangelo)

## Biography

Arcangelo Corelli was born in 1653 at Fusignano near Ravenna and trained in Bologna, a centre of violin playing. By 1675 he was in Rome, where he spent the rest of his life under the patronage of Queen Christina of Sweden, Cardinal Benedetto Pamphili and above all Cardinal Pietro Ottoboni, in whose palace he lived. He led the orchestras for great Roman occasions and was admired as Europe's leading violinist and teacher. He died in Rome in 1713 and was buried in the Pantheon.

Corelli published four collections of trio sonatas, a book of violin sonatas (Op. 5, 1700) and the twelve Concerti grossi, Op. 6, issued after his death. His music is notable for its clarity, balance and polish, and for using chains of suspensions and strongly directed harmony that helped establish tonal major and minor keys. Reprinted constantly, his works became models for Handel, Bach, Vivaldi and generations of violinists.

## Key works

### Concerti grossi, Op. 6 (pub. 1714)

- **Date:** pub. 1714
- **Type:** Twelve concerti grossi
- **Premiere / first published:** Published by Estienne Roger, Amsterdam, 1714; many performed in Rome from the 1680s
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Concerti%20grossi%2C%20Op.%206%20Arcangelo%20Corelli)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Concerti+grossi+Op.6+Corelli)

Corelli's Op. 6 was published in Amsterdam in 1714, a year after his death, but many of the concertos had been played in Rome for years; the German composer Georg Muffat heard some there in 1682. In the concerto grosso a small group of soloists, two violins and a cello, alternates and blends with the full string orchestra. The first eight concertos follow the serious church sonata style, and the last four the lighter chamber style, with dance movements. No. 8 in G minor, marked 'made for the night of Christmas', ends with a gentle pastorale. The set was reprinted throughout the eighteenth century and became the model for Handel's Op. 6 and countless others.
