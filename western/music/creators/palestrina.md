# Giovanni Pierluigi da Palestrina

**c. 1525 – 1594** · Italian  
Born: Palestrina, Papal States · Died: Rome  
Eras & schools: [Late Renaissance & Venetian](../movements/late-renaissance-music.md)  

> Palestrina's serene, balanced polyphony for the Catholic church became the model of Renaissance sacred style, studied by composers for centuries.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Palestrina,_Giovanni_Pierluigi_da)

## Biography

Giovanni Pierluigi took his name from the town of Palestrina near Rome, where he was born around 1525. He was organist and choirmaster at the cathedral there before Pope Julius III, formerly bishop of Palestrina, brought him to Rome in 1551. He held posts at the Cappella Giulia of St Peter's, St John Lateran and Santa Maria Maggiore, sang briefly in the Sistine Chapel choir, and from 1571 until his death directed the Cappella Giulia again. He lost his first wife and two sons to plague, and later married a wealthy widow, whose fur business helped fund the printing of his works. He died in Rome in 1594.

Palestrina wrote more than a hundred masses, several hundred motets, hymns, Magnificats and madrigals. His style is a model of controlled flowing lines, careful treatment of dissonance and transparent texture, so that the words remain audible. A legend that his Missa Papae Marcelli persuaded the Council of Trent not to ban polyphony is now regarded as a myth, but it shows his reputation as the saviour of church music. Johann Joseph Fux's 1725 treatise Gradus ad Parnassum codified a version of his style, and generations of students, including Haydn and Mozart, learned counterpoint from it.

## Key works

### Missa Papae Marcelli (c. 1562)

- **Date:** c. 1562
- **Type:** Mass (six voices)
- **Premiere / first published:** Printed in Palestrina's Missarum liber secundus, Rome, 1567
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Missa%20Papae%20Marcelli%20Giovanni%20Pierluigi%20da%20Palestrina)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Missa+Papae+Marcelli+Palestrina)

The Pope Marcellus Mass is named after Marcellus II, who reigned for only three weeks in 1555 and reportedly urged that church music be sung with reverence and intelligible words. Palestrina's six-voice setting balances polyphonic richness with unusual clarity of text, especially in the long Gloria and Credo, where voices often declaim together. A story first told decades later, and elaborated in Hans Pfitzner's 1917 opera Palestrina, holds that the mass convinced the Council of Trent not to ban polyphony; historians regard this as a legend, though the Council did debate the intelligibility of sacred music. The work was printed in 1567 and became the classic model of Counter-Reformation polyphony, sung in the Sistine Chapel for centuries.
