# Aaron Copland

**1900 – 1990** · American  
Born: Brooklyn, New York · Died: Sleepy Hollow, New York  
Eras & schools: [Neoclassicism & mid-century tonal modernism](../movements/neoclassicism-music.md)  

> Copland created an American concert style of open harmonies and folk-inflected melodies in works such as Appalachian Spring.

## Biography

Aaron Copland was born in Brooklyn in 1900 to Lithuanian Jewish immigrants. In 1921 he went to France and studied with Nadia Boulanger, the first of many Americans to do so. Back in New York he wrote jazz-influenced works and dissonant modernist pieces, and organised concerts of new American music. In the 1930s and 1940s he turned to a simpler, more accessible style in the ballets Billy the Kid, Rodeo and Appalachian Spring, which won the Pulitzer Prize in 1945, as well as Fanfare for the Common Man and film scores. In 1953, at the height of McCarthyism, he was questioned by a Senate committee about his left-wing sympathies. He taught for many years at the Tanglewood summer school and in later life composed little but conducted widely. He died in 1990.

Copland's music is marked by wide-spaced open chords, clear textures and spare melodies, sometimes based on cowboy songs or Shaker hymns, evoking American landscapes and ideals. He also adopted twelve-tone technique in later works such as Connotations. Through his books, teaching and advocacy he became the dean of American composers.

### Fanfare for the Common Man (1942)

- **Date:** 1942
- **Type:** Fanfare for brass and percussion
- **Premiere / first published:** Cincinnati, 12 March 1943, Cincinnati Symphony Orchestra conducted by Eugene Goossens
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Fanfare%20for%20the%20Common%20Man%20Aaron%20Copland)

In 1942 the conductor Eugene Goossens asked American composers to write fanfares to open concerts of the Cincinnati Symphony Orchestra as a contribution to the war effort. Copland's title was inspired by a speech by Vice President Henry Wallace proclaiming the century of the common man. Scored for brass and percussion, its slow, wide-spaced themes, interrupted by timpani and bass drum, create an impression of vastness. Goossens premiered it at income tax time, which Copland thought appropriate. He later reused it in the finale of his Third Symphony. It has been widely used at public events and covered by the rock group Emerson, Lake and Palmer.

### Appalachian Spring (1943–44)

- **Date:** 1943–44
- **Type:** Ballet, later orchestral suite
- **Premiere / first published:** Library of Congress, Washington, D.C., 30 October 1944, Martha Graham Dance Company
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Appalachian%20Spring%20Aaron%20Copland)

Copland wrote this ballet for the dancer and choreographer Martha Graham, commissioned by the patron Elizabeth Sprague Coolidge. Its scenario concerns a young pioneer couple building a farmhouse in Pennsylvania in the early nineteenth century, with a revivalist preacher and a pioneer woman. The original score was for thirteen instruments; the orchestral suite of 1945 made it famous. Graham suggested the title, from a poem by Hart Crane, after the music was written. Its open, spacious harmonies and the variations on the Shaker song Simple Gifts became an image of American landscape and community. It won the Pulitzer Prize for Music in 1945.
