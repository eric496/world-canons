# Sergei Prokofiev

**1891 – 1953** · Russian  
Born: Sontsovka, Yekaterinoslav Governorate, Russian Empire · Died: Moscow  
Eras & schools: [Neoclassicism & mid-century tonal modernism](../movements/neoclassicism-music.md)  

> Prokofiev combined steely modernism with lyrical melody in ballets such as Romeo and Juliet, the Classical Symphony and Peter and the Wolf.

## Biography

Sergei Prokofiev was born in 1891 at Sontsovka, an estate in what is now eastern Ukraine, where his father was manager. A prodigy, he entered the Saint Petersburg Conservatory at thirteen and shocked his teachers with his percussive, dissonant piano works. After the Revolution he left Russia in 1918, living in the United States, Germany and above all Paris, where he worked with Diaghilev. In 1936 he returned to the Soviet Union for good, writing Romeo and Juliet, Peter and the Wolf and the film score for Eisenstein's Alexander Nevsky. In 1948 he was denounced, with Shostakovich, for formalism, and his first wife Lina was sent to a labour camp. In poor health, he died in Moscow on 5 March 1953, the same day as Stalin, so his death went largely unnoticed.

Prokofiev's music combines motoric rhythms, sharp dissonance and sardonic wit with soaring lyrical melodies and traditional forms. His seven symphonies, five piano concertos, nine piano sonatas and the operas The Love for Three Oranges and War and Peace show his range. His later Soviet works are simpler in style, a shift both he and critics have explained partly by political pressure and partly by his own artistic development.

### Peter and the Wolf (1936)

- **Date:** 1936
- **Type:** Symphonic fairy tale for narrator and orchestra, Op. 67
- **Premiere / first published:** Moscow, 2 May 1936, Central Children's Theatre
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Peter%20and%20the%20Wolf%20Sergei%20Prokofiev)

Natalia Sats, director of the Central Children's Theatre in Moscow, commissioned Prokofiev to write a work introducing children to the instruments of the orchestra. He wrote both the text and music within a few weeks. A narrator tells the story of the young Peter, who, ignoring his grandfather's warnings, captures a wolf with the help of a bird, while the hunters arrive too late. Each character has an instrument and a theme: Peter the strings, the bird a flute, the duck an oboe, the cat a clarinet, the grandfather a bassoon, the wolf three horns and the hunters woodwind and timpani. It has been recorded with narrators ranging from Eleanor Roosevelt to David Bowie.

### Romeo and Juliet (1935–36)

- **Date:** 1935–36
- **Type:** Ballet, four acts, Op. 64
- **Premiere / first published:** Mahen Theatre, Brno, 30 December 1938; Kirov Theatre, Leningrad, 11 January 1940
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Romeo%20and%20Juliet%20Sergei%20Prokofiev)

Prokofiev wrote Romeo and Juliet for the Kirov in Leningrad and then the Bolshoi in Moscow, but the theatres rejected it as undanceable, and an early version with a happy ending caused controversy. It was finally staged in Brno in Czechoslovakia in 1938, and in Leningrad in 1940, choreographed by Leonid Lavrovsky, with Galina Ulanova as Juliet. The score portrays the characters vividly, from the menacing Dance of the Knights of the Montagues and Capulets to the tender music of Juliet as a young girl and the tragic final scenes. The concert suites Prokofiev extracted helped it gain popularity. It is now one of the most performed full-length ballets.
