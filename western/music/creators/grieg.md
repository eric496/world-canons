# Edvard Grieg

**1843 – 1907** · Norwegian  
Born: Bergen, Norway · Died: Bergen  
Eras & schools: [Nationalist schools](../movements/nationalist-schools.md)  

> Norway's leading composer, Grieg wrote the Piano Concerto in A minor, the music for Ibsen's Peer Gynt and many lyric piano pieces and songs.

**Links:** [Scores on IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/Category:Grieg,_Edvard)

## Biography

Edvard Grieg was born in Bergen in 1843 into a merchant family. On the advice of the violinist Ole Bull he studied at the Leipzig Conservatory. In Copenhagen he met the young Norwegian nationalist composer Rikard Nordraak, who inspired him to seek a Norwegian voice. He married his cousin, the soprano Nina Hagerup, who sang many of his songs. His Piano Concerto (1868) won the praise of Liszt, and Ibsen invited him to write music for Peer Gynt, first staged in 1876. A state pension from 1874 let him concentrate on composing. From 1885 he lived at Troldhaugen near Bergen, touring Europe as conductor and pianist, and he died in Bergen in 1907.

Grieg drew on Norwegian folk music, its dance rhythms, drones and modal colour, and was at his best in short forms: ten books of Lyric Pieces for piano and around 180 songs. His fresh harmonies influenced Debussy and Ravel. Pieces such as Morning Mood and In the Hall of the Mountain King became among the most familiar in all classical music.

### Piano Concerto in A minor (1868)

- **Date:** 1868
- **Type:** Piano concerto, Op. 16
- **Premiere / first published:** Casino Theatre, Copenhagen, 3 April 1869, with Edmund Neupert as soloist
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Piano%20Concerto%20in%20A%20minor%20Edvard%20Grieg)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Piano+Concerto+Op.16+Grieg)

Grieg wrote his Piano Concerto at the age of twenty-four during a summer in Denmark. Its opening, a timpani roll and a cascade of chords down the keyboard, is one of the most recognisable in the repertoire, and its themes are infused with Norwegian folk rhythms and harmonies. The form follows Schumann's Piano Concerto, in the same key, which Grieg had heard in Leipzig. Liszt played through the manuscript at sight in Rome and encouraged him warmly. Grieg revised the concerto many times, until shortly before his death. It was the first piano concerto to be recorded, in a shortened version in 1909.

### Peer Gynt (1874–75)

- **Date:** 1874–75
- **Type:** Incidental music, Op. 23; later two suites
- **Premiere / first published:** Christiania Theatre, Christiania (Oslo), 24 February 1876
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Peer%20Gynt%20Edvard%20Grieg)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Peer+Gynt+Grieg)

Ibsen asked Grieg to write music for the first staging of his verse play Peer Gynt, about a boastful, wandering Norwegian peasant who seeks himself around the world and finally returns home to the faithful Solveig. Grieg found the task difficult and called some parts loathsome, but the score became one of his best-loved works. He later extracted two orchestral suites (1888 and 1891). Morning Mood, actually set in the Moroccan desert, evokes a sunrise; In the Hall of the Mountain King grows from a quiet march into a frantic dance of trolls; and Solveig's Song is a melancholy Norwegian melody. The music has become inseparable from images of Norway.
