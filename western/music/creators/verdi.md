## Biography

Giuseppe Verdi was born in 1813 in the village of Le Roncole near Busseto, the son of an innkeeper. A local merchant, Antonio Barezzi, supported his studies; he was rejected by the Milan Conservatory but studied privately and married Barezzi's daughter Margherita. The deaths of his two children and wife in 1838–40 devastated him, but Nabucco at La Scala in 1842 made him famous. Through what he called his years in the galley he wrote opera after opera, culminating in Rigoletto, Il trovatore and La traviata (1851–53). Later works included Don Carlos for Paris and Aida for Cairo (1871). He became a national figure during the Risorgimento, briefly served in Italy's first parliament and farmed his estate at Sant'Agata with his second wife, the soprano Giuseppina Strepponi. In old age, with the librettist Arrigo Boito, he produced Otello and Falstaff. He died in Milan in 1901.

Verdi's operas centre on intense human conflicts, between fathers and daughters, love and duty, the individual and power, conveyed through memorable melody and strong theatrical pacing. Over his long career he loosened the conventional number structure of Italian opera into more continuous drama, while keeping the primacy of the voice. His Requiem of 1874 is one of the great choral works. Verdi's operas remain the core of the international repertory.

### Rigoletto (1851)

[![Rigoletto](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e7/Philippe_Chaperon_-_Rigoletto.jpg/1280px-Philippe_Chaperon_-_Rigoletto.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Philippe_Chaperon_-_Rigoletto.jpg)

<sub>Philippe Chaperon, stage design for Rigoletto Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Philippe_Chaperon_-_Rigoletto.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1851
- **Type:** Opera, three acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Teatro La Fenice, Venice, 11 March 1851
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Rigoletto%20Giuseppe%20Verdi)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Rigoletto+Verdi)

Rigoletto is based on Victor Hugo's play Le roi s'amuse, which had been banned in France after one performance. The Austrian censors in Venice objected to the depiction of a licentious king, so Verdi and his librettist Francesco Maria Piave changed the setting to the court of the Duke of Mantua. The jester Rigoletto mocks the victims of his master, the womanising Duke, but is cursed by one of them, the Count Monterone. When the Duke seduces Rigoletto's daughter Gilda, Rigoletto hires an assassin, but Gilda sacrifices herself for the Duke. Verdi's dramatic treatment, including the storm scene and the famous quartet in which four characters express different emotions simultaneously, marked a new stage in his career. The Duke's carefree 'La donna è mobile' was so catchy that Verdi kept it secret until the final rehearsal.

### La traviata (1853)

[![La traviata](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/9e/Violetta%2C_1853_Premiere_Costume.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Violetta%2C_1853_Premiere_Costume.jpg)

<sub>Costume design for Violetta at the 1853 premiere Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Violetta%2C_1853_Premiere_Costume.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1853
- **Type:** Opera, three acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Teatro La Fenice, Venice, 6 March 1853
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=La%20traviata%20Giuseppe%20Verdi)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=La+traviata+Verdi)

La traviata (The Fallen Woman) is based on the play by Alexandre Dumas fils, which Verdi saw in Paris, drawn from the life of the courtesan Marie Duplessis. Violetta Valéry, a courtesan suffering from tuberculosis, gives up her life of pleasure for the young Alfredo Germont, but his father persuades her to leave him to protect his family's reputation. Alfredo publicly insults her, and the lovers are reunited only as she dies. Unusually for its time, the opera dealt with a contemporary subject, though the censors had it set in the early eighteenth century. The premiere failed, partly because of casting, but a revised production in Venice in 1854 was a success. With its brindisi drinking song, Violetta's 'Sempre libera' and the delicate preludes, it is now one of the most performed operas in the world.

### Aida (1870–71)

- **Date:** 1870–71
- **Type:** Opera, four acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Khedivial Opera House, Cairo, 24 December 1871
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Aida%20Giuseppe%20Verdi)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Aida+Verdi)

Aida was commissioned by Isma'il Pasha, the Khedive of Egypt, for his new opera house in Cairo; the story was devised by the French Egyptologist Auguste Mariette and the libretto written by Antonio Ghislanzoni. Aida, an Ethiopian princess enslaved in Egypt, loves the Egyptian general Radamès, who is loved in turn by the Pharaoh's daughter Amneris. Radamès is tricked into betraying military secrets and is buried alive, and Aida joins him in the tomb. The opera combines grand spectacle, as in the triumphal scene with its long trumpets, with intimate drama. The Cairo premiere was delayed by the Franco-Prussian War, as the sets and costumes were trapped in besieged Paris. It is one of the most popular operas.

### Messa da Requiem (1873–74)

[![Messa da Requiem](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cc/Requiem_%28Verdi%29_Titelblatt_%281874%29.jpg)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Requiem_%28Verdi%29_Titelblatt_%281874%29.jpg)

<sub>Title page of the 1874 edition Image: Wikimedia Commons. [Full resolution, credit and licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Requiem_%28Verdi%29_Titelblatt_%281874%29.jpg).</sub>

- **Date:** 1873–74
- **Type:** Requiem mass
- **Premiere / first published:** San Marco, Milan, 22 May 1874, conducted by Verdi
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Messa%20da%20Requiem%20Giuseppe%20Verdi)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Requiem+Verdi)

After the death of Rossini in 1868, Verdi proposed a joint requiem by Italian composers, to which he contributed the Libera me, but the project collapsed. When the poet and novelist Alessandro Manzoni, author of The Betrothed, whom Verdi revered, died in 1873, he wrote a whole requiem in his memory, incorporating a revised version of the Libera me. It was first performed in the church of San Marco in Milan on the first anniversary of Manzoni's death. The Dies irae, with its hammer-blows on the bass drum and offstage trumpets in the Tuba mirum, became famous. Hans von Bülow called it an opera in church vestments, a judgement Brahms rejected. It is now one of the most performed choral works.

### Otello (1884–86)

- **Date:** 1884–86
- **Type:** Opera, four acts
- **Premiere / first published:** La Scala, Milan, 5 February 1887
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Otello%20Giuseppe%20Verdi)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Otello+Verdi)

After Aida Verdi announced his retirement, but his publisher Giulio Ricordi and the composer-poet Arrigo Boito lured him back with a libretto based on Shakespeare's Othello. Boito compressed the play, dropping the Venice act, and wrote Iago a 'Credo' in which he declares his belief in a cruel God. The opera opens in the middle of a storm and moves through continuous music, with Otello's love duet with Desdemona, his growing jealousy and the final scene, in which Desdemona sings the Willow Song and Ave Maria before Otello kills her and then himself. The premiere at La Scala was a national event. It is widely regarded as the summit of Italian tragic opera.
