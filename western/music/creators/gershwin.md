## Biography

George Gershwin was born in Brooklyn in 1898 to Russian Jewish immigrant parents. He left school at fifteen to work as a song plugger on Tin Pan Alley, and his song Swanee, recorded by Al Jolson in 1920, made him rich. With his brother Ira as lyricist he wrote a series of Broadway musicals, including Lady, Be Good and Of Thee I Sing, and standards such as The Man I Love and I Got Rhythm. Paul Whiteman's commission of Rhapsody in Blue (1924) launched his concert career, followed by the Piano Concerto in F and An American in Paris. Porgy and Bess (1935) was his most ambitious work. He moved to Hollywood to write film songs and died there of a brain tumour in 1937, aged thirty-eight.

Gershwin's music fuses the melody and harmony of popular song, the blue notes and syncopation of jazz and the forms of classical music. Ravel and Schoenberg admired him. Porgy and Bess, written for an African American cast, was long controversial for its portrayal of Black life by white creators, but songs such as Summertime became jazz standards and the opera is now staged by major companies worldwide.

### Rhapsody in Blue (1924)

- **Date:** 1924
- **Type:** Rhapsody for piano and jazz band
- **Premiere / first published:** Aeolian Hall, New York, 12 February 1924, Paul Whiteman's orchestra with Gershwin as soloist
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Rhapsody%20in%20Blue%20George%20Gershwin)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Rhapsody+in+Blue+Gershwin)

The bandleader Paul Whiteman announced a concert entitled An Experiment in Modern Music, and Gershwin found from a newspaper that he was writing a jazz concerto for it. He composed it in about three weeks, and Ferde Grofé orchestrated it for Whiteman's jazz band. It opens with a clarinet trill and a rising wail; according to legend the glissando was developed by Whiteman's clarinettist Ross Gorman during rehearsals. The work alternates piano solos, jazz-band episodes and a broad romantic melody. In the audience were Rachmaninoff, Stravinsky, Heifetz and Sousa. Later orchestrated for full symphony orchestra, it became a symbol of the Jazz Age and of New York.

### Porgy and Bess (1934–35)

- **Date:** 1934–35
- **Type:** Opera, three acts
- **Premiere / first published:** Colonial Theatre, Boston, 30 September 1935 (tryout); Alvin Theatre, New York, 10 October 1935
- **Read more:** [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?search=Porgy%20and%20Bess%20George%20Gershwin)
- **Links:** [Free scores on IMSLP (search)](https://imslp.org/index.php?search=Porgy+and+Bess+Gershwin)

Porgy and Bess is based on DuBose Heyward's novel Porgy and the play by DuBose and Dorothy Heyward, with lyrics by DuBose Heyward and Ira Gershwin. Set in Catfish Row, a fictional African American tenement in Charleston, South Carolina, it tells of the disabled beggar Porgy, who tries to rescue Bess from her violent lover Crown and the drug dealer Sportin' Life. Gershwin called it a folk opera and insisted on an all-Black cast of classically trained singers. Its songs, including Summertime, I Got Plenty o' Nuttin' and It Ain't Necessarily So, mix jazz, blues, spirituals and operatic writing. The first Broadway run was a financial failure. Some African American critics criticised its stereotypes, and its reception is still debated, but the Metropolitan Opera staged it in 1985 and it is now part of the international repertory.
